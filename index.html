  • Cryptographer validates Claude’s reconstruction of five NIST curve seeds

    AI 2026-10-18
    Cryptographer Filippo Valsorda validated Anthropic’s reconstruction of five seeds that the U.S. National Security Agency supplied for elliptic curves adopted by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), resolving a long-running question about how the parameters were generated [8][6]. Anthropic said its Claude research model traced the seeds to lrand48, a 1990s C-library random-number generator initialized with Unix timestamps. Valsorda rewrote the check in Go and accepted the solution [8]. The result explains how the public curve parameters were produced but does not provide a way to recover private keys or break encryption, Anthropic said [6]. Its researchers published code to reproduce the results but said some related choices remain unexplained, including why the original process skipped other seemingly suitable candidates [6][8].
