  • NASA’s Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth after 49 years

    Technology 2026-11-19
    NASA’s Voyager 1 reached a distance of one light-day from Earth at 5:16:07 a.m. Eastern on Nov. 18, becoming the first human-made object to reach that distance. A light-day is about 16.1 billion miles; radio commands now take roughly 24 hours to reach the probe, and replies take another day to return [21][26][32]. Launched in 1977 to study the outer planets, Voyager 1 entered interstellar space in 2012 and still sends measurements from beyond the Sun’s protective bubble. Its remaining magnetometer and plasma-wave instrument record magnetic fields and charged particles, but the spacecraft’s plutonium power supply continues to weaken [12][18][33]. Engineers must wait roughly two days to check whether a command worked, and a recent loss of contact lasted two days. NASA’s small team continues to manage the aging craft remotely; Kareem Badaruddin, Voyager mission manager at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory, estimated its operational life could extend another three to five years [21][11][26].

Researchers continue searching southern China’s caves for fossils that could help identify the mystery tooth and clarify the species’ history [12][2].
