  • Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS formed in a cold, nitrogen-rich region

    Science 2026-09-15
    Astronomers say interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS likely formed in an extremely cold, nitrogen-rich region far from its parent star. The object is only the third confirmed interstellar visitor detected in the Solar System. Its unusually high nitrogen content indicates it formed where temperatures may have been below 30 kelvins, cold enough to trap nitrogen in cometary ice [2][3][4][6]. Researchers used the WEAVE Large-IFU instrument on the William Herschel Telescope in Spain to examine the comet’s plasma tail on Nov. 30 and Dec. 2, 2025, after its closest approach to the Sun. They identified ions associated with nitrogen, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, water and methylidyne, then compared the nitrogen-to-carbon-monoxide ratio with those of Solar System comets. The result is consistent with earlier evidence of elevated deuterium and other isotopic signatures associated with cold, distant formation conditions [2][5][4].

  • Mercury shrank by up to 19 kilometers, study finds

    Science 2026-09-15
    Mercury has contracted by nearly 19 kilometers (12 miles) in diameter since its formation, more than earlier estimates suggested, according to research led by scientists at the German Aerospace Center’s Institute of Space Research. The team inferred the additional contraction from surface wrinkles and other tectonic structures that earlier studies may have missed because impact-crater debris covered them [4][1][3]. The researchers combined existing maps of Mercury’s contraction-related faults with planet-wide maps of surface roughness. They found that rougher, geologically fresher areas preserve more of these structures, while resurfacing by impact ejecta can conceal them. The study estimates that Mercury’s original radius may have been up to 10 kilometers larger than it is today [5].
