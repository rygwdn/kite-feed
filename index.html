  • Researchers measure AI-written webpages as detection tools emerge

    AI 2026-09-04
    Researchers and analysts published measurements and tools for tracking AI-generated writing online. One academic project found that by mid-2025 roughly 35% of newly published websites in its sample were classified as AI-generated or AI-assisted, up from zero before ChatGPT’s public launch in late 2022 [3]. A separate Pew Research Center analysis examined signs of AI authorship in nearly half a million English-language webpages from the Common Crawl archive [4]. The academic project, led by Jonas Dolezal, Sawood Alam, Mark Graham and Maty Bohacek, reported evidence that rising AI-generated text correlates with lower semantic diversity and more positive sentiment. Pew said the upward trend began after OpenAI released ChatGPT in November 2022 and continued as chatbots including Claude and Gemini followed; Pew also reported that around half of U.S. adults use AI chatbots, including 24% who use them daily [4]. Developers have built consumer-facing detection tools, including the open-source Stop the Slop browser extension, which analyzes YouTube transcripts and flags likely AI-generated scripts or robotic narration with thumbnail badges, in-player indicators, sentence breakdowns and local or edge caching [1]. Separate essays by Arnold Kling and Benedict Evans argued that AI’s effect should be assessed against earlier computing shifts and the practical realities of enterprise software adoption, rather than by model capability alone [2][5].
