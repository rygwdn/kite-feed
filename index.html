  • AI developer Anthropic pitches draft bill limiting federal power over models

    AI 2026-10-22
    Anthropic, the developer of the Claude AI assistant, has circulated a draft bill limiting when the U.S. Commerce Department could seek a court order to halt an advanced AI model. The model would have to pose a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than 50 deaths or serious injuries, or more than $1 billion in damage. The threat would have to involve a cyberattack, a model evading its developer’s controls, or the creation or release of a biological weapon. A federal judge would decide whether to block the model [7][17][10]. The proposal would bar other federal agencies from independently suspending a model. Critics say the high thresholds and court requirement constrain regulators, while Anthropic says its approach would allow intervention against dangerous models. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei defended the company’s safety record, arguing that it must stay at the frontier to influence industry standards. Anthropic has said it will revise parts of the draft, including its language on preempting state rules, and develop evaluator requirements [21][7].

  • Northwestern physicists trap single positron for months to test antimatter symmetry

    Science 2026-10-22
    Physicists at Northwestern University have trapped a single positron—the antimatter counterpart of an electron—for months and controlled its quantum states, enabling them to measure the particle’s magnetic moment [2][4][5]. The experiment could test whether positrons and electrons obey a key symmetry predicted by the Standard Model, the theory describing fundamental particles and forces. A mismatch could point to physics beyond that model and provide clues to why the universe contains far more matter than antimatter [4][3]. The team used a tungsten crystal to slow positrons emitted by radioactive sodium, then confined one in a vacuum with electric and magnetic fields cooled to 300 millikelvins [2][8]. Researchers used microwaves to move it between quantum energy levels and detect changes without disturbing the measured state [5][8]. The team has not yet reported the positron’s magnetic-moment measurement and aims to publish a result early in 2027, though the timing could slip [2].
