The dung likely came from a large predatory theropod, possibly a Tyrannosaurus rex or Nanotyrannus, and also contained fish scales and bones of a hesperornithiform, an extinct loon-like diving bird [1][2][8]. The researchers said the feathers probably came from the same aquatic bird and described them as the first hesperornithiform feathers identified [1][8]. The study, published in Current Biology, uses the fossil to examine why neornithines, the ancestors of modern birds, survived the asteroid-driven extinction event 66 million years ago while hesperornithiforms and other bird lineages disappeared [8][10]. Jingmai O'Connor said the find indicates that living near water alone cannot explain survival because hesperornithiforms were aquatic yet went extinct [8].

  • Study finds Mercury shrank by up to 23 kilometers

    Science 2026-09-11
    A study published Sept. 10 in Geophysical Research Letters says Mercury has contracted 10% to 30% more than earlier estimates indicated since the planet formed, equivalent to about 11.6 kilometers in radius or as much as about 23 kilometers in diameter [9][8][19][21]. Scientists from the German Aerospace Center’s Institute of Space Research reported the result for Mercury, the smallest planet and the closest one to the sun, which measures roughly 4,900 kilometers across today [9][12]. Mercury’s contraction is linked to internal cooling: as the planet’s interior loses heat, its crust buckles into ridges and scarps. The amount of shrinkage helps scientists test whether Mercury has a larger metallic core, fewer light elements such as silicon, or a higher initial temperature than prior models assumed [10][15][16]. The finding was reported one week after the European-Japanese BepiColombo spacecraft pair shed its cruising platform ahead of an expected Mercury orbit insertion in November 2026 [9][12].
