  • Study says 'hobbit' humans scavenged Komodo dragon leftovers

    Science 2026-07-04
    A study published July 3 in Science Advances argues that Homo floresiensis, the extinct small-bodied human relative found in Liang Bua cave on Indonesia’s Flores island, probably scavenged meat left by Komodo dragons rather than hunting dwarf elephants or cooking them over controlled fires [2][6][7][10]. The findings challenge earlier interpretations that stone tools, cut-marked Stegodon bones and burned remains at the site pointed to relatively advanced behavior in the species [2][6][9]. The international team, led by Elizabeth Grace Veatch and colleagues, reexamined animal bones from Liang Bua and compared fossil damage with marks produced in an experiment in which a Komodo dragon fed on a goat carcass [5][6][10][11]. Researchers said they found no evidence that Homo floresiensis hunted large animals, and that the bone damage fits a sequence in which Komodo dragons consumed the meatier portions first while hominins later cut remaining tissue from carcasses [1][9][11]. Homo floresiensis stood about 1 meter tall, had a brain only slightly larger than a chimpanzee’s and disappeared around 50,000 years ago as Homo sapiens spread through Southeast Asia [6][2][11]. The authors said the findings call for reassessing the species’ abilities and evolutionary pathway because controlled fire use and big-game hunting are behaviors usually associated with larger-brained hominins such as Neanderthals, Homo sapiens and Homo erectus [6][10].
