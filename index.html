  • Mars orbiter detects volcanic gas above Alba Mons fissures

    Science 2026-11-05
    The European Space Agency’s ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter has detected hydrogen chloride above fissures near Alba Mons, a vast Martian volcano, in a pattern researchers say may indicate gas escaping from magma beneath the surface [6][4][5]. The gas appeared at a location and during a season that atmospheric models could not explain, and images show narrow fractures nearby [2][5]. The study, published in Science Advances, identifies a possible volcanic origin but does not confirm an eruption [13][5]. Researchers propose that the fractures connect to underground magmatic sources. Ice may seal them in winter, then sublimate as temperatures rise, allowing trapped gas to escape. The orbiter observed recurring, localized signals over four Earth years of targeted measurements [4][6]. Atmospheric processes and surface storage could also explain the gas, and no new lava flow or eruption has been directly observed [5][13]. If magma heats subsurface water, it could create hydrothermal environments of interest in the search for microbial life, but the study found no evidence of life itself [4]. Researchers say further observations, including over the Tharsis and Cerberus regions, could test whether similar gas emissions occur elsewhere [2].
