  • Deep partial lunar eclipse spans the Americas and Europe

    Science 2026-08-27
    Skywatchers across the Americas can see a deep partial lunar eclipse overnight Thursday, Aug. 27, into Friday, Aug. 28, as the full Moon moves through Earth’s shadow and roughly 96% of its visible surface is covered at maximum [6][44][54][11]. The event begins at 9:23 p.m. EDT, the darker umbral phase starts between 10:33 p.m. and 10:34 p.m., maximum eclipse occurs shortly after midnight, and the eclipse ends around 3:01 a.m. EDT [6][16][10]. In the UK, Ireland and parts of continental Europe, the eclipse occurs before dawn Friday while the Moon sits low near the western horizon; UK accounts place the penumbral start at 02:23 BST, the partial phase at 03:33 BST and maximum at 05:12 BST, when about 90% to 93% of the Moon’s diameter will be inside Earth’s darkest shadow [1][2][15]. The Moon can appear red or coppery because sunlight passing through Earth’s atmosphere loses much of its shorter-wavelength blue light while redder light bends toward the lunar surface [12][21][63]. The eclipse is not a total lunar eclipse because a thin part of the Moon remains outside the umbra, leaving a bright edge while the shadowed area darkens [44][23]. Weather and horizon obstructions will determine local visibility, with BBC forecasters pointing to better UK chances in southern and eastern England and possible breaks in parts of Northern Ireland and western Scotland [1][59].
