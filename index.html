    Science 2026-11-22
    BepiColombo, a joint mission of the European Space Agency and Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency, entered orbit around Mercury on Nov. 21, completing an eight-year journey and becoming the second mission to orbit the planet [4][2][13]. The two orbiters remain attached; they are scheduled to separate on Dec. 9–10 and take up different polar orbits, with science operations due to begin in April 2027 [1][2]. Their instruments are designed to examine the planet’s surface and interior, its magnetic field and the thin gas envelope around it, including how the magnetic field interacts with the solar wind [2][4]. Controllers in Germany confirmed the spacecraft’s capture several hours after a series of braking thruster firings, with the spacecraft about 152 million kilometers away [4]. A propulsion-system problem delayed BepiColombo’s arrival from December 2025. Its nominal science mission is planned to last one Earth year, with an extension through 2029 [2].
