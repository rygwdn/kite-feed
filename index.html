  • EU starts enforcing AI labels and model rules

    AI 2026-08-02
    The European Commission’s AI Office and national authorities began enforcing parts of the EU AI Act on 2 August 2026, including transparency rules that require some AI systems to tell users when they are interacting with AI and some AI-generated or manipulated content to carry labels [35][39][11][12][14]. The obligations cover chatbots and other interactive AI systems, as well as deepfakes and certain images, audio and video resembling real people, objects, places, entities or events [35][17][13]. The rules also require machine-readable marks for certain AI-generated or manipulated content and apply to text published to inform the public on matters of public interest when no human review or editorial control has taken place [35][22]. Existing AI systems have until 2 December 2026 to adapt, and the law includes exemptions for artistic, creative, satirical and fictional work [11][12][14][15]. For advanced general-purpose AI models, the AI Office can test models, seek access from companies and, in some cases, require access before models enter the market; EU officials said deployment in the bloc can be restricted if companies do not address concerns [12][14][15]. The Commission said national market surveillance authorities, the AI Office and the European Data Protection Supervisor can enforce transparency rules, with companies facing sanctions of up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover [35].
