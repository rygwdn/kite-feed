  • Thai scientists identify two new long-necked dinosaur species

    Science 2026-07-11
    Thai paleontologists have identified Uragasaurus kalasinensis, a newly named long-necked, plant-eating dinosaur from fossils found at the Phu Noi fossil site in Kalasin Province in northeastern Thailand [2][3][4][5][6][7]. The animal lived about 150 million years ago in the Late Jurassic, measured up to 20 metres and belonged to Mamenchisauridae, a sauropod family known for extremely long necks [2][4][5][6]. Researchers identified the species from a dorsal vertebra recovered from the Phu Kradung Formation. CT scans showed diagnostic features, including a Y-shaped arrangement of supporting laminae and an internal air-cavity system [2][5][6]. Most known mamenchisaurid fossils have been found in China. The Thailand specimen is reported as the first formally named mamenchisaurid from Thailand and, according to VietnamPlus, the first formally named member of the family in Southeast Asia [2][3][4][5]. A separate discovery in Thailand is a sauropod from Chaiyaphum Province dated to roughly 100 million to 120 million years ago [1]. The fossils were first noticed near a drying pond in 2016, and scientists formally confirmed the species on May 14, 2026; ScienceBlog reported that it was 27 metres long, weighed 27 tonnes and was the largest dinosaur yet found in Southeast Asia [1].
