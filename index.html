    Researchers at Japan’s Toho University have documented how dwarf reed snakes roll rapidly to flee danger while keeping their heads pointed forward. They reached speeds of 1.7 to 3.7 meters per second, about four times their usual slithering speed [1][6][7]. Each cycle begins with the snake rolling its body against the ground and lifting its front end. It then swings its tail past its head and down to start another roll. The researchers recorded the behavior in three Pseudorabdion species, while six comparison snakes from the genus Calamaria did not cartwheel [6].
