    A Science Advances study led by researchers at UC San Diego’s Scripps Institution of Oceanography used computer models to examine whether marine cloud brightening could weaken an anticipated “super” El Niño by cooling parts of the Pacific Ocean [2][10][11]. Researchers evaluated whether brighter marine clouds could reflect more sunlight and reduce heat linked to floods, extreme heat and other El Niño-driven conditions [2][5][7]. Jessica Wan, a University of Chicago postdoctoral researcher who led the study during graduate work at Scripps, told Gizmodo the modeled intervention turned an extreme or super El Niño into a neutral event [11].
