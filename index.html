  • Solar telescope reveals first whirlpools on sun’s surface

    Science 2026-08-06
    Scientists using the National Science Foundation’s Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope on Maui, Hawaii, captured the highest-resolution images to date of the sun’s visible surface, or photosphere, revealing small whirling and feathery patterns not previously seen in such detail [7][13][9][5]. The findings, published Wednesday in Nature, identify the patterns as Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities, a fluid-dynamics phenomenon long predicted on the sun but not directly observed there before [5][7][12][26]. The patterns form where magnetized plasma streams move past one another at different speeds, creating ripples and vortices comparable to waves stirred by wind over water [13][26][12]. Researchers said the motions can twist the sun’s magnetic field and may help explain how magnetic energy builds before solar flares and coronal mass ejections, which can disrupt satellites, GPS, communications and power grids on Earth [7][5][19]. The observations came from a telescope built for high-resolution solar studies near the summit of Haleakalā, where clear skies support detailed imaging [7][34][53]. NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day described the false-yellow featured image, recorded in deep blue light, as the highest-resolution visible-light image yet of the sun and said it spans about the radius of Earth [9].
