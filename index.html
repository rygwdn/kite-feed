  • Harvard-led team explains basketball sneaker squeaks with pulses

    Science 2026-02-26
    Researchers have pinned down a courtside mystery: why basketball sneakers squeak. Using high-speed imaging and careful measurements, the team linked the squeak’s pitch to how often these detachment pulses happen—not to a simple, steady sliding motion [2][3][8]. Several outlets describe the work as a sharper, more detailed take on classic stick-slip friction: at soft–rigid interfaces, the motion can break into organized pulses. That helps explain why some shoe–floor pairings squeal sharply while other seemingly smooth contacts slide more quietly [3][6][8]. Beyond satisfying curiosity, the same physics could guide the design of synthetic materials and may offer clues about how frictional slipping works on geological faults, as noted in coverage [3][8].
