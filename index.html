  • OpenAI claims Navier-Stokes solution using 10,000 agents

    AI 2026-09-09
    OpenAI said on Sept. 8 that an unreleased AI model produced a solution to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems designated by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000 [27][19][25][55]. The company said roughly 10,000 AI agents worked in parallel for 88 hours and that the result shows a smooth three-dimensional fluid flow can develop a finite-time singularity, or “blowup,” under the problem’s conditions [19][21][25][55]. The Clay Mathematics Institute has not yet accepted the claim, and several reports noted that formal prize recognition requires scrutiny by mathematicians beyond OpenAI [55][30][36]. OpenAI said it does not intend to claim the $1 million Millennium Prize if the result is confirmed [30][36][80]. The announcement prompted a dispute over credit and the origins of the research after NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster said he and Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge had been working on closely related problems and had used OpenAI’s Codex during their project [7][25][55]. Buckmaster said he learned on Sept. 3 that information about their progress had been passed to OpenAI; OpenAI researcher Sebastien Bubeck denied that the company used the pair’s work or accessed material shared with OpenAI’s servers [25][55][33].
