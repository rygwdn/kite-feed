    AI 2026-10-19
    AI company Anthropic has presented U.S. lawmakers with a draft bill that would let the federal government block an AI model’s release if it poses an imminent risk of killing or seriously injuring more than 50 people or causing more than $1 billion in damage [1][15][13]. The proposal limits qualifying threats to the creation or release of a biological weapon, AI-enabled cyberattacks, or an AI lab losing control of a model or agents [15][13]. The draft would require AI developers to test their models and publish the findings. Independent evaluators registered with the U.S. Commerce Department would periodically review companies and models [13][14][15]. If an evaluator identifies an imminent qualifying threat, it must report it within 72 hours. The department would then present evidence to a federal judge in Washington, D.C., who would decide whether to halt a release [15]. The draft says no other federal agency or official could order a company to stop releasing a model [15]. Anthropic said the bill reflects proposals in its Advanced AI Framework, which it has advocated to federal and state lawmakers since June [13][15]. The Trump administration has rejected calls for increased AI oversight, Deccan Chronicle reported [13].
