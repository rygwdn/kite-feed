  • Study challenges genetic theory of insect social evolution

    Science 2026-08-10
    A Current Biology study by Arizona State University researchers analyzed nearly 69,000 insect species and questioned a long-standing genetic explanation for how eusocial insect societies evolved [1]. The study examined haplodiploidy, an inheritance system in which females carry two chromosome sets and males carry one, after earlier theories linked that system to societies with queens and workers [1]. The researchers found that eusociality initially appeared to evolve more often in haplodiploid insects, but almost the entire association traced to one branch: aculeate Hymenoptera, which includes ants, bees and stinging wasps [1]. Across insects as a whole, haplodiploidy did not independently predict whether eusociality evolved, and the authors said traits tied to particular lineages better explain repeated origins of complex insect societies [1].
