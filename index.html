    A team led by Griffith University archaeologist Adam Brumm said dental evidence from Sulawesi, Indonesia, suggests prehistoric hunter-gatherers used betel nut as a psychoactive substance as early as 25,000 years ago, potentially the oldest known evidence of habitual human drug use [1][3][4][5][6][7][8][9][10][12][13]. The study, published Sept. 9 in Science Advances, examined two rare pre-agricultural skeletons from Sulawesi: one dated to between 25,000 and 16,000 years ago, and another to between 7,600 and 6,300 years ago [4][9][10][13]. The team suggested the practice may have begun as a way to relieve toothache before becoming habitual, placing betel use well before agriculture and before the Neolithic psychoactive use previously associated with fermented drinks [4][6][9].
