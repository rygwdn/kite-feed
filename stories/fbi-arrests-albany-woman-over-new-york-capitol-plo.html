FBI arrests Albany woman over New York Capitol plot

Category: World

Federal authorities charged Jessica Bowie, 35, of Albany, New York, with attempting to provide material support or resources to a designated foreign terrorist organization, accusing her of planning an Islamic State-inspired bombing at the New York State Capitol in Albany [7][9][12][13][17][20]. Prosecutors said Bowie wanted to use an explosive device to damage the Capitol and kill lawmakers. The Justice Department said she was arrested on Wednesday, August 19, after obtaining what she believed was an explosive device [7][12][17][24][28][20]. The criminal complaint says Bowie communicated with FBI confidential sources whom she believed were linked to ISIS, asked for help learning to make explosives, and pledged allegiance to the group in audio recordings and online messages [9][15][14][17][45]. Authorities said she chose the Albany statehouse after considering other targets, including the White House, and allegedly told agents she wanted the attack to have an “affect on the American system” [7][12][42][30]. Bowie appeared in federal court in Albany on Thursday and was ordered detained pending trial, according to local reporting [1][28][18]. If convicted on the material-support charge, she could face up to 20 years in prison, a fine of up to $250,000 and lifetime supervised release [23][28][20].

Key Points

  • Capitol surveillance: Investigators said Bowie visited the Capitol grounds at least five times, took dozens of photos and studied security arrangements before the alleged plot advanced [7][14][17][24].
  • Delivery disguise: Court filings say Bowie discussed hiding a bomb in a DoorDash-style food delivery bag to enter the building, and images of the bag appeared in charging materials [7][13][21][39].
  • Online activity: The complaint alleges accounts tied to Bowie posted support for terrorism between May and July, including praise for the September 11, 2001 attacks [15][13][10][12].
  • Sting operation: FBI sources supplied an inert explosive device and a nonfunctioning or defective gun, and agents arrested Bowie after a meeting [7][1][28][52].

Perspectives

Justice Department National Security Division: Assistant Attorney General John A. Eisenberg said investigators stopped Bowie “before she was able to act on her plot to bring terror to the Capital Region.”
