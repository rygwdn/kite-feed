Smile spacecraft captures sunlit auroras in first Earth images

Category: Science

The European-Chinese Smile spacecraft has released its first images, showing a vast aurora around the sunlit North Pole in ultraviolet light [2][3]. The spacecraft took the images in July, and the mission released them Sept. 30 as science operations began. They show an auroral ring covering about a third of Earth’s visible disk [2]. Smile launched May 19 and is designed to observe the North Pole from an elongated orbit for up to 45 hours at a time. Its soft X-ray imager also captured a test observation of the supernova remnant Cassiopeia A, but its detector needs further calibration when pointed at Earth [2].

Key Points

  • Instrument suite: Alongside its ultraviolet aurora imager, Smile carries instruments to detect X-rays, magnetic fields and light ions near Earth [2].

Timeline

  • 19 May 2026: Smile launched on a three-year Earth-observation mission [2]
  • July 2026: Smile’s ultraviolet imager captured the aurora around the North Pole [2]
  • 30 September 2026: The mission released its first images to the public [2]

Technical Details

  • Extended viewing orbit: Smile’s highly elliptical orbit reaches about 75,000 miles (121,000 kilometers) above the North Pole, allowing observations lasting up to 45 hours [2].
  • Ultraviolet imaging: The Ultraviolet Aurora Imager records auroras that are difficult to see in visible light against the sunlit Earth [2].
