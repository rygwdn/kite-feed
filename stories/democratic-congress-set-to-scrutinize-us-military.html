Democratic Congress set to scrutinize U.S. military support for Israel

Category: World

Democrats won control of both chambers of the U.S. Congress in the Nov. 3 midterm elections, with several incoming lawmakers campaigning to restrict military aid or arms sales to Israel [2][17][1]. The results could bring new congressional scrutiny of U.S. support and Israeli actions in the occupied West Bank, but do not themselves change policy. President Donald Trump can veto legislation, and Democrats are unlikely to have the two-thirds majority needed to override him [17][10]. On election day, Trump said the United States would end about $4 billion in annual military assistance to Israel by the end of 2026, saying he had reached an agreement with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The existing aid agreement runs through September 2028. The announcement left unclear whether Trump can halt the funding or how arms sales and other defense cooperation would be affected [1][17]. The 2027 National Defense Authorization Act contains proposals to expand U.S.-Israeli defense-industry cooperation. It remains unclear whether Congress will act on the bill before Democrats take control and whether Democratic leaders will permit votes or hearings on aid and the wars in Gaza and Iran [1][4]. Israel's Oct. 27 election left Netanyahu's bloc without a parliamentary majority, and opposition politicians blamed his leadership for weakening bipartisan U.S. support [5][14].

Key Points

  • Defense legislation: Fifty-six advocacy groups urged Congress to remove Section 219 of the House-passed 2027 defense bill. Ro Khanna and Thomas Massie proposed an amendment to remove it, but the House did not vote on it [1].
  • West Bank scrutiny: In September, 46 of 47 Democratic senators backed a resolution requiring the administration to report on rising settler violence against Palestinians in the West Bank; Republicans blocked it [17][20].
  • Different positions: Incoming Texas Senator James Talarico has supported defensive systems such as Iron Dome while calling for an end to offensive weapons sales [1].
  • Potential cross-party pressure: Strategist Price Floyd said progressives and Republicans in the America First wing might unite in opposing taxpayer spending abroad, amid anger over the Iran war [17][10].
  • Lobbying tactics: The pro-Israel advocacy group AIPAC said last month that its open support was hurting some candidates and announced it would stop making direct endorsements and contributions [3].

Perspectives

Yair Lapid, Israeli opposition leader: Lapid called for Israel to return to bipartisan cooperation with Washington, saying it should work with both U.S. parties rather than choose sides [5].

  • Defense cooperation: Section 219 of the proposed National Defense Authorization Act would expand cooperation between U.S. and Israeli defense industries through joint weapons development, research, production and technology sharing. The Senate draft includes a comparable initiative [1].

Historical Background

In 2015, Netanyahu addressed Congress to oppose President Barack Obama's negotiations over Iran's nuclear program, aligning himself with Republican opponents of the deal [5].
