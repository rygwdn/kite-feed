B.C. coastal communities face flood risk as king tides meet storms

Category: Canada

British Columbia’s coast faced heightened flood risk as king tides coincided with heavy rain, strong winds and storm surge, prompting evacuation alerts, local states of emergency and ferry cancellations [4][6]. Environment and Climate Change Canada warned that water levels in parts of Metro Vancouver could rise 40 to 70 centimetres above the year’s highest astronomical tides; levels along Vancouver Island and the central coast were forecast to rise 30 to 50 centimetres above those tides [4][3]. The same high-tide episode brought flooding and destructive surf to California, while B.C.’s immediate threat came from tides combined with a series of storms [3]. Alerts covered low-lying communities on Vancouver Island, including Lantzville, Cedar and French Creek. Ahousaht declared a local state of emergency as water eroded its shoreline. Squamish also declared an emergency, as did the Comox Valley Regional District on Wednesday evening in response to rising water and flood risk [4][6]. The B.C. River Forecast Centre upgraded a flood watch between Nanaimo and Campbell River, and Environment Canada warned that warm temperatures could bring rain on snow, increasing runoff. Authorities also warned of coastal erosion and urged residents to move valuables and toxic chemicals out of basements. Forecasters expected the highest tides Thursday [4][6].

Key Points

  • Rainfall totals: A Vancouver Island monitoring station recorded 469 millimetres of rain between 11 a.m. Monday and 1 p.m. Wednesday, while winds reached nearly 160 km/h off the island’s northern tip [3][6]
  • Road disruption: Highway 16 on Haida Gwaii closed Tuesday after debris and water covered the road [4]
  • Local preparation: Delta said it was preparing low-lying coastal neighbourhoods, while Tofino and Ucluelet closed public beach access ahead of the storms [4]
  • Residents at risk: Squamish officials said they were paying particular attention to an encampment along waterways, where unhoused residents faced direct exposure to flooding [4]

Perspectives

Quadra Island resident Marc Doll said a smaller bridge on his property was swept away, leaving 12 pigs cut off. He waded through thigh-deep water to feed a sow and her eight piglets [3].
