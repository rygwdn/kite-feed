Astronomers find Neptune-sized planet orbiting M dwarf in reverse

Category: Science

Astronomers have identified GJ 3090 b, a Neptune-sized planet that orbits its small, cool M-dwarf star in the direction opposite the star’s rotation. Researchers say it is the first confirmed planet with a retrograde orbit around an M dwarf, a class that accounts for most stars in the Milky Way [1][2][3]. An international research team used high-resolution observations to determine the planet’s orbital orientation. The study included researchers at Queen Mary University of London. Scientists have not established how the planet acquired its unusual orbit [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Formation possibility: Researchers plan to test whether a second, misaligned disk of material around the young star could have shaped the system [3].
  • Further observations: The team says additional observations are needed to find out whether other planets in the system have similarly extreme orbital alignments [3].
  • Expected alignment: Planets forming from the same rotating disk of gas and dust as their star are generally expected to orbit in the star’s rotation direction [1].

Perspectives

Dr. Vincent Bourrier, University of Geneva: Bourrier suggested the young star’s surroundings may have a larger role in determining planetary architecture than expected [3].

Technical Details

  • Orbital obliquity: The measured angle between the star’s spin axis and the planet’s orbital axis is about 136 degrees; an angle above 90 degrees indicates a retrograde orbit [1].
  • NIRPS observations: NIRPS is a high-resolution near-infrared spectrograph that separates incoming light by wavelength [1].
