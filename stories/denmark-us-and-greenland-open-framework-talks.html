Denmark, US and Greenland open framework talks

Category: World

Denmark, the United States and Greenland have begun senior-level technical talks in Washington on a possible “framework” arrangement tied to Greenland and Arctic security, with Danish officials describing the first meeting as “very constructive” and expressing increased optimism about the dialogue [1][4][2]. The discussions are framed as a way to address President Trump’s push for a bigger U.S. role on the strategically located Arctic island while stopping short of any transfer of sovereignty, which Denmark and Greenland say is not on the table [2][3]. Greenland’s Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen and Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen have emphasized that cooperation can expand in areas like security, but they also stress “red lines” and democratic principles, with sovereignty described as non-negotiable [3]. As the diplomatic effort widens, Denmark’s King Frederik is set to visit Greenland in February, while reporting from Greenland highlights Inuit views that the land is shared rather than owned—adding an on-the-ground perspective to a debate often discussed in strategic terms [7][8].

  • Royal visit: Denmark’s King Frederik announced a Greenland trip for February 18–20 as Denmark underscores its position that the Arctic island remains within the Danish realm [7].
