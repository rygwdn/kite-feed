McGill and Concordia drop Quebec tuition hike lawsuit

Category: Canada

McGill University and Concordia University say they’re ending their court fight over Quebec’s decision to keep higher tuition for students from other Canadian provinces [1][2][3]. The move winds down their legal challenge even after a court ruling last year said the government didn’t justify the increase [1]. The dispute centres on Quebec’s plan to raise tuition rates for out-of-province students starting in 2024 [1]. For students and families, the bottom line is unchanged for now: the higher sticker price stays in place [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Scale of increase: Quebec is keeping a 33% tuition increase for students from other provinces, the universities said [3].
  • What changes now: Dropping the lawsuit closes off the active court challenge and shifts the fight to political and administrative channels [2][3].
