FBI briefly halts RCMP cooperation over CBC 9/11 memo

Category: Canada

The FBI temporarily halted some cooperation with Canada’s Royal Canadian Mounted Police this month after CBC circulated an internal memo telling journalists not to describe the Sept. 11, 2001, attacks as “terrorist attacks” without attributing the term to an authority or expert, according to officials cited by The New York Times. FBI Director Kash Patel reportedly ordered meetings with RCMP counterparts canceled and told Canadian officials the bureau would be unreachable for a period. The disruption did not affect other U.S. government agencies involved in intelligence sharing [2][6][10]. CBC changed its guidance after the memo became public and now permits journalists to describe the 9/11 attacks as terrorism without attribution. FBI-RCMP cooperation resumed around the Sept. 11 anniversary, although U.S. officials reportedly sought an apology and it remains unclear whether Canada provided one [3][6][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: CBC’s attribution guidance resembled practices used by AP and BBC when reporting on political or ideological violence [8].

Key Points

  • Broader criticism: The leaked CBC memo drew criticism from the U.S. ambassador to Canada and Patel before the broadcaster reversed the guidance [6].
  • Report sourcing: The New York Times based its account on multiple officials with knowledge of the episode who spoke anonymously because of sensitive law-enforcement dynamics [6].
  • Policy scope: Before the revision, CBC's attribution rule applied to describing an event or person as “terrorist,” not only to coverage of 9/11 [3].
  • Political context: The reported move came as President Trump intensified his attacks on Canada [2].
