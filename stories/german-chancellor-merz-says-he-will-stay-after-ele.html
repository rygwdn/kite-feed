German Chancellor Merz says he will stay after election defeats

Category: World

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he would remain in office and continue his coalition with the center-left Social Democratic Party after the Christian Democratic Union suffered heavy losses in state elections Sunday in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania. The far-right Alternative for Germany won 38.2% in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, while the CDU received 4.9% and fell below the 5% threshold for representation in the state parliament for the first time in postwar Germany. In Berlin, the left-wing Die Linke finished first with 25.7%, ahead of the CDU at 18.8%, while the AfD won 16.3% [1][4][16][49]. Merz called the Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania result a “disaster” but rejected speculation that he would resign and defended his economic and social reform program. He said the results reflected changes in Germany’s party system, while CDU figures and analysts cited voter dissatisfaction with economic conditions, living costs and the government’s direction [5][9][62].

Key Points

  • SPD resilience in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania: State premier Manuela Schwesig’s SPD won 35.5%. Schwesig had distanced herself from federal policy, particularly on social issues [2].
  • AfD governing path: Although the AfD led in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, the other represented parties ruled out cooperating with it, leaving it without an apparent coalition partner [66][46].
  • Berlin coalition math: Die Linke’s first-place result ended the previous CDU-SPD majority in Berlin and opened coalition talks among the Left, SPD and Greens [49][27].
  • Party leadership pressure: Several conservative state premiers met after the vote, but participants denied discussing a challenge to Merz [5].

Perspectives
