Anthropic bill would require judicial approval to block high-risk AI models

Category: AI

Anthropic, the maker of Claude, has privately pitched Congress a draft bill that would let the federal government block an AI model’s release or continued use only if it poses a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than 50 deaths or serious injuries, or more than $1 billion in damage. The risk would have to involve a biological weapon, a model escaping its developer’s control or a cyberattack. Federal agencies could not halt a model on their own: the Commerce Department would have to seek an order from a federal judge in Washington, D.C. [2][6][5] The draft would use independent evaluators registered with the Commerce Department. AI labs could pay them, provided their compensation did not depend on their findings. Evaluators would have 72 hours to notify the department of an identified threat. Critics questioned the safeguards and potential conflicts of interest. After days of criticism, Anthropic said it would rewrite parts of the proposal. The draft remains a proposal, not federal law. [5][4][1]

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Agent incidents: Recent reports of AI agents testing or breaking into online platforms have intensified cybersecurity concerns; the draft’s thresholds are far higher than the harms reported in those cases. [2][6]
  • State authority: The proposal would override some state AI rules, as would the House FRONTIER Act under consideration by Reps. Lori Trahan and Jay Obernolte. [2]
  • Voluntary oversight: AI labs have also proposed embedding third-party observers inside organizations as a voluntary alternative to mandatory government oversight. [6]
  • Other proposals: Senate Majority Leader John Thune and Sens. Amy Klobuchar and Ted Cruz are negotiating a separate bill on catastrophic AI risks, while Rep. Greg Casar and Sen. Bernie Sanders have called for halting advanced AI development. [2]

Anthropic: The company described the draft’s proposals as “sensible policies” for Congress. [2][6]
