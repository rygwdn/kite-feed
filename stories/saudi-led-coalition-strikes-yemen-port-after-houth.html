Saudi-led coalition strikes Yemen port after Houthis threaten Saudi ports

Category: World

A Saudi-led coalition supporting Yemen’s internationally recognized government said Wednesday it struck operational targets at Hodeidah port and sites in Saada and Amran after Yemen’s Houthi movement threatened to attack Jeddah and other Saudi Red Sea ports if Hodeidah was hit [13][11]. The exchange followed a Houthi drone strike Tuesday on a tower in Riyadh’s financial district, which Saudi authorities said injured 11 people [3][8]. Witnesses identified the Riyadh building as the 80-story Public Investment Fund Tower, headquarters of Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund. The same tower was hit on Nov. 19, causing damage but no reported injuries [6][10]. The Houthis had not claimed responsibility for Tuesday’s strike at the time of the reports, while the coalition blamed them and vowed a response [19][3]. Hodeidah is a main entry point for food and other essential imports into Houthi-controlled northern Yemen, where millions depend on imported supplies [13]. Local officials said a separate coalition strike on buses and trucks in Lahj province killed 26 people on Tuesday; government forces blamed the Houthis [1][10].

Key Points

  • Port warnings: Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree called on workers and nearby residents to evacuate, and urged shipping companies to withdraw vessels from Saudi Red Sea ports [13][11].
  • Sanctions announced: Britain imposed an asset freeze on the Houthi group and asset freezes and travel bans on four senior figures, citing attempts to undermine stability [19][1].

Perspectives

Saudi-led coalition: Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki called the Riyadh strike a “cowardly terrorist attack” and said authorities were inspecting the site [21].

Sources: Gulf Times and Al-Monitor

Timeline

  • 8 November 2026: A Houthi strike hit an Aramco building in Riyadh’s financial district [30][16]
  • 19 November 2026: The PIF Tower was struck, damaging the building but causing no reported injuries [10][3]
  • 24 November 2026: A second tower strike injured 11 people in Riyadh’s financial district [29][8]
  • 25 November 2026: Houthis threatened Saudi ports; coalition said it struck Hodeidah and other targets [13][11]

Industry Impact

  • Aviation and events: Attacks on Riyadh infrastructure led to a two-week suspension of airport flights and cancellations of international events, including the Future Investment Initiative conference [1][19].
  • Saudi investment district: The strike hit the PIF Tower in King Abdullah Financial District, which also houses the Saudi stock exchange and offices of international firms including Goldman Sachs [6][21].

Historical Background

The Saudi-led coalition intervened in Yemen in early 2015 after the Houthis captured Sanaa and the internationally recognized government fled to Aden [1][19]. The war caused hundreds of thousands of deaths and a humanitarian catastrophe before an uneasy ceasefire took effect in 2022 [1][32]. Fighting resumed in July 2026, with reports linking its resumption to the wider U.S.-Iran war [3][14].

International Reactions

  • 🇮🇳 India: India condemned the Riyadh strike and other attacks on Saudi civilian, economic and energy infrastructure, and said its embassy was assisting injured Indian nationals [8].
  • 🇵🇰 Pakistan: Pakistan denounced the attack, voiced solidarity with Saudi Arabia and said the kingdom had the right to defend its territory [10].

Travel Advisory

  • India’s embassy asked Indian nationals in Saudi Arabia to follow local rules and regulations, while authorities restricted access to some streets in Riyadh’s financial district [22][32].

Q&A

Q: What other Saudi sites have been hit in the current escalation?

A: An Aramco building in Riyadh was struck earlier in November; an October attack on King Khalid International Airport killed 14 people [30][1].
