Environment Canada warns atmospheric river to soak B.C.

Category: Canada

Environment Canada says an atmospheric river will target British Columbia’s South Coast this weekend, with the heaviest rain expected Sunday [1][3]. A rainfall warning covers parts of Metro Vancouver, where 75 to 125 millimetres of rain is forecast through Monday, before conditions ease by Monday night [3]. Forecasters warn of pooling water on roads, possible travel delays, and landslide risk in vulnerable terrain such as steep slopes, deforested areas, and recent burn scars [3]. The incoming system arrives as heavy rain continues to affect B.C.’s North Coast, adding to broader flood and avalanche concerns highlighted in related coverage [1][2].

Did you know: Metro Vancouver’s steep North Shore mountains can rapidly enhance rainfall as moist Pacific air is forced upward, a process known as orographic lift [common].

Historical Background

British Columbia’s South Coast regularly sees wintertime Pacific storms that can deliver intense rainfall when warm, moisture-rich air flows inland from the ocean—often described as an “atmospheric river” [common]. In recent years, communities in the Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley have faced repeat impacts from heavy-rain events, including flooding and slope failures, which has increased attention on preparedness and local mitigation [3].
