Researchers report new ways to make DNA

Category: Science

Scientists reported a bacterial system that can generate DNA without using DNA as the direct template, ScienceAlert reported on August 23, 2026 [1]. In one part of the system, a protein acts as a molecular template for building a specific repeating DNA sequence, allowing information encoded in amino acids to help determine information written in DNA [1]. Samuel Sternberg, a biochemist, told ScienceAlert that the system uses two reverse transcriptases to independently produce complementary DNA strands [1]. The strands then pair into double-stranded DNA that helps the bacterium defend itself against viruses [1]. Phys.org separately described work on hierarchical DNA materials made with two classes of biomolecular nanomachines, including enzymes and molecular motors, that use chemical energy to synthesize and organize biomolecules [2]. The article said such machines help living systems build and maintain ordered structures away from thermodynamic equilibrium [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Rather than copying one DNA strand to make the other, as happens in normal DNA replication, the system uses two different reverse transcriptases to independently generate the two complementary strands

— Samuel Sternberg (ScienceAlert)

Key Points

  • Unusual direction: ScienceAlert compared the protein-templated DNA synthesis to using a cake as the template for writing a recipe, rather than the other way around [1].
  • Materials approach: The DNA-materials research focuses on coordinating two classes of biomolecular nanomachines rather than relying on a single molecular process to organize complex materials [2].

Historical Background

Francis Crick described the central dogma of molecular biology in 1958, summarizing the usual flow of sequence information from nucleic acids to proteins rather than from proteins back to nucleic acids [common]. Reverse transcriptase showed that RNA can be copied into DNA, but the ScienceAlert-reported system adds a different templating role for protein sequence information [common][1].
