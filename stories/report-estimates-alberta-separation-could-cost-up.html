Report estimates Alberta separation could cost up to $170B

Category: Canada

A government-commissioned University of Calgary School of Public Policy report estimates that creating an independent Alberta would cost between $50 billion and $170 billion over its first five years. The analysis models a “smooth” separation involving quick, favourable negotiations with Canada and a “difficult” separation involving prolonged, unfavourable negotiations [7][4][6]. The report says Alberta would assume a share of Canada’s federal debt and would need to build or replace federal functions, employing as many as 70,000 civil servants. Under the smooth scenario, Alberta’s economy would be 2.2% smaller than otherwise expected in the short term but 3.4% larger after about 20 years. The report warns that the difficult scenario could leave the economy and public finances weaker for many years [3][5][7]. Albertans are scheduled to vote on Oct. 19 on whether to remain in Canada or hold a binding referendum on separation [2][5].

Did you know: The smooth scenario projects average annual worker earnings could fall by more than $1,200 in the short term before rising by more than $1,800 over the longer term [3].
