Houthis claim missile and drone attack on Saudi base

Category: World

Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthi movement said Sunday that it used missiles and drones against the Sharurah military base in southern Saudi Arabia, targeting weapons depots and command-and-control centers. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree did not specify when the operation occurred, and Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm whether the base was damaged [3][7][44]. The claim followed a week of intensified fighting along Yemen’s Red Sea coast between the Houthis and the Saudi-backed, internationally recognized government. Government forces said they struck Houthi positions near the port city of Mocha and in the Mocha-Dhubab area, while Saudi Civil Defence reported that a separate projectile hit Jazan province, wounding two people and damaging a mosque, buildings and vehicles [3][37][8].

Key Points

  • Red Sea gains: The Houthis seized Mocha on Thursday and later took Dhubab and Perim Island, positions overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait [69][41].
  • Government targets: Yemeni forces said strikes hit Houthi vehicles, weapons and fighter concentrations near Mocha’s port, an animal-quarantine site and a former commander’s headquarters [37][39].
  • Airstrike claim: Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said Saudi aircraft carried out 129 strikes across seven Yemeni provinces during the preceding 48 hours, a figure not independently verified in the supplied reports [56][43].

Perspectives

Houthi movement: Military spokesman Yahya Saree described the Sharurah operation as retaliation for Saudi military action and warned that further Saudi attacks would prompt larger strikes deeper inside the kingdom [30][36].

Industry Impact

  • Maritime shipping: Control of territory near Bab al-Mandab threatens a route connecting the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden, potentially affecting commercial traffic and Saudi oil exports [69][16].
  • Energy logistics: Saudi oil exports face greater route risk because the nearby Strait of Hormuz is reported to be closed or severely restricted, increasing reliance on Red Sea access [2][18].
  • Regional aviation and ports: Government and Saudi strikes have targeted or affected Mocha’s port and airport, complicating the movement of people and military supplies [53][37].

Humanitarian Impact

The renewed offensive has killed hundreds of people and displaced tens of thousands, according to reports. One report cited roughly 76,000 people displaced since July, while another reported about 1,400 people crossing from Yemen into Djibouti in a 24-hour period [9][64][15][60].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇸 United States: Axios reported that Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman twice called President Trump to seek US strikes, but Trump did not announce any such action [2].
  • 🇮🇷 Iran: Iranian media celebrated the Houthi capture of Yemen’s Red Sea coast and strategic positions around Bab al-Mandab [2].
  • 🇺🇳 United Nations: The UN migration agency reported that roughly 46,000 people had been displaced by the offensive as of Friday [6].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Fighting and airstrikes are active around Mocha, Dhubab and other Red Sea coastal areas, while authorities have reported projectile damage in Jazan [53][8]. Avoid travel to conflict zones and follow local evacuation and road-closure instructions.
  • Transport: Yemeni authorities urged civilians not to use the Taiz-Mocha and Mocha-Dhubab roads during military operations [6]. Do not use those roads unless authorities declare them open and safe.
