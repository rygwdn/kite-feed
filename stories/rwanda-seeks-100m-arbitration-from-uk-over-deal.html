Rwanda seeks £100m arbitration from UK over deal

Category: World

Rwanda has filed an arbitration case seeking about £100 million from the United Kingdom after London cancelled the countries’ Migration and Economic Development Partnership, which would have sent some asylum seekers who arrived in Britain to Rwanda [2][3][4][6]. Kigali says it took legal action after what it calls UK “intransigence” on meeting financial and other commitments tied to the now-scrapped arrangement [1][3][5]. The UK Home Office says it will contest the claim, keeping the dispute in the legal arena rather than in day-to-day diplomacy [2].

Key Points

  • Deal’s legal hurdle: The original arrangement faced repeated court challenges and culminated in a UK Supreme Court ruling in November 2023 that the plan was unlawful under international law, a decision that helped derail implementation [1].
  • UK policy shift: Rwanda says the partnership was agreed before Prime Minister Keir Starmer took office and that his government later scrapped the scheme in 2024 [4][6].
  • Claims outlined: Rwanda says London failed to honor key financial and resettlement commitments under the MEDP and that arbitration became the only option after unsuccessful efforts to resolve the dispute [5].
  • UK response: Britain’s Home Office says it disputes Rwanda’s claim for additional payments and will fight the case [2].
  • Process chosen: Rwanda has opted for arbitration—typically a private, rules-based dispute process—rather than another round of domestic litigation, signaling both sides’ preference for a structured mechanism to settle the contract disagreement [4][3].

Technical Details

  • Arbitration: Arbitration is a dispute-resolution process in which parties submit a contractual dispute to an agreed neutral forum rather than a domestic court, the route Rwanda says it has taken against the UK [3][4].
  • MEDP framework: The Migration and Economic Development Partnership linked UK funding to Rwanda hosting certain migrants who arrived in Britain illegally, according to Rwanda’s account of the agreement [4][5].
  • International-law finding: The UK Supreme Court’s November 2023 decision found the policy unlawful under international law as applied in the UK, shaping the viability of removals under the scheme [1].

Industry Impact

  • Asylum contracting: The dispute highlights how cross-border migration arrangements can create long-tail legal and financial liabilities for governments when implementation stalls or political leadership changes [2][6].
  • Legal services: Arbitration and related advisory work can shift migration-policy disputes from domestic courts into specialist international dispute-resolution channels, potentially raising costs and extending timelines [3][4].
  • Aid-linked policy: Rwanda’s framing of the MEDP as involving financial and resettlement commitments underscores how development funding and migration management can intertwine, complicating unwind scenarios when a policy ends [5].

Historical Background

The UK and Rwanda signed the Migration and Economic Development Partnership to relocate certain asylum seekers who arrived in the UK irregularly, with Rwanda receiving payments to host them [4]. UK courts repeatedly scrutinized the plan, and the UK Supreme Court ruled in November 2023 that the arrangement was illegal under international law as applied in the case, a turning point that prevented the policy from taking effect as designed [1].
