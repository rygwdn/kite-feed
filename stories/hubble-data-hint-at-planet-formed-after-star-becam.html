Hubble data hint at planet formed after star became white dwarf

Category: Science

Astronomers have found signs of a possible planet around the white dwarf HS0209+0832. The planet may have formed from gas and dust expelled as its host star became a white dwarf [3][1][2]. TESS observations show periodic changes in the star’s brightness consistent with a planet about 6 million kilometers away. Researchers estimate the candidate may be Jupiter-sized and losing its atmosphere under the star’s radiation, but its existence has not been confirmed [3]. The finding came from a new analysis of archival Hubble data. The telescope first observed the object in 1999. Researchers plan additional Hubble observations to investigate the candidate [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Chemical clue: The reanalysis found unusually high levels of niobium after researchers compared the spectrum with an updated database of chemical elements [3].
  • Formation contrast: Most planets are thought to form much earlier, alongside their host stars, rather than from material expelled as a star evolves [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Researchers could not explain some features in the object's spectrum when Hubble observed it in 1999 [3].
