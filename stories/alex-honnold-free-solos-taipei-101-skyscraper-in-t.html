Alex Honnold free-solos Taipei 101 skyscraper in Taiwan

Category: World

American climber Alex Honnold scaled Taipei 101 in Taipei on Sunday, climbing the 101-story tower without ropes or safety gear in a “free solo” ascent [1][3][4][10]. Large crowds watched from the street as he worked his way up Taiwan’s tallest building, and he reached the top spire in roughly an hour and a half [4][9][2]. The climb was organized and broadcast live by Netflix and took place after wet weather delayed the attempt by a day [6][7]. Taiwan’s president publicly congratulated Honnold after he finished [3].

Key Points

  • Height and scale: Taipei 101 stands 508 metres (1,667 feet) tall, underscoring the scale of the route Honnold climbed [2][9][7][5].
  • Global niche: The New York Times notes Honnold is among only “a dozen or so” known skyscraper climbers worldwide [5].
  • Top-spire moment: After reaching the spire, Honnold told reporters, “What a beautiful way to see Taipei,” describing the view from the summit [6].
  • Longtime goal: Honnold said he first became interested in climbing the building more than a decade ago [8].
  • Local spotlight: DW reports Taiwan’s president congratulated Honnold, turning the feat into a moment of national attention for the landmark tower [3].

Technical Details

  • Free solo: A climbing style done without ropes or safety systems, making any fall likely fatal; sources describe Honnold’s Taipei 101 ascent as “free solo” [6][3].
  • Route duration: Multiple outlets report the ascent took about 90–91 minutes from start to the spire [2][6][4].
  • Live broadcast: The climb was organized and broadcast live by Netflix, turning the ascent into a public media event [6][7].

Historical Background

Taipei 101 was once the world’s tallest building and remains an iconic landmark in Taiwan’s capital [common]. Honnold is widely known for popularizing “free solo” climbing—ascending without ropes—through high-profile feats such as his ropeless ascent of El Capitan in Yosemite National Park [common].
