ArXiv AI papers focus on agent safety and evaluation

Category: AI

The Aug. 24 arXiv AI submissions focused on systems designed to make large language models act across tools, memories, retrieval systems and domain workflows. Papers proposed spec-driven agentic software development, memory-primed coding-agent spawning, multi-agent communication control, graph-engineered agent systems and stateful authorization for delegated agent effects [1][2][8][98][99]. Other submissions introduced benchmark and audit frameworks for spatial reasoning in vision-language models, long-context multilingual comprehension, formal-proof quality, meeting-assistant grounding and reference-free factuality judging [6][23][61][55][64]. Safety, reliability and security made up another large group of submissions. Researchers described attacks and defenses involving semantic camouflage, multimodal RAG visual poisoning, agent skill injection, persistent-memory poisoning and physical visual attacks on vision-language-action models [3][68][95][102][72]. Several papers found that apparent model fluency can mask failures in clinical risk calibration for youth mental-health chatbots, clinical long-context EHR reasoning, therapy-related language understanding, occupational-bias representations and judge-model truth assessments [42][44][43][32]. Applied AI papers covered radiology, pathology, maritime dangerous-goods compliance, bridge inspection, e-commerce catalog enrichment, telecom fault analysis, malaria drug discovery and Islamic retrieval-grounded assistance [7][31][30][53][22][88][58][54]. Many of these studies framed deployment around evidence provenance, auditability or human oversight rather than model output alone, including finance analytics, regulatory compliance, medical coding and software-security surveys [14][40][79][34].

Did you know: The Ansari Islamic AI assistant paper says the deployed system has handled more than 140,000 conversations in more than 25 languages since June 2023 [54].

Key Points

  • Agent memory: Weighted Memory Tree reported a 9.97 percentage-point average accuracy gain compared with linear memory on GAIA-Text, while a separate memory-poisoning study found that poisoning 1.2% of a LongMemEval corpus cut accuracy from 0.850 to 0.300 [13][102].
  • Safety trade-offs: CLEAR reduced Llama-3-8B-Instruct HarmBench attack success rate from 32.3% to 0.5%, and ClawSentry cut contextual attack success on SkillInject with Codex/GPT-5.4 from 39.55% to 2.61% [39][95].
  • Evaluation gaps: StateSight found GPT-5.5 reached 59.3%, 33.3% and 28.3% accuracy across three single-image spatial tasks, below a 30-participant human baseline of 80.8%, 68.8% and 64.3% [6].
  • Deployment costs: Quantization-Aware Healing matched or beat its bfloat16 source on seven of nine benchmarks in a GPT-OSS 120B-to-60B-to-MXFP4 pipeline at roughly one-quarter the weight memory and half the teacher model's parameter count [83].
  • Domain risk: DGEval showed the best model exceeded a human practitioner baseline on IMDG multiple-choice questions, but all tested models remained weakest on stowage, segregation and regulatory recall [30].

Technical Details

  • KV-cache translation: XKV trains only a translator that maps cached latent information between frozen heterogeneous language models, which may differ in family, depth, KV-head count, head dimension and tokenizer [12].
  • Conformal calibration: Consilience uses round-wise conformal calibration to bound one-step regret for multi-agent communication actions with marginal probability of at least 1-alpha, conditional on reaching each discussion round [8].
  • Sparse attention retrofit: BF1 uses a deterministic dyadic sparse-attention route with local, global-first-block and logarithmically spaced historical blocks, achieving a 10.91x per-layer prefill speedup at 32K tokens on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU [60].

Industry Impact

  • E-commerce: TRACE reported 98.2% attribute-value accuracy at 74.7% attribute coverage offline, a 90.4% increase in impression-weighted enrichment coverage in production and a 0.48% checkout-conversion lift in an online experiment [22].
  • Civil infrastructure: BridgeGuard evaluated Delaware's full 2023 NBI inventory of 874 bridges and a 200-bridge Texas sample, reaching 99.77% and 100.0% structurally deficient classification accuracy without external network access [53].
  • Telecom: A 5G fault-analysis study found lightweight models achieved at least 90% accuracy on fault diagnosis, while zero-shot recall of 3GPP and O-RAN specifications stayed below 60% [88].
  • Healthcare: CellPath-Bench evaluates 30 pathology-specific and general-purpose foundation models through 304,920 runs across cellular readouts, magnifications, taxonomies and protocols [31].

Scientific Significance

  • Measurement focus: Several papers introduce benchmarks that isolate narrower failure modes, including latent spatial-state reconstruction, claim-level unlearning, visual life-sciences interpretation and source-grounded machine-translation adequacy [6][27][41][81].
  • Limits of claims: Multiple authors explicitly restrict conclusions to controlled settings, such as text-only medical multiple-choice benchmark movement, synthetic LLM-agent populations or evaluated provider contracts. Those limits restrict direct generalization to clinical, social or production environments [17][62][99].
  • Replication signals: Several submissions released or described code, datasets, prompts, raw responses, hashes or evaluation traces, including FlavourBench, ProofJudge, MeetingProbe and BridgeGuard [10][61][55][53].

Action Items

  • Screen AI agents before giving tool access: Review whether an agent runtime has skill-package review, session-level anti-bypass monitoring and post-action evidence handling similar to the controls evaluated in ClawSentry [95].
  • Audit retrieval systems for poisoning: Test whether the system verifies retrieved evidence before generation and calibrates thresholds by domain; Trustworthy RAG found cross-dataset generalization required domain-specific calibration [93].
  • Validate high-stakes AI with domain experts: Use human oversight and authoritative source checks where models perform safety-critical compliance, clinical or youth mental-health tasks [30][42][44].
