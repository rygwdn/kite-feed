EU ministers weigh response to deadly Ceuta crossings

Category: World

European Union interior ministers were scheduled to hold emergency video talks on Tuesday, Aug. 4, after tens of thousands of migrants crossed from Morocco into Spain’s North African enclave of Ceuta late last week, forcing Madrid and Brussels to respond to the bloc’s largest recent border surge [1][4][20][29]. Associated Press reported 50,000 to 60,000 arrivals on Thursday and Friday. Spain later estimated that about 69,500 people had returned to Morocco over four days, exceeding the initial arrival estimates [4][10][18][14]. Spanish and Moroccan authorities said more than 80 people died, including migrants who drowned or were trampled, with the BBC reporting 72 deaths on the Spanish side and 11 on the Moroccan side [4][18][37]. The crisis quickly became a dispute inside the EU over border controls, Schengen rules and solidarity with Spain [18][20][21]. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen wrote to Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez that the EU needed “united action” on border security and said protection of external borders was a shared European responsibility [7][10][18][52]. Italy temporarily reintroduced checks on arrivals from Spain, while Finland and Denmark backed Italy’s move and Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš urged a temporary suspension of Spain’s Schengen membership. Sánchez said Ceuta is not part of Schengen and accused some governments of attacking Spain for political reasons [18][53]. Morocco’s role remained disputed as Spain defended its sovereignty over Ceuta and Melilla, the two Spanish enclaves on the North African coast [54][57][43]. Ceuta leader Juan Jesús Vivas said the crossings were viewed locally as an attack that was “if not orchestrated, at least encouraged or permitted by Morocco,” while Morocco said recent mass crossings were fueled by misinformation on social media, human trafficking networks and instability [69][34][15]. Spanish officials also pointed to social media misinformation after a Spanish court ruling on migrant deportations as one factor behind the surge [34][26][60].

Did you know: Ceuta covers about 18.5 square kilometres, meaning the roughly 60,000 arrivals reported by some sources equaled about 70% of its population [25].

Key Points

  • Diplomatic agenda: EU ambassadors held about two and a half hours of preparatory talks on Monday before ministers’ Tuesday meeting, where Spain planned to seek a common response and criticize governments it said politicized the emergency [20].
  • Border measures: Von der Leyen urged action in five areas: cooperation with partner states, stronger external borders, early-warning systems, action against smuggling networks and more effective returns [52][15][29].
  • Mainland movement: Spanish officials said no onward movement to mainland Europe had been reported, a point EU diplomats cited as the immediate situation eased [20].
  • Remaining migrants: Ceuta’s central government delegate estimated about 2,500 migrants remained, while Vivas put the figure at 3,000 to 5,000 [54][57][34].
  • EU criticism: Italy and Denmark led a letter, signed by 22 EU leaders, calling for stronger external borders, more Frontex support, closer cooperation with Morocco, action against smugglers and scrutiny of policies that may encourage irregular migration [4][29][26].

Perspectives

Spanish government: Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said he had “serious concerns” about some European governments and argued that Ceuta is outside Schengen, rejecting criticism he said was driven by prejudice, fake news, ignorance or political interest.

Historical Background

Ceuta and Melilla are Spain’s two territories on the North African coast bordering Morocco and form the EU’s only land borders with Africa [25][15]. Morocco has claimed sovereignty over both cities since its independence in 1956, while Spain rejects negotiations over them [54][57]. EUobserver reported that a previous major Ceuta crisis in 2021 followed Spain’s decision to admit Polisario Front leader Brahim Ghali for coronavirus treatment, after which Morocco opened the border at Ceuta [45].

Humanitarian Impact

Ceuta still had an estimated 3,000 to 5,000 migrants after the mass returns, with overcrowded shelters unable to accommodate everyone and many people sleeping on beaches or relying on local residents for food and water [8][16][17]. More than 800 unaccompanied minors remained in the enclave, and Spanish authorities face legal obligations to care for them [5][17][6].

International Reactions

  • 🇪🇺 European Commission: Ursula von der Leyen called border protection a “shared European responsibility,” urged stronger monitoring and physical barriers where needed, and said the EU could improve technical and financial support to Morocco [7][10][15][18].
  • 🇫🇷 France: France temporarily reintroduced border checks on arrivals from Spain, with the interior ministry citing the need to prevent irregular secondary movements [7].
  • 🇦🇹 Austria: Foreign Minister Beate Meinl-Reisinger thanked Morocco for its cooperation in resolving the crisis and said no migrants had reached the Spanish mainland [59].
