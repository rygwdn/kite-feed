South Korean tax exam top scorer admits AI cheating before national entrance exam

Category: AI

Han Won-seok, the top-ranked candidate in South Korea’s tax accountant qualification exam, admitted on Nov. 15 that he used a tiny camera, a smartphone and generative AI to cheat in both stages of the exam and the first stage of the certified public accountant exam [1][3][4]. He surrendered to police, who assigned the case to a Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency investigative unit [4][5]. The admission prompted a government response four days before South Korea’s College Scholastic Ability Test, the national university entrance exam, scheduled for Nov. 19. Presidential Chief of Staff Im Kwang-hyun said the government viewed the matter seriously. Policy Chief Ha Joon-kyung was scheduled to lead a meeting on Nov. 15 to discuss countermeasures [1][3][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Exam scores: Han scored 397.5 out of 400 on the tax exam’s first stage, missing just one of 160 questions; he scored 100 in several subjects [1][4].
  • Equipment sales: A Korean news report found online listings for camera-and-earpiece kits priced from 100,000 to 500,000 won [5].
  • Association demands: The Korea Association of Certified Tax Accountants called for Han’s qualification to be invalidated and urged a review of exam controls, proctor training and electronic-device restrictions [5].
  • Detection concerns: Education commentator Kang Sung-tae argued that visual checks focused on glasses could miss other designs of AI-enabled eyewear [4].

Perspectives

Han Won-seok, tax accountant exam candidate: He said anxiety and fear about starting his exam preparation late, along with a desire not to disappoint his parents, led him to cheat. He apologized to the runner-up and other candidates and said he would cooperate with investigators [2].
