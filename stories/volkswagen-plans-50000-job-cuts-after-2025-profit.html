Volkswagen plans 50,000 job cuts after 2025 profit slide

Category: Business

Volkswagen Group said 2025 earnings fell sharply, and it plans to cut about 50,000 jobs in Germany by 2030 as part of a wider restructuring to bring costs down and stay competitive [3][5][6][8]. Several outlets linked the pressure on profits to weaker results in key markets—especially China—plus tougher trade conditions in the United States, including tariffs tied to President Trump [7][6][3]. One of the biggest drags came from Porsche. A strategy shift and hefty one-off charges weighed on its results; Euronews reported a €4.7 billion writedown at Porsche AG, alongside other charges that nearly wiped out operating profit in 2025 [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Profit level: VW said net income nearly halved to €6.9 billion in 2025, while revenue slipped to just under €322 billion—its weakest result since 2016, according to RT’s account of the annual report [8].
  • Cost and labor backdrop: RT said VW pointed to soaring energy costs and noted a 2024 deal with unions to avoid involuntary redundancies and plant closures at production sites in Germany as the restructuring moves ahead [8].
  • EV strategy: Euronews tied part of Porsche’s struggles to a shift away from EVs, a reminder of how quickly demand can change—and how expensive mid-course corrections can be [2].
  • Restructuring scope: RT said these reductions would add to earlier headcount cuts, and executives signaled more cost-cutting steps could follow [8].
  • Geopolitical risk: VW warned that an Iran war could hit demand for Audi and Porsche, adding another layer of uncertainty to planning [6].

Perspectives

Volkswagen management: RT quoted executives saying VW will “systematically reduce our costs,” and noted the group has an existing agreement with unions meant to avoid involuntary redundancies and plant closures at production sites in Germany as the restructuring continues [8].

Sources: RT

Volkswagen (strategic view): DW said VW’s profit slide was driven largely by Porsche’s strategic readjustment, along with weaker demand in VW’s two biggest export markets: China and the United States [7].

Sources: DW

Porsche AG: Porsche said it expects some margin improvement after a tough 2025, and it is aiming for better results under a new CEO [4].

Sources: Investing.com

Euronews analysis: Euronews said two one-time charges linked to Porsche’s strategy shift nearly wiped out operating profit, leaving it down 98% in 2025—and showing how costly course changes can be during an uncertain EV transition [2].

Sources: Euronews

Historical Background

Volkswagen’s 2016 profits took a major hit from the diesel-emissions scandal, making 2016 a common benchmark when comparing today’s downturns [5][8]. Since then, the group has been navigating a long and capital-intensive shift toward electrification across multiple brands, which raises the financial stakes when model and technology bets land out of sync with demand [2][7].
