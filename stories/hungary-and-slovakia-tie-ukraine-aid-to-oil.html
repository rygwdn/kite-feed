Hungary and Slovakia tie Ukraine aid to oil

Category: World

Hungary says it will block a planned €90 billion European Union loan for Ukraine unless Russian oil shipments resume through the Druzhba pipeline, tying financial support for Kyiv to energy transit flows [4][1]. Slovakia has issued a similar warning: Prime Minister Robert Fico said his government could stop supplying Ukraine with emergency electricity unless the transit of Russian oil to Slovakia restarts [2][3]. Ukraine has pushed back hard, calling the threats “blackmail” over electricity supplies and the oil dispute [5]. The standoff adds fresh strain between EU members and Ukraine as the war nears its fourth anniversary, and it underscores how closely energy infrastructure and wartime logistics remain linked across Central and Eastern Europe [1][3].

Key Points

  • Electricity pressure beyond Slovakia: The Straits Times reported that Hungary and Slovakia both threatened to halt electricity supplies to Ukraine unless Russian oil transit resumes [5].
  • Transit chokepoint: Al Jazeera described the disrupted shipments as Russian oil flows “via Ukraine,” highlighting how a stoppage on Ukrainian territory can cut deliveries to Hungary and Slovakia [3].
  • Loan-and-oil linkage: Hungary’s foreign minister said Budapest will keep blocking the EU loan plan for Ukraine until Druzhba oil flows restart [4][1].
