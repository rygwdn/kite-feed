Rescuers in Venezuela free guard after eight days buried

Category: World

International rescue teams pulled Venezuelan security guard Hernán Alberto Gil Flores alive from the collapsed Galerías Playa Grande shopping center in Catia La Mar on Thursday, eight days after magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 earthquakes struck northern Venezuela on June 24 [2][6][9][16]. Gil, 43, had been working a night shift in a basement security booth when the building collapsed; rescuers carried him out on a stretcher with an oxygen mask and transferred him to a Red Cross ambulance [2][16][17]. Teams from Venezuela, Chile, the United States, Portugal, Costa Rica, El Salvador and Mexico worked for days at the site, supplying Gil with water, food or other liquids, and oxygen while digging through unstable concrete [1][19][23][17]. The Costa Rican Red Cross first detected signs of life over the weekend, and rescuers kept working despite aftershocks, rain and the risk of further collapse [2][9][16]. The disaster killed at least 2,595 people, according to the latest figures cited by BBC and Le Monde, while tens of thousands remained missing [6][8]. Acting President Delcy Rodríguez visited Gil at a hospital Thursday and said thousands of officials had been deployed after the quakes [6].

Key Points

  • Medical status: A Costa Rican Red Cross paramedic said Gil had “emerged just perfect,” while a Venezuelan Red Cross paramedic said he remained conscious, focused and stable during the ambulance ride [6][26].
  • Rescue threshold: Emergency teams kept Gil alive well beyond the 48-to-72-hour window many disaster operations use for finding survivors under rubble [2][32].
  • Engineering hazard: Rescuers had to dig two tunnels because the ruins were unstable, and Chilean team leader Cristian Vera said reaching Gil’s exact location was not easy [25][14][24].
  • Other rescues: A three-year-old boy was found alive Tuesday, six days after the quakes; search teams later reported fewer signs of life in many collapsed buildings [14][23][68].

Historical Background

The June 24 quakes struck less than a minute apart and measured magnitude 7.2 and 7.5, according to Reuters reporting carried by The Korea Times [25]. The U.S. Geological Survey said the two shocks occurred 39 seconds apart, according to Anadolu reporting carried by Evrensel [48]. La Guaira, north of Caracas, was the hardest-hit state as tens of thousands of buildings across northern Venezuela were damaged or destroyed [16][54].
