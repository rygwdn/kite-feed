Canada's finance minister signals small-business tax simplification in Nov. 5 budget

Category: Canada

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne said Monday that Thursday’s federal budget will seek to simplify rules for small and medium-sized businesses, but he did not identify specific measures. Speaking at a Canadian Club of Toronto event, he said the budget would address barriers including a complex tax code that smaller firms can struggle to navigate without tax advice [1][2][3][4]. Champagne also signalled possible further changes to the Scientific Research and Experimental Development tax-credit program, which supports companies’ research and technology development. The budget is expected to continue the government’s push to attract private investment and raise productivity. Ottawa has set a five-year target of $1 trillion in private investment. Business groups have pressed for tax relief and regulatory changes, but Champagne has not specified which proposals the government will adopt [2][5][1].

Primary Source: View original article

It's our government's signal to Canadians to use their capital right here in Canada, to invest in new machinery and technology, to expand their enterprises and to support a Canadian economy that nurtures and develops more workers and innovators.

— Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne (Times Colonist)

Key Points

  • Tax proposals: The Canadian Federation of Independent Business (CFIB) wants the small-business tax rate cut from 9% to 6% and the income threshold for that rate raised from $500,000 to $700,000 [1][5].
  • Investment deduction: The productivity mega-deduction is estimated to cost $36 billion over five years and lets firms deduct eligible capital investments immediately rather than spread the deductions over an asset’s useful life [2][4].
  • Business demands: Business groups have also called for faster, more predictable regulatory approvals, tariff support for affected industries and measures to help firms find new export markets [5].
  • Startup incentives: Investor and technology groups have proposed capital-gains deferrals and a tax incentive modeled on the U.S. qualified small-business stock system to encourage investment in Canadian companies [1].

Perspectives

Canadian Federation of Independent Business: The organization says small businesses want broad policies that help them reinvest and plan, rather than handouts; it also argues that tax burdens, regulation, financing access and economic conditions constrain growth [2][4].

Sources: Times Colonist, Le Devoir

Canadian Manufacturers & Exporters: Chief executive Dennis Darby says announced projects need to move into investment, construction and jobs, and that faster, more predictable approvals would reduce investment risk [5].

Sources: Castanet

Business Council of Canada: Chief executive Goldy Hyder argues that the government’s performance should be judged by whether businesses actually invest and deploy Canadian capital, not by policy intentions [5].

Timeline

  • September 2026: Ottawa announced the productivity mega-deduction as an investment measure [2]
  • 29 October 2026: The CFIB issued its pre-budget call for tax and tariff-relief changes [2][4]
  • 5 November 2026: Champagne is scheduled to present the federal budget in Parliament [1][2]

Industry Impact

  • Small businesses: The productivity mega-deduction could apply to ordinary purchases such as a café’s coffee machine or a small firm’s tools and office technology [1][2].
  • Manufacturing: Manufacturers are seeking quicker approvals, protection against unfairly traded steel imports and stronger Canadian-content requirements for major infrastructure projects [5].

Historical Background

Last year’s federal budget expanded eligibility and spending limits for the Scientific Research and Experimental Development tax credit and set out plans to use artificial intelligence to simplify its review process [1].

Business Angle

  • The CFIB wants employers to pay the same employment insurance premium rate as employees on the first $500,000 in payroll, arguing that payroll taxes are a direct burden on jobs [5].
  • Business representatives cite regulatory delays, tariff uncertainty and difficulty finding skilled workers as barriers that tax measures alone may not resolve [5].

Q&A

Q: Will the budget include details on a sovereign wealth fund or private airport investment?

A: Champagne did not say whether the budget would provide further details on either proposal [2].
