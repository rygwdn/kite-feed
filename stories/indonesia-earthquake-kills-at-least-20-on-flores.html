Indonesia earthquake kills at least 20 on Flores

Category: World

A magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck off Indonesia’s Flores Island early Saturday, killing at least 20 people, injuring six and leaving two others missing or trapped, officials and rescuers said [2][3][6][16][23][49]. The U.S. Geological Survey said the quake hit at 5:58 a.m. local time at a depth of 10 km, with its epicenter about 68 km north-northwest of Ende in East Nusa Tenggara province [6][16][23][21]. Authorities issued a tsunami warning and urged coastal residents to move to higher ground, then lifted the warning after Indonesia’s Meteorology, Climatology and Geophysics Agency said monitoring showed no significant sea-level changes threatening coastal communities [3][16][23][21]. Reuters reported tsunami waves of less than one meter in several areas before the warning was lifted about three hours after the quake [19][22][49][62]. Search teams around Maumere recovered bodies as collapsed buildings, landslides, power outages and communications failures slowed damage assessments and rescue work, especially in Nagekeo, the regency closest to the epicenter [6][19][22][40]. East Nusa Tenggara police chief Rudi Darmoko said authorities were still collecting damage and casualty reports while communications remained disrupted [21][26][42].

Key Points

  • Aftershocks: Three aftershocks followed within half an hour, with magnitudes of 5.9, 5.6 and 6.1, while later reports described dozens of aftershocks [6][18][22][13].
  • Hospital evacuations: Television footage showed hospital staff moving patients, beds, IV stands and oxygen cylinders outdoors to set up temporary treatment areas after the quake [21][26][42][46].
  • Damage reports: BNPB said preliminary reports showed 54 houses heavily damaged and five health facilities damaged, while other reports cited damage to warehouses and government facilities [10][19][27].

  • 15 August 2026: East Nusa Tenggara Governor Emanuel Melkiades Laka Lena said five people died from collapsing rubble as they slept [43][50].

Historical Background

Indonesia sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, an arc of volcanoes and fault lines that makes the country highly prone to earthquakes and volcanic activity [60][55]. In 2018, a magnitude 7.5 earthquake and tsunami killed about 2,200 people in Palu, Central Sulawesi [60].

Humanitarian Impact

About 2,000 residents in Nagekeo evacuated, while traffic congestion, power outages and damaged communications complicated the emergency response [6][19][40]. Authorities warned people not to return to damaged buildings because aftershocks could bring them down [13][14][48].

International Reactions

  • 🇦🇺 Australia: Australia’s tsunami warning centre said the earthquake posed no tsunami threat to the Australian mainland, islands or territories [10][33][44].

Action Items

  • Check official Indonesian disaster updates: Use BMKG and BNPB channels for aftershock and coastal safety instructions, as authorities specifically told coastal residents to follow official information [10].
  • Avoid nonessential travel to damaged zones: Postpone travel into Nagekeo and nearby damaged areas until roads, communications and building safety are reassessed [6][19].
