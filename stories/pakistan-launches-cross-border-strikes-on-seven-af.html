Pakistan launches cross-border strikes on seven Afghan sites

Category: World

Pakistan says it carried out “intelligence-based” cross-border strikes into Afghanistan, targeting what it described as militant hideouts linked to the Pakistani Taliban (TTP). The strikes hit seven locations along the Durand Line in Afghanistan’s Nangarhar and Paktika provinces [2][11][4][7][8][10][5]. Islamabad cast the operation as a response to a surge in attacks inside Pakistan, including suicide bombings it says were planned from Afghan territory; Pakistan’s information minister said the strikes followed three attacks since the start of Ramadan last week [2][4][3][5]. Afghan officials and media reports said the strikes killed and wounded civilians. Afghanistan’s Defence Ministry alleged that Pakistan hit homes and a school, and said children were among the casualties; Afghan authorities said they would respond, raising fears of further escalation between the neighbors [9][10][6]. Accounts differ on the exact strike locations and the casualty totals. Still, the immediate diplomatic picture is plain: both sides are hardening their public stance at the border, even as they face pressure to stop militants from operating in frontier areas [8][5][4].

Key Points

  • Additional claimed targets: NDTV said Pakistan also described one target as linked to an affiliate of the Islamic State, alongside alleged TTP camps [11].
  • Public confirmation detail: Pakistan’s information minister Attaullah Tarar confirmed the operation in a post on X and linked it to three attacks since the start of Ramadan [2].
  • What was hit (varying accounts): Some reports said the strikes hit a madrasa during Ramadan, alongside other civilian buildings Afghan officials mentioned [6].
  • Wider violence backdrop cited in coverage: NDTV’s explainer said Pakistan has blamed a recent rise in militant violence not only on the TTP but also on outlawed Baloch separatist groups [1].

Perspectives

Pakistan government: Pakistan called the strikes “intelligence-based” and “selective,” and said it targeted militant camps after a spike in suicide bombings and other attacks it blames on Afghan-based groups.

Sources: The Guardian

Afghanistan Defence Ministry: Afghan officials said the strikes hit civilian locations, including homes and a school, and reported children among the casualties while vowing a response.

Sources: Al Jazeera

Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty reporting: RFE/RL said Pakistan presented the strikes as a “retributive response,” and pointed to rising tensions as both sides trade blame over cross-border militancy.
