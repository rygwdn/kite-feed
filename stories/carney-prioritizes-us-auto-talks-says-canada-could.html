Carney prioritizes U.S. auto talks, says Canada could seek other partners

Category: Canada

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada could turn to European or Asian automakers if trade talks with U.S. President Donald Trump fail to protect Canadian jobs and an integrated North American auto industry. Speaking in Cambodia on Nov. 16, Carney said U.S. trade actions have weakened that integration, while Ottawa remains focused on reaching a deal. Canada faces threatened tariffs of up to 50% on its autos by January [3][1][12]. Automakers build vehicles through a cross-border production network. Carney said Canada would consider other partnerships if its workers and manufacturing assets were not fully used in North America, but Chinese participation would have to involve manufacturing rather than simple assembly [10][9]. Carney said the U.S. negotiations were making progress but differences remained. He is scheduled to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen on Nov. 17, where they are expected to discuss auto manufacturing [5][3].

Primary Source: View original article

It’s not leverage. But action, reaction.

— Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney (The Toronto Star)

Key Points

  • Investment terms: Any Chinese auto venture would need to involve substantial manufacturing, meet Canadian labour standards, develop domestic supply chains over time and use Canadian critical minerals in electric vehicles [9][10].
  • Canada-China trade-off: Under the January pact, China agreed to lower tariffs on Canadian agricultural products, including canola [3][9].
  • No announced ventures: The January Canada-China pact set a goal of attracting substantial Chinese joint-venture auto investment within three years, but no such deals had been announced by Nov. 16 [8].
  • Tariff retaliation: After Ottawa rejected late U.S. demands, Washington imposed 50% tariffs on $20 billion of Canadian exports; Canada vowed dollar-for-dollar retaliation [5].

Perspectives

Ontario Premier Doug Ford: Ford has warned that admitting Chinese electric vehicles could put Canadian auto jobs at risk and threaten access to the U.S. market, particularly for Ontario, the centre of Canada’s auto industry [9].

Sources: Euronews

Trump administration: U.S. officials have criticized Ottawa for opening its market to Chinese automakers [8].

Sources: The Straits Times

Industry Impact

  • Plant shift: Canada recently threatened to sue Stellantis after it moved planned Jeep Compass production from Brampton, Ontario, to Illinois and agreed to sell its idled Canadian assembly plant [3][9].
