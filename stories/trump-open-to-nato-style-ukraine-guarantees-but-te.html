Trump open to NATO-style Ukraine guarantees, but terms remain disputed

Category: World

U.S. special envoy Jared Kushner said on Nov. 19 that President Donald Trump is open to giving Ukraine security guarantees modeled on Article 5, the collective-defense clause of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO). Kushner said he believed Russia was “comfortable” with the concept, but stressed that any commitment must serve U.S. interests and be part of a wider security package. He also said Trump would work through Congress. [4][31][6] Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy questioned whether Kyiv and Washington mean the same thing by an Article 5-style guarantee, saying Ukraine would need partner forces on its territory. Russian President Vladimir Putin said military cooperation between Ukraine and Europe would be a problem. [7][4] German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said new talks involving Ukraine, Russia, the United States and European countries should take place soon, but no date or venue has been set. He named the United Arab Emirates as a possible location and said any negotiations must include Kyiv and Europe. [4][2]

Primary Source: View original article

I’m not sure we have the same understanding of Article 5, do we? Because as I understand it… what does Article 5 mean to me? Article 5 means that certain partner forces are present on your territory.

— Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy (Meduza)

Key Points

  • Ukraine’s demand: Zelenskyy said U.S. officials had described the guarantees as bilateral and argued that any agreement must specify what partners would do if Russia attacked again [18].
  • Postwar recovery planning: Kushner said the U.S. had made progress on a recovery plan with Zelenskyy’s team, the World Bank, BlackRock and the EU [31].
  • European proposal: French President Emmanuel Macron urged Ukraine’s allies to provide air-defense systems and interceptors and to reopen debate over Ukraine’s NATO membership [16].
  • Earlier talks: A round of talks in Abu Dhabi in mid-November produced a large prisoner exchange but no major progress on territory or a ceasefire, according to UNIAN [20].

Perspectives

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz rejected a “Yalta-style” format for negotiations, saying it could not bring peace [2][4].

Historical Background

Russia seized Crimea in 2014 and launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 [6][11]. Ukraine has long sought NATO membership [3].
