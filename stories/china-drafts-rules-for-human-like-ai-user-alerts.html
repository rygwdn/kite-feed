China drafts rules for human-like AI user alerts

Category: Technology

China released draft rules aimed at consumer-facing AI services that simulate human personalities and emotional interaction, as Beijing seeks tighter oversight of “human-like” AI experiences [1][2][3]. The proposals would require providers to clearly tell users they are interacting with AI at login and then again at two-hour intervals, alongside requirements focused on ethics, security, and transparency [1][2]. The draft also calls for ethical review systems and includes restrictions on content deemed to threaten national security, with public feedback open until January 25, 2026 [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: The draft rules’ public comment period runs until January 25, 2026 [2].

Key Points

  • Addiction safeguards: The draft includes addiction-prevention requirements targeted at AI products designed for sustained, emotionally engaging interaction [3].
  • Emotional simulation: The rules specifically address AI that can present human-like personas and emotional responses, a category of consumer chatbots that authorities want more tightly regulated [3].
  • Ethics process: Providers would need to set up ethical review systems as part of compliance, signaling a push toward formal internal governance for consumer AI deployments [2].
  • Security red lines: The draft bans certain content framed as threats to national security, expanding the compliance burden beyond product design into ongoing content controls [2].

Technical Details

  • Human-like AI disclosures: The draft would require prominent notices telling users they are dealing with AI at login and at two-hour intervals, aiming to reduce confusion between human and automated interaction [1][2].
  • Ethical review systems: Providers would be required to establish an ethical review mechanism, formalizing internal checks before and during deployment of human-like AI features [2].
  • National security content limits: The proposals include bans on content that could threaten national security, implying ongoing monitoring and control over what these AI services generate or distribute [2].

Industry Impact

  • Consumer chatbot design: The combination of repeated AI-identification prompts and addiction-prevention requirements may push developers to redesign engagement loops, especially for emotionally interactive “companion” experiences [1][3].
  • Compliance tooling: Mandated ethical review systems and content restrictions can increase demand for governance, risk, and compliance workflows tailored to generative AI deployments [2].
  • User transparency norms: If implemented, the requirement to repeatedly inform users they are interacting with AI could normalize more explicit AI labeling practices in consumer apps [1][2].
