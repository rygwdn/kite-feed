Ottawa ends veterans employer’s federal contract priority

Category: Canada

The federal government is ending the Canadian Corps of Commissionaires’ long-standing priority access to federal security-guard contracts, a change the organization says puts a contracting right worth about $330 million at risk [1][2][3]. The Commissionaires, described in the coverage as the largest private-sector employer of veterans in Canada, is asking the Liberal government to reverse the decision and has also launched a legal challenge [1][3]. The change matters because the priority gave this veterans-focused employer first access to a large stream of federal guarding work, helping support jobs for former military members across Canada [1][2][3]. Ottawa’s move marks a shift in how those public contracts will be awarded [1][3].

  • Legal fight: Alongside its push to persuade the government, the organization has taken Ottawa to court to try to preserve the entitlement [3].
  • Policy shift: At the centre of the change is a special entitlement that gave the Commissionaires first dibs on federal security contracts instead of making it compete on the same basis as other bidders [1][3].
