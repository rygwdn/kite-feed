Israeli opposition files Oct. 7 inquiry bills as Eisenkot seeks to form government

Category: World

Opposition lawmakers in Israel’s newly sworn-in parliament introduced bills targeting Netanyahu-era policies as former military chief and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot tries to form a government to replace caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The proposals include a state inquiry into Hamas’s Oct. 7, 2023 attack, term limits for prime ministers and repeal of judicial changes. The Oct. 27 election left Netanyahu’s alliance with 53 of the Knesset’s 120 seats and Eisenkot’s bloc with 48, both short of the 61 needed for a majority [15][4][11]. The 19 lawmakers from Arab-led parties could give Eisenkot’s camp enough votes for a majority, but coalition talks remain divided over whether to rely on their support and over military-service requirements for ultra-Orthodox parties [5][17]. Netanyahu urged a unity government, citing Iran, and attacked the Arab-led parties; Eisenkot condemned his speech and canceled a planned meeting with Netanyahu’s Likud party [11][22]. The opposition has nominated Yashar party lawmaker Matan Kahana to replace Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana, with a vote scheduled for Nov. 17 after 67 lawmakers requested it [11][16]. Eisenkot has until Dec. 6 to form a government and may seek an extension through Dec. 20; Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister in the meantime [4][8].

Key Points

  • Election claims: Netanyahu has alleged Arab voter fraud, but election officials said they found no evidence it affected the result; the Central Elections Committee said the vote was valid [4][41].
  • Coalition conditions: Eisenkot’s four tests for partners include recognition of Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, acceptance of its Declaration of Independence, service for all and recognition of Hamas as a terrorist organization—demands contested by potential Arab and ultra-Orthodox partners [5][41].
  • Parliamentary powers: Political studies lecturer Ilana Shpaizman said the Knesset can conduct oversight and pass private members’ legislation before a new government is formed, while Netanyahu’s cabinet continues to govern [34].
  • Ultra-Orthodox demands: United Torah Judaism has signaled that a signed pledge alone would not suffice and wants enforceable guarantees protecting yeshiva students and Torah study [2].

Perspectives

Yisrael Beiteinu leader Avigdor Liberman rejected a government dependent on Arab-party support and told Eisenkot he would instead bring ultra-Orthodox parties into a potential coalition [2].
