Iceland votes on restarting EU membership talks

Category: World

Icelanders voted Saturday in a referendum on whether the country should resume accession negotiations with the European Union, 13 years after a Eurosceptic government paused earlier talks [4][2][12][15]. The ballot does not decide EU membership: a “yes” vote would reopen negotiations with Brussels, while any final accession agreement would need a second referendum [1][14][22][18]. The vote has divided a country of just under 400,000 people, with polls showing narrow, shifting margins: Gallup put the “No” side at 51.6%, while an earlier Maskína survey put the “Yes” side at 51.3% [4][17][26][36]. Prime Minister Kristrún Frostadóttir’s government brought the vote forward after geopolitical tensions rose over Russia’s war in Ukraine and President Trump’s threats toward Greenland, but debate over fisheries, sovereignty, inflation and interest rates has dominated the campaign [4][12][1][29]. Polling stations were scheduled to be open from 09:00 to 22:00 GMT on Saturday, with results expected in the early hours of Sunday [4][17][18]. If voters reject the proposal, several reports said the EU question would be shelved for the foreseeable future; if they approve it, Iceland would begin talks on membership terms across 35 negotiating areas, including fisheries [14][22][4].

Key Points

  • Existing ties: Iceland already participates in the EU single market through the European Economic Area and belongs to the Schengen border-free zone, but EU membership would add customs-union participation and eventual euro adoption [4][12][26].
  • Early turnout: More than one in five voters had cast ballots before referendum day, according to Icelandic public broadcaster RUV as cited by the BBC [4].
  • Fishing stakes: Accession talks would force negotiations over fish stocks and quota rules in North Atlantic waters [54][14][24].
  • Economic argument: Supporters link EU talks to currency stability and lower borrowing costs, with one Icelandic economics student telling ABC that ordinary mortgage rates are around 9% [1][53].
  • Negotiation scope: EU accession talks cover 35 chapters, and Iceland’s previous application was well advanced [4].

  • 29 August 2026: Voters decided whether to reopen accession negotiations [4][17].

Industry Impact

  • Seafood exporters: Marine products account for roughly 40% of Icelandic goods exports, making fisheries policy the main industry issue in any accession negotiation [54].
  • Small fishers: Tagesschau reported from Grundarfjördur that some coastal fishers argue EU talks could push Iceland’s government to weigh their interests against those of large domestic quota holders [55].
  • Agriculture: Farmers cited in campaign reporting said they feared EU membership could reduce profitability and expose small and medium-sized farms to tougher market conditions [29].

Historical Background

Iceland applied to join the EU in 2009 after the global financial crisis hit its banking system and economy [8][15][18]. Accession negotiations began the following year, but a Eurosceptic government later suspended the process and Reykjavik eventually withdrew its candidacy [18][40][58]. The EU still considers Iceland a candidate country with a frozen accession process [40]. When talks stopped, Iceland had closed 11 of 35 negotiating chapters, while fisheries remained unresolved [40][58].
