Motorola teases Razr Fold with stylus, AI

Category: Technology

Motorola used CES 2026 to unveil the Razr Fold, its first book-style foldable phone, and said it is expected to launch in summer 2026 [7][8][15]. The device aims to position the Razr brand as a productivity-focused foldable, pairing a large cover display with a tablet-like inner screen for multitasking [7][22]. Motorola is also emphasizing on-device AI features as part of its unified “Moto AI/Qira” approach across Motorola and Lenovo devices [7]. Key open questions remain, including price, availability specifics, and several core internal specifications that Motorola says it will share later [15][8][22].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Motorola also showed a FIFA World Cup 2026 Edition Razr with custom branding and software enhancements alongside the Razr Fold tease [7].

Key Points

  • Display sizes: The Razr Fold is described with a 6.6-inch outer display and an 8.1-inch 2K LTPO inner screen, a combination several outlets frame as competing directly with Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold line and Google’s Pixel Fold family [3][15][24].
  • Camera emphasis: Motorola highlighted a triple 50MP rear camera array, including a periscope telephoto, plus separate internal and external selfie cameras; some coverage also notes Dolby Vision video capture and a Sony LYTIA sensor [7][15][5].
  • Stylus comeback: Moto Pen Ultra stylus support is a central feature of the Razr Fold pitch, and Engadget notes Samsung dropped stylus support on the Galaxy Z Fold 7, potentially giving Motorola a differentiator for note-taking and sketching [15][14][9].
  • Color and texture: Hands-on reports describe two Pantone-branded finishes—Blackened Blue with a “ballistic nylon” feel and Lily White with a soft-touch finish—continuing Motorola’s recent focus on materials and color identity [4][5].
  • More than phones: Alongside the phone, Motorola showcased additional “moto things,” including a smartwatch that it says can last 13 days and a new AI pendant concept, signaling a broader accessories push around the Razr Fold launch [12][5].

Perspectives

Motorola: Motorola frames the Razr Fold as a productivity-focused device, leaning on multitasking, stylus support, and on-device AI features (including “Catch Me Up” and “Next Move”) tied to its Qira platform across Motorola and Lenovo devices.

Sources: Mashable

Hands-on reviewers (positive): Several early hands-on impressions describe the hardware as competitive versus current US big-fold options, highlighting design, materials, and the promise of stronger foldable competition beyond Samsung and Google.

Sources: 9to5Google

Hands-on reviewers (cautious): Some reviewers say they have “many questions” about audience fit and how the Razr Fold will compete in practice, pointing to unresolved details like final specs and market positioning.

Technical Details

  • LTPO OLED: The inner screen is described as a 2K LTPO panel; LTPO is a display backplane technology commonly used to enable variable refresh rates for smoother scrolling and potential power savings [3][24].
  • Periscope telephoto: A periscope telephoto camera (reported as part of the triple 50MP setup) uses folded optics to achieve longer zoom ranges in a thin phone body, and it is notable to see it called out on a foldable launch spec list [7][5].
  • On-device assistant features: Motorola’s AI pitch includes features like “Catch Me Up” and “Next Move” that aim to summarize context and suggest actions without constant app switching, positioned under its unified Qira platform [7].

Industry Impact

  • Foldable competition: Multiple outlets frame the Razr Fold as a direct challenger to Samsung and Google in the US “big fold” category, which could intensify feature competition (screens, cameras, pen support) and broaden consumer choice [6][19][22].
  • Stylus ecosystem: By bundling the narrative around Moto Pen Ultra support, Motorola nudges foldables toward pen-based productivity again—especially notable as Engadget reports Samsung dropped stylus support on the Z Fold 7 [15][9].
  • Accessory strategy: Motorola’s parallel “moto things” announcements (watch, tracker, AI pendant concept) suggest it is trying to grow an ecosystem around its phones, which can increase customer retention if the cross-device software experience lands well [12][5].

Historical Background

Motorola revived the Razr brand in recent years around clamshell (flip) foldables, building recognition in a different foldable category than the larger “book-style” designs [19][15]. Book-style foldables have been led mainly by Samsung and joined by Google’s Pixel Fold line, leaving relatively limited choice in the US compared with some other regions [4][6]. The Razr Fold represents Motorola’s first attempt to expand from flip foldables into this larger, tablet-like form factor [8][12].

Q&A

Q: Will the Razr Fold run Motorola’s AI features entirely on-device, or will some require cloud processing?

A: Motorola highlights “on-device AI” and names features like “Catch Me Up” and “Next Move,” but the coverage does not fully specify which tasks run locally versus via servers [7].

Q: What chipset and RAM/storage options will the Razr Fold ship with?

A: Motorola has not provided internal specs yet, and reports say more specifications will come in the months ahead [15][8].

Q: How much will the Razr Fold cost compared with other book-style foldables?

A: Pricing has not been announced in the CES reveal coverage, with several outlets explicitly noting that cost and availability details are still pending [7][22][15].

Action Items

  • Decide if stylus support is a must-have: If you take handwritten notes or annotate PDFs, compare the Razr Fold’s Moto Pen Ultra support against current alternatives—Engadget notes Samsung dropped stylus support on the Galaxy Z Fold 7 [15].
  • Create a foldable “try list” for hands-on testing: Before pre-ordering any foldable, plan a quick in-store check focused on hinge feel, crease visibility, and one-handed use—several outlets note Motorola is still light on full specs ahead of launch [8][22].
