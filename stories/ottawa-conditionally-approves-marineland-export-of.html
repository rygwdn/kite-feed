Ottawa conditionally approves Marineland export of belugas

Category: Canada

Canada’s federal fisheries minister has given conditional approval for the closed Marineland park in Niagara Falls, Ontario, to export its remaining captive cetaceans—beluga whales and dolphins—to several facilities in the United States [2][5][6][4]. The decision offers a near-term path to relocate animals that Marineland previously said it might euthanize after Ottawa denied a separate proposal to send them to China [1]. Marineland says it is in discussions with multiple U.S. institutions, with the minister describing the company’s relocation proposal as a “solid plan,” though conditions must still be met before any transfer can proceed [2][6][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Marineland’s plan involves placing the animals across several U.S. destinations rather than a single site [6].

Key Points

  • Animals involved: The proposal covers 30 beluga whales and four dolphins, described as Canada’s last remaining captive whales and dolphins [2][6][4].
  • U.S. recipients: Marineland says it is talking with four U.S. institutions about taking in the animals, suggesting the transfers could be split among more than one destination [6].
  • Government meeting: Fisheries Minister Joanne Thompson met with Marineland officials on Monday to discuss the export plan before issuing conditional approval [6].
  • China route blocked: The New York Times reports Ottawa denied Marineland’s application to send the whales to China, which Marineland had linked to the possibility of euthanasia [1].
  • Conditions remain: Multiple outlets emphasize the approval is conditional, meaning specific federal requirements still need to be satisfied before the animals can legally leave Canada [2][5][6].

The New York Times reports the park previously sought approval to send whales to China, but Ottawa denied that application, which Marineland said could lead it to euthanize the animals [1].
