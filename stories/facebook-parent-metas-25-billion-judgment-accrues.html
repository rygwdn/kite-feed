Facebook parent Meta’s $25 billion judgment accrues 15% annual interest

Category: Technology

New Mexico’s $25 billion judgment against Meta accrues interest at 15% a year until paid, and state Attorney General Raúl Torrez says the consumer-protection approach could also apply to artificial-intelligence companies. The state filed the case in 2021, and a jury found that Facebook misled users about privacy and other platform practices tied to the Cambridge Analytica data scandal [8][11][17]. Judge Francis J. Mathew said the calculation under state law supported a penalty exceeding $219 billion but reduced the award to $25 billion. The jury found 43,899,725 willful violations based on statements made over more than a decade. The court has reserved possible injunctive relief, which could require product changes, for later briefing [27][17]. Meta Chief Legal Officer C.J. Mahoney says the company will appeal, arguing that the case improperly targets online speech. Torrez told POLITICO that companies making claims about how their AI products handle consumer data should expect scrutiny. Meta says it expects to prevail on appeal [11][4].

Anyone in artificial intelligence, people who are building products that they can’t control, people who are making representations in the marketplace about what a product does or doesn’t do, or how it uses or doesn’t use specific types of consumer data, we are going to be watching, and we are prepared to do this again

— Raúl Torrez, New Mexico attorney general (POLITICO)

Key Points

  • Trial limits: Jurors rejected claims that Facebook falsely said it removed harmful content, including COVID-19 misinformation [4].
  • Market reaction: Meta shares traded 2% higher after the ruling was announced, before the company reported quarterly earnings [4].
  • Verdict scope: Jurors found 26 of 29 statements misleading, covering privacy, third-party access, misinformation, hate speech and policy enforcement [17].
  • School funding: New Mexico’s consumer-protection penalties are directed to the state’s public education system [25][6].

Perspectives

New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez: Torrez urged other states to bring independent cases rather than settle, saying trials can make corporate records public and raise the cost companies assign to regulatory violations [11].

Meta Platforms: Meta’s lawyers argued that New Mexico had not shown consumers were actually misled and urged the judge to cap the penalty at $3.45 billion, saying a larger award would be unconstitutional [12].

D.A. Davidson & Co.: Technology research head Gil Luria said the judgment is costly but unlikely to change Meta’s overall value, given its scale and expectation that the company will continue appealing [12].

  • 21 October 2026: Judge Francis Mathew entered the $25 billion penalty order [27]

Historical Background

The case traces back to a personality-quiz app that collected information from roughly 270,000 users and their Facebook friends, ultimately reaching about 87 million profiles [15][3]. Facebook learned in December 2015 that Cambridge Analytica had obtained the data, but the scandal became public in 2018 after a whistleblower came forward [15]. Cambridge Analytica used the information for voter profiling and targeting, including work connected to Donald Trump’s 2016 presidential campaign [15][2].

Business Angle

  • Meta faced a separate New Mexico youth-safety case that resulted in $942 million in penalties and court-ordered measures [8].
  • The company’s shares traded 2% higher after the ruling was announced [4].
