Lenovo unveils two rollable OLED laptop concepts at CES

Category: Technology

Lenovo used CES 2026 to showcase two prototype Windows laptops built around flexible OLED displays that change size depending on what you’re doing: a Legion gaming concept that expands into an ultrawide, and a ThinkPad concept that grows taller for productivity [2][3][4]. The Legion Pro Rollable Concept stretches from a standard 16-inch layout to much wider formats (up to roughly 24 inches diagonally) with high-end “Legion Pro 7i”-class internals, including Intel Core Ultra processors and Nvidia’s top RTX 50-series graphics mentioned in early specs discussions [2][13][14]. The ThinkPad Rollable XD Concept expands from 13.3 inches to nearly 16 inches in a portrait-friendly shape and uniquely wraps unused screen area around the lid to create an outward-facing secondary display [3][6][12]. Across outlets, the big takeaway is upbeat: Lenovo keeps turning experimental display engineering into tangible demos—while still treating both machines as concepts that may need more durability and refinement before any real release [4][5][16].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: The ThinkPad Rollable XD concept keeps ThinkPad’s signature red TrackPoint pointing stick even while adding a transforming flexible OLED display [3].

Key Points

  • Control scheme: On the Legion Pro Rollable Concept, widening or shrinking the display is triggered via FN plus arrow keys (rather than a dedicated hardware button) in at least one hands-on demo [13].
  • Ultrawide use-cases: Several previews note that an ultrawide laptop can benefit slower-paced or simulation-style games (like flight sims) and strategy titles by showing more world/map, and can also leave side space for chat or companion apps [5].
  • Rear screen ideas: Lenovo pitched the ThinkPad Rollable XD’s rear-facing display as useful for business workflows—showing items like calendars, widgets, and even a live translation-style readout in a meeting concept demo [6].
  • Build caveats: Hands-ons describe the concept hardware as delicate and not fully rigid yet, with one report noting a less-sturdy-feeling lid and faint lines attributed to the roll mechanism/motors [4][13].
  • Protection layer: The ThinkPad Rollable XD Concept includes a Corning Gorilla Glass Victus cover over the outward-facing portion of the display to help protect it when closed and carried [6][12].

Perspectives

Lenovo (concept strategy): The company frames these prototypes as proof-of-concept explorations meant to spark discussion and feedback, continuing a pattern of showing rollable designs at CES even before products are finalized [5].

Sources: PCWorld

Hands-on testers (optimism): Multiple hands-ons say both rollable concepts already look and function impressively for prototypes, suggesting the core mechanisms work and feel polished in demos [4][7].

Sources: Mashable

Hands-on testers (skepticism): Some reviewers question the practical value of the ThinkPad’s outward-facing “world-facing” display and argue many users would still prefer a traditional external monitor for collaboration or presenting [12].

Sources: Engadget

Gaming-laptop observers: Coverage of the Legion Pro Rollable emphasizes the appeal of bringing an ultrawide experience on the go, effectively mimicking a desktop ultrawide setup in a portable form factor [7][10].

Sources: PCMag

Technical Details

  • Horizontally rollable OLED: The Legion Pro Rollable Concept uses a flexible OLED panel that expands sideways to switch from a standard laptop aspect ratio to wider ultrawide modes (reported as 21:9 and 24:9 options) [13][2].
  • Wraparound ‘lid’ display: On the ThinkPad Rollable XD Concept, the flexible OLED wraps over the top edge and onto the outside of the lid, enabling a rear-facing display area when the screen is not fully extended [3][12].
  • Dual-motor roll system: At least one report describes the Legion concept as using a dual-motor rollable display mechanism to extend the panel to different widths [14].

Industry Impact

  • Display supply chain: More rollable prototypes from a major PC maker can encourage deeper investment across flexible OLED components—panels, motors, hinges, and protective glass—because the laptop form factor demands repeated bending plus portability safeguards [12][6].
  • Gaming laptops: If an ultrawide rollable design becomes shippable, it could blur the line between portable gaming and desktop-style multi-app setups, potentially shifting accessory demand (fewer travelers needing a separate external ultrawide monitor) [7][5].
  • Enterprise collaboration: A rear-facing “world-facing” lid display, even as a niche feature, suggests new UI patterns for meetings (shared agendas, captions/translation-style text, or status widgets) that software vendors could target if the concept matures [6][12].

Historical Background

Lenovo has been iterating on rollable laptops for several years, regularly bringing concepts to CES as a way to test ideas publicly [14]. In 2025 it released a commercial rollable-screen laptop, the ThinkBook Plus Gen 6, whose flexible display extended vertically to add more workspace—an approach that influenced the ThinkPad Rollable XD’s engineering while changing where the screen rolls from [6][12].

Q&A

Q: How loud and power-hungry might the rollable mechanism be during repeated use?

A: The hands-on reports focus on the mechanism’s behavior and early build feel, but they do not publish measured noise, power draw, or long-cycle endurance data for the motors yet [13][4].

Q: Will the outward-facing rear display support independent apps or only mirroring?

A: At least one preview says the rear area can mirror elements from the main display or act as a small secondary monitor, but details on app-level support and software controls are not fully specified [12].

Q: Could Lenovo add a dedicated expand/retract button in a final design?

A: One report notes the concept uses FN plus arrow keys and calls it clunky compared with Lenovo laptops that have dedicated controls, implying the control scheme could change if it ever ships [13].

Action Items

  • Pressure-test your favorite games for ultrawide support: If you game on PC, check whether titles you play support ultrawide aspect ratios (21:9 / 24:9) before buying into an ultrawide-focused setup, since some games still letterbox or lock aspect ratio [5][13].
  • Try a ‘portrait’ workflow today: To see whether a taller, portrait-style laptop screen would help you, rotate a monitor (if it supports it) or use Windows display settings with a vertical external monitor to mimic the ThinkPad concept’s tall workspace for coding, reading, or docs [common].
