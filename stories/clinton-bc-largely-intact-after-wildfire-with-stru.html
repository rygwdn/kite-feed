Clinton, B.C., largely intact after wildfire, with structures lost

Category: Canada

The village of Clinton, B.C., was largely still standing Saturday after crews fought overnight to keep the Pear Lake wildfire from moving farther into the community, though local officials said some structures were lost when flames reached the outskirts late Friday [3][5][6]. Brian Doddridge of Clinton’s emergency operations centre said the “vast majority” of the residential area appeared to have survived, while a village Facebook update said damage was concentrated on the south side of town in the industrial area [3][5]. Residents had been ordered to leave Friday as the fire moved toward the community, and some families were waiting to learn what remained of their homes and businesses [1][8][9]. Steve Larabie said he, his wife, their children, son-in-law and two grandchildren escaped safely, but two homes and his towing and autobody business about three kilometres south of Clinton were destroyed [4][8]. The Pear Lake blaze was one of three fires in the Big Bar wildfire complex, and the BC Wildfire Service said all three were expected to keep growing [3][5][6]. Global News reported that evacuation orders and alerts were in place for Clinton, Boston Bar and parts of the Thompson-Nicola Regional District, with about 120 wildfires burning across British Columbia and stronger winds expected from an approaching cold front [11].

Industry Impact

  • Local services: Steve’s Towing and Autobody, which Larabie said he had operated outside Clinton for 12 years, was destroyed along with two homes on the property [4].
  • Emergency management: A handful of village staff initially stayed behind to help manage the evacuation before leaving around 5 p.m. as the fire approached [6].
  • Power disruptions: Spreading wildfires triggered power disruptions across British Columbia, affecting utility operations and customers [2].

Humanitarian Impact

Evacuation orders displaced residents from Clinton and nearby areas, with families waiting for damage assessments and property notifications after structural losses were reported [1][5][8]. Immediate needs include safe lodging, evacuation support and information on whether homes and businesses survived [11][5].

Business Angle

  • Businesses with concentrated physical assets in wildfire corridors face risk-management costs beyond seasonal firefighting expenses [common].
  • Small local operators can lose productive assets and owner housing at the same time, as Larabie said happened to his towing and autobody business and two homes [4].
  • Insurers and infrastructure-heavy companies face underwriting and capital-planning risks from repeated evacuation orders, smoke disruption and fire suppression costs [11][common].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Avoid travel to evacuated areas around Clinton unless authorities permit return. Check official sources for current evacuation orders and alerts.
  • Air quality: Check smoke and wildfire conditions before travelling in interior British Columbia, as numerous wildfires are affecting air quality.
  • Route planning: Confirm local evacuation orders and alerts before driving near Clinton, Boston Bar or the Thompson-Nicola Regional District, as orders may change rapidly.

Action Items

  • Check evacuation status before returning: Use official local government or BC Wildfire Service updates because Clinton remained under evacuation order after the Pear Lake fire advanced toward the village [11][5].
  • Prepare pets for evacuation: Keep carriers, food, medication and identification ready; a Clinton evacuee told Global News he lost time when cats escaped before departure [11].
