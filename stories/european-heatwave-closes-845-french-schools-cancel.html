European heatwave closes 845 French schools, cancels trains

Category: World

Much of Europe prepared on Monday for an intensifying heatwave, with France placing 49 of its 96 mainland departments under red alert, up from 35 over the weekend, closing 845 schools and allowing pupils at another 1,800 schools to leave early [43][52][22][54]. Parts of France recorded temperatures above 40C, and officials in the southwest Gironde region said extreme heat contributed to the deaths of three people aged 80 to 95 [52][43][54]. France restricted public alcohol consumption in red-alert areas for Fête de la Musique events, canceled some concerts and sports events, and reduced rail services after SNCF warned high temperatures could affect overhead lines and expand tracks [4][6][10][53][3]. Spain's AEMET warned of extremely high day and night temperatures through Wednesday, with some areas forecast to reach 44C, while Madrid officials canceled a public World Cup screening and Italy issued red alerts in eight cities, including Bologna, Florence, Milan and Turin [31][52][54]. Meteorologists said hot air moving north from the Sahara and a strong high-pressure system known as the African anticyclone created a heat dome over western and central Europe, allowing temperatures to build day after day [31][3][41][60]. The World Health Organization's Europe office said this month that more than 200,000 people across Europe died from heat-related causes over the past four years, and urged countries and institutions to use heat plans such as cooling centers and flexible work shifts [6][10][26][59].

Historical Background

France created its color-coded heat warning system after the 2003 European heatwave, which killed nearly 15,000 people in France [69][58][52]. The current episode follows an unusually early May heatwave in Europe, and a rapid study cited by AP-linked reports found human-caused climate change was responsible for about 1,500 deaths during that May event [6][10][59].

Humanitarian Impact

The most urgent risks are heat exhaustion, life-threatening heat stroke, drownings among people seeking water, and risks to older people and those working or gathering outdoors [6][10][26]. In France, red and orange alerts covered more than three-quarters of the mainland population on Sunday, according to AFP calculations cited by Deccan Chronicle and Newstalk ZB [24][39].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Spain's AEMET warned that very high day and night temperatures posed significant danger to vulnerable people and anyone taking part in outdoor activities [14]. Avoid strenuous outdoor activity during peak heat.
  • Transport: French and Belgian rail operators reduced services because heat can damage rails, overhead lines and train systems [3][22][54]. Check rail status before traveling in France or Belgium.
  • Health: Paris kept parks and gardens open around the clock and venues including the Eiffel Tower set up misting stations for crowds [14][6][10]. Use official cooling sites and carry water.

Action Items
