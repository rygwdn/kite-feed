Fish Audio raises $52 million for AI voice models

Category: AI

Palo Alto-based Fish Audio said Tuesday that it raised a $52 million seed round to expand AI voice models for creators and enterprise customers, with Coreline Ventures and Capital Today leading the financing and 359 Capital, Parable, Play Time, Alphalist Partners, Bayhouse Ventures, Carya Venture Partners and HF0 participating [2]. The company says it now has more than 8 million users across open-source and hosted versions of its models and has reached $21 million in annual recurring revenue after launching last year [2]. Fish Audio has open-sourced three speech-generation models, while its newest S2.1 Pro model is available through its paid API and a free developer tier for development and testing under fair-use limits [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Fish Audio’s first model was trained on a single GPU before the project became a company [2].

Key Points

  • Open-source traction: The Fish Speech GitHub repository has more than 31,000 stars and is used by indie developers, video game designers and creators [2].
  • Founder background: Shijia Liao, Fish Audio’s founder and a former Nvidia researcher, began the project after finding existing synthetic voices insufficiently expressive [2].
  • Natural language controls: The company offers a library of more than 15,000 natural language controls for steering voice output [2].

Perspectives

Company position: Fish Audio describes S2.1 Pro as a production model for real-time conversational speech, not the scripted narration older text-to-speech systems target.

Sources: TestingCatalog

Enterprise pitch: Fish Audio is targeting creative users that need more expressive generated voices and enterprises that want more steerable voice systems for customer support and sales.

Sources: TechCrunch

Developer offering: The free tier gives developers access to S2.1 Pro for prototyping rather than limiting unpaid users to a lower-grade model.

Sources: TestingCatalog

Timeline

  • 2025: Fish Audio launched and began releasing voice models [2]
  • 28 July 2026: Company announced $52 million seed financing round [2]
  • 28 July 2026: S2.1 Pro launched with API access and free tier [3]

Technical Details

  • Time-to-first-audio: S2.1 Pro has a reported time to first audio of about 90 milliseconds on standard calls, a latency target aimed at natural turn-taking in live dialogue [3].
  • Cross-language voice identity: The model covers 83 languages and is designed to maintain a single voice identity across languages for multilingual agents and localized creator content [3].
  • Inline style control: Developers can steer delivery with free-form bracket tags placed directly in text, such as instructions for a whisper or nervous laugh, rather than only fixed emotion presets [3].
  • Short-sample cloning: S2.1 Pro supports voice cloning from reference samples typically lasting 10 to 30 seconds and can capture tone and speaking style without additional fine-tuning [3].

Industry Impact

  • Creator tools: Fish Audio’s paid creator and team plans include generation-minute allowances and voice-cloning features, putting AI voice generation into subscription workflows for creators [2].
  • Voice agents: S2.1 Pro supports MCP and agent-skill integrations for developers building speech into voice agents, phone systems and long-form audio pipelines [3].
  • AI video platforms: HeyGen already uses Fish Audio’s enterprise platform, linking the startup’s voice technology to AI video and avatar production workflows [2].

Business Angle

  • Fish Audio reports annual recurring revenue, but customer concentration and gross margins remain undisclosed [2].
  • Fish Audio has open-sourced earlier models, giving developers and competitors access to parts of its technology [2][common].

Action Items

  • Test S2.1 Pro before committing budget: Use the free developer tier for development and testing under fair-use limits, then evaluate latency, voice quality and multilingual performance against production needs [3].
  • Check cloning consent policies: Teams using voice-cloning features should verify that reference samples come from authorized speakers and document permission before deployment [2][common].
