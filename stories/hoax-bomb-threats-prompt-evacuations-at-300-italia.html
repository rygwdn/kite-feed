Hoax bomb threats prompt evacuations at 300 Italian schools

Category: World

More than 300 schools in Italy were evacuated Wednesday after bomb-threat emails reached about 6,300 schools; similar messages were sent to schools in Scotland, Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland. Authorities in all four areas treated the warnings as hoaxes. Italian checks found no suspicious devices or evidence of a concrete danger, and no injuries were reported [2][4][5][14]. The Italian email claimed explosives had been placed in schools and also threatened Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni [19][4]. Italy’s education minister, Giuseppe Valditara, said 312 schools had been evacuated, while the education ministry said the messages reached more than 6,300 of Italy’s 7,476 state schools [4][2]. In Scotland and Northern Ireland, hundreds of schools received threats; police advised against evacuations based solely on the emails, though some Scottish schools briefly evacuated their buildings [21][2]. Meloni said the threats appeared aimed at causing alarm, and Italian authorities are investigating their origin with European counterparts. Officials have not attributed the school threats to a state or group; Russia has denied allegations of involvement in related disruption campaigns [5][4][12].

Key Points

  • Italian schools: The education ministry said private schools were unaffected [2].
  • Classroom disruption: A Milan head teacher said he recognized the message as spam and kept his school open, while some families elsewhere collected their children early [2][12].
  • Investigation: Italian officials said identifying the sender could be difficult [18].

Perspectives

Giorgia Meloni, Italian prime minister: She warned that similar messages could arrive again, but urged people not to speculate about who sent them before the investigation establishes responsibility.

Sources: CNN

Guido Crosetto, Italian defense minister: He said the threats could be the work of foreign actors but warned that individuals seeking attention or causing chaos could also be behind them.

Sources: DW

Antonello Giannelli, head of the National Association of School Principals: He said that even fake threats create widespread disruption for school communities and called for the perpetrators to be identified.

Historical Background

The school warnings followed a series of false bomb alerts affecting European flights and media organizations in the preceding week [4][18]. European countries had also reported cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration systems; France summoned Iran’s ambassador after accusing Tehran of involvement in an attack that disabled supermarket freezers, an allegation Iran denied [22][7]. These incidents fueled discussion of possible hybrid operations targeting civilian infrastructure [14][26].

Q&A

Q: Did schools in Scotland and Northern Ireland receive the same number of threats as first estimated?

A: Scotland’s estimate was revised upward from at least 470 schools to about 1,000; more than 300 schools in Northern Ireland were also targeted [21][26].
