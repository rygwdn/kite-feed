Snowbirds complete final Tutor jet flight before years-long hiatus

Category: Canada

The Canadian Forces Snowbirds performed their final public display in CT-114 Tutor jets over Calgary’s McMahon Stadium on Nov. 15, ahead of the Grey Cup, ending the team’s 55-year run with the aircraft [2][3]. The flypast capped the squadron’s farewell season before it is grounded during the transition to replacement aircraft [3]. The federal government plans to retire the Tutors after the 2026 flying season and replace them with Swiss-made CT-157 Siskin II turboprops, which are not expected before the 2030s [2][3]. Costs, delays and an increasing shortage of replacement parts undermined plans to keep the aging jets flying longer [3].

Key Points

  • Aircraft role: The Siskin II is intended for both aerobatics and training pilots for more advanced aircraft [3].
  • Training role: The Canadian Forces used the Tutors as basic pilot-training aircraft from 1963 to 2000 [3].
  • Squadron makeup: The Snowbirds’ pilots, technicians and support staff include Canadian Armed Forces members and National Defence public-service employees [3].

Perspectives

Historical Background
