Canadian bill would bar MAID for mental illness alone, allow advance requests

Category: Canada

Canada’s Liberal government introduced Bill C-41 on Oct. 27 to indefinitely exclude people whose sole underlying condition is a mental illness from medical assistance in dying (MAID). It would also allow advance requests from people diagnosed with incurable illnesses that could later leave them unable to consent [1][4][3]. The bill must pass both chambers before March 17, 2027, when the scheduled expansion to mental illness as a sole qualifying condition is due to take effect. Justice Minister Sean Fraser said he wants the House of Commons to pass it before Parliament’s December break [1][9]. Advance requests would not trigger MAID automatically: two independent practitioners would have to confirm eligibility, the person would have to be suffering intolerably and in irreversible decline, and any demonstrated refusal or resistance would stop the procedure. Provinces and territories would decide whether to allow advance requests and provide MAID under them [4][2]. The bill follows a parliamentary committee’s recommendation to exclude mental illness indefinitely, citing unresolved questions about safe and consistent assessment [4][5].

Key Points

  • Provincial divide: Quebec legalized advance requests in October 2024 and had registered 3,463 by Oct. 4, 2026; Alberta passed a law barring them last March [8].
  • Consent safeguards: If a practitioner is unsure whether words, sounds or gestures constitute refusal, the bill requires a 30-day wait before another attempt; involuntary sounds or gestures do not count as refusal [11][2].
  • Public opinion: A federal consultation published in October 2025 found that 67% to 69% of Canadians supported advance requests for people with serious or incurable conditions expected to leave them unable to make decisions [4].
  • Political criticism: Conservative MP Tamara Jansen argued that the mental-illness exclusion and advance requests are separate issues and criticized the government for combining them [1].

Q&A

Q: Will advance requests be recorded in a registry?

A: Yes. The bill requires all advance requests to be recorded in a provincial or territorial registry [5].

Q: What does an advance request have to specify?

A: Under Quebec’s existing system, requests identify illness-related effects that would indicate the person wants MAID, such as no longer recognizing family; the province also permits one or two trusted people to help communicate the request [8].
