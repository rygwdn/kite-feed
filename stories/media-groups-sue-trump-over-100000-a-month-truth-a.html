Media groups sue Trump over $100,000-a-month Truth API

Category: Business

The Intercept and the Freedom of the Press Foundation filed a federal lawsuit in New York against President Trump on Wednesday, seeking to block paid early access to his Truth Social posts through Trump Media & Technology Group’s Truth API service [4][6][10][37][35]. The complaint says the service gives paying subscribers faster access to posts from Trump and other high-profile Truth Social accounts, and argues that official government announcements should reach the press and public at the same time [4][15][57][41]. Trump Media charges Truth API customers between $60,000 and $100,000 a month, with the lower price tied to a three-year commitment, and more than 10 customers have signed up, according to Trump Media’s interim CEO, Kevin McGurn, and court filings cited in the articles [2][1][4][10][39]. McGurn said most early customers are high-frequency trading firms. Trump Media has also discussed selling the feed to cloud computing firms, AI companies, large news organizations and other data buyers [2][1][44][50]. The plaintiffs say Trump’s posts can affect markets because he uses Truth Social to announce policy developments on tariffs, war and other U.S. government matters [1][17][29][31]. Trump Media defended the product, saying information from President Trump spreads through many channels that offer subscription APIs, while the White House did not immediately respond to several requests for comment [15][31][10][6].

Key Points

  • Legal claims: The lawsuit alleges First Amendment violations tied to unequal access to presidential announcements and Fifth Amendment violations over charging what plaintiffs call unreasonable sums for access to government information [37][57][41][45].
  • Named defendants: The complaint names Trump in his official capacity and White House aides Natalie Harp and Daniel Scavino, according to accounts of the filing [37][41][45][42].
  • Accounts included: Truth API covers posts from 10 prominent Truth Social accounts, including Trump, Vice President JD Vance, press secretary Karoline Leavitt and other senior officials [4][15][32][39].
  • Company finances: Trump Media reported a $238 million loss for April through June while pursuing businesses beyond social media, including digital assets [2][58].

Perspectives

The Intercept and Freedom of the Press Foundation: The plaintiffs asked the court to declare Truth API unconstitutional and bar Trump and aides from posting government information exclusively on Truth Social before the public can access it on equal terms.

Technical Details

  • Low-latency data feed: Trump Media describes Truth API as delivering posts in milliseconds through a business-to-business feed. Algorithmic trading systems can ingest machine-readable messages faster than ordinary platform notifications [41][53][39].
  • Scraping limits: The lawsuit says Truth Social intends to restrict web-scraping tools and make its API the only way to access archived posts that were deleted or edited [21][41].

Historical Background
