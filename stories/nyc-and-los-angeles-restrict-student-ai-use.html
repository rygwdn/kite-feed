NYC and Los Angeles restrict student AI use

Category: AI

New York City Public Schools will bar student-facing generative AI tools for children from 2-K through eighth grade during the 2026-27 school year, under a one-year moratorium Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced Sept. 2 and discussed publicly Sept. 3 [18][8][21]. The policy affects about 600,000 students in the nation’s largest school district and will halt or disable roughly 38 to 40 AI-enabled educational tools already used in classrooms [8][12][27][35]. The Los Angeles Unified School District, the second-largest U.S. school system, also adopted a moratorium on student generative AI use for the rest of the school year, covering district-owned devices and student accounts across grade levels [1][15][38][54]. New York will allow teachers to use approved AI for lesson preparation, scheduling and some administrative work, while high school students may use AI only in approved cases [8][35][45]. San Francisco Unified School District has not adopted a similar policy and will not vote on a technology policy until March, The San Francisco Standard reported [20].

Key Points

  • AI literacy: New York high school students will receive twice-yearly, 45-minute AI literacy or critical-thinking modules on bias, ethics, risks and effects on careers [8][18][40][29].
  • Screen limits: New York’s policy recommends 30 minutes of individual screen time per day for grades 3 through 5 and 45 minutes for grades 6 through 8, while individual devices are barred for the youngest students until third grade [18][30][52].
  • Companion chatbots: New York will prohibit companion chatbots across all grades, including high school, even as it allows limited supervised AI use for older students [17][18][52].
  • Implementation questions: Chalkbeat reported that New York officials had not yet shared the names of the 38 software products whose AI features would be disabled, leaving schools with implementation questions days before classes begin [27].

Historical Background

New York City public schools temporarily banned ChatGPT soon after its 2022 rollout and later lifted that restriction, replacing it with a custom AI-powered teaching assistant, according to Microsoft [35][41][45]. In March 2026, the city issued a framework that allowed students to use AI for research, exploration and creative projects, but Deccan Chronicle reported that many parents and teachers considered it too lenient [17].
