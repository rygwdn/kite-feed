Hubble detects 10-sided wave around Saturn’s south pole

Category: Science

NASA/ESA Hubble Space Telescope observations have revealed a giant, evolving 10-sided atmospheric wave encircling Saturn’s south pole, the first large, regular-sided jet pattern observed in the planet’s southern hemisphere [7][10][3]. The Spanish-led team, led by Agustín Sánchez-Lavega of the University of the Basque Country, published the findings Wednesday in Science Advances [7][10][12]. The decagon has appeared in Hubble observations since 2023, after earlier searches found no comparable southern feature, and researchers suspect it may have formed while Saturn’s south pole was tilted away from Earth between 2017 and 2023 [12][11][14]. The structure resembles Saturn’s long-known northern hexagon but appears less stable and still evolving; Sánchez-Lavega told ScienceAlert the finding suggests Saturn’s atmosphere can form polygonal waves around both polar regions [2][12].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Although disturbances that generate waves occur on all solar system planets with atmospheres, AP reported that only Saturn is known to produce polygonal waves [12].

Key Points

  • Cloud scale: The feature swirls in Saturn’s icy ammonia clouds, and one side of the decagon spans more than 10,000 miles, or 16,700 kilometers [12].
  • Layered structure: NASA said the decagon extends through multiple layers of Saturn’s atmosphere, making it more than a surface-level cloud marking [7].
  • Observer network: Ground-based observers helped identify the southern pattern, complementing Hubble's observations [10][9].
  • Annual monitoring: The observations came through Hubble’s Outer Planet Atmospheres Legacy program, which has photographed the outer planets annually for more than a decade [10][9].

Perspectives

Agustín Sánchez-Lavega: Sánchez-Lavega said the team confirmed the decagon “with absolute clarity” after examining Hubble images from 2023 to 2025.

Sources: New Scientist

Amy Simon: Simon said the southern pattern “most certainly wasn’t expected” and noted that it appears to move more with Saturn’s rotation than the northern hexagon.

Sources: New Scientist

Timeline

  • 2012: Saturn’s southern hemisphere dropped from Earth’s view [9][14]
  • 2017: Cassini left Saturn without a replacement orbiter [11]
  • October 2023: Hubble data showed a faint polygon near 63°S [9][14]
  • August 2024: The southern shape sharpened in follow-up observations [9][14]
  • August 2025: Observers captured all 10 vertices fully formed [9]
  • 2 September 2026: Science Advances published the team’s results [7][10]

Scientific Significance

  • Formation questions: Researchers do not know exactly when or why the decagon formed, and New Scientist reported that a large nearby storm may have contributed to it [11][8].
  • Observation limits: No spacecraft has orbited Saturn since 2017, so current monitoring depends on Hubble and Earth-based observations, rather than continuous spacecraft data from Saturn [11][10].

Historical Background

Saturn’s northern hexagon was first seen in Voyager spacecraft images taken in 1980 and 1981 and later observed by Hubble and the Cassini-Huygens mission [2][17]. That six-sided polar wave has been visible for at least 44 years [2]. Cassini returned detailed views of the northern structure before the mission ended in 2017 [17][11].
