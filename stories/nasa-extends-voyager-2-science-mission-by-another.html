NASA extends Voyager 2 science mission by another year

Category: Science

NASA engineers used a power-saving procedure nicknamed the “Big Bang” to keep Voyager 2’s science mission running for at least another year, avoiding the shutdown of one of its three remaining instruments later in 2026 [1][2][5][7][10]. The maneuver turned off selected devices and shifted functions to lower-power alternatives while preserving enough heat for the spacecraft to operate in interstellar space [1][5][7]. Voyager 2 and Voyager 1 launched in 1977 and have operated for nearly 49 years. Both rely on radioisotope thermoelectric generators powered by plutonium-238 decay [1][7]. Voyager 2 now runs only three science instruments after NASA began shutting down nonessential systems and instruments as available power declined [1][9]. Mission engineers are preparing a similar intervention for Voyager 1, which is farther from Earth and has also been losing science power [5][8][10].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Voyager 2 remains the only spacecraft to have made close visits to all four giant outer planets: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune [10].

Key Points

  • Annual loss: Each Voyager loses about four watts of power per year, forcing mission controllers to decide which instruments and support systems can remain active [1][7].
  • Instrument count: Voyager 2 originally carried 10 instruments, but only three remain active after decades of planetary encounters and energy-saving shutdowns [9][7].

Perspectives

NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory: JPL said the maneuver swapped powered devices for lower-power alternatives, with spacecraft temperature treated as a mission constraint rather than an afterthought.

Sources: Live Science

NASA spokesperson: A NASA spokesperson said the procedure redirected two heaters and one device that stayed powered because its waste heat was in the right location and used less energy than a nearby heater.

Sources: Bug

Voyager mission team: The team treated Voyager 2 as the safer first test because it is closer to Earth and had more remaining power before applying the same approach to Voyager 1.

Sources: Gizmodo

Timeline

  • 20 August 1977: NASA launched Voyager 2 toward the outer planets [10].
  • 2012: Voyager 1 entered interstellar space beyond the planets [8].
  • 2018: Voyager 2 crossed into interstellar space [1].
  • 2024: NASA began shutting down nonessential Voyager equipment [1].
  • 4 August 2026: NASA said Voyager 2 avoided another instrument shutdown [5].
