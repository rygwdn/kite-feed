Update: Swiss investigators probe sparklers in ski resort fire

Category: World

In the latest development, Swiss investigators say preliminary evidence suggests a New Year’s Eve/early New Year’s Day blaze at the Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana may have been sparked by sparklers (or similar celebratory candles) attached to champagne bottles held too close to the ceiling [24][22][5][9]. The fire killed at least 40 people and left well over 100 injured, with many victims reported to be teenagers who had been celebrating the holiday [12][21][18]. Authorities are also examining whether the venue’s design and safety setup—including ceiling materials and escape routes—helped the fire spread rapidly, and whether any criminal proceedings may be warranted once the investigation is complete [17][24].

Key Points

  • Identification effort: Officials said the severity of burns has complicated identification, with families asked to provide DNA samples and some injured people still not identified in the days after the fire [7][10].
  • Survivor accounts: Survivors described a sudden, terrifying rush of heat and smoke, with one telling media, “My face was half-burned,” underscoring how quickly conditions turned life-threatening [3].
  • Material scrutiny: Investigators are looking at whether soundproofing or acoustic foam on the ceiling contributed to the speed and intensity of the blaze, based on early investigative focus and imagery from inside the venue [2][26].
  • Owner’s stance: The bar’s owner has publicly insisted the venue followed regulations and cited past inspections, as debate grows over whether safety standards were met [1][4][19].
  • Cross-border care: Some victims were transferred to specialized burn units in hospitals in France and Italy, reflecting rapid international medical cooperation for severe injuries [15][16].

Technical Details

  • Bottle sparklers: Sparklers (sometimes described as Bengal candles) can throw hot sparks upward; officials believe sparks reached the ceiling area and ignited materials inside the venue [9][22].
  • Acoustic foam risk: Investigators are examining whether ceiling soundproofing/acoustic foam helped flames spread quickly once ignited [2][26].
  • Egress constraints: Authorities are reviewing capacity, evacuation routes and how people could exit the basement area as smoke and flames spread [17].

  • Venue safety practices: The investigation’s focus on ceiling materials and escape routes may prompt wider reviews of nightclub/bar fire-safety choices in entertainment districts and resort towns [17][24].
