Update: OpenAI CEO to apologize, tighten safeguards after B.C. shooting

Category: Canada

In the latest development after the Tumbler Ridge, B.C., mass shooting, B.C. Premier David Eby says OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has agreed to apologize to local families and residents [1][5][8][2]. The request follows scrutiny of how OpenAI handled the alleged shooter’s online interactions with ChatGPT — and whether the company did enough to alert authorities about concerning behaviour [1][2][4]. Federal Artificial Intelligence Minister Evan Solomon says Altman also agreed to specific safety steps aimed at lowering future risks. Those include a report on new systems OpenAI is building to spot high-risk offenders and policy violators [3]. Solomon has said he wanted concrete changes after a virtual meeting with Altman, with media reporting that his asks included clearer ways to reach Canadian law enforcement as part of stronger safeguards [6][9]. The tragedy remains the heart of the story. But in the near term, it has also sharpened calls for accountability: provincial and federal leaders are publicly pressing for better safety practices, and OpenAI says it’s willing to adjust how it responds [1][3][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • RCMP contact: Solomon said he wanted a direct point of contact between OpenAI and the RCMP. He later indicated OpenAI agreed to strengthen safeguards after his discussions with Altman [6][9][7].
  • Retroactive review: BetaKit reports Solomon asked OpenAI to apply new safety standards retroactively — reviewing previously flagged cases and referring any that meet updated criteria to the RCMP — which Altman agreed to do [9].
  • Support referrals: BetaKit reports OpenAI committed to safety protocols that guide people in distress toward appropriate local support services [9].
  • Context sensitivity: An OpenAI spokesperson told BetaKit the company is working to better account for “country and community context” when applying safety measures [9].
  • Apology backing: Solomon said he “absolutely” supports Eby’s request for an apology to the community, according to reporting on his comments [7].

Perspectives

British Columbia government (David Eby): Eby said Altman agreed to apologize to Tumbler Ridge families and residents after the shooting, following a video call that also included the mayor [1].

Sources: The Globe and Mail

Federal government (Evan Solomon): Solomon said he pressed Altman for concrete safety changes after the shooting, including a direct point of contact with the RCMP and clearer rules for when OpenAI should escalate concerning cases. He also said he “absolutely” supports Eby’s request for an apology [3][6][7].

Sources: Canada's National Observer, Toronto Star, Toronto Star

OpenAI (spokesperson, via BetaKit): An OpenAI spokesperson said Altman discussed steps such as tightening law-enforcement referral criteria and improving how the company’s systems account for country and community context [9].
