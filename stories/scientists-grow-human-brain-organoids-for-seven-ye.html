Scientists grow human brain organoids for seven years

Category: Science

Researchers grew human brain organoids in a laboratory for a record seven years, creating the oldest reported human brain-cell clusters of their kind, according to reports [1][3]. The work found that the lab-grown cells aged in ways similar to brain cells inside the human body, leading scientists to say the organoids appeared to have “recorded the passage of time” [1]. Researchers grew the organoids outside the body, where they developed in a pattern resembling natural human brain-cell development, Slovenia’s STA reported [3]. STA said the findings could give researchers long-lived laboratory models for studying the brain [3].

Key Points

  • Tiny models: Phys.org described the organoids as peppercorn-sized clumps of human brain cells grown in the lab [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Uncertainty and confidence: The reports do not include sample size, statistical measures or replication details, so the strength of the evidence cannot be assessed from the article metadata and summaries alone [1][3].
  • Limitations and future work: The articles describe clumps of brain cells, not complete brains. Further work would be needed to show how closely the organoids reproduce specific brain structures, circuits or disease processes [1][common].
