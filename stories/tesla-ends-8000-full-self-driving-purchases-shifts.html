Tesla ends $8,000 Full Self-Driving purchases, shifts subscriptions

Category: Technology

Tesla says it will stop selling its Full Self-Driving (Supervised) option as a one-time purchase after Feb. 14, 2026, and will offer the feature only via a monthly subscription, according to CEO Elon Musk’s post on X [1][6][3][9]. Multiple outlets note Tesla did not disclose a new subscription price or a specific reason for the change [6][9][2]. The shift reframes FSD as an on-demand software service, letting drivers pay for access when they want it rather than committing upfront—an approach that could lower the barrier for some owners to try advanced driver-assistance features [6][10].

Primary Source: View original article

Tesla will stop selling FSD after Feb 14. FSD will only be available as a monthly subscription thereafter.

— Elon Musk (Engadget)

Did you know: Fast Company reports that one milestone in Musk’s shareholder-approved pay package is reaching 10 million monthly active FSD subscriptions before 2035 [7].

Key Points

  • Current pricing: Tesla currently lists FSD (Supervised) as an $8,000 add-on for some vehicles, while other trims include it in the vehicle price; outlets also point to existing subscription pricing of $99 per month or $999 per year [1][6].
  • Naming and supervision: Tesla’s own materials emphasize the system requires “active driver supervision” and does not make the car autonomous, and some coverage argues the branding has drawn scrutiny because drivers must remain responsible for control [1][7][6].
  • Legal pressure: Engadget reports a California judge recently found Tesla used “deceptive language” to market Autopilot and recommended a 30-day suspension of sales in the state, while noting Tesla has 90 days to comply and could still avoid punishment [6].
  • Investor milestone: Fast Company says Tesla shareholders approved a pay package for Musk tied in part to growing FSD to 10 million monthly active subscriptions before 2035, making recurring subscriptions a metric that matters beyond just product packaging [7].
  • Product promise gap: Several reports highlight that FSD has not achieved the fully autonomous capability implied by its name, and note Musk has promoted self-driving timelines for years while the feature remains supervised driver assistance [1][6][5].

Perspectives

Tesla (product position): Tesla describes Full Self-Driving (Supervised) as a suite that can drive “almost anywhere with minimal driver intervention,” while stating enabled features require active supervision and do not make the vehicle autonomous, and that some features depend on development and regulatory approval that may vary by jurisdiction.

Sources: Mashable

Elon Musk: Musk announced Tesla will stop selling FSD after Feb. 14 and move to subscription-only availability, without providing pricing details or an explanation in the posts cited by multiple outlets.

Sources: TechCrunch

Consumer and legal scrutiny (as reported): Coverage notes longstanding criticism that “Autopilot” and “Full Self-Driving” can be misleading labels, and highlights a recent California judge’s finding that Tesla used “deceptive language” to market Autopilot and a recommendation for a temporary sales suspension in the state.

Sources: Engadget

Tesla owner and buyer considerations (as reported): Some outlets frame the subscription-only move as potentially beneficial for shoppers who want to try FSD without an upfront purchase, or who plan to sell or trade in their vehicle and don’t want to pay for a feature they may not use long-term.

Sources: Engadget

Timeline

  • Wednesday: Musk posts that Tesla will stop selling FSD [1].
  • Feb 14: Subscription-only access is set to begin after this date [1][2][9].

Technical Details

  • Full Self-Driving (Supervised): Tesla’s FSD (Supervised) bundles driver-assistance functions intended to handle tasks like steering and lane changes, but it requires active driver supervision and is not autonomous driving, according to Tesla’s disclosures cited in coverage [1][7][6].
  • Autonomy levels (L3–L5): Engadget notes FSD requires constant supervision and says it does not qualify as Level 3 conditional automation, underscoring that the system remains in the driver-assist category rather than true self-driving [6].
  • Feature gating by jurisdiction: Tesla indicates some capabilities depend on development and regulatory approval and may take longer in some jurisdictions, which helps explain why feature availability can differ across regions [1].

Industry Impact

  • Vehicle software monetization: The move reinforces a broader auto-industry trend toward selling driver-assistance and infotainment features as recurring services rather than permanent options, potentially changing how buyers compare total cost of ownership across brands [6][10].
  • Used-car dynamics: Because access would no longer be tied to a one-time purchase that stays with the vehicle, subscription-only FSD could reduce the resale-value premium associated with a car having the feature “owned,” while making it simpler for second owners to activate it when needed [6].
  • Regulatory and labeling scrutiny: Ongoing debate about what counts as “self-driving” could accelerate clearer, more standardized naming and disclosure practices across advanced driver-assistance systems, especially if courts and regulators focus on marketing language [6].

Historical Background

Tesla has marketed Autopilot and Full Self-Driving for years as advanced driver-assistance capabilities, while also stating the features require active driver supervision and do not make the car autonomous [6][1]. Engadget notes Musk has promoted self-driving progress since at least 2015, including predictions of “complete autonomy” by 2018 that did not materialize as described [6]. Several reports add that FSD remains supervised and does not meet higher self-driving autonomy levels cited in industry discussions [6].

Q&A

Q: Will current owners who already paid upfront keep lifetime access after the change?

A: The articles describe Tesla ending new one-time sales and moving future access to subscription-only, but they do not clearly state in these sources how previously purchased FSD will be handled going forward [1][3].

Q: What will Tesla charge per month once subscriptions are the only option?

A: Multiple outlets say Musk did not announce a new price or explain pricing changes alongside the subscription-only shift [6][9][2].

Q: How will this change affect FSD availability outside the US?

A: Mashable notes that feature rollout and timing can depend on development and regulatory approval and may take longer in some jurisdictions, but the sources here do not provide a country-by-country plan for the subscription-only era [1].

Action Items

  • Decide whether you want to buy FSD outright before it ends: If you’re shopping for a Tesla that offers FSD as an option, Tesla owner reporting says the one-time purchase ends after Feb. 14, 2026; compare the $8,000 upfront price to the subscription option in your region before committing [1][6].
  • Treat FSD like a trialable feature: If you already own a compatible Tesla, consider subscribing only for periods you expect to use it most (for example, long highway trips), since coverage notes the subscription model can let owners pay only when needed [6].
