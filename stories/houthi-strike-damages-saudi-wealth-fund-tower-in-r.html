Houthi strike damages Saudi wealth fund tower in Riyadh

Category: World

Saudi authorities said a Houthi attack damaged several floors of a tower in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District and two nearby buildings on Thursday, Nov. 19. They reported no injuries. Witnesses and images identified the building as the Public Investment Fund (PIF) Tower, home to Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund. The Saudi-led coalition said it had responded to a missile threat and that debris caused the damage [4][3][1][14]. The Houthis later claimed they had targeted the financial district twice that day [14]. The 80-storey, 385-metre tower houses the PIF, which manages more than $900 billion and is central to the kingdom’s economic diversification plans. The strike was the second attack in less than two weeks to damage a landmark in Riyadh’s financial district; an Aramco building was hit on Nov. 8 [4][11][3]. Fighting in Yemen, where Saudi Arabia backs the internationally recognised government against the Houthis, has continued to intensify since resuming in July. Saudi officials have told Western officials they expect the campaign to last up to six months and want their allies to make gains before negotiations with the Houthis [3][12]. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman met U.S. Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper in Riyadh on Thursday to discuss military ties and regional developments [31][14].

Key Points

  • Other claimed targets: Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree also claimed attacks on King Khalid International Airport, Khamis Mushait air base and a petrochemical facility in Jubail; Saudi Arabia had not immediately confirmed those strikes [3][12].
  • Saudi response: The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted five ballistic missiles headed toward the country’s southern region [25].
  • Yemen battlefield: The Houthis have made territorial gains along Yemen’s Red Sea coast and surrounded Taiz, while forces aligned with the internationally recognised government withdrew from an area overlooking the Bab al-Mandeb Strait [12][31].

Industry Impact

  • Aviation and events: The October attack on Riyadh’s airport forced a two-week flight suspension, with operations only partially resuming late that month. Saudi Arabia also postponed or canceled major events [13][31].
  • Finance and investment: The damaged financial district houses the Saudi stock exchange and offices of international firms including Goldman Sachs, while the PIF is central to Saudi Arabia’s economic diversification plans [4][1].

Historical Background

The Houthis took control of Yemen’s capital, Sanaa, in 2014; Saudi Arabia led a military coalition into Yemen the following year to support Yemen’s internationally recognised government [32][12]. The sides reached a ceasefire in 2022 that largely halted cross-border attacks [10]. Fighting resumed in July 2026 after Saudi Arabia struck Sanaa airport to prevent an Iranian plane carrying Houthi members from landing, the Financial Times reported [28].

Humanitarian Impact

The United Nations estimates that more than 1,600 people have been killed and more than 300,000 displaced in the latest round of Yemen’s fighting [4][14].

Business Angle

  • The King Abdullah Financial District was closed to visitors after the strike, restricting access for businesses operating there [3][5].
