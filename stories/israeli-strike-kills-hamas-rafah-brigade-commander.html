Israeli strike kills Hamas Rafah brigade commander in southern Gaza

Category: World

Israel killed Samir Abu Younis, commander of Hamas’s Rafah brigade, in an airstrike in Khan Younis on Saturday, Nov. 14. Hamas confirmed his death the following day. Israel’s military and Shin Bet said they had targeted a senior figure in Hamas’s armed wing, while Hamas confirmed Abu Younis’s role as commander and said he helped plan and oversee the Oct. 7, 2023, attack on Israel [1][6][5]. Medics said the strike hit Abu Younis on a street in the al-Mawasi area and wounded two other people. Israel said it used precision munitions and aerial surveillance to limit civilian harm; Al Jazeera reported that the strike hit a tent sheltering displaced people [6][1]. The attack took place during a ceasefire that began in October 2025 and halted major fighting but has not ended Israeli strikes or secured Hamas’s disarmament. Gaza’s Health Ministry said that, as of Saturday, Israeli attacks had killed 1,643 Palestinians and wounded 5,964 since the truce began. The ministry also reports more than 74,000 Palestinians killed since the war began in October 2023 [9][1].

The offensive policy that I am leading in Gaza together with the prime minister and the IDF will continue until Gaza is demilitarized and Hamas is no more.

Key Points

  • Command succession: Abu Younis took command after Israel killed his predecessor, Nael Abu Obeid, on Sept. 15. Abu Obeid had succeeded Muhammad Shabana, who was killed in May 2025 [6].
  • Israeli account: The Israeli military said Abu Younis had served as the Rafah brigade’s deputy commander and led the Tel Sultan Battalion before taking command of the brigade [6][10].
  • Operating area: Although Israeli forces control Rafah, Hamas continued to appoint brigade commanders who operated from nearby Khan Younis, according to The Times of Israel [6].
  • Ceasefire dispute: Implementation of President Donald Trump’s Gaza peace plan has stalled over Hamas’s refusal to disarm and Israel’s refusal to withdraw from any part of Gaza until Hamas disarms [6].

Perspectives

The Israeli military and Shin Bet said Abu Younis directed attacks against Israeli civilians and troops and worked to rebuild the Rafah brigade’s military capabilities [10].

Timeline

  • 7 October 2023: Hamas-led fighters attacked southern Israel, triggering the war [3]
  • 13 May 2025: Israel killed Rafah brigade commander Muhammad Shabana in a strike [6]
  • 15 September 2026: Israel killed Nael Abu Obeid, Abu Younis’s predecessor [6][18]
  • 11 October 2025: The Gaza ceasefire took effect [1]
  • 14 November 2026: An Israeli airstrike killed Abu Younis in Khan Younis [6]

Humanitarian Impact

Many displaced Gaza residents are living in tents and damaged buildings affected by heavy rain; the Health Ministry reported 11 cold-related deaths after a nine-month-old baby died of hypothermia, the first such death this winter [1].
