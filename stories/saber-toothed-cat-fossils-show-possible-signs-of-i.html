Saber-toothed cat fossils show possible signs of inbreeding

Category: Science

A study of saber-toothed cat fossils from California’s La Brea Tar Pits links deformed vertebrae and rare spinal tumors to possible inbreeding before the animals went extinct [2][3][4]. Science reported that population declines may have preceded an increase in spinal deformities and rare tumors, while Science News described the remains as evidence that the species’ spines recorded signs of decline [3][4]. The findings focus on fossilized vertebrae, and researchers interpret the abnormalities as possible signs that shrinking populations reduced genetic diversity and impaired movement [2][3]. Discover Magazine said the spinal tumors may connect nerve problems, difficult hunts and inbreeding as saber-toothed cats neared extinction [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Scientific Significance

  • Evidence type: The claims rest on fossilized vertebrae rather than DNA evidence, so the inbreeding link is presented as a suggested explanation for the observed abnormalities [2][4].
