Ubisoft shuts Rainbow Six Siege servers after breach

Category: Technology

Ubisoft took Rainbow Six Siege and its Marketplace offline after a breach let attackers manipulate player accounts and the in-game economy, including credit balances, rare cosmetics, and account ban status across platforms [7][6][2][5][4]. The company said it is restoring integrity by rolling back transactions starting from 6AM ET on Saturday, while it runs additional checks before bringing services back [7][4]. While the outage has been frustrating for players, the response aims to put everyone back on fair footing and preserve trust in the game’s competitive and marketplace systems [7][6].

Primary Source: View original article

This matter is being handled with extreme care and therefore, timing cannot be guaranteed

— Rainbow Six Siege X account (Engadget)

Did you know: Ubisoft said the rollback would include “all transactions starting from Saturday, 6AM ET,” implying it has transaction logging granular enough to rewind the in-game economy to a specific cutoff point [7].

Key Points

  • What players saw: Reports from players showed accounts suddenly displaying either zero or billions of R6 credits, with ultra-rare weapon skins appearing in inventories and unexpected ban or unban changes [7][2][4].
  • Company clarification: Ubisoft said players would not be banned for spending the credits they received during the incident, even though it planned to unwind the affected period [7][4].
  • Status visibility: As of Sunday afternoon, the Rainbow Six Siege status page still listed an “unplanned outage” across PC, PlayStation, and Xbox while remediation continued [7].
  • Attack surface: Coverage described attackers gaining the ability to send custom messages to the ban ticker and to unlock items, indicating access beyond simple currency injection [6].
  • Severity debate: Separate coverage noted claims that the hacking impact was “blown way out of proportion,” highlighting disagreement over how extensive the compromise was [3].

Perspectives

Ubisoft / Rainbow Six Siege team: The team intentionally shut down Siege and the Marketplace, began a rollback from Saturday 6AM ET, and said it will run extensive quality-control tests before restoring service; it also told players they would not be banned for spending credited currency received during the incident.

Sources: Engadget

Players affected: Players shared evidence of wildly altered credit totals, unexpected rare items, and sudden ban/unban changes, prompting confusion about account integrity and competitive fairness.

Sources: Tom's Hardware

Independent observers: Some reporting framed the incident as a “widespread breach” affecting credits, rare skins, and bans, while other coverage pointed to commentary suggesting the situation may have been overstated.

Sources: Slashdot

Timeline

  • Saturday morning: Ubisoft said it was aware of an incident affecting Siege [7].
  • Saturday afternoon: Ubisoft said rollback begins from Saturday 6AM ET [7][4].
  • Sunday afternoon: Ubisoft said it was performing rollback and QC tests [7].

Technical Details

  • Rollback window: Ubisoft said it would roll back “all transactions starting from Saturday, 6AM ET,” a common recovery step that restores account state to a known-good snapshot after large-scale tampering [7][4].
  • Account entitlements: The incident involved changes to entitlements such as premium currency, ultra-rare skins, and ban status—data typically stored server-side to prevent client manipulation in live-service games [7][6].
  • Service shutdown as containment: Taking servers and the Marketplace offline limits further unauthorized changes while engineers validate fixes and run integrity checks before reopening access [7][6].

Industry Impact

  • Live-service operations: The incident highlights how central account services (currency, entitlements, moderation tools) can become high-impact targets, pushing publishers toward stronger internal access controls and faster anomaly detection [6][7].
  • Digital economies: Injecting massive currency and rare items into random accounts can destabilize pricing and perceived scarcity, so a rollback approach prioritizes restoring economic integrity over preserving individual windfalls [7][4].
  • Platform ecosystems: Because the outage affected PC, PlayStation, and Xbox, it underscores how cross-platform service dependencies can create a single point of failure even when gameplay happens on different hardware [7].

Historical Background

Rainbow Six Siege is an always-online competitive shooter where cosmetics and currency are tied to persistent player accounts and an official Marketplace, so integrity problems can quickly spill from “just a bug” into fairness and trust issues for the whole community [6][7]. Live-service games typically rely on centralized servers and account services to prevent tampering and to synchronize purchases and entitlements across platforms [common].

Q&A

Q: What systems were taken offline besides matchmaking?

A: Coverage said Ubisoft shut down Siege servers and also took the game’s Marketplace offline while it worked on resolution and rollback [6][7].

Q: What kind of access did attackers appear to have beyond currency changes?

A: Reporting described capabilities such as banning/unbanning users and sending custom messages to the ban ticker, suggesting control over moderation and messaging surfaces as well [6][2].

Q: When did Ubisoft say the rollback period begins?

A: Ubisoft said it would roll back transactions starting from Saturday at 6AM ET [7][4].

Action Items

  • Check whether your Siege account changed unexpectedly: When servers return, review credit balance, skins, and ban status, since reports included altered balances and unlocks during the incident [7][2].
  • Document anything still wrong after service returns: If your account looks inconsistent post-rollback, capture screenshots and timestamps to share with Ubisoft support, given Ubisoft said it is running integrity checks and QC testing [7].
