Canada weighs response as Trump escalates trade restrictions

Category: Canada

Canada is assessing its next response after President Donald Trump imposed new tariffs and import bans on Canadian goods, while Prime Minister Mark Carney’s government considers whether to adjust its retaliatory measures. The United States announced bans on products including most alcohol and higher tariffs on motorcycles, dairy products, aluminum and furniture; the restrictions are scheduled to take effect Sept. 29. Canada imposed tariffs worth $27.6 billion on U.S. goods on Tuesday, matching the value of U.S. Section 338 duties launched Aug. 22 after trade talks broke down [8][6]. The dispute is affecting industries with integrated cross-border supply chains. Canadian cement exporters recently received relief after U.S. officials removed a 50% levy on cement following pressure from American infrastructure and precast-concrete businesses [5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Tariff coverage: Fitch said U.S. tariffs cover about 5 per cent of Canadian exports, while Canadian tariffs cover about 7 per cent of U.S. exports [6].

Perspectives

Canadian government: Prime Minister Mark Carney characterized the latest U.S. measures as significant for some sectors but relatively modest compared with previous moves by the United States [8].

Sources: National Observer

Fitch Ratings: Fitch said Canada’s AA+ rating can withstand the latest measures, but further escalation could pressure the credit profiles of both federal and provincial governments [6].

Sources: Financial Post

U.S. precast-concrete industry: National Precast Concrete Association CEO Nick Rhoad said his organization directly explained to Washington how the cement tariff would affect U.S. manufacturers and believed officials listened [5].

Sources: Financial Post

Timeline

  • 22 August 2026: Trump launched Section 338 duties after talks collapsed [6]
  • 8 September 2026: Canada imposed matching tariffs on U.S. imports [6]
  • 29 September 2026: U.S. bans and expanded tariffs are scheduled to begin [6]

Industry Impact

  • Automotive manufacturing: Canadian and U.S. auto-parts networks rely on cross-border content, creating supply-chain uncertainty as the two countries exchange tariffs [7].
  • Construction materials: U.S. precast-concrete producers warned that tariffs on Canadian cement would increase costs because domestic production cannot satisfy demand [5].
  • Agriculture: Pork producers and the integrated North American agricultural economy face renewed uncertainty as the dispute extends beyond individual farms into cross-border trade flows [11].
  • Air and aerospace: President Trump has threatened to restrict Canadian Bombardier aircraft sales in the United States, adding aerospace to the sectors facing trade pressure [21].
