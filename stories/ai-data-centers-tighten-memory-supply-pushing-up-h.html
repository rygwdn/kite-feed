AI data centers tighten memory supply, pushing up HDD costs

Category: AI

The rapid buildout of AI-focused data centers is starting to strain the supply of key components beyond GPUs. Memory and storage are emerging as the next pinch points. TechCrunch says running modern AI models is increasingly “a memory game,” which is pushing infrastructure costs higher as capacity and bandwidth become constraints alongside compute [3]. On the storage side, Western Digital CEO Irving Tan said the company is “pretty much sold out for calendar 2026,” as hyperscale customers reserve output for higher-capacity drives. Gizmodo casts this as AI data center demand soaking up Western Digital’s forward HDD commitments—potentially tightening availability and lifting prices for other buyers [1][2]. There’s a modest bright spot for everyday upgraders. Futurism points to early signs that RAM prices have started falling in some markets, a hint that supply can catch up over time—though storage may be next in line for pressure as AI and cloud demand keeps growing [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Allocation nuance: Futurism says Tan’s “sold out” comment refers to Western Digital’s production capacity and how it’s allocating output across customers—so drives may still be available in the near term even if future capacity is already spoken for [1].
  • RAM cools, storage next: Futurism points to RAM prices falling “across the pond” as an early sign the recent memory price spike can ease, while warning that hard drives could be the next upgrade pain point as AI data center buildouts expand [1].
