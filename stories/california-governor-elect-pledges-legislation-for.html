California governor-elect pledges legislation for AI shutdown powers

Category: AI

California Gov.-elect Xavier Becerra said he will work with lawmakers to put “full incident reporting and real shutdown authority” into law [12]. Gov. Gavin Newsom’s recommendations, released Nov. 18, call for enforceable safety standards, independent evaluations and emergency shutdown authority when developers cannot contain a danger; they are not law and require legislative approval [12][2]. Newsom has argued that California needs to act because federal AI oversight is lacking [12][3]. The plan would cut the reporting deadline for most critical safety incidents from 15 days to 48 hours, while retaining a 24-hour deadline for imminent catastrophic risks [12][7]. California lawmakers are due back in Sacramento on Dec. 7, when they can take up legislation based on the recommendations [12].

Key Points

  • Auditor oversight: Recommendations include licensing independent evaluators, allowing the state to revoke licenses for insufficient independence or expertise, and considering state-assigned audits instead of company-selected auditors [12].
  • Public computing: The report proposes funding CalCompute, a public cloud initiative to broaden AI access, through a surcharge on fees developers pay to auditors; Becerra supported funding it during his campaign [12].
  • Workplace liability: Legal analyst Steven Carlson warns that companies deploying AI agents in factories may face injury claims and developers’ legal bills under supplier rules, while insurance policies may exclude AI-related injury or property damage [13].
  • Federal proposal: U.S. Sen. Mark Warner said he is working on legislation requiring rigorous testing before AI tools are released, citing risks including AI-generated cyberattacks [1].

Perspectives

Anthropic: Chief global affairs officer Tino Cuéllar said the company welcomed continued work on independent evaluations and would keep working with California and other leaders on the issues [12].

  • September 2026: Newsom ordered agencies and experts to develop AI safety recommendations [14]
  • 18 November 2026: Newsom released the recommendations and presented them in Washington [12][6]
  • 7 December 2026: California lawmakers are scheduled to return to Sacramento [12]

Industry Impact

  • AI deployment contracts: Anthropic’s rules extend to some users accessing its models through cloud providers or resellers, while OpenAI agreements cap most other liability at 12 months of customer payments, according to Bloomberg Law’s analysis [13].
  • AI-led development: The recommendations would let the state limit the use of AI to design AI and require developers to disclose computing resources used to automate AI development [12].

Historical Background

California enacted Senate Bill 53 in 2025, requiring covered developers of advanced AI systems to report certain serious incidents and disclose safety practices [6]. A June 2025 expert panel report recommended independent outside evaluations of AI systems [12]. In late 2023, Newsom issued an executive order directing state agencies to assess generative AI’s opportunities and risks in government [12].

Q&A

Q: Which AI risks do these recommendations focus on?

A: The report centers on catastrophic risks; it does not address harms such as automated discrimination, job losses, or threats to children’s safety and mental health [12].
