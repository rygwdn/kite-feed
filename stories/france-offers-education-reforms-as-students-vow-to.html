France offers education reforms as students vow to keep protesting

Category: World

France’s government announced education concessions on Oct. 26, including replacing the Parcoursup university admissions platform by 2028, adding 15,000 higher-education places and limiting some first-year high-school classes to 28 students. Student representatives said the package fell short and called for demonstrations to continue [23][3][1]. The protests began with school blockades in mid-September over overcrowded classrooms, teacher shortages, deteriorating buildings and admissions decisions students say are opaque. They have since drawn in labor unions and Yellow Vest activists pressing broader cost-of-living demands. The Oct. 17 demonstrations escalated into nationwide rioting, looting and fires; authorities reported about 2,400 arrests. The following Saturday’s rallies were less violent, with the Interior Ministry estimating 410,000 participants [8][23][14]. Student groups plan further demonstrations for Oct. 31, followed by a union-backed nationwide strike and protests on Nov. 5. France’s debt is approaching 120% of gross domestic product, and the admissions-system replacement is scheduled for after President Emmanuel Macron leaves office in 2027 [1][4][6].

Key Points

  • Admissions changes: For the 2027 admissions round, universities are to publish fuller selection criteria and explain rejected applications on request; a student-and-teacher committee is expected to make initial recommendations for the replacement system by the end of November [4][44].
  • Staffing commitments: Education Minister Édouard Geffray said the government would cancel 836 planned secondary-school job cuts and add 500 teaching posts; the government separately pledged on Oct. 8 to deploy 3,000 substitute teachers [21][8].
  • Student demands: Student groups sought 250,000 additional higher-education places, compared with the government’s pledge of 15,000 [23][3].
  • Budget pressure: Finance Minister Roland Lescure said any further spending would need to be matched by savings; France’s 2027 deficit target is 5% of GDP, while its 10-year bond yield reached 5% last week [17].
  • Local commerce: CCI France chief Alain Di Crescenzo said many retailers stayed closed after the Oct. 17 violence and that sales had fallen about 80% [2].

Historical Background

The Parcoursup admissions platform has been in use since 2018 and has drawn criticism over opaque selection and unequal access to sought-after courses [25]. France also saw nearly two years of Yellow Vest protests beginning in 2018, driven by cost-of-living concerns and opposition to President Emmanuel Macron’s economic policies [14].
