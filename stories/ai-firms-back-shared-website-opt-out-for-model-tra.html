AI firms back shared website opt-out for model training

Category: AI

Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic plan to honor a shared signal in websites’ robots.txt files, allowing publishers to state AI-training preferences once rather than separately for each provider. An Internet Engineering Task Force working group is developing the proposed policy token. Google and Microsoft plan to support it in 2027, while OpenAI says it will implement it in the coming months [1][2][3][4]. The signal is voluntary and does not replace providers’ existing controls. Microsoft distinguishes training from “grounding,” in which a model accesses a page to answer a user’s question. OpenAI says the shared signal will not replace its existing crawler controls [2][3]. The draft remains under discussion, and publisher participant Nate Hake criticized a provision that he said prevents the preferences from applying to any user actions [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Microsoft controls: Microsoft’s Bing will let publishers separately allow or block model training and AI grounding, or permit grounding for Microsoft products but not third-party services. Traditional Bing search rankings will remain unaffected [5][4].
  • Meta’s position: Meta expressed support for the shared signal but has not committed to honoring it, unlike the four providers that announced plans to adopt it [2][4].

Timeline

  • 15 December 2026: Temporary Bing grounding controls become available for eligible sites [5]
  • 1 February 2027: Microsoft begins honoring new preferences as it crawls pages [5]
  • 1 March 2027: Publishers must configure new preferences to retain old restrictions [5]
  • 30 September 2027: Microsoft’s temporary grounding preferences expire [5]
