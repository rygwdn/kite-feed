B.C. towns issue evacuation alerts as storm tides threaten coast

Category: Canada

Squamish declared a local state of emergency as communities across British Columbia issued evacuation alerts and closed waterfront areas Tuesday amid coastal flooding risks from king tides, storm surge and heavy rain. Squamish said the declaration would allow it to carry out flood-mitigation work. Parksville and the Tla-o-qui-aht First Nation warned shoreline residents to be ready to leave [2][8][6]. Environment Canada warned that tides could run 20 to 50 centimetres above the highest astronomical tides through Thursday. Forecasts called for up to 225 millimetres of rain on parts of Vancouver Island, wind gusts up to 90 km/h in Greater Victoria and rapidly rising rivers across Vancouver Island and the South Coast [4][2]. The alerts cover coastal areas from Metro Vancouver and Vancouver Island to Haida Gwaii and the central and Sunshine coasts. Victoria closed sections of its Dallas Road waterfront seawall, and Ucluelet closed beaches and trails. The province increased highway patrols and warned that debris or flooding could force sudden road closures [5][8][9].

Key Points

  • Highway preparations: Contractors are clearing debris from roadways and drainage infrastructure and are ready to respond [9]
  • River conditions: The B.C. River Forecast Centre said peak flows could reach levels associated with a two- to five-year return period, with some weather scenarios indicating higher flows [2]
  • Parksville alert: The evacuation alert includes two RV resorts, a curling club, the community park, three resort or hotel properties and two Sutherland Crescent addresses; residents were told to prepare to leave on short notice [10]

Perspectives

District of Squamish: The district said declaring a local state of emergency gives it the legal authority to complete flood-mitigation work under B.C.’s Emergency and Disaster Management Act [2].

Historical Background

A storm and king tide caused significant damage to Vancouver’s Jericho Pier in November 2021 and left a barge stranded at Sunset Beach for more than a year. In January 2022, another storm-and-tide combination damaged the Stanley Park seawall and Kitsilano Pool [5].

Travel Advisory

  • Road safety: Check DriveBC for current conditions before travelling [9].
  • Coastal access: Stay away from closed waterfront pathways and coastlines, and follow municipal updates [8][5].
