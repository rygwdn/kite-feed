U.S. trade chief says Canada deal is close, flags China ties

Category: Canada

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said Sunday that Canada and the United States are fairly close to a trade agreement, but warned that Canada’s expanding trade ties with China are a concern for Washington. Greer said negotiators were holding constructive talks on autos, steel and aluminum, but Canada’s chief trade negotiator, Janice Charette, told a federal advisory committee that several unresolved issues still block a deal [1][3]. Talks collapsed in August after Ottawa rejected last-minute U.S. terms, including restrictions on future Canadian trade deals with other countries. Negotiations resumed this month, and officials in Prime Minister Mark Carney’s office said Carney and President Donald Trump agreed during a meeting in Shenzhen last week to keep working toward an agreement [3].

Primary Source: View original article

We want to make sure that we’re protecting the North American economy while also landing on a deal that’s good for the United States and good for Canada

— U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer (Global News)

Key Points

  • Auto tariffs: The 25% U.S. tariff on Canadian automobiles is the largest outstanding issue, according to people briefed on the federal government’s discussions [3].
  • Chinese vehicles: Greer cited Canada’s plan to admit 49,000 Chinese electric vehicles annually at a lower tariff rate as a specific source of U.S. concern [3].
  • Trade balance: Greer said most goods traded between Canada and the United States still move duty-free and described the overall trade relationship as more positive than many people realize [1].

Perspectives

Prime Minister Mark Carney’s office: The office said a deal should create good jobs, improve affordability and fully respect Canada’s languages, culture and sovereignty [3].
