Google satellite runs Gemma AI in orbit; two-satellite test planned for 2027

Category: Technology

Google said its Project Suncatcher prototype satellite has returned its first AI-generated response from orbit. The satellite ran the Gemma 4 model on a data center-class Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) and transmitted a haiku during a ground-station pass over Svalbard, Norway [4][1][5]. The satellite launched on Oct. 1, and Google described the response as an early milestone in research into operating AI computing hardware in space [10][12]. The experiment tests whether the hardware can function in orbit; it is not a deployed computing service. Google’s next tests will examine performance under thermal stress and radiation exposure. The company says a successful trial could lead to two interconnected satellites in 2027 [4][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Our chips have officially earned their space wings — an important achievement on the long road ahead for this cosmic moonshot.

— Travis Beals, senior director of Google’s Paradigms of Intelligence research team (Google Blog)

Key Points

  • Limited power: The spacecraft carries four TPUs and can generate up to 1 kilowatt, but power constraints allow only one processor to run for about 20 minutes at a time [2][8].
  • Further prompts: After the haiku, Google tested prompts for space-themed limericks and payload-system telemetry [4][3].

Timeline

  • November 2025: Google unveiled Suncatcher plans for two prototype satellites and optical-link tests [10]
  • 1 October 2026: The Suncatcher prototype launched aboard SpaceX’s Transporter-18 rideshare mission [5][10]
  • 30 October 2026: Google announced the satellite had returned its first AI response from orbit [10][7]
  • 2027: Google plans to test two interconnected satellites with higher power generation [4][8]

Technical Details

  • Radiation-related errors: Google researchers estimated about one silent data corruption event for every 17 rad for typical transformer workloads. At an estimated orbital dose of 150 rad(Si) per year, they calculated a low failure probability for inference but noted that effects on training jobs require further study [3].
  • Orbital networking: Google plans to use its two-satellite test to demonstrate optical links between spacecraft, a capability a single-satellite prototype cannot test [10][4].

Historical Background

Google’s 2025 research modeled an illustrative constellation of 81 satellites in low Earth orbit, arranged in a cluster with neighboring spacecraft roughly 100 to 200 meters apart. The proposed dawn-dusk orbit could provide near-continuous solar power [10].
