Tesla unveils thruster-powered Roadster, targets production in 2028

Category: Technology

Tesla unveiled a redesigned Roadster developed with SpaceX at a Texas test site on Oct. 15, demonstrating a prototype that drove upside down on a ramp, lifted off, flipped and landed using compressed-gas thrusters [9][10][6]. Tesla also showed the car accelerating from zero to 60 mph in 0.81 seconds, a company-reported demonstration time rather than an independently verified production result [24][48]. The company outlined three versions: two-seat Performance and Thrust models, and a single-seat Rocket model with thrusters positioned around the car for aerial maneuvers. Tesla claims more than 300 miles of range and a top speed above 230 mph. Its reservation page listed deposits of $50,000 for Performance and $250,000 for Thrust; Rocket is available by invitation only [9][13]. Tesla did not announce final prices or complete specifications [2][48]. Chief Executive Elon Musk said Tesla is targeting production in 2028. The company first showed the second-generation Roadster in 2017 with production planned for 2020, a schedule it missed [9][32]. The demonstration involved short, controlled airborne movements and did not establish that the car could sustain flight. The Rocket configuration is not approved for public-road use [34][13].

Did you know: Tesla launched its original Roadster into space in 2018 as a publicity stunt aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket [6].

Key Points

  • Compressed air: Tesla vehicle-engineering vice president Lars Moravy said 10 cold-gas thrusters draw on two tanks holding 600 liters of compressed air; Tesla says the tanks can be recharged [9].
  • Trim targets: Tesla lists 0-to-60-mph targets below 1.6 seconds for Performance, below 0.9 seconds for Thrust and below 0.59 seconds for Rocket [13].
  • Control systems: The redesigned Roadster also uses steer-by-wire, brake-by-wire and throttle-by-wire technology [2].
  • Schedule changes: Musk repeatedly shifted the planned reveal in 2026: first to April, then late April, a month after Q1 earnings, August and Oct. 1, before holding the event on Oct. 15 [13].

Timeline

  • November 2017: Tesla showed the second-generation Roadster and promised production in 2020 [9][32]
  • 2020: The original production target passed without the new Roadster entering production [11][7]
  • 15 October 2026: Tesla staged the Roadster's thruster demonstration at its Texas-area SpaceX test site [9][6]
  • 2028: Tesla now targets production for the redesigned Roadster [9][24]

Technical Details

  • Cold-gas control: Tesla says electrically actuated thrusters make rapid adjustments to control the vehicle's orientation during airborne movement; compressed gas provides thrust without conventional rocket fuel [17][27].
  • Suction downforce: Fans and a ground-sealing system beneath the car create suction that Tesla says can hold the prototype to an inverted ramp, including while stationary [17][6].

Historical Background

Tesla launched the original Roadster, its first production vehicle, in California in 2008 and discontinued it in 2012 [24][7].

Business Angle

  • Investor timing: The event came days before Tesla's third-quarter results, which analysts said mattered more to investors than the Roadster reveal [58].
