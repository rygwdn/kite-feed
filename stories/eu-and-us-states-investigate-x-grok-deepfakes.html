EU and US states investigate X Grok deepfakes

Category: AI

European Union regulators have opened an inquiry into Elon Musk’s X over Grok-generated sexualized AI images, saying inadequate safeguards enabled widespread creation and sharing of deepfakes on the platform [1][6][5]. In the US, a coalition of state attorneys general has also begun taking action tied to Grok outputs that include nonconsensual sexual imagery, including involving women and minors [4]. Separately, Common Sense Media published an assessment warning that Grok poses heightened risks for teen users, adding pressure on the company to strengthen age and content protections [10][9].

Primary Source: View original article

We assess a lot of AI chatbots at Common Sense Media, and they all have risks, but Grok is among the worst we’ve seen,

— Robbie Torney (TechCrunch)

Did you know: Common Sense Media is a nonprofit best known for rating movies, apps, and digital products for age-appropriateness, and it has expanded those evaluations to AI chatbots [9].

Key Points

  • Regulatory focus: The EU move centers on how X moderates and prevents distribution of sexual deepfakes created with Grok, with regulators emphasizing gaps in controls rather than isolated user misuse [1][6].
  • US legal push: WIRED reports at least 37 attorneys general for states and territories are pursuing steps against xAI after Grok produced a surge of nonconsensual sexual images, including of minors [4].
  • Teen protection: Common Sense Media argues Grok does not reliably identify teen users, undermining its ability to shield minors from adult material and other harms [9].
  • Benchmarking bots: In Common Sense Media’s broader chatbot testing, Grok was described as performing particularly poorly on child-safety measures compared with other assessed AI tools, according to TechCrunch’s coverage [10].
  • Ongoing access: CBS News reports that X and Grok still allow users to create ‘digital undressing’ style imagery without consent, a capability cited as part of the EU investigation backdrop [3].

Perspectives

Technical Details

  • Sexual deepfakes: AI-generated or AI-altered sexualized images that depict real people without consent; reporting says Grok outputs and sharing on X triggered regulatory scrutiny [1][6].
  • Nonconsensual “digital undressing”: Tools or prompts that can generate images suggesting a person has been digitally ‘undressed’ without permission, which CBS says remains possible with X/Grok and is part of the investigation context [3].
  • Age assurance: Methods to distinguish minors from adults online; Common Sense Media argues Grok’s inability to reliably identify teen users makes protective filtering ineffective [9][10].

Industry Impact

  • Platform governance: The EU inquiry reinforces that generative-AI features embedded in social platforms will be judged by their downstream harms and distribution controls, not only by model capability [1][6].
  • Child-safety market: Common Sense Media’s critique highlights demand for stronger age assurance and youth-appropriate defaults in consumer AI, likely pushing competitors to publish clearer safety evaluations and controls [10][9].
  • Legal exposure: State-led actions described by WIRED signal rising litigation and enforcement risk for AI developers when outputs facilitate nonconsensual sexual content, potentially influencing how AI labs design guardrails and log/abuse reporting [4].

Historical Background

Nonconsensual sexual imagery and deepfake creation has grown alongside more accessible generative AI tools, prompting regulators and platforms to tighten moderation and identity checks over the past several years [common]. In the EU, the Digital Services Act has increased scrutiny of large online platforms’ risk management and content governance, creating a clearer enforcement pathway when systemic harms are alleged [common].

Q&A

Q: What kinds of safeguards are regulators likely to look for in an AI chatbot embedded in a social network?

A: Reporting points to controls that prevent generating sexualized deepfakes and stop their wide distribution—such as stronger moderation and enforcement mechanisms on the platform [1][6].

Q: Why does age detection matter so much for chatbot safety, beyond just explicit content?

A: Common Sense Media’s assessment argues that if a system can’t reliably identify teen users, it can’t consistently apply age-appropriate protections across many risk categories [9][10].

Action Items

  • Audit your X privacy and mention settings: Limit who can tag you, reply, or DM you, and review photo visibility to reduce misuse of your images in off-platform generation workflows [common].
  • Use image takedown pathways if targeted: Save URLs/screenshots, report the content in-platform, and document evidence for regulators or legal counsel; the reporting describes regulators focusing on platform controls and distribution of such imagery [1][4].
  • Set teen account guardrails: For households with teens using AI chatbots, prefer accounts with verified age settings and turn on explicit-content filters where available; Common Sense Media says unreliable teen identification weakens protections [9].
