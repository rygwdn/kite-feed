Venezuela earthquakes kill 920 as rescuers enter La Guaira

Category: World

Venezuelan and foreign rescue teams searched collapsed buildings in La Guaira, Caracas and nearby areas after magnitude-7.2 and magnitude-7.5 earthquakes struck northern Venezuela about a minute apart on Wednesday evening, June 24 [2][8][abcnews.com#3]. National Assembly President Jorge Rodríguez said the confirmed toll had risen to 920 dead and 3,360 injured, while U.N. aid chief Tom Fletcher told AFP more than 50,000 people were missing [2][9][40]. La Guaira, a coastal city north of Caracas, suffered some of the heaviest destruction, with at least 100 buildings, including high-rise apartment blocks, collapsing [2][8][79]. Residents said they dug for relatives with hand tools and bare hands as heavy equipment and state rescue teams remained scarce in some of the worst-hit areas [2][abcnews.com#3][40]. Authorities said they would restrict access to La Guaira from Friday night and require permits after traffic and crowding began hampering search operations [abcnews.com#3][34][81]. Foreign rescue teams and aid supplies began arriving nearly two days after the quakes. Officials and aid agencies warned that the first 48 to 72 hours are critical for finding people alive under rubble [2][abcnews.com#3][40]. The U.S. Geological Survey warned of a high potential for more than 10,000 deaths, while a magnitude-4.9 tremor on Friday was felt in Caracas and Maracay [2][37][35].

Key Points

  • Hospital damage: At least eight hospitals, the Venezuelan Red Cross headquarters and the French embassy were reported badly damaged after the quakes [63].
  • Infrastructure strain: A preliminary U.N. report put direct economic damage at about $6.7 billion, while Moron, near the epicenter, remained without electricity [77].
  • Alert gap: Venezuela has no national earthquake early-warning system, and its seismic monitoring network has fewer than 40 stations, according to Xataka [106].
  • Aid logistics: The United States dispatched two Navy ships, transport planes and helicopters, along with search-and-rescue teams from Virginia and California, to support relief operations [41][38].
  • Rupture sequence: A magnitude 7.2 event was followed 39 seconds later by a magnitude 7.5 mainshock; both epicenters were near Yumare, about 300 km from Caracas [89].
  • Energy sector: Venezuela's oil production held at 1.2 million barrels per day, although one loading terminal near the epicenter was damaged [30][68].
  • Building risk: The USGS said many residents in the affected region live in unreinforced brick masonry and adobe-block structures that are not earthquake resistant [89].
  • Rescue bottlenecks: Rodríguez said 871 international rescue workers were in Venezuela by Friday afternoon, including crews from Mexico, El Salvador and Spain [72][83].
  • Fault setting: Venezuela lies along the boundary between the Caribbean and South American plates, where major active faults, including Boconó, San Sebastián and El Pilar, accommodate lateral plate motion [117][113].

Technical Details

  • Doublet rupture: The two main shocks struck less than a minute apart at reported depths of about 22 km and 10 km; shallow quakes generally cause stronger surface shaking near populated areas [33][86].
  • Aftershock hazard: By Friday, more than 200 aftershocks had been recorded, including a magnitude-4.7 offshore quake felt in Caracas, complicating debris searches and building safety assessments [11].

Industry Impact

  • Air transport: Damage to Simón Bolívar International Airport in Maiquetía closed or disrupted operations at Venezuela's main airport, slowing the arrival and movement of rescue teams and relief supplies [11][63].
  • Health care: At least eight hospitals, the Venezuelan Red Cross headquarters and other facilities were badly damaged, while hospitals and morgues in affected areas were overwhelmed [63][29][43].
  • Housing and construction: At least 383 buildings were totally or substantially destroyed, with dozens of high-rise apartment blocks collapsing in La Guaira's Playa Grande neighborhood [5][32].

Scientific Significance

  • Seismic sequence: The quakes occurred in a plate-boundary zone where a first rupture may have helped trigger a second large rupture within seconds [89][109].
  • Historical context: The magnitude 7.5 quake was the strongest to hit Venezuela since 1900, according to the U.S. Geological Survey [63].
  • Risk implications: The event struck faults that scientists had previously flagged after centuries of accumulated strain, according to Science [87].

Historical Background

The U.S. Geological Survey said the second quake was among the strongest to hit Venezuela in a century, and Reuters described the doublet as the most powerful earthquake sequence to strike the country in 125 years [48][5]. The Hindu noted that Venezuela’s 1812 earthquake occurred during the War of Independence [21].

Humanitarian Impact

The International Organization for Migration estimated that up to 6.76 million people may have been affected, while relief groups identified urgent needs for search and rescue, emergency shelter, health care, safe water and sanitation [5][55]. Thousands of displaced residents were sleeping in parks, plazas, highway shoulders, cars and under trees after homes collapsed or were deemed unsafe [25][36].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Venezuela planned to restrict access to La Guaira from Friday evening to speed rescue operations, and officials asked residents not to travel there [115]. Avoid nonessential travel to La Guaira and heavily damaged northern areas.
  • Transport: Caracas airport was closed after suffering damage, with witness videos showing ceiling panels falling in the terminal [63][97]. Check airport status before attempting travel through Caracas.
  • Safety: Seismologists expect aftershocks to continue in the coming days. Prepare for aftershocks if already in affected areas.
