Meta tracks employee activity to train AI agents

Category: Technology

Meta has begun using an internal program to capture some US-based employees’ and contractors’ keyboard input, mouse movements, clicks, and periodic screen snapshots on certain work applications, according to multiple reports citing internal memos and company statements published on April 22-23, 2026 [2][3][5][6][7][8]. Meta said the data will help train AI agents that can use computers more like people do, including for tasks such as navigating menus and using keyboard shortcuts. Spokesperson Andy Stone said the company needs these real-world examples if it wants agents to help complete everyday tasks on computers [1][2][7][8]. Reports say the initiative is part of a broader push inside Meta to build agents that can handle more workplace tasks on their own, with CTO Andrew Bosworth describing a future in which agents do most of the work while people direct, review, and improve them [7][8]. The move has also raised concerns about workplace surveillance and employee choice, because reports say some workers complained internally and were told there was no opt-out for those covered by the program [6][3][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Tool name: Meta refers to the monitoring system internally as the Model Capability Initiative, or MCI [5][7][8].
  • Research appeal: An internal memo cited by reports said employees could help models improve "simply by doing their daily work," casting ordinary computer use as training data for AI systems [4][8].
  • Task gaps: Reports say Meta’s current AI systems still struggle with practical interface tasks such as clicking through dropdown menus and executing complex shortcuts, which is why the company wants interaction data from real users [7][8].

Technical Details

  • Human-computer interaction traces: The training data described in the reports goes beyond text and includes fine-grained interaction traces such as mouse movement paths, clicks, keystrokes, and periodic screenshots. That can help models learn multistep interface behavior, not just language patterns [3][5][8].
  • Computer-use agents: The target system is an AI agent that can operate existing software through the same graphical interfaces people use, which makes reliable learning from dropdown navigation, button selection, and shortcut execution especially important [1][7][8].

Industry Impact

  • Workplace software: The reports highlight growing interest in AI agents that do more than generate text and can also operate standard workplace applications [1][7][8].
  • Labor practices: The story adds to the debate over how far employers can go in monitoring digital work, especially when the resulting data is used for model development rather than only for performance oversight [6][3][4].

Historical Background

Employee monitoring software has been common in offices for years, especially for logging activity on company devices [common]. Separately, the generative AI boom since late 2022 has pushed major tech companies to gather larger and more specialized datasets for training models [common]. Meta’s reported program sits at the intersection of those two trends: workplace monitoring tools and the push to build AI agents that can operate software interfaces more like humans [1][8].

Action Items

  • Review your workplace monitoring policy: If your employer uses productivity or AI-training tools, read the company device and privacy policies to see what inputs, screenshots, or app activity may be collected on work systems [common].
  • Separate personal from work activity: Avoid using employer-managed devices for personal accounts or sensitive non-work tasks, since several reports say Meta’s program runs on work-related applications and captures detailed interaction data [1][8].
