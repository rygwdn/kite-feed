OpenAI agents claim breakthrough on century-old math problem

Category: AI

OpenAI says its AI agents solved a problem from the Navier–Stokes equations that was formulated in 1845 and studied by generations of mathematicians. The company’s Sept. 8 announcement has prompted debate over whether the result is an independently verified mathematical solution and how much credit should go to the human researchers whose work underpins the systems [5][4]. Mathematicians have differed over AI’s role in research, with some expressing concern about the systems’ place in the profession and others considering their potential as research assistants [4][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Attribution dispute: Critics said OpenAI’s paper cited sources but did not give sufficient credit to mathematicians who had come close to solving the problem, including Tristan Buckmaster [5].
  • Research incentives: Some mathematicians questioned AI companies’ commercial motives in mining open mathematics problems and called for more careful regulation [3].
  • Recognition stakes: The Guardian noted that a human solver would have received prize money and plaudits from fellow mathematicians, while the AI announcement focused attention on the system and its developers [5].

Perspectives

Terence Tao: The Fields Medalist told AFP that he felt he was experiencing an existential crisis in his professional field after the AI developments.

Sources: Mediapool

Steven Strogatz: The Cornell professor said AI systems could readily solve mathematical questions that researchers choose to investigate if the systems are directed toward them.

Sources: Mediapool

The Guardian editorial board: The editorial argued that AI results depend on absorbing human mathematicians’ work and said insufficient attribution could erase human ingenuity while allowing companies to claim the credit.
