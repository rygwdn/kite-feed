Saskatchewan launches independent review, expands hospital security measures

Category: Canada

Saskatchewan has launched an independent, third-party review of safety and security in its hospitals after the Saskatchewan Health Authority requested it, aiming to strengthen protections for patients, staff and visitors [1][3]. Alongside the review, the province is adding metal detectors at hospitals in Saskatoon, Regina, North Battleford and Prince Albert and plans to broaden screening to additional urban and rural emergency departments [2][3]. The move responds to rising safety concerns in health-care settings, and the government says the review will help identify practical improvements and next steps [2][1].

Key Points

  • Added staffing: Saskatchewan says it will hire 51 new protective service workers to support the expanded screening and security presence in emergency departments [3].
  • How review works: The Ministry of Health will commission an independent, third-party assessment of hospital safety and security, an approach intended to provide external recommendations rather than an internal audit [1].
  • Scope expansion: Beyond the four cities slated for metal detectors, the province plans to extend the use of detectors to more emergency departments in both urban and rural areas [3].
  • What triggered action: The Saskatchewan Health Authority asked for the review, and reporting links the decision to increasing safety concerns in hospitals and emergency rooms [1][2].
