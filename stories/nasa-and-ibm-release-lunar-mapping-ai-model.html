NASA and IBM release lunar mapping AI model

Category: Technology

NASA and IBM released the NASA-IBM Lunar Foundation Model on Thursday, an open-source AI tool built to help scientists analyze decades of lunar observations and plan future Moon missions [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][9][10][13][14]. The model can help identify possible ice deposits in permanently shadowed lunar regions, map craters for safer landing-site selection and study volcanic features, NASA and IBM said [1][2][3][4][9][10][12][13][14]. NASA and IBM trained the tool on more than 30 data layers from nine instruments across four NASA missions, including the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter. It joins their Prithvi family of open foundation models [1][9][13]. The release supports NASA's Artemis program, which aims to return astronauts to the lunar surface in 2028 after the last Apollo Moon landing in December 1972 [2][14]. NASA and IBM made the model available through Hugging Face. Engadget reported that the organizations also released a dataset researchers can use to build their own models [4][5][6].

Primary Source: View original article

The NASA-IBM Lunar Foundation Model gives scientists a foundation to explore the Moon at scale, connecting observations across instruments, revealing patterns that are difficult to see in isolation, and providing an open platform the global research community can build on

— Juan Bernabe-Moreno (Fast Company)

Did you know: NASA says the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter has produced more data than all of its other planetary missions combined [12].

Key Points

  • Crater benchmark: Engadget reported that the lunar model outperformed Microsoft's SwinV2-B by 19% in crater identification and classification while using half the training data [6].
  • Manual workload: Researchers previously had to inspect maps and images by hand or use task-specific, lower-resolution machine-learning tools [1][4][9][13].

Timeline

  • December 1972: Apollo 17 became the last crewed Moon landing [2][6].
  • 6 April 2026: Artemis II completed a crewed lunar flyby [6].
  • 10 September 2026: NASA and IBM released the open-source lunar model [1][6].
  • 2028: NASA plans a crewed Artemis lunar landing [2][14].

Technical Details

  • Multisensor input: Live Science reported that lunar datasets include high-resolution optical camera images, laser altimeter readings, radar reflectance data and spectrometer measurements of elemental density, creating a difficult sensor-fusion problem [11].
  • Ice benchmark: For possible ice mapping, NASA and IBM compared the model's predictions with a map built from terrain, thermal and other environmental data, then tested it against SwinV2-B [6].
  • Foundation model approach: Xataka reported that the model can be adapted to specific lunar-science tasks rather than requiring researchers to build and train a separate specialized model for each one [12].

Scientific Significance

  • Resource mapping: The model targets candidate lunar ice rather than announcing a new confirmed deposit; Reuters reported that ice interests space agencies because it can indicate water and oxygen resources needed for longer human activity [1][9].
  • Method limits: The reported accuracy gains come from benchmark comparisons against existing workflows and SwinV2-B, so field validation and mission planning decisions still depend on scientific review of the model's outputs [6].

Historical Background

IBM and NASA have worked together for more than 60 years, including during the Apollo-era effort to put the first humans on the Moon [4]. NASA's Artemis program plans to test technologies for a sustained lunar presence and later Mars missions [14].
