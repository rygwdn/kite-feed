Judge blocks FCC’s early review of eight Disney-owned ABC station licenses

Category: Business

A federal judge on Wednesday barred the Federal Communications Commission from continuing its early review of eight broadcast licenses held by Disney-owned ABC, ruling that the network is likely to show the FCC retaliated against it for speech critical of President Trump [3][5]. The preliminary injunction pauses the review while ABC and Disney’s lawsuit proceeds; U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan also rejected the FCC’s bid to dismiss the case [6][10]. The FCC ordered the stations to seek early renewals in April, although their licenses were not due for renewal until 2028 at the earliest. The agency said its action related to an investigation of Disney’s diversity and employment practices. AliKhan found that the timing, public statements by Trump and FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, and the potential loss of licenses supported ABC’s retaliation claim [5][16][20]. AliKhan did not bar the FCC from taking other future actions against ABC, saying she could not issue a blanket injunction against unspecified threats [5][20]. The FCC said it disagreed with the decision and expects to prevail; the lawsuit will continue [10][14].

Chairman Carr has taken President Trump's rhetoric as his marching orders

— U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan (NPR)

Did you know: Judge AliKhan counted 11 public calls by President Trump over two years for the FCC to strip ABC’s licenses, with the latest in July 2026 [3][16].

Key Points

  • Business exposure: ABC’s owned stations serve six of the 10 largest U.S. markets, including New York, Los Angeles and Houston, and contribute significantly to the network’s bottom line [3].
  • Editorial changes: ABC said pressure had affected its editorial choices: “The View” stopped inviting political candidates ahead of the 2026 elections, and a Senate candidate’s “Jimmy Kimmel Live!” interview appeared on YouTube rather than broadcast television [3][6].
  • FCC rationale: The commission said it accelerated the review because it was investigating whether Disney’s diversity, equity and inclusion practices violated anti-discrimination rules. It said Disney had not adequately answered its information requests [3][20].
  • Narrow injunction: AliKhan barred further steps in the early license-renewal proceedings but denied ABC’s request to prohibit any other formal or informal FCC actions aimed at influencing editorial discretion [5][11].
  • FCC response: The FCC called the ruling wrong on the facts and the law and said it would continue using its authority to enforce broadcasters’ public-interest obligations [10][9].

Perspectives

Federal Communications Commission: The agency maintains that the review arose from its investigation into Disney’s diversity and employment practices, not ABC’s programming, and says broadcasters must meet public-interest obligations [20][10].

Sources: CBS News

ABC and Disney: The companies argued the early review itself caused irreparable First Amendment harm by forcing broadcasters to weigh independent editorial decisions against the risk of losing valuable licenses [20].

Sources: CBS News

Anna M. Gomez, FCC commissioner: The commission’s sole Democratic member said the ruling should prompt broadcasters facing pressure to defend their rights rather than capitulate [3][19].

Sources: NPR

Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: President Bruce Brown called the decision a win for all broadcasters, arguing that the case was novel because of the administration’s punitive actions [11].

Sources: Deadline

Timeline

  • March 2025: FCC opened an investigation into Disney’s employment practices [6]
  • 28 April 2026: FCC ordered ABC stations to file early license-renewal applications [12]
  • August 2026: ABC and Disney sued to block the early review [10]
  • 6 October 2026: Judge heard arguments and requested supplemental briefs on alternatives available to the FCC [10]
  • 25 November 2026: AliKhan issued an injunction halting the early license review [5][3]

Historical Background

FCC broadcast licenses ordinarily run for up to eight years, with renewal dates staggered by station location [16]. Before the April 2026 order, the agency had not ordered an early review of this kind in more than 50 years, and the stations’ scheduled renewals were not due before October 2028 [13][17].

Business Angle

  • The license review could have put ABC stations in major markets at risk; the stations contribute significantly to the network’s bottom line [3].
  • The injunction is preliminary, and the judge declined to restrict other potential FCC actions [5].
  • ABC said regulatory pressure had affected its candidate bookings and program distribution before any license was revoked [3][6].
