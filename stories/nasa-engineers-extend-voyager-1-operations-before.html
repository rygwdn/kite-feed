NASA engineers extend Voyager 1 operations before one-light-day mark

Category: Science

NASA engineers have kept Voyager 1’s remaining science instruments running longer by replacing three power-hungry heating devices with lower-power alternatives, as the probe approaches a distance of one light-day from Earth on Nov. 18, 2026 [1][3][4]. Engineers completed the change, called the “Big Bang,” in September to delay another instrument shutdown that otherwise could have come by summer 2027 [1][4]. The probe, launched in 1977, briefly stopped communicating after scheduled communication attempts on Oct. 18 and 19; its signal returned on Oct. 20. Engineers found that a set of thrusters had gone offline and the spacecraft had automatically switched to its backup, but NASA’s Voyager team has not identified the cause of the interruption [3][4]. On Nov. 18, Voyager 1 will be about 16.09 billion miles from Earth, with a 24-hour one-way signal delay and a 48-hour round trip [1][4].

“This is the first time we’ve seen the spacecraft do something like this, but we are also at a point in the mission where we have to expect the unexpected.”

— Kareem Badaruddin, Voyager mission manager at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory (BBC Sky at Night Magazine)

Key Points

  • Power decline: Voyager’s radioisotope generators lose about 4 watts of output each year as plutonium decays, forcing the team to switch off equipment to conserve electricity [1][3][4].
  • Instrument record: The spacecraft launched with 10 dedicated science instruments; power limits have already led NASA to deactivate two in recent years [4].
  • Golden Records: Each Voyager carries a phonograph record with images and sounds representing Earth’s life and culture, intended as a greeting to any extraterrestrial civilization that might find it [2].

Timeline

  • 5 September 1977: Voyager 1 launched to explore the outer Solar System [1][3]
  • August 2012: Voyager 1 crossed beyond the heliosphere into interstellar space [2]
  • September 2026: Engineers completed the replacement of three devices on Voyager 1 to save power [1][4]
  • 20 October 2026: Voyager 1’s signal returned after an interruption of about 48 hours [3][4]
  • 18 November 2026: Voyager 1 is expected to reach one light-day from Earth [1][4]

Technical Details

  • Radioisotope generators: These systems convert heat from plutonium’s radioactive decay into electrical power [1][4].
  • Coordinated heater swap: In the Big Bang, engineers simultaneously replaced three devices with lower-power alternatives, maintaining heat distribution while reducing electrical demand [3][4].
  • Fault protection: After a thruster group stopped working, onboard fault protection switched Voyager 1 to backup thrusters and placed it in a stable configuration so ground controllers could diagnose the problem [3][4].

Scientific Significance

  • Voyager 1 delivered the first measurements of the full intensity of cosmic rays and the galactic magnetic field from interstellar space [2].

Historical Background

The Voyager missions transformed knowledge of the outer planets, revealing previously unknown moons and rings, active volcanoes on Io, Neptune’s Great Dark Spot, and geysers on Triton [2].

Q&A

Q: Will reaching one light-day from Earth change how Voyager 1 operates?

A: No. The milestone will not cause a physical change to Voyager 1 or its surroundings; it will continue traveling through the same interstellar environment [4].
