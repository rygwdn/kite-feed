Apple readies Gemini-powered Siri as AI revamp

Category: AI

Apple is preparing to show a new, “smarter” Siri that uses Google’s Gemini models, with reports pointing to an unveiling in February [3][2]. The move follows an internal AI reorganization aimed at accelerating Apple’s assistant roadmap, including plans for two Siri variants rather than a single one-size-fits-all assistant [1]. If it lands well, the upgrade could make everyday iPhone tasks feel more conversational and capable—while also marking a notable new phase in Apple’s AI strategy and its relationship with Google [3][2][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Siri began as a standalone app before Apple acquired it and built it into iOS [common].

Key Points

  • Personal data use: The iOS 26.4 Siri upgrade is expected to better connect to a user’s data to answer questions and take actions, echoing Apple’s earlier example of Siri pulling details from Mail and Messages to help with plans [9][7].
  • Two Siri tracks: Bloomberg’s reporting describes Apple planning two Siri experiences: a nearer-term personalized Siri in iOS 26.4 and a more chatbot-like version that enables longer back-and-forth conversations later on [8][10].
  • Two Siri tracks: Bloomberg reports Apple is planning “two new versions of Siri,” signaling a split roadmap rather than a single assistant product line [1].
  • How it runs: The Gemini partnership is described as powering future Apple Intelligence features using Apple’s Private Cloud Compute infrastructure, rather than simply routing everything to Google’s servers [6].
  • High expectations: Gizmodo frames the update as arriving within weeks and argues Apple needs a clear “win” in assistant quality to satisfy users who have watched rivals move fast on generative AI [2].
  • High expectations: Some commentary frames the move as Apple seeking an AI credibility boost while Google benefits from broader distribution of Gemini-powered experiences through Apple’s ecosystem [2].
  • February preview: TechCrunch reports Apple is expected to unveil the Gemini-powered Siri assistant in February, offering the first public look at the Apple–Google AI partnership’s results [3].
  • Borrowed AI: Gizmodo says Apple is leaning on Google tech for this Siri push, a setup where Google benefits from wider Gemini distribution if Apple ships it at scale [2].
  • Core app push: Apple is reportedly planning to integrate the Gemini-backed Siri experience more deeply across several of its core apps, expanding where Apple Intelligence features show up day to day [5].

Timeline

  • Earlier this month: Apple and Google officially announced an AI partnership [6].
  • Second half of February: Apple plans demos of Gemini-powered Siri features [8][9].
  • February: iOS 26.4 is slated to enter beta testing [8][7].
  • March or early April: iOS 26.4 is expected to release to customers [8][9].
  • Summer: Apple may give a larger Siri update reveal at WWDC [8].

Technical Details

  • Assistant “versions”: Bloomberg’s description of two Siri versions implies different product tiers or modes (for example, lightweight vs. more capable), though specifics are not detailed in the provided sources [1].
  • Gemini models: Gemini is Google’s family of large language models; the reports describe Siri using Gemini to power more capable conversational behavior [3][2].
  • AI partnership integration: TechCrunch frames the February reveal as the first visible output of Apple and Google’s announced AI partnership, implying Siri will call into Google’s AI stack in some form [3].
  • Personalized Siri: The reported iOS 26.4 Siri upgrade focuses on personalization—using a user’s information and on-screen context to complete tasks—rather than only answering generic questions [9].
  • Chatbot-style assistant: A later Siri iteration is described as behaving more like a chatbot, enabling sustained back-and-forth conversations similar in style to tools like ChatGPT [8].
  • Private Cloud Compute: Apple’s Private Cloud Compute is described as the infrastructure used to run some Gemini-enabled Apple Intelligence features, aiming to keep more processing under Apple’s control while scaling beyond on-device limits [6].

Industry Impact

  • Developer planning: With iOS 26.4 beta timing and a summer WWDC focus mentioned in reporting, app developers may start preparing for new Siri behaviors and Apple Intelligence expectations across platforms [8].
  • AI partnerships: The reported Apple–Google implementation would be a high-profile example of a device-maker outsourcing core generative AI capability, potentially encouraging similar deals across consumer electronics [3][2].
  • Consumer assistants: A Gemini-backed Siri would intensify competition among voice assistants by pushing frontier LLM capabilities into a default, pre-installed assistant on iPhones [3].
  • Platform leverage: If Apple ships Google AI inside Siri, it could expand Gemini’s reach beyond Android users, strengthening Google’s position in consumer LLM distribution [2].
  • Mobile AI features: If Siri gains deeper access across Apple’s core apps, it could raise the bar for how tightly assistants integrate with OS-level experiences versus stand-alone chat apps [5][8].
  • Cloud AI competition: Apple leaning on Gemini for Apple Intelligence highlights how frontier model providers can partner with platform owners, potentially reshaping competitive dynamics among major AI labs [6][10].

Historical Background

Siri debuted in 2011 as Apple’s built-in voice assistant on iPhone, and it has since competed with assistants like Google Assistant and Amazon Alexa [common]. The current reporting suggests Apple is now leaning on third-party frontier models (Gemini) as the industry shifts toward generative AI assistants that can handle more open-ended language and multi-step tasks [3].

Q&A

Q: Will the Gemini-powered Siri run on-device or in the cloud?

A: The provided reports describe Gemini powering Siri but do not specify whether requests are handled on-device, in the cloud, or via a hybrid approach [3][2].

Q: What might “two versions of Siri” mean in practice?

A: Bloomberg reports Apple is planning two Siri versions, but the articles provided do not detail whether that split is by device class, subscription tier, or capability level [1].

Q: How could Apple differentiate Siri if it uses the same model family as others?

A: Even with shared underlying models, differentiation can come from tighter OS integration, tool access (apps, settings), and user context—areas the sources imply Apple is emphasizing via a broader Siri roadmap rather than model branding alone [1][3].

Q: Will Apple hold a full event or do briefings?

A: Bloomberg’s reporting says Apple plans demonstrations in late February, but it was not clear whether that would be a full event or private media briefings [9].

Q: What is the reported internal codename for the new Siri?

A: Engadget reports the Gemini-powered Siri work is currently codenamed “Campos” [8].

Q: How did the Gemini deal reportedly come together?

A: 9to5Mac, citing Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman, says he reported new details on how the partnership came to be, alongside the claim it could debut in iOS 26.4 beta [6].

Action Items

  • Try the beta on a secondary device: If you want early access, watch for the iOS 26.4 beta expected in February, but install betas only on a non-essential device and back up first [8][7][common].
  • Test real-world workflows: Try 3–5 recurring tasks (setting reminders from messages, summarizing notes, controlling smart home routines) to see whether the new assistant reduces steps compared with your current routine [common].
  • Review Siri privacy and AI settings after the update: When the new Siri rolls out, open Settings → Siri (and any Apple Intelligence/AI sections) to see which features are on-device vs. cloud-backed and adjust permissions for microphone, location, and app access accordingly [common].
  • Check whether your iPhone will be eligible: If the reported rollout holds, the more personalized Siri features are expected to reach iPhone 15 Pro or newer models as iOS 26.4 ships in the coming months [9].
