State-by-state living costs vary widely across multiple expenses

Category: Business

Recent analyses show that where you live can make a big difference in major household costs — from in-home caregiving for aging parents to wedding spending and overall state tax burdens [1][2][3]. MarketWatch found that in-home caregiving costs vary sharply by state, with the priciest state coming in at almost $9,000 per month [1]. Separately, GoBankingRates cites a JJ’s House analysis showing that “bridal look” costs — like a wedding dress plus hair and makeup — also swing widely: West Virginia is listed with an average wedding dress cost of $1,400 and bridal hair and makeup starting at $140, and Oregon is among the states highlighted as well [2]. WalletHub, meanwhile, ranked all 50 states by tax burden, combining real estate, vehicle, income, sales, and excise taxes into one measure [3]. Taken together, the numbers underline a simple budgeting reality: location can materially shape what families pay for caregiving, milestone events, and taxes [1][2][3].

Key Points

  • Care setting comparison: The MarketWatch analysis highlights that eldercare costs vary not just by state, but also by whether care happens at home or in a nursing facility [1]
  • Bridal costs in context: The JJ’s House-based analysis puts bridal look costs alongside an overall wedding cost estimate (for example, West Virginia’s total wedding cost is listed at $12,300) to calculate budget share (12.54%) [2]
  • How the bridal totals are built: The study builds its bridal look figures from components such as average wedding dress cost plus hair-and-makeup pricing shown as a starting cost, then compares the totals across states [2]
  • Tax-burden framing: WalletHub ranks states using an overall tax-burden measure that rolls multiple tax types into a single, state-by-state comparison [3]
