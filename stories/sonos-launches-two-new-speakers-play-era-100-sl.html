Sonos launches two new speakers: Play, Era 100 SL

Category: Technology

Sonos has introduced two new speakers—the $299 Sonos Play, a portable model, and the lower-priced Sonos Era 100 SL—in its first new speaker announcements in roughly the last couple of years. The launch signals a return to core hardware after a turbulent stretch tied to a widely criticized app redesign and the company’s ongoing effort to rebuild customer trust [1][5][8][9]. The new devices broaden Sonos’ lineup with more modular options for first-time buyers and existing multi-room users, with AirPlay 2 support highlighted in several reports [4][3][9]. The Sonos Play slots between the smaller Roam 2 and larger Move 2, with both Wi‑Fi and Bluetooth, a claimed 24-hour battery life, and an IP67 dust-and-water rating for more flexible listening around the house—or outside it [5][7][6][9]. The Era 100 SL, meanwhile, is pitched as a simpler, more affordable entry point: it drops the microphone and voice-assistant features but keeps the Sonos ecosystem and AirPlay 2 compatibility [9][11][4]. Both speakers are available to pre-order now, with availability set for March 31, 2026, giving Sonos a fresh hardware moment and buyers more choice under $300 [9][8][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: MacRumors notes the Play can also act as a power bank to recharge an iPhone, a feature it calls out as part of the speaker’s portable design [9].

Key Points

  • Design and portability: Engadget describes the Play as 7.6 inches tall, under 3 pounds, and thinner than the Era 100—meant to be easier to carry than the 6.5-pound Move 2 [5].
  • Audio architecture: The Play uses two tweeters set at a 90-degree angle plus a midwoofer, along with two passive radiators for bass—an Era 100-like setup tuned for a portable enclosure [5].
  • Bluetooth grouping: MacRumors says up to four Play or Move 2 speakers can be paired over Bluetooth (rather than Wi‑Fi) through the Sonos app for synced playback when away from home [9].
  • Budget audio-first positioning: Digital Trends frames the Era 100 SL as Sonos moving into more budget-friendly territory by prioritizing audio quality over voice features, while MacRumors notes the microphone-free design removes voice-assistant support [11][9].

Perspectives

Sonos (product strategy): Sonos said the Play and Era 100 SL “reflect a renewed focus on strengthening the Sonos system” after its 2024 app redesign hurt customer trust, positioning the launch as a rebuild moment [9].

Sources: MacRumors

Tech press (back-to-basics framing): Multiple outlets frame the launch as a return to core speaker hardware after the 2024 app troubles, calling it a “back-to-basics” move focused on sound and modular expansion [1][2][8].

Sources: 9to5Google

AppleInsider (scrapped expansion): AppleInsider reports Sonos recently canceled an internally named streaming device project (“Pinewood”) meant to compete with Apple TV 4K, and instead returned attention to speakers with this launch [13].

Sources: AppleInsider

Timeline

  • 2024: A Sonos app redesign draws criticism, prompting months of fixes [7][8].
  • 10 March 2026: Sonos announces Play and Era 100 SL speakers [3][4].
  • 31 March 2026: Sonos lists March 31 availability after preorders begin [9][3].

Technical Details

  • IP67 ruggedization: Engadget and MacRumors describe the Play as carrying an IP67 rating [5][9].
  • Driver layout: The Play’s two angled tweeters (90-degree separation), a single midwoofer, and dual passive radiators are designed to deliver stereo separation and stronger bass from a compact cabinet [5].
  • Automatic Trueplay: MacRumors says Automatic Trueplay adjusts the Play’s sound to its surroundings, helping it stay consistently tuned as it moves between spaces [9].

Historical Background

Sonos’ new speaker launch follows months of fallout from its mobile app revamp. Mashable dates the redesign to July 2024 and says it took months to fix—an episode multiple outlets describe as damaging customer trust [7][8]. 9to5Google points to broader turbulence and leadership change as Sonos worked through that period and readied a return to core hardware [1]. AppleInsider adds that, alongside the software recovery, Sonos explored expanding beyond speakers with a streaming device meant to rival Apple TV 4K, but later canceled the effort [13].
