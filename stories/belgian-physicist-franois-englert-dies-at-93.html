Belgian physicist François Englert dies at 93

Category: Science

Belgian theoretical physicist François Englert, who shared the 2013 Nobel Prize in Physics for work that helped form the basis of Higgs boson theory, has died at 93, CERN and news agencies reported [1][3][4][5][6][7][8][9]. Reports in Denmark and Taiwan citing CERN said he died in Uccle, near Brussels [5][8][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Early career: Englert was born Nov. 6, 1932, in Etterbeek. He initially studied civil engineering and later earned a doctorate in theoretical physics [5].
  • Brout partnership: Englert went to Cornell University after his studies, where he met Robert Brout. Brout later joined him in Belgium to co-lead theoretical physics at Université Libre de Bruxelles [5].
  • Parallel work: Peter Higgs independently proposed the same mechanism in 1964; Englert and Brout developed their version together [5][6].

Perspectives

CERN announced Englert's death on Facebook and paid tribute to his contributions to particle physics.
