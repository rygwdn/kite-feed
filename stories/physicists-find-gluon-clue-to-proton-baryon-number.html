Physicists find gluon clue to proton baryon number

Category: Science

Physicists reported new experimental evidence that a proton’s baryon number, a quantum property linked to the distinction between matter and antimatter, may be carried by a Y-shaped “baryon junction” made from gluons rather than simply by the proton’s three valence quarks [5][4][7]. The study, published in Science, tests a hypothesis first proposed in the 1970s [5][1]. ScienceAlert reported that the study used high-energy particle collisions to distinguish between two pictures that had been difficult to separate for decades: baryon number attached to valence quarks, or baryon number attached to the gluon junction that connects them [5]. Science News described the finding as evidence that gluons may do more than bind quarks together, while Scientific American said the work narrows an elusive quantity that may help physicists investigate the matter-antimatter imbalance in the universe [7][4].

Key Points

  • Proton stability: Protons are the lightest baryons, and no proton decay has been observed, making baryon-number conservation central to why ordinary matter persists [5].
  • Related gluon evidence: Science also highlighted separate new evidence for an elusive “glueball,” a particle made only of gluons [10][9].
