Saudi-backed Yemeni forces claim to have recaptured strategic Bab al-Mandab coast

Category: World

Saudi-backed Yemeni forces said they had retaken key positions around the Bab al-Mandab Strait and the Red Sea port of Mocha in a counteroffensive launched Monday, October 5, against Houthi forces [9][18][3]. Yemen’s Defense Ministry said Mocha had fallen to government forces, while government sources told Reuters troops had reached its outskirts. The Houthis denied losing the area, and Reuters said it could not independently verify the extent of the government’s gains [9][17]. The government said its troops, backed by Saudi airstrikes, had seized Dhubab and positions near the strait after the Houthis captured coastal territory in September. The waterway is a key shipping passage between the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden. The fighting threatens a second regional energy-export route while the Strait of Hormuz is restricted [9][3]. Houthi forces have advanced around Taiz, where government military sources said they had taken sections of a mountain road, though another government source disputed that the road had been cut [9]. The government said its forces were continuing operations to secure areas around Dhubab. Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan agreed to deploy military forces and capabilities in Saudi Arabia under their joint defense pact [45][9].

Key Points

  • Air campaign: Saudi-led coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki said 100 fighter jets supported the campaign and 324 high-value targets had been destroyed; the BBC reported the figures as coalition claims [3][17].
  • US assistance: U.S. and regional officials told the Associated Press that Washington shared intelligence and provided target identification and planning support for the offensive [4].
  • Naval targets: The Saudi-led coalition said its operation in Hodeidah destroyed weapons depots and storage sites for explosive-laden boats and naval mines, and thwarted a planned attack on southern Red Sea routes [19][11].
  • Saudi threats: The Houthis claimed they targeted King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and an Aramco refinery in Rabigh. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the reported strikes [11][22].
  • Ground forces: The offensive involved the National Shield Forces and Giants Brigades. Government accounts of the operation around Mocha also named Tihama Resistance and Popular Resistance forces [31][16].

Industry Impact

  • Commercial shipping: Even if government forces hold the coastal positions, the Houthis could still target vessels with drones launched from inland or other coastal areas, Reuters reported [9][46].
  • Energy transport: Reuters reported that the renewed fighting had pushed up global prices, adding pressure to energy markets [9][23].

Historical Background

The conflict has flared intermittently since Saudi Arabia launched an air campaign in 2015, contributing to one of the world’s worst humanitarian crises in what was already the Arab world’s poorest country [9].

Humanitarian Impact

The U.N. migration agency said 184,000 people had been displaced since the war resumed. The World Food Programme said it had reached more than 70,000 people in displacement hotspots, was preparing to assist up to 1.5 million if needs increased, and warned that food stocks were depleting rapidly [9][26].

International Reactions

  • Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan: Their joint statement reaffirmed their opposition to any attack or threat against Saudi Arabia’s security and territorial integrity, but did not specify the forces’ role [9][11].
  • 🇺🇸 United States: The U.S. Embassy in Riyadh urged American citizens to stay alert to possible drone or missile attacks in Saudi Arabia [26].
