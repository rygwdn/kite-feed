Haiti gang attack kills 47 near Port-au-Prince

Category: World

At least 47 people were killed and 22 injured in an overnight gang raid in Kenscoff, a hillside community overlooking Haiti’s capital, Port-au-Prince, the United Nations Integrated Office in Haiti said Monday. It said casualty estimates could still change [11][1][9][10][24][44]. Kenscoff Mayor Massillon Jean told AFP that about 40 bodies had been counted after armed gang members attacked displaced people sheltering in a church. Some victims were killed execution-style, while others were wounded, abducted or still missing [2][3][5][6][8][12]. The attack began late Sunday, around 10:30 p.m. local time, according to Haiti’s RNDDH rights group, which estimated 30 to 40 deaths and 15 to 20 injuries while warning that a full tally remained difficult [2][29][33][52]. The office of Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé condemned what it called a “heinous attack,” said security forces were on highest alert and announced reinforcements for Kenscoff. Residents demanded the local police chief resign, saying officers failed to protect people killed minutes from a police station [2][29][23][33].

Key Points

  • Strategic area: Kenscoff overlooks routes toward Pétion-Ville, an area with embassies, luxury hotels and the temporary government headquarters. It also sits near roads linking the capital to southern Haiti [46][33][36].
  • Prior attacks: UN estimates cited in Reuters reporting said more than 260 people were killed, 200 homes were destroyed or burned and about 3,000 people fled during more than two months of Kenscoff attacks in early 2025 [29][23][21].
  • Gang coalition: Mayor Jean Massillon blamed Viv Ansanm, a gang alliance the U.S. government designated as a foreign terrorist organization in 2025 [7][41].
  • Election pressure: Xinhua reported that the violence could complicate Haiti’s presidential and legislative elections scheduled for December 13, 2026 [14].

Historical Background

Haiti has not held national elections since 2016. President Jovenel Moïse was assassinated at his home in July 2021 [40][common]. Xinhua reported that the assassination created a power vacuum that allowed armed gangs to expand their influence [14].

Humanitarian Impact

Gang violence has displaced about 1.5 million people across Haiti. Many are living in overcrowded temporary camps after fleeing killings, kidnappings and arson [15][18][52][54]. UN figures cited by LSM said at least 3,100 people were killed in gang attacks and clashes in Haiti during the first six months of 2026 [18][52].
