Oil tops $90 as Iran war snarls shipping

Category: Business

Oil and gasoline prices jumped this week after the war in Iran disrupted energy supplies and made shipping through the Strait of Hormuz—a critical global oil route—far more difficult [1][3]. US crude settled at $90.90 a barrel on Friday, up 36% from a week earlier, while Brent ended at $92.69, up 27% on the week [3]. The spike is already hitting US drivers, and analysts warned pump prices could rise further if disruptions drag on [6]. Economists are split on what comes next: some see a renewed inflation pulse, while others expect slower growth as higher energy costs squeeze demand [9].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Hormuz disruption: Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has slowed to a near standstill as safety worries spread across Gulf routes [3][1].
  • Supply hits: The conflict has disrupted supply and damaged key oil and gas facilities in the region, tightening markets beyond the shipping delays [3].
  • At the pump: US gasoline prices rose 14% over the past week as higher crude costs flowed through to retail prices [6].
  • Historic move: Bloomberg reported that US oil prices notched their biggest weekly gain on record during the surge above $90 [1].

Perspectives

Rosenberg Research (David Rosenberg): Rosenberg said stagflation is unlikely. He expects a short-term inflation bump, followed by easing later as higher energy costs weaken demand.
