Avalanche in Pakistan kills Nirmal Purja, nine other climbers

Category: World

Elite Exped said Saturday that British-Nepali mountaineer Nirmal Purja and nine other climbers died after an avalanche hit their expedition Thursday, July 30, on Broad Peak in northern Pakistan [3][6][7][12][51]. The team lost contact with base camp after the avalanche struck while it was trying to climb the mountain, Karrar Haidri of the Alpine Club of Pakistan said [6][42]. Broad Peak, in the Karakoram range of Gilgit-Baltistan, is the world’s 12th-highest mountain at about 8,050 metres [16][50][51]. Pakistani rescuers, local high-altitude climbers and army helicopters searched the area after the accident; officials said four bodies were found and three were recovered Friday before bad weather disrupted helicopter operations [3][12][51]. Search teams kept working to recover the remaining bodies after the company confirmed that no one in the 10-member expedition survived [3][6][12]. Purja, 43, led an international team with climbers from Nepal, Pakistan, Oman, the United States and China [21][44][19]. He became widely known for climbing all 14 of the world’s 8,000-metre peaks in just over six months in 2019, a feat later chronicled in the 2021 Netflix documentary “14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible” [7][10][50].

Did you know: Before the avalanche, Purja’s Pakistan trip could have left him one peak short of becoming the first person to climb all 14 peaks above 8,000 metres twice without supplemental oxygen [1].

Key Points

  • Victim identities: Identified victims included Purja’s climbing partners Pur Bahadur Gurung and Nima Sherpa, Omani climber Nadhira Al Harthy, American climber Mallory Geis, Pakistani climber Sohail Sakhi and a Chinese climber identified as Wang [19][8][44][61].
  • Military operation: Two Pakistan Army Aviation helicopters joined the operation Friday while ground teams deployed to the group’s last known area [18][51].
  • Record career: Purja broke the previous seven-year record for climbing the 14 highest peaks, and later led the first successful winter ascent of K2 [10][8][19][22].
  • Omani milestone: Al Harthy was described as the first Omani woman to summit Everest, Manaslu and K2, and the first Arab woman to climb Nepal’s Ama Dablam [8].
