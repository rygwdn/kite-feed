Quebec premier skips Canada-EU summit, sends chief negotiator instead

Category: Canada

Quebec Premier Paul St-Pierre Plamondon has withdrawn from the Canada-European Union summit in Montreal and is sending the province’s chief negotiator, Pascal Paradis, instead after Ottawa denied his request to address delegates. St-Pierre Plamondon says Quebec is being excluded from discussions affecting its interests and warns that agreements negotiated without provincial participation or consent could face implementation risks [4][10][1]. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Ottawa had consulted Quebec officials four times and offered St-Pierre Plamondon a seat at the dinner head table and a chance to welcome European leaders, but not a speaking role [2][13]. The premier rejected those offers and asked for a separate meeting with European leaders. The summit opens Thursday, Oct. 29, and runs through Friday in Montreal; Carney described it as the start of a formal process expected to last about 12 months [4][10].

Primary Source: View original article

As premier, I will always refuse to see Quebec reduced to the role of spectator when it comes to representing and protecting the economic and political interests of Quebecers

— Quebec Premier Paul St-Pierre Plamondon (Global News)

Key Points

  • Trade balance: Quebec imported $30.3 billion in goods from the European Union in 2025 and exported $13.8 billion, leaving a $16.5-billion deficit [13].
  • Summit agenda: The leaders are expected to discuss trade, digital policy, critical raw materials, defence and security, education, research and supply chains [13].
  • Trade growth: Goods trade between Quebec and the EU reached nearly $44 billion in 2025, up almost 77% from 2016, Statistics Canada figures cited by CityNews show [13].
  • EU partnership: The bloc reportedly moved away from considering unprecedented associate-member status for Canada after objections from some member states, including Germany, and instead favored a broader political partnership [13].

Perspectives

Quebec government: Chief negotiator Pascal Paradis said Ottawa rejected Quebec’s requests to hold an event on the eve of the summit and to give the premier a speaking role. Provincial officials would attend parallel events and represent Quebec, he said [12].

Sources: Journal de Montréal

Canadian federal government: Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne suggested the premier was provoking a dispute, saying, “It almost feels like someone is looking for a fight” [13].

Sources: CityNews

Quebec Liberal Party: Leader Charles Milliard argued that skipping the summit sacrifices a chance to defend Quebec’s interests, saying a letter cannot replace being present at the table [15].

Sources: Global News

Quebec Conservative Party: Leader Éric Duhaime said St-Pierre Plamondon should attend despite the disagreement, arguing that leaving an empty chair would not advance Quebec’s interests [4].

Sources: CBC News

Historical Background

Quebec’s Gérin-Lajoie doctrine, established in 1965, asserts the province’s right to represent its interests abroad in areas under provincial jurisdiction, including education and culture [1][4]. Quebec also took part in discussions leading to the Canada-EU free-trade agreement, signed about 10 years before the summit. St-Pierre Plamondon cited former premier Jean Charest’s participation as a precedent [13].
