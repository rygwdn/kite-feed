Google brings Gemini 4 Hydrogen to Search for faster AI tasks

Category: Technology

Google began rolling out Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Nov. 12 across Search, the Gemini app, developer tools and business products. Google describes Hydrogen as a faster model for coding and other multistep AI work, intended for routine execution, while its Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon models handle more complex, extended reasoning [25][7][17]. Hydrogen replaces Gemini 3.8 Flash in Google’s model lineup. Google says it completes tasks 2.5 times faster than Flash at its default setting and reduces fabricated claims more than fivefold. Hydrogen scored 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, compared with Flash’s 18.2%. Developers pay $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens [2][15][20]. In Search, Hydrogen is the default for AI Mode and handles more complex questions in AI Overviews. Google says it better parses multipart or typo-filled queries and displays more links and visual formats. Free Gemini app users get it through automatic model selection when Google deems it suitable. In independent comparisons, Claude Sonnet 5.5 scored 63.9% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, compared with Hydrogen’s 57% [8][25][19].

Did you know: Google charges for Search grounding per generated search query once the allowance is exhausted, so one user request can incur charges for several queries [19].

Key Points

  • Model teamwork: Google’s Antigravity Orchestrator can hand work between Gemini 4 Carbon and Hydrogen; Google says the combination came close to Carbon’s success rate on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster [13][18].
  • Safety claims: Google says Hydrogen reduces agent overreach by up to 81%, makes it up to twice as unlikely to cross permission boundaries on blocked tasks, and achieved a 96.7% honesty rate in an internal evaluation [17][15].
  • Reasoning trade-off: Artificial Analysis testing cited by WinBuzzer found Hydrogen’s xhigh setting raised its index score from 51 to 54, while estimated task time rose from six to 13 minutes and cost from $1.87 to $3.16 [14].

Perspectives

Search Engine Land editor Barry Schwartz said the model’s expanded links and use in AI Mode and AI Overviews may change how websites appear in Google Search [29].

Technical Details

  • Shared token budget: The API’s 1,048,576-token limit covers input, reasoning and output combined. A direct synchronous response can generate up to 65,536 tokens per turn; longer outputs require continuation or background processing [19].
  • Implicit caching: Google’s API documentation lists cached input at $0.075 per million tokens for prompts of at least 4,096 tokens that qualify for implicit caching, a 95% discount from the standard input rate [19][14].

Business Angle

  • In an early-access Box test, an agent’s accuracy on the Complex Work Eval rose from 74% with Gemini 3.8 Flash to 79% with Hydrogen, according to a chart posted by Box CEO Aaron Levie [20].

Q&A

Q: When does Gemini 3.8 Flash become more expensive?

A: Google’s listed Flash API rates of $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens remain through Dec. 31, 2026, then rise to Hydrogen’s $1.50 and $7.50 rates on Jan. 1, 2027 [20][14].
