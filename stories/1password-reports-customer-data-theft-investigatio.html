1Password reports customer data theft; investigation continues

Category: Technology

Password manager 1Password says an attacker used a compromised employee GitHub account to access private source code, insert malicious code into backend services and download customer administrative records. The company began investigating on Nov. 19 and disclosed the incident on Nov. 25. Its investigation, assisted by independent cybersecurity experts, continues [3][12][16]. The stolen records included customer names, sign-in email addresses, account identifiers, activity timestamps, public keys and authentication parameters, as well as tokens in some invitation and recovery emails. The attacker also copied private source code and accessed the company’s software build-and-deployment system, which may hold infrastructure credentials. 1Password says the attacker did not access customer vault contents, passwords, recovery keys or the keys protecting vaults [3][5][13]. 1Password says exposed invitation and recovery tokens cannot be used alone to access accounts and reports no evidence of account takeovers. It disabled the compromised account, revoked a stolen token, rotated potentially exposed credentials and restored clean versions of affected services. The company has not disclosed the number of affected customers or how the attacker first accessed the employee’s GitHub account. It advises customers to watch for phishing messages [12][20][4].

At no time were customer vault contents accessed by, or available to, the attacker.

— 1Password (Help Net Security)

Key Points

  • Development controls: 1Password says it tightened protections for GitHub, its software build-and-deployment system and employee devices, and added tools to detect intrusions [5][12].
  • Customer notifications: 1Password said it individually notified a small number of people with outstanding provisioning or recovery invitations [5][12].
  • Company reach: 1Password serves millions of individual users and about 200,000 businesses, according to The Lec [16].
  • Phishing exposure: Names and email addresses linked to 1Password accounts could help scammers tailor fake recovery messages, a risk highlighted by the company and security coverage [6][13][9].

Perspectives

1Password: The company says the Secure Remote Password verifiers in the stolen records cannot authenticate users directly. Attempts to brute-force account passwords would also require Secret Keys, which 1Password says it does not store [3].

Sources: SecurityWeek

Sarkis Darbinyan, IT lawyer and digital-rights expert: Darbinyan said customer metadata can reveal who uses a service and when they were active, a concern for journalists and activists. He identified phishing as the immediate concern [25].

Sources: Novaya Gazeta Europe

Timeline

  • 2022: LastPass disclosed a breach that later exposed encrypted customer vault copies [7]
  • October 2023: 1Password disclosed an Okta-linked security incident without customer data exposure [8]
  • June 2026: LastPass reported another breach exposing customer information, not saved passwords [8][20]
  • 19 November 2026: 1Password began investigating suspicious access to an employee’s corporate GitHub account [12][16]
  • 25 November 2026: 1Password publicly disclosed the incident and warned customers about phishing [4][13]

Technical Details

  • Public keys: The exposed keys are public by design, and 1Password says their disclosure does not affect account security [12][4].
  • Invitation tokens: Tokens taken from some invitation, provisioning and recovery emails require an additional single sign-on login or email verification code; 1Password lengthened that code to 10 alphanumeric characters [5][12].

Historical Background

LastPass’s 2022 breach began with a development-environment compromise; information stolen then helped attackers in a later intrusion that exposed encrypted vault backups. The UK Information Commission’s Office later fined LastPass £1.2 million over the incident [7][25].

Q&A

Q: Does 1Password recommend that users change their account passwords?

A: No. The company says customers do not need to change their account passwords or Secret Keys, but should be alert to unsolicited phishing attempts [3][13].

Q: Could the breach disrupt use of the service?

A: 1Password said the incident did not affect its services, availability or product security, and that its apps and browser extensions were unaffected [5][3].

Action Items

  • Verify unexpected 1Password messages through the official service: Do not follow links or act on requests in unexpected emails, texts or calls about invitations or account recovery. Open the official app or type the company’s website address yourself [4][13].
  • Do not disclose account secrets: 1Password says it will never ask for an account password or Secret Key; enter those only in the official app or website [4].
