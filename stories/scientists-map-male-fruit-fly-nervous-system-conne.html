Scientists map male fruit fly nervous system connections

Category: Science

Scientists released the most detailed wiring diagram yet of an adult male fruit fly's central nervous system, mapping more than 166,000 neurons and about 124 million to 125 million synaptic connections in the brain and nerve cord of Drosophila melanogaster [1][2][4][6][7][8]. The work, published in Cell and led by HHMI Janelia Research Campus with Google Research, Cambridge researchers and other collaborators, creates what Google Research described as the largest brain map to date [7]. Researchers can compare the male map directly with a female fruit fly connectome released in 2024 to examine sex-linked wiring differences tied to fly behaviors, including aggression, courtship and mating-related song [4][6]. Researchers said the fly comparisons do not explain differences between men and women, but said the resource could help scientists study how genetic differences affect brain wiring in conditions such as autism and schizophrenia [4].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: A fruit fly brain is about the size of a poppy seed, yet contains more than 100,000 neurons [6].

Key Points

  • Long project: Science described the studies as capping a 20-year effort to map an insect's entire nervous system, while Google Research described its role as part of a decade-long partnership in connectomics [2][7].
  • AI reconstruction: Google said artificial intelligence helped reconstruct the male fly's neural pathways and identify nearly 12,000 distinct cell types in the map [8].
  • Imaging process: BBC reported that researchers made the map by cutting the fly brain into 66 pieces, then imaging each piece through repeated rounds of scanning [4].
  • Model organism: Google Research said Drosophila melanogaster has been central to genetics research that contributed to multiple Nobel Prizes because of its stereotyped behavior and short life cycle [7].

Perspectives

Prof Gregory Jefferis: Jefferis called the work "a really important new avenue in neuroscience" and compared it to a powerful new telescope for studying the universe.

Sources: BBC

Google Research: Google Research said researchers use AI to map smaller organisms because mapping the roughly 86 billion neurons in the human brain is not yet possible.

Sources: Google Research

Scientific Significance

  • Scientific meaning: The map gives researchers a cellular-scale reference for tracing how sensory inputs connect to movement and behavior across a complete adult animal nervous system [8].
  • Future work: Google Research said related connectomics work is continuing in other species, including fish and mice, as researchers use smaller nervous systems to study general brain organization [7].
