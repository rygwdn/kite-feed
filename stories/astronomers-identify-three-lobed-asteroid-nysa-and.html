Astronomers identify three-lobed asteroid Nysa and satellite

Category: Science

Astronomers said main-belt asteroid (44) Nysa may be the first known asteroid with three connected lobes and its own small moon, based on high-resolution imaging described by Sci.News and related coverage [4][1][2]. Nysa orbits in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter and is one of the brightest and largest E-type asteroids, a rare class with enstatite-rich surfaces linked to aubrites and enstatite chondrites found on Earth [3][4]. The observations used ESO’s Very Large Telescope and the Large Binocular Telescope to resolve Nysa’s unusual shape. Phys.org reported that Hubble Space Telescope observations had already helped establish Nysa as the largest and brightest known member of its rare E-type category [4][3]. Sci.News said the data show an asteroid composed of three distinct lobes; the February 15, 2026, image of the Nysa system was credited to Minker et al. and an arXiv paper listed as 2607.25786 [4].

The most likely explanation is that Nysa is either a contact trinary, consisting of three connected components, or an extremely irregular coherent body unlike anything we’ve previously observed.

— Dr. Kate Minker (Sci.News)

Perspectives

Dr. Kate Minker of Lowell Observatory said the ground-based observations approached spacecraft-quality imaging, showing the capabilities of adaptive optics on large telescopes.

Sources: Sci.News

Scientific Significance

  • If confirmed, Nysa would add a three-lobed asteroid with a satellite to known Solar System shape classes, giving researchers a case for testing whether some multi-component asteroids form through contact between bodies or through coherent deformation [4][2].
