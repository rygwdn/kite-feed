Studies link gut metabolite to Alzheimer’s changes, test rapamycin

Category: Science

A study of nearly 1,200 older adults found that higher blood levels of imidazole propionate, a metabolite produced by some gut bacteria, were associated with poorer cognitive scores and faster decline. Experiments in mice used to model Alzheimer’s disease also found that the metabolite worsened brain pathology [1]. The human observations do not show that lowering the metabolite prevents dementia. Separately, a four-week pilot study gave low-dose rapamycin to healthy adults aged 45 to 65. Brain blood flow improved only among participants carrying the APOE4 Alzheimer’s risk variant. The study did not test whether the change prevents Alzheimer’s or preserves memory over time [2].

Primary Source: View original article

We also found that females with APOE4 saw the greatest improvement in brain blood flow, which is significant given that nearly two-thirds of people with Alzheimer’s are women

— Ai-Ling Lin (SciTechDaily)

Key Points

  • Barrier damage: Imidazole propionate reduced electrical resistance in layers of human brain-vessel cells. A dye injected into treated mice leaked into brain tissue, indicating a weakened blood-brain barrier [1]
  • Prior mouse findings: Before the human pilot, rapamycin had slowed brain aging and improved cerebral blood flow in APOE4 mice, prompting researchers to test it in middle-aged adults [2]
  • Microbial clue: In fecal samples from 294 participants, a higher abundance of gut bacteria carrying urdA was associated with lower cognitive scores and elevated Alzheimer’s biomarkers [1]

Technical Details

  • Bacterial urdA gene: Researchers found urdA, which encodes an enzyme involved in producing imidazole propionate, in several families of gut bacteria, including some Streptococcus strains [1]
  • Tau phosphorylation: The experiments implicated GSK3-beta, an enzyme that adds phosphate groups to tau. Inhibiting the enzyme prevented tau damage triggered by the metabolite in cultured neurons [1]

Scientific Significance

  • Genetic evidence: A chromosome 12 variant associated with naturally higher imidazole propionate levels was also associated with increased Alzheimer’s risk in an analysis using inherited variation [1]
  • Additional trial outcomes: The rapamycin pilot also reported changes in metabolic, inflammatory, and microbiome profiles, beyond cerebral blood flow [2]
  • Preclinical limitations: In the metabolite experiments, all mice were male and received the chemical in drinking water. The researchers said further work is needed to assess sex differences and whether the findings apply to the metabolite produced naturally in people [1]
