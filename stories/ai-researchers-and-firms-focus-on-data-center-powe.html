AI researchers and firms focus on data center power

Category: AI

New reporting on AI energy use highlights two sides of the same fast-growing story: major AI companies are making ever-larger claims about future electricity access for data centers, while researchers are building tools to measure and reduce the power needed to run AI systems [4][3]. The New York Times described an emerging race in which companies tout access to vast amounts of power — sometimes without public evidence — to signal scale or competitive strength, calling the phenomenon "bragawatts" [4]. At the same time, MIT and IBM researchers said they developed a method called EnergAIzer that can estimate how much electricity an AI workload will consume on a specific processor in seconds rather than hours or days [2][3]. The goal is both practical and encouraging: if developers and data center operators can quickly forecast energy demand before deployment, they may be able to allocate computing resources more efficiently and cut wasted energy as AI infrastructure expands [2][3]. Taken together, the picture is of an AI industry moving from abstract debate to measurable constraints. MIT cited a Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory estimate that data centers could consume up to 12% of total U.S. electricity by 2028, underscoring why credible energy planning and better efficiency tools now matter for AI deployment decisions [3].
