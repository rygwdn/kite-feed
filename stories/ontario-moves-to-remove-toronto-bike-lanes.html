Ontario moves to remove Toronto bike lanes

Category: Canada

Ontario’s Progressive Conservative government said Tuesday it will begin removing and reconfiguring bike lanes on some of Toronto’s busiest streets after an Ontario Court of Appeal ruling cleared the province to proceed [1][3][6][7]. The province said it will start restoring motor-vehicle lanes on Bloor Street West immediately and issue a request for proposals to study design options for sections of Bloor Street, Yonge Street, University Avenue, Queen’s Park, Queen’s Park Crescent and Avenue Road [1][7]. Transportation Minister Prabmeet Sarkaria said the work would begin “almost immediately” on a stretch of Bloor Street east of Islington station, and Canada’s National Observer reported the province plans to replace 19 kilometres of bike lanes on Bloor, Yonge and University with vehicle lanes [3][6]. The move follows litigation over Bill 212, a 2024 Ontario law that gave the province control over specified Toronto bike-lane removals and set provincial approval requirements for municipal bike lanes that take space from motor vehicles [6][7].
