Iran begins six-day funeral for slain Khamenei

Category: World

Iran began public funeral ceremonies in Tehran for the late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, who was killed at age 86 in U.S. and Israeli airstrikes on February 28 at the start of the war with Iran [2][15][36][41]. Authorities unveiled his casket in a glass case at the Grand Mosalla, where mourners wept, carried banners and flags, and chanted calls for revenge [15][36][25][6]. Iranian authorities said they expect 15 million to 20 million people in Tehran over three days, while other official estimates ranged from 12 million to 20 million for the ceremonies [6][8][13][41]. The proceedings are scheduled to run for six days, with Khamenei’s body lying in state in Tehran before processions through Qom and the Iraqi holy cities of Najaf and Karbala, followed by burial in Mashhad on July 9 [10][7][38][41]. Foreign dignitaries paying respects included Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, former Russian President Dmitry Medvedev and Afghanistan’s Taliban foreign minister, while officials and representatives from more than 100 countries were expected [1][11][41][14]. Khamenei’s son and successor, Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei, has not appeared publicly since his father’s death, and reports said he was expected to miss the ceremonies for security reasons [6][9][10][7].

Key Points

  • War pause: Qatari and Pakistani mediators said they would schedule the next U.S.-Iran meetings “at the earliest possible time” after the funeral commemorations [33].
  • Family coffins: Authorities displayed caskets for Khamenei’s slain family members beneath his, including relatives killed in the same February 28 strike [16][36][43][55].
  • Security measures: Iran planned airspace closures and street shutdowns in cities where the coffin would be displayed during the ceremonies [38].
  • Guard appearance: Ahmad Vahidi, head of the Revolutionary Guards, appeared publicly for the first time since the war began in February [2][9][11][7].
