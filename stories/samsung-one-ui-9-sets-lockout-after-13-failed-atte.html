Samsung One UI 9 sets lockout after 13 failed attempts

Category: Technology

Samsung's Android 17-based One UI 9.0 will make Galaxy phones stop accepting PIN, pattern or password attempts after 13 failed entries, leaving a factory reset as the only way to use the phone again [1][3]. Samsung designed the change to limit brute-force attacks, in which someone repeatedly guesses passcodes until one works [1][3][5]. The policy replaces earlier behavior that relied mainly on escalating waiting periods rather than a mandatory final lockout. Samsung previously offered an optional erase-after-15-failures setting that users had to enable manually [3]. Android Headlines said Samsung confirmed it cannot remotely recover local credentials, so a reset would permanently delete local files, photos and settings that were not backed up [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Escalating delays: One UI 9.0 blocks new entries for one minute after five failed attempts, then increases the delay to five, 15 and 30 minutes after the sixth, seventh and eighth failures [1][3].
  • Final warnings: Samsung will show warnings and display how many attempts remain before the permanent lockout takes effect [3].
  • Rollout signs: A Google News listing cited an alleged One UI 9 timeline that put stable Galaxy S26 and S25 updates in late August or September [2].

Technical Details

  • Smart Counting: One UI 9 adds Smart Counting, so the same incorrect PIN entered twice in a row counts as one failed attempt rather than two [3].
  • Factory Reset Protection: Factory Reset Protection requires the phone's registered Samsung and Google account credentials after a wipe, limiting reuse of a reset device by someone without those accounts [1].

Action Items

  • Back up local phone data: Use Samsung, Google or another backup method before installing One UI 9 [1].
  • Verify account access: Confirm that you can sign in to the Samsung and Google accounts registered on the device [1].
