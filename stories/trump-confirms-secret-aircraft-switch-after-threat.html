Trump confirms secret aircraft switch after threat in Turkey

Category: World

President Trump confirmed that the Secret Service and U.S. military officials directed him to leave Air Force One secretly after the NATO summit in Ankara on July 8 because of a security threat [6][80][17][37]. He publicly boarded the presidential aircraft, then moved by airport catering truck to a smaller C-32A military aircraft. The original aircraft departed separately with senior officials, White House staff and journalists who did not know he was no longer aboard [9][16][23][80]. A person briefed on the matter told Reuters the threat involved a possible shoulder-fired missile attack on Air Force One, arose on the final day of Trump's Ankara visit and was deemed credible and imminent by the Secret Service [23][78][15]. CBS News reported that the National Security Agency, CIA and Turkey's MIT intelligence service had gathered information about a credible Iranian plot to fire a surface-to-air missile at the presidential jet [26]. The New York Times reported that U.S. intelligence had information about someone near the summit seen with a shoulder-fired missile [6][34][59]. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stayed on the original aircraft, while Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and several close aides traveled with Trump on the C-32A [9][16][24][19]. Trump told reporters he followed the guidance of the Secret Service and military and said he did not ask many details about the threat [6][80][49].

Key Points

  • Intelligence dispute: The Washington Post reported that some U.S. intelligence officials considered an Israel-provided warning about an Iran-linked plot low-confidence or unconvincing, while security officials still proceeded with the aircraft switch [10][18][43].
  • Succession issue: CBS, cited by the BBC, reported that Rubio and Bessent stayed on the first aircraft to preserve presidential succession arrangements in case something happened to Trump [9].
  • Flight labels: The original aircraft kept the Air Force One call sign after Trump left, while the smaller military aircraft carried him out of Ankara under a different military flight designation [9][35].
  • Press criticism: Some traveling reporters objected that they were left uninformed on a plane used as a decoy, and one unnamed White House reporter told The Washington Post that covering the president does not mean defending him [40][76][72].
  • Congress questions: Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer sought a White House briefing, and Sen. Richard Blumenthal demanded details on safeguards for passengers left on the original aircraft [54][60].

Perspectives

President Trump: Trump said he deferred to the Secret Service and military, that the aircraft he used may have faced greater risk, and that the incident did not scare him.
