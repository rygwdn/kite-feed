NASA Genesis capsule crash revisited 22 years later

Category: Science

Science Blog and Space.com revisited NASA’s Genesis sample-return mission on the 22nd anniversary of its Sept. 8, 2004, crash in the Utah desert, where the capsule struck the ground with its parachutes undeployed after returning solar-wind collectors from space [1][3][2]. Science Blog, citing NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory report, said the capsule hit the Utah Test and Training Range at 311 kilometers per hour, or 193 mph [1]. Genesis carried wafers of sapphire, silicon and other ultrapure materials that collected solar-wind atoms during more than two years in space, but the impact broke much of the collector material and exposed it to terrestrial contamination [1][3]. NASA’s Mishap Investigation Board found that an erroneous design had left four gravity switches in two redundant avionics units installed upside down, preventing the deceleration signal needed to start parachute deployment [1]. Scientists later recovered useful measurements from the damaged collectors [1].
