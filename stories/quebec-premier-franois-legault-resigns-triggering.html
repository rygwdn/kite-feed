Quebec Premier François Legault resigns, triggering CAQ leadership race

Category: Canada

Quebec Premier François Legault says he will resign after more than seven years in office and will not lead the Coalition Avenir Québec (CAQ) into the province’s fall election [1][2]. He plans to remain premier until the CAQ chooses a new leader, setting up a leadership contest just months before voters are due to head to the polls [2][7]. Several reports link the timing to a tougher political moment for his government, including declining polling support and pushback over major reforms during his tenure [2][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Reporting notes Legault also co-founded Air Transat before entering provincial politics [8].

Key Points

  • Pandemic popularity: Legault was widely seen as a reassuring figure during the COVID-19 pandemic, a period when he became one of Canada’s most popular provincial leaders, according to The New York Times [6].
  • Language and sovereignty: His government strengthened Quebec’s language laws while also shelving active talk of Quebec separation during his time as premier [2].
  • Party challenge: With Legault stepping aside, the CAQ begins searching for a successor on a short runway before the election campaign, with coverage describing the party as facing immediate organizational and political headwinds [7][5].
  • Career timeline: Legault co-founded the CAQ in 2011, and reporting also notes he previously co-founded Air Transat, a detail frequently cited in retrospectives of his career [8].
  • Energy file: As he exits, Legault says Quebec will seek a “win-win” energy deal with Newfoundland and Labrador, while N.L. Progressive Conservative Premier Tony Wakeham says Legault’s resignation does not affect the agreement’s future [12].

Timeline

  • Wednesday: Legault announces he will resign; stays until successor chosen [1][2].
  • Fall election: Quebec is scheduled to vote, now with CAQ leadership race underway [1][7].

Historical Background

Legault co-founded the Coalition Avenir Québec in 2011, a party that later formed government and reshaped Quebec’s electoral landscape [8][10]. Over his tenure as premier, his government advanced stricter language measures while keeping Quebec sovereignty off the immediate agenda, framing a nationalist approach inside the Canadian federation [2].
