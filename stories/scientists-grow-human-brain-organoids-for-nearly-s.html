Scientists grow human brain organoids for nearly six years

Category: Science

An international research team reported in Nature that it kept human brain organoids alive and maturing in laboratory culture for nearly six years, extending a model system that researchers had generally used to study early developmental stages [2][4][5][6]. The organoids are three-dimensional, miniaturized tissue models grown from human pluripotent stem cells, and the work was funded by the U.S. National Institutes of Health, according to ABC [6]. The team found that genetic markers of the organoids' biological age matched their time in vitro; Futurism described the finding as showing that the cultures tracked the passage of time over multiple years [2]. Le Monde reported that the long-lived cell clusters diversified and matured, while Phys.org described related work showing lab-grown brain models developing a sense of cortical place, with the cerebral cortex divided into areas associated with movement, vision, memory, language and social understanding [5][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Research uses: Futurism said brain organoids have been used to study drug testing and the effects of viral infections on neurological systems [2].
  • Development scale: ABC noted that the human brain takes about 20 years to fully mature, far longer than the survival window of earlier organoid models [6].

Scientific Significance

  • Evidence base: The reported age correlation rests on genetic marker changes that matched time in culture, while the articles do not provide sample size, replication statistics or error estimates from the Nature paper [2].
  • Remaining limits: The sources describe organoids as models or miniaturized replicas, not complete brains, and do not report sensory inputs, vascular systems or whole-brain architecture comparable to those of a living human brain [6][5].
