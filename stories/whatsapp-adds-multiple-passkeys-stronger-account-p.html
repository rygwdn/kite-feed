WhatsApp adds multiple passkeys, stronger account passwords

Category: Technology

WhatsApp announced account security updates on Aug. 25 that let users add more than one passkey to an account, replace the existing six-digit two-step verification PIN with a longer password, and, on Android, see more context for some calls from people outside their contacts [1][2][4][5][8][10]. The Meta-owned messaging app said more than 1 billion people already use passkeys to log back into WhatsApp with a fingerprint, Face ID or a screen-lock code [1][5][8][10][12]. The two-step verification change upgrades the previous numeric PIN into a full password that can include letters, numbers and special characters. WhatsApp says the added layer can help prevent account takeover even if someone obtains a user’s one-time passcode [1][11][8][10][9]. On Android, incoming calls from non-contacts can now show details such as whether the number is from another country and whether the caller shares WhatsApp groups with the recipient [1][5][8][10][7].

Scammers rely on urgency – now you can take a beat with some more info before answering.

Key Points

  • Android Authority said the stronger two-step verification password will be available on Android and iOS, and that the improved caller context feature is rolling out gradually [7].
  • SecurityWeek said the announcement followed WhatsApp’s Scam Alert feature, an optional AI tool designed to flag suspicious messages from non-contacts [8].

Perspectives

The Next Web described WhatsApp’s passkey adoption as the largest passwordless deployment known to it, saying no other consumer service has moved a comparable number of people off codes and onto device-based authentication.

Sources: The Next Web

MakeUseOf warned that a stronger two-step verification password cannot stop Linked Devices scams if a user unknowingly authorizes an attacker’s device.

Sources: MakeUseOf

CNET said WhatsApp did not immediately respond to a request for comment after announcing the new security measures.

Sources: CNET

Timeline

  • 2009: WhatsApp launched as a messaging app [4].
  • October 2023: WhatsApp added passkey support on Android [5][10].
  • April 2024: WhatsApp expanded passkey support to iPhone [5][29].
  • June 2025: Meta integrated passkeys into Facebook logins [10].
  • 25 August 2026: WhatsApp announced multiple passkeys and stronger two-step verification [1][8].

Industry Impact

  • Consumer authentication: The Hacker News called the update a phishing-resistant sign-in expansion across iOS and Android, reflecting growing adoption of device-based authentication in large consumer apps [10].
  • Messaging security: CNET said the new measures follow recent privacy-centered WhatsApp tools, including a late-June username feature intended to keep phone numbers from being visible [4].
  • Mexico usage: Expansión cited Statista data from the second quarter of 2025 showing 91.4% of Mexican internet users used WhatsApp, indicating wide local reach for the security changes [28].

Business Angle

  • A larger passkey base reduces reliance on SMS-style verification flows that attackers can target [27][common].
  • The caller context feature is available on Android; WhatsApp has not announced a comparable iOS version [7][5].

Action Items

  • Add another passkey: Open WhatsApp and go to Settings > Account > Passkeys, then add a passkey for each device platform you use [1][10].
  • Replace weak two-step codes: Change the old six-digit PIN to a longer password with letters, numbers and special characters when the option appears in your app [1][11].
  • Check linked devices: Review WhatsApp’s linked devices regularly and do not follow unsolicited pairing instructions, because stronger passwords do not stop scams where users authorize an attacker’s device [16].
