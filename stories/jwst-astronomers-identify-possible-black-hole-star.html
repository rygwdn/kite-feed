JWST astronomers identify possible black hole star MoM-BH*-1

Category: Science

Astronomers using NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope reported finding MoM-BH*-1, a red early-universe source they describe as a possible candidate for a new class of astrophysical object: a “black hole star” [2][3][5][4][9]. The Nature study says the object existed about 660 million years after the Big Bang; other accounts describe it as less than 700 million years after the Big Bang, placing it in the cosmic dawn era [2][3]. Researchers propose that MoM-BH*-1 is powered not by ordinary stellar fusion but by a black hole embedded inside a dense gas envelope, making the object resemble a star roughly the scale of the Solar System [2][3][5][9]. The object is described as about 100,000 times the Sun’s mass and as emitting more than 100 billion times as much energy as any known star, an output the researchers say fits black-hole activity more closely than conventional stars [2][5][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: The phrase “black hole sun” became culturally familiar decades before this discovery through Soundgarden’s 1994 song, which Chris Cornell wrote after mishearing a news broadcast [5].

Key Points

  • Detection clue: The object’s light showed a signature commonly associated with stellar atmospheres, but its luminosity exceeded what researchers expect from an ordinary star [3][5].
  • Gas cocoon: The Nature study places the central source inside a turbulent, nearly dust-free hydrogen cocoon extending tens of astronomical units [3].
  • Search context: The team found MoM-BH*-1 while using JWST to search for some of the universe’s oldest galaxies [5].
  • Open question: Researchers connect the candidate object to the debated population of JWST “little red dots” seen across the early universe [3].

Perspectives

Rohan Naidu, lead author: Naidu said the identity of JWST’s little red dots has been one of the most debated topics of the JWST era, linking MoM-BH*-1 to an unresolved research question.

Sources: ZME Science

Diario Norte: The Spanish-language account said NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope detected an astronomical object previously unknown to science.

Sources: Diario Norte

Scientific Significance

  • Early-universe relevance: Because MoM-BH*-1 dates to roughly 660 million years after the Big Bang, researchers cite it as a candidate mechanism for the rapid growth of massive black holes in the young universe [3][8].

Historical Background

Theoretical versions of black hole stars were proposed nearly two decades ago under the name “quasi-stars” [2]. The James Webb Space Telescope was designed to observe faint infrared light from the early universe, allowing it to detect highly redshifted sources from cosmic dawn [common].
