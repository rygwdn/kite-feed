Peru Congress impeaches interim president José Jerí amid probe

Category: World

Peru’s Congress voted to remove interim President José Jerí on Feb. 17, ending his four-month tenure [2][8][3][17]. Lawmakers pointed to allegations involving undisclosed meetings with Chinese executives and business figures, alongside a preliminary investigation into corruption and influence peddling [1][2][7]. The ouster adds a fresh layer of uncertainty weeks before Peru’s April 12 general election [6].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Vote tally: Congress approved Jerí’s removal 75–24, with three abstentions, according to multiple reports [9][10].
  • ‘Chifagate’ label: The scandal picked up the nickname “Chifagate” after reports of late-night, unregistered meetings at a Chinese restaurant tied to a Chinese state contractor [9].
  • Succession context: Jerí took office last October, replacing Dina Boluarte after Congress removed her from the presidency [3].
  • Age profile: During his brief time in office, some coverage described Jerí as one of the world’s youngest heads of state [11].

Perspectives

Peru’s Congress: Lawmakers backed the removal after debating allegations tied to undisclosed meetings and a preliminary corruption and influence-peddling probe involving Chinese business figures [2].

Sources: PBS NewsHour

José Jerí’s critics in media coverage: Some outlets described the vote as an unusually fast-track, or “express,” impeachment, arguing the scandal — and the timing — pushed Congress to move quickly [10].

Sources: The Guardian

China-focused reporting: Coverage highlighted the China-linked angle of the allegations, including references to a Chinese state contractor and businessman Zhihua Yang [9].

Sources: South China Morning Post

Timeline

  • Last October: Jerí was sworn in as interim president [3].
  • Feb. 17: Congress opened debate on whether to remove Jerí [12].
  • Tuesday: Congress voted 75–24 to remove Jerí [9].
  • April 12: Peru is scheduled to hold a general election [6].

Historical Background

Peru has cycled through presidents repeatedly since 2016, with Jerí described as the country’s seventh president in that span [8][1]. Several reports link the steady turnover to recurring corruption allegations and sharp political fragmentation between the executive branch and Congress [1][5]. Jerí’s predecessor, Dina Boluarte, was impeached amid protests over corruption and a surge of violence linked to organised crime, according to one account [16].
