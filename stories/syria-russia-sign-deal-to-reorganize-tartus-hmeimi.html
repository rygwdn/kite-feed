Syria, Russia sign deal to reorganize Tartus, Hmeimim bases

Category: World

Syria and Russia signed a memorandum of understanding to reorganize Moscow’s presence at the Hmeimim and Tartus facilities on Syria’s Mediterranean coast, Syria’s Foreign Ministry said Sunday via state news agency SANA [2][22][5][8][11]. Under the deal, Syria will assume management of civilian facilities, including Hmeimim Airport and the fourth commercial berth at Tartus port, while Russian military facilities will be redefined as joint training and capacity-building centers under arrangements Damascus said preserve mutual interests [2][12][18][58]. The agreement followed about 18 months of negotiations and sets a deadline of no more than three months to complete the transition, after which the new arrangements will take effect [2][22][12][68]. The two bases remained operational during the talks, and Russia had not issued an official comment on the agreement by the time of publication [12][1][33][55]. Russia used Hmeimim and Tartus to support Bashar al-Assad during Syria’s civil war. Assad’s ouster in December 2024 put the future of Moscow’s coastal foothold under negotiation with Syria’s new leadership [4][7][41][43]. Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani visited the sites Sunday to inspect facilities included in the memorandum, according to SANA [12][1][58].

Key Points

  • Civilian handover: Omar al-Hasri, head of Syria’s Civil Aviation Authority, said specialized teams had begun technical and operational assessments at Latakia airport before drafting a rehabilitation plan [12][31].
  • Regional foothold: Hmeimim and Tartus have long served as Moscow’s main military footholds in the Mediterranean and Middle East, and AFP described them as Russia’s only official military bases outside the former Soviet Union [33][16][50].
  • Diplomatic contacts: President Vladimir Putin has hosted Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa in Moscow twice since al-Sharaa took power, while Damascus has repeatedly sought Assad’s extradition from Russia [12][71][57].

Perspectives

Syrian Foreign Ministry: The ministry described the memorandum as the most significant development since negotiations began about 18 months earlier and said it opens the way for a new phase in Syrian-Russian relations.
