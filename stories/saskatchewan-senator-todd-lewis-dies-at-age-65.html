Saskatchewan senator Todd Lewis dies at age 65

Category: Canada

Todd Lewis, a Saskatchewan senator, farmer and agricultural advocate, died at age 65 [1][2][3][5]. Senate Speaker Raymonde Gagne announced his death Saturday afternoon, and RealAgriculture reported that Lewis died suddenly on Friday, July 31 [1][2][5][6]. Former prime minister Justin Trudeau appointed Lewis to the Senate on Feb. 7, 2025, after decades in Saskatchewan agriculture [1][2][3][5]. Lewis farmed near Gray, Sask., about 39 kilometres south of Regina, for more than 40 years [2][5]. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Lewis’s public service took him from his family farm to the Senate, while Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe offered condolences and called him a community leader and advocate for the province [1][2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Farm leadership: Lewis served five years as president of the Agricultural Producers Association of Saskatchewan and was also vice-president of the Canadian Federation of Agriculture [5][6].
  • Local service: Before entering the Senate, Lewis served as a municipal councillor and took part in community organizations in rural Saskatchewan [5][6].
  • Public tributes: Gagne called Lewis a “steadfast champion of Saskatchewan’s agricultural sector,” and Carney described him as a “steadfast voice” for rural communities [1][5].

Perspectives

Raymonde Gagne, Senate Speaker: Gagne said Lewis’s Senate work reflected his farm background, citing his more than four decades on the land and his leadership in farmers’ organizations.
