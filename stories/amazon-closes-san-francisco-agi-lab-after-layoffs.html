Amazon closes San Francisco AGI lab after layoffs

Category: AI

Amazon confirmed it is closing its San Francisco AGI site as part of layoffs in its artificial general intelligence organization, but said work on its frontier models will continue [5][7]. The company did not disclose how many jobs were affected; GeekWire reported that impacted employees will be able to explore other roles at Amazon, and The Next Web cited Amazon saying the changes involved “eliminating some roles within parts of our AGI organization” [5][7]. The lab was founded in December 2024 around several dozen employees Amazon hired from Adept, including Adept co-founder and CEO David Luan, and the team grew to about 80 people at its peak, according to The Information as cited by GeekWire [5]. GeekWire reported that frontier model research will continue under Pieter Abbeel, a UC Berkeley professor who joined Amazon in 2024 after Amazon licensed technology and hired a team from Covariant, the robotics startup he co-founded [5]. Amazon said Nova Act, the browser-agent model and service that came out of the group, remains available on AWS and is used by customers [5]. Amazon is also promoting agentic AI offerings including Bedrock AgentCore and applications such as Kiro, Quick, Continuum and Transform, alongside a $1 billion AWS effort to embed engineers with businesses building AI agents [5].

Historical Background

Rohit Prasad previously led Amazon's AGI efforts and departed before the layoffs. The San Francisco AGI Lab was one of several initiatives in that organization, and its head, David Luan, also left before the closure [5][7].
