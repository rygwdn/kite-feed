Anthropic IPO filing shows $895 billion in compute commitments

Category: AI

Anthropic’s updated initial public offering filing disclosed $895.4 billion in computing and infrastructure commitments as the Claude maker prepares for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT [22][15][8]. The November 2 amendment added third-quarter results: revenue reached $16.96 billion, but quarter-on-quarter growth slowed to 47% from 144% in the second quarter [15][9][19]. The filing leaves the offering price and share count blank; the company has not announced its fundraising target [22][15]. Anthropic reported a $567 million third-quarter operating loss, reversing a $974 million profit in the previous quarter. It attributed much of the swing to $5 billion in short-term computing capacity, which costs more than longer-term arrangements. Computing and infrastructure costs rose to about 58 cents per dollar of revenue from 48 cents [9][16][24]. Its $23.3 billion quarterly net loss largely reflected non-cash accounting changes to financing instruments rather than operating cash outlays [9][15]. The filing says about $69.8 billion in commitments fall due within a year and $447.5 billion by the end of 2030; some contracts extend to 2045 [22][15]. Anthropic had $131.9 billion in cash and short-term investments at Sept. 30 [15]. The amended prospectus does not set a listing date, though market participants anticipate a November debut [1][22].

Key Points

  • Customer concentration: About 80% of spending on Anthropic and OpenAI tools came from the top 1% of firms in Ramp’s transaction data; Anthropic’s filing also showed that its highest-spending Claude Code users spent far more than users who did not use the coding agent [19][1].
  • Founder control: A non-dilutable Class F share held by an entity jointly owned by Anthropic’s seven founders carries 50.1% of shareholder voting power, while the Long-Term Benefit Trust will appoint four of seven directors after the IPO [1].
  • Contract flexibility: About $213.8 billion in disclosed commitments include termination rights or depend on conditions. The filing warns that supplier financing problems could delay, change or prevent capacity delivery [22][15].
  • Retail access: Anthropic expects to reserve some IPO shares for customers of Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi and Morgan Stanley’s E*Trade, according to a November 2 filing [7].
  • Convertible-note terms: Broadcom’s facility can provide up to $42 billion for eligible rent. Notes generally carry 9% interest and convert at 90% of market price, but specified revenue shortfalls increase the interest rate to 13% and lower the conversion price to 85% of market price [18].

Technical Details

  • Employee donation-matching charge: About $2 billion in third-quarter operating costs came from a non-cash program matching employee equity pledges to charity; without that charge, Anthropic would have reported about $1.4 billion in operating profit [9].
  • Lease-loss backstop: Under the Broadcom-linked financing, Broadcom covers the difference between 72.5% of the remaining lease balance and equipment resale proceeds if Anthropic defaults. If $100 remained owed and the equipment sold for $60, the reported backstop would pay $12.50 [18].

Industry Impact

  • AI infrastructure financing: Broadcom’s equipment-financing backstop and hardware-linked funding arrangements expose chip suppliers and financing partners to the same demand and equipment-resale risks as Anthropic [18].
  • Private AI-company valuations: Morningstar analyst Harrison Rolfes said Anthropic’s public financial reporting could become a yardstick for private AI companies such as OpenAI, Mistral, Cohere and Scale AI [3].

Historical Background

Anthropic’s annual revenue rose from $41 million in 2023 to $4.6 billion in 2025, before first-half 2026 revenue reached $16.3 billion [1].

Business Angle

  • Usage-based token fees generated $13.06 billion, or 80.2% of first-half revenue, while subscriptions contributed $3.22 billion, making customer consumption a key driver of sales [1].
  • Funding tied to particular hardware may limit Anthropic’s ability to shift workloads among suppliers, despite its effort to run Claude across Amazon, Google, Nvidia and AMD chips [18].
  • Customers can test rival models and reduce Claude usage without ending contracts, leaving Anthropic’s business exposed to competitors’ releases, which it makes about twice a year [3].

Q&A

Q: What are Anthropic’s main sources of revenue?

A: Usage-based token charges generated $13.06 billion, or 80.2% of first-half revenue; subscriptions accounted for another $3.22 billion [1].

Q: Did Anthropic disclose its latest run-rate revenue?

A: No. The amended filing leaves the Oct. 31 figure blank. It defines run-rate revenue as trailing 28-day consumption fees multiplied by 13, plus monthly subscription fees multiplied by 12 [15].
