U.S. ambassador meets B.C. premier on trade

Category: Canada

British Columbia Premier David Eby met with U.S. Ambassador to Canada Pete Hoekstra in Victoria on Tuesday to talk through cross-border “trade irritants,” with particular focus on the softwood lumber dispute and tariffs affecting Canadian producers and B.C.’s forestry economy [1][3][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Beyond tariffs: They also touched on daylight time, a reminder that cross-border talks often stretch past trade [1].
  • Broad brush: Hoekstra said he and Eby covered “a whole range of issues that you would expect,” without spelling out what those issues were [2].
  • Details deferred: Hoekstra wouldn’t outline specific trade steps or concessions, saying any concrete progress would be for the two countries’ leaders to announce [3].
  • Not full alignment: Eby said they didn’t agree on everything, but both said they hope for a “positive outcome” from Canada–U.S. trade negotiations [5].

Perspectives

U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra: Hoekstra called it a “great meeting” and said he was “still smiling,” but offered few details about what they discussed.

Sources: Global News

B.C. Premier David Eby: Eby said the two had a “frank discussion” that centred on the softwood lumber dispute and tariffs.
