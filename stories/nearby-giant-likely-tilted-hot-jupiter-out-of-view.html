Nearby giant likely tilted hot Jupiter out of view

Category: Science

Astronomers say the hot Jupiter TOI-2535 b, about 1,000 light-years away, stopped transiting its star in March 2026 because the gravitational pull of a nearby, still-unseen planet tilted its orbit out of Earth’s line of sight. NASA’s Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite observed the system from April 2019 to March 2026; the planet circles its star every 8.25 days [2][3][4]. Before the transits vanished, they arrived as much as six hours early or late and grew shorter and shallower, signs that the orbit’s alignment was changing. How the planet moved close to its star remains uncertain. The study appeared in *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* on Nov. 9. TOI-2535 b is not expected to transit again for at least 30 years, while its companion could become visible in transit sooner [7][9][2].

We think of the universe as resembling clockwork, where everything is regular, but that’s not true.

— Samuel Yee, University of California, Los Angeles postdoctoral fellow and study corresponding author (Futurism)

Key Points

  • Companion estimate: Analysis using the NEID spectrograph at Arizona’s WIYN telescope points to a companion at least as massive as Saturn, with an orbit tilted at least 5 degrees relative to TOI-2535 b’s [5].
  • Extreme changes: Researchers describe the system’s variations in transit duration and changes in orbital inclination as the largest measured for a planet around a single star [7].
  • Hot-Jupiter rarity: These planets usually lack close, massive companions; TOI-2535 b’s neighbor may itself be a short-period giant, unlike most known hot-Jupiter companions [7].

Timeline

  • April 2019: TESS observations of TOI-2535 b began [3][4].
  • March 2026: TESS no longer detected the planet’s characteristic transits [4][9].
  • 9 November 2026: The study of the disappearing transits appeared in The Astrophysical Journal Letters [9].

Scientific Significance

  • Migration constraints: The authors say high-eccentricity migration cannot explain this system; migration through a protoplanetary disk would also require a later event to account for the planets’ observed orbital tilt [7].
  • Observation limits: A planet can remain in its system yet stop appearing in transit when its orbital plane shifts out of alignment with Earth; the researchers report this as the first confirmed planet around a single star whose transits were directly observed to disappear [7].

Historical Background

Other mechanisms have also been linked to disappearing or changing exoplanet transits: Kepler-413b stopped transiting for 800 days under the influence of its two stars, while TOI-1518b’s orbital orientation is expected to shift because of its star’s shape [5].
