Berkshire buys back $9.6 billion in shares as profits fall

Category: Business

Berkshire Hathaway bought back $9.6 billion of its own shares in the third quarter as operating earnings fell 5.8% to $12.71 billion and net income dropped 28% to $22.11 billion. The Omaha-based conglomerate, led by Chief Executive Greg Abel, said weaker insurance results and lower investment gains weighed on earnings [2][1][7]. Insurance underwriting earnings fell 43%, with auto insurer Geico’s pretax profit down 45% as it lowered premiums to regain customers and faced higher losses and expenses. Railroad, energy and manufacturing businesses posted higher profits, partially offsetting insurance weakness [2][1]. Berkshire reported about $2 billion in additional share repurchases in October, after the quarter ended [2].

Limited disclosures are fine when everything goes in the right direction, but become a point of ⁠vulnerability

— Cathy Seifert, CFRA Research analyst (Reuters)

Key Points

  • Segment gains: BNSF railroad profit rose 19%, Berkshire Hathaway Energy profit increased 8%, and Precision Castparts’ pretax profit climbed 30% [2].
  • Revenue growth: Revenue rose 11% to $105.65 billion, marking a second consecutive quarter of double-digit growth and reflecting acquisitions including Occidental Petroleum’s chemicals business and homebuilder Taylor Morrison [2].
  • Market performance: Berkshire shares gained more than 12% during the month covered by CNBC’s report, while the S&P 500 fell 5.5%, as investors shifted away from technology stocks [7].
  • Insurance investment income: Income fell 14% to $2.734 billion, largely because of lower short-term interest rates [1][7].

Perspectives

Cathy Seifert, CFRA Research analyst: Seifert said Geico’s results were weak compared with Allstate and Progressive [2].

Chris Ballard, Check Capital Management managing director: Ballard said Geico’s lower underwriting result was expected as the insurer increased advertising and wrote more policies to attract customers [6].

Sources: ABC News

Bill Stone, Glenview Trust chief investment officer: Stone said Berkshire could take advantage if the AI infrastructure trade collapses by buying pieces of companies [3].

Sources: Financial Times

Timeline

  • May 2025: Warren Buffett announced plans to step down as chief executive [2].
  • January 2026: Greg Abel took over as Berkshire Hathaway chief executive [2][6].
  • September 2026: Howard Buffett replaced his father as chairman [2].
  • 7 November 2026: Berkshire disclosed third-quarter earnings and share repurchases [2][7].

Industry Impact

  • Insurance: Claims also grew in Berkshire’s other insurance and reinsurance businesses [2].
  • Railroad: BNSF benefited from fuel surcharges and higher volumes of consumer, agricultural and energy products [2].

Historical Background

Warren Buffett took control of Berkshire Hathaway in 1965, when it was a struggling textile maker, and built it into a large conglomerate over six decades [3].

Business Angle

  • Berkshire was a net seller of $426 million in listed shares during the quarter, even as it bought back its own stock [3].
  • Investment gains fell nearly 50% to $9.407 billion, contributing to the decline in net income [7].
  • Berkshire said uncertainty over economic and geopolitical events, including tariffs and wars, was weighing on consumer confidence and leaving several retail businesses with sluggish demand [2].
