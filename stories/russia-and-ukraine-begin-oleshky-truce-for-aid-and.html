Russia and Ukraine begin Oleshky truce for aid and evacuation

Category: World

Russia and Ukraine began a localized ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky at 3 a.m. Kyiv time on Oct. 27, allowing a U.S.-brokered humanitarian mission to proceed later this week [8][13][6]. The United Nations says mines and drone attacks have trapped up to 2,000 residents facing shortages of food and medicine [4][5]. U.S. First Lady Melania Trump brokered the pause after talks with civilian negotiators from both countries and briefings from U.N. officials [7][10]. The mission is expected to bring doctors, medicine, food and water, then open evacuation corridors; residents may choose to travel to Russia or Ukraine [8][13]. Reports say the truce could last through Saturday or longer, but its precise duration remains unclear, and neither side has identified who will monitor it [8][5][34]. Attacks continued elsewhere. Russian strikes damaged Kyiv’s Southern Bridge and metro infrastructure, while an attack near a university late Monday killed at least one person and injured 10, city officials said [4][13]. Authorities in Kharkiv reported two people killed and four injured in attacks late Monday [8][10].

Key Points

  • Population decline: Oleshky had about 24,000 residents before Russia’s 2022 invasion; Ukrainian and U.S. estimates put today’s population at roughly 1,700 to 2,000, including up to 50 children [5][13].
  • Talks planned: Ukraine’s president said negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States plan technical-level talks in Abu Dhabi in the first half of November [11][26].

Perspectives

Ukrainian government: Ukraine has accused Russia of preventing residents from leaving, while Moscow has denied that charge [5].

Sources: NDTV

Russian Defense Ministry: The ministry blamed Ukrainian shelling and drone attacks for blocking aid and evacuation routes, claiming that Ukrainian forces carried out 18 artillery strikes and more than 120 drone strikes in the area and on nearby roads in the week before the truce [2][14].

Kremlin: Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Russia was open to short-term humanitarian ceasefires but that November peace talks would not constrain ongoing military operations [17].

Historical Background

Russian forces seized Oleshky during the first days of the full-scale invasion in February 2022 [8][6]. The collapse of the nearby Kakhovka dam in June 2023 caused extensive flooding in the town [8][5].
