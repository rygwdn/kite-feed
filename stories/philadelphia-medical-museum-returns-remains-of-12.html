Philadelphia medical museum returns remains of 12 Canadians

Category: Canada

A medical museum in Philadelphia has returned partial human remains of 12 Canadian First World War soldiers to Canada, according to the Department of National Defence [1][2][4]. Canadian officials say the remains were medical specimens collected during the war and later kept in the museum’s collection [1]. DND says it is now working to identify the soldiers and reach out to their descendants—steps that could help guide what happens next for commemoration and care [2][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Time in custody: The remains were held for more than a century before the Philadelphia museum returned them, the reports say [4].
  • Provenance details: The reports say U.S. medical personnel collected the specimens after the war at a military hospital, and they later entered the museum’s holdings [4].
  • Identification focus: DND says it is working to identify the soldiers, which could help shape how they are respectfully commemorated and cared for [1][2].
  • What is withheld: Officials have not said what the remains consist of, describing them only as partial remains and medical specimens [4][1].
