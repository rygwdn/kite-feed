Waymo suspends US freeway robotaxi rides as California ban persists

Category: Technology

Waymo has halted paid driverless freeway rides across the United States after California barred the Alphabet-owned robotaxi company from operating or testing on state freeways following construction-zone and traffic-control incidents. Waymo voluntarily paused freeway service in other markets. Surface-street rides continue, and the company says trained autonomous specialists remain involved in freeway testing [14][1][17]. The California Department of Motor Vehicles says the restriction will remain in force until Waymo demonstrates that it has corrected the identified deficiencies. No public timetable has been given. Waymo says the recent U.S. 101 construction-zone encounters caused no injuries or collisions and that it is working with state officials to resume freeway operations [14][1][26]. The current order follows a similar pause and software recall earlier this year. Waymo resumed freeway rides in late July after changes intended to improve construction-zone detection, but the California agency has again barred driverless freeway operations in the state [7][18][19].

Key Points

  • Service footprint: Before the suspension, Waymo offered freeway rides in the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, Phoenix, Miami and Austin; its city-street service continues in other markets [14][12].
  • Business disruption: University of San Francisco researcher Henriette Cornet said the pause could affect airport trips that use freeways, as surface-street detours can lengthen journeys [21][23].
  • Federal scrutiny: The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an investigation into a separate Waymo software fix concerning vehicles passing stopped school buses [18].
  • Service scale: Waymo says its ride-hailing operation provides more than half a million trips each week, including ongoing surface-street rides [14][26].

Perspectives

California Department of Motor Vehicles: The department described repeated entries into active construction zones as an imminent public-safety hazard because workers, vehicles and equipment were present [14][1].

Historical Background

Waymo's earlier construction-zone problems included six incidents in Phoenix in April in which vehicles passed ramp-closure signs, followed by seven vehicles entering active construction lanes in the Bay Area in May [7][23]. The June recall filing said the systems had failed to recognize construction zones or had prioritized other freeway hazards [23].
