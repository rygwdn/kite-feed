CD Projekt co-founder buys GOG for $25 million

Category: Technology

Michał Kiciński, a co-founder of CD Projekt, has acquired 100 percent ownership of GOG, the PC game storefront known for selling DRM-free titles, for PLN 90.7 million (about $25 million) [1][3][2]. GOG says it will keep operating independently under its new owner while maintaining its DRM-free approach and its work to keep classic games playable on modern PCs [1][3][2]. CD Projekt and GOG also signed a distribution agreement so CD Projekt Red games will continue to be sold on GOG, including future releases [1][2][8]. CD Projekt described the sale as aligned with its strategy to focus on its core business of developing and publishing games and related projects based on its franchises [1].

Primary Source: View original article

GOG stands for freedom, independence, and genuine control.

— Michał Kiciński (Fudzilla)

Did you know: CD Projekt said the transaction followed a “competitive sale process,” indicating GOG drew interest beyond its eventual buyer [1].

Key Points

  • What’s included: The purchase covers both the GOG.com storefront and the GOG Galaxy platform that provides optional client features for users [2][3].
  • Customer continuity: GOG told users that accounts and existing game libraries will carry over, with offline installers still available as before [2].
  • Funding structure: GOG said the acquisition was fully financed via committed funding at closing and did not require Kiciński to sell any of his CD Projekt shares [1][2].
  • Process signals: CD Projekt’s disclosure referenced a “competitive sale process,” suggesting more than one party may have pursued the asset [1].
  • Retro roadmap: Kiciński said he is personally involved in developing several retro-spirited games planned for release on GOG in 2026 [8].

Perspectives

GOG (storefront team): GOG framed the change as continuity for players—keeping DRM-free distribution central, preserving offline installers, and continuing efforts to prevent older games from becoming hard to find or run on modern systems.

Sources: GOG

CD Projekt (parent company): CD Projekt said selling GOG fits its group strategy of concentrating on its core business: developing and publishing video games and related projects built on owned and new franchises.

Sources: Engadget

The Verge (market framing): The Verge positioned GOG as a Steam-alternative PC game store that is leaving CD Projekt while remaining DRM-free under Kiciński’s ownership.

Sources: The Verge

Timeline

  • 2008: CD Projekt launched GOG with a preservation-focused “Good Old Games” mission [1][3].
  • Monday: GOG announced Kiciński’s purchase and reiterated its DRM-free commitment [3][2].

Technical Details

  • DRM-free distribution: GOG’s DRM-free approach means games are sold without always-on copy-protection requirements, and GOG highlighted that DRM-free will remain central after the ownership change [1][3][2].
  • Offline installers: GOG said it will continue providing offline installers, which lets users download and keep installable game files outside the client app [2].
  • GOG Galaxy (optional client): GOG Galaxy is the platform’s optional software client, and GOG said it remains optional for customers after the acquisition [2].

Industry Impact

  • Game preservation: By reiterating its goal to keep classic games playable on modern PCs, GOG’s ownership change spotlights the growing industry challenge of compatibility breaks and disappearing builds as platforms and rights change [1][2].
  • Consumer ownership norms: GOG’s renewed emphasis on DRM-free and offline installers reinforces an alternative model to account-tied licensing, which could keep pressure on other PC stores and publishers to offer more consumer-friendly access options [2][common].
  • Platform competition: With GOG described as a Steam-alternative storefront, the split from CD Projekt may test whether a smaller store can remain independent while relying on differentiation and curated catalog strategy rather than exclusives [3][9].

Historical Background

GOG launched in 2008 with a stated preservation mission around “Good Old Games,” aiming to keep classic titles accessible for PC players over time [1][3]. Over the years it differentiated itself by emphasizing DRM-free purchases and offering offline installers, a model that appeals to players who want long-term access without platform lock-in [2].

Q&A

Q: Will GOG change its staffing, headquarters, or day-to-day operations after the ownership shift?

A: GOG said it will operate independently under its new owner, but the articles do not provide details on staffing levels or office locations [1][3].

Q: What specific commitments does the CD Projekt–GOG distribution agreement include beyond listing future games?

A: The sources say CD Projekt Red games will continue to be available on GOG and upcoming titles will also release there, but they do not describe revenue splits, timing parity, or other contract terms [1][2][8].

Q: Which retro-spirited games are planned for GOG in 2026?

A: One source reports Kiciński said he is involved in several retro-spirited games slated for 2026, but it does not name the titles [8].

Action Items

  • Back up your GOG offline installers for favorite games: Use GOG’s offline installer downloads (which the company says will remain available) to keep local copies for long-term access [2].
  • Check whether you actually need a client app: If you prefer a minimal setup, you can keep using GOG without GOG Galaxy, which the company says remains optional [2].
