Update: Warner Bros Discovery gives Paramount seven-day offer window

Category: Business

Warner Bros. Discovery has reopened takeover talks with Paramount Skydance, giving the bidder a brief window to submit what WBD calls a “best and final” proposal. The move follows a waiver from Netflix that lets WBD engage with a rival suitor if it could lead to a “reasonably superior offer” [2][8][6]. Even so, WBD is still backing its separate deal with Netflix focused on the studio and streaming businesses, and it says shareholders will vote on that transaction next month [1][2]. Paramount’s earlier bid was rebuffed, and it has said it would pay more if talks reopened [6][9]. The renewed talks set up a high-stakes contest that could reshape Hollywood’s competitive landscape, as investors weigh the Netflix deal against Paramount’s push for an acquisition it says would deliver major cost savings [7][10][11]. One major point of tension is talent and execution risk. In regulatory filings, WBD warned that picking Paramount’s offer could lead to bigger employee and talent departures before closing, partly because Paramount has projected $6 billion in “synergies.” WBD’s board said those savings would likely come from headcount cuts, given overlap across the businesses [10]. Still, the reopened window keeps competition alive as WBD decides whether Paramount’s final offer can beat the Netflix agreement [2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Hostile escalation: After WBD turned down multiple sweetened approaches, Paramount has taken its case directly to shareholders with a hostile takeover effort described as a roughly $108.4bn bid [8][11].
  • Process posture: By asking for a single “best and final” submission, WBD is signaling it wants one last, clean comparison point—rather than another round of back-and-forth after weeks of revisions [2][8].
  • Board messaging tightrope: WBD is still recommending the Netflix transaction while agreeing to consider Paramount’s final push, leaving investors to judge how firm the board’s preference really is [11][1].
  • Public sparring: Netflix co-CEO Ted Sarandos accused Paramount of “flooding the zone” as WBD reopened talks, a reminder that merger fights often play out as much in public messaging as in boardrooms [4].

Perspectives

Warner Bros. Discovery board: WBD said it will consider Paramount Skydance’s “best and final” proposal during a short window while continuing to back its Netflix agreement, after receiving a waiver that allows talks with a rival bidder under certain conditions.

Sources: PBS NewsHour

Paramount Skydance: Paramount said it would pay more if WBD reopened negotiations, as it tries to revive a bid WBD previously rejected in favor of the Netflix arrangement.

Sources: The New York Times

Netflix leadership: Netflix co-CEO Ted Sarandos accused Paramount of “flooding the zone” and said in interviews that Netflix sees no reason regulators should block its deal. He also said he expects the transaction to create value for Warner Bros. shareholders.

Sources: Variety (via Google News)

Employee-risk caution: In an SEC filing, WBD warned that a Paramount tie-up could lead to larger losses of employees and talent before closing, pointing to the job-cutting implications of Paramount’s targeted synergy plan.

Sources: Business Insider

Timeline

  • January: WBD said in a filing that a Paramount deal could increase employee and talent departures before closing, a risk it reiterated again this week [10].
  • Tuesday: WBD reopened talks with Paramount, set a seven-day deadline for a “best and final” offer, and confirmed that Netflix granted a waiver allowing the outreach under defined conditions [2][8].
  • Next month: WBD plans a shareholder vote on the Netflix deal [1][2].
