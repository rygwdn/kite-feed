Google report details AI’s expanding role in cybercrime and influence operations

Category: AI

Google’s Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant say generative AI is being used across cybercrime, espionage and influence campaigns to automate operations and manipulate online audiences. Their latest report also documents an AI framework that independently analyzed patched software, developed exploits and ran them against targets. Telemetry pointed to French media, entertainment and education websites [21][19][3]. The report describes a platform that used Google’s Gemini application programming interface to manage more than 100,000 fake accounts through cloud-based phone systems. It also says China-linked attackers used large language models to turn stolen data into intelligence reports, while North Korea-linked actors used AI-generated identities to approach cryptocurrency firms [21][1]. Google said it detected and disrupted misuse of Gemini, disabled related accounts and is strengthening safeguards based on its findings [13]. The report also tracks the resale of stolen access to AI services, with fraudulent usage charges topping $100,000 in a day in some cases and reaching $1 million before the activity was stopped [2].

Primary Source: View original article

The reality we're facing today is threat actors are using this tech to operate at speed and scale, and they don't need zero-days to do it.

— John Hultquist, chief analyst at Google Threat Intelligence Group (SecurityBrief)

Key Points

  • Synthetic personas: Some AI-generated identities used to target cryptocurrency companies carried Google’s SynthID watermark, a marker intended to identify AI-generated content [21][13].
  • Phishing automation: The Jieyou platform integrated a large language model chatbot so customers could configure phishing panels and modify templates with natural-language commands [5].
  • Attack frameworks: Google tracked ARTEX, CAIRN and PentAGI in active use or testing; CAIRN appeared in an intrusion against the U.S. aerospace sector [13][5].
  • Detection trade-off: Google says current autonomous attack frameworks can leave conspicuous forensic traces, suggesting operators may favor speed over concealment [2][16].

Perspectives

Google Threat Intelligence Group: Google says defenders must also use AI agents. It described a service combining Gemini models, vulnerability prioritization, automated patching and Mandiant expertise to find and fix flaws [20].

Sources: Google Cloud Blog

Timeline

  • August 2026: Google’s report counted 22 zero-day exploits during August [2][19].
  • September 2026: Google previously described early, exploratory use of AI in vulnerability research [3].
  • 13 November 2026: The FBI issued an alert on ARTEX use in attacks since August [22].
  • 16 November 2026: Google published its AI threat tracker and detailed observed agentic activity [20][19].

Technical Details

  • AI persona management: The fake-account platform used cloud phone farms and simulated smartphone-camera distortions to make generated material appear authentic [1][21].
  • Exploit framework artifacts: Investigators found scripts for internal reconnaissance, password guessing and installing webshells, including a script that erased rival attackers’ webshells [19].

Industry Impact

  • Healthcare cybersecurity: The FBI said ARTEX-based attacks targeted health care organizations, among others. The American Hospital Association warned that machine-speed intrusions can give defenders less than three hours before attackers gain privileged access [22].

Action Items

  • Inventory internet-facing applications: The American Hospital Association’s cybersecurity adviser recommends that organizations identify all internet-facing applications and apply mitigation steps outlined by the FBI and Google [22].
