Amazon blocks Meta’s Muse AI agent from shopping

Category: AI

Amazon has blocked Meta’s Muse personal AI agent from browsing Amazon.com and making purchases for users after Meta declined Amazon’s request to remove the retailer from the service. Users attempting to shop through Muse began seeing a notice stating that continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon’s Conditions of Use [4][5][6]. Amazon said Meta had not disclosed that Muse would access its store, that the agent does not identify itself while browsing, and that it appeared to capture and store customer credentials, raising privacy and security concerns [4][7][16]. Meta has said Muse cannot see users’ passwords or payment details because credentials are kept in secure storage [4][10]. Meta launched Muse on September 8 as an agent that can perform multistep tasks, including shopping, travel booking and email management [6][10].

Primary Source: View original article

We think it’s fairly straightforward that third-party applications that offer to make purchases on behalf of customers from other businesses should operate openly and respect service provider decisions about whether or not to participate.

— Lara Hendrickson, Amazon spokesperson (GeekWire)

Key Points

  • Commercial stakes: Amazon generated more than $68 billion in advertising revenue last year, and AI agents can bypass product-search pages and sponsored listings [21].
  • Competitive landscape: Amazon has its own foundation models and Amazon Bedrock inference platform as it disputes Meta’s agent access to its marketplace [18].
  • User adoption: Muse surpassed 2.5 million downloads within 13 days of launch, according to Sensor Tower data cited by GeekPark [21].
  • Platform defenses: Amazon reportedly removed specific product names from confirmation emails and expanded its robots.txt file to block 47 AI crawlers, making automated parsing and access more difficult [21].

Perspectives

Amazon: Amazon says that, when instructed, Muse can access account pages and order histories, potentially handling sensitive information without the retailer’s knowledge or consent [10][22].

Sources: Digital Trends

Meta: Meta’s launch materials show Muse comparing options and seeking approval before placing an order, emphasizing user confirmation at sensitive stages [4][15].

Sources: Canaltech

Shopify: Shopify has agreed to support Muse through its merchant checkout infrastructure, treating AI agents as an additional distribution channel rather than blocking access [21][23].

Sources: MarketWatch

Timeline

  • 8 September 2026: Meta launched Muse with shopping and other task automation capabilities [6][10]
  • 18 September 2026: Muse reached first place on U.S. App Store free-app rankings [21]
  • 20 September 2026: Amazon began blocking Muse access during attempted shopping sessions [8][7]

Industry Impact

  • Online retail: Retailers and AI companies are negotiating whether agents may browse, access account pages and complete purchases without direct merchant integration [10][22].
  • Payments: Visa and Mastercard have introduced infrastructure intended to support automated-agent purchases, indicating that payment networks are preparing for transactions initiated by software rather than directly by shoppers [14].

Historical Background

Amazon sued Perplexity in 2025 over its Comet browser’s alleged automated access to Amazon accounts. A federal judge later issued a preliminary restriction, but the Ninth Circuit subsequently ruled that the available evidence pointed to users, rather than Perplexity, accessing the system under federal anti-hacking law [13][21][19]. Amazon continued pursuing claims based on contractual restrictions and conditions of use [8][21].
