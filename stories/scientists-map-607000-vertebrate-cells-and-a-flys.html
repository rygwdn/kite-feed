Scientists map 607,000 vertebrate cells and a fly’s entire central nervous system

Category: Science

Researchers have published two large-scale maps of nervous systems: a complete brain-and-nerve-cord wiring diagram for a fly, covering about 160,000 neurons and more than 200 million connections, and a body-wide cell atlas of the adult fish Danionella cerebrum, containing 607,370 cells [1][2]. The fly map links neural circuits to body parts and behaviors, while the fish atlas records cell types and gene activity across the animal [1][2]. The fish’s roughly 12-millimeter size made whole-body profiling possible. Researchers also used the atlas to track molecular changes during brain-tissue regeneration [2]. The fly study found that many control circuits are organized as local sensory-motor loops rather than around a single central controller [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Synapse predictions: A convolutional neural network predicted neurotransmitter use with about 90% accuracy among validated neurons, helping researchers infer whether connections excite, inhibit or modulate their targets [1].
  • Rare cell coverage: The fish atlas detected rare neuropeptidergic cells, with only 30 to 150 marker-positive cells in the entire animal. Researchers said this indicated near-saturation coverage of cell types [2].
  • Regeneration sequence: During telencephalon repair, the atlas recorded a sequence of wound response and immune-cell recruitment, radial-glia proliferation, neurogenesis and neuronal maturation [2].

Technical Details

  • Spatial transcriptomics: The Danionella atlas combined single-cell RNA sequencing from 12 dissected regions with spatial mapping using a 500-gene MERFISH panel and HCR-FISH to link gene-expression profiles to anatomy [2].
  • Connectome influence metric: For the fly, researchers simulated neural activity across a weighted, directed network to estimate how body parts influence one another, then related those scores to long-range brain-to-cord projections [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Neural regionalization: In Danionella, neural cells grouped more strongly by regional or developmental origin than by neurotransmitter identity, including in comparisons with other species [2].
  • Adult positional memory: Connective-tissue cells retained regional Hox gene expression, while fins expressed conserved appendage-patterning programs — molecular signatures tied to body position in adult tissues [2].
