Crypto exchange Binance names Justin Yek CFO after $6.3 billion theft

Category: Technology

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance said Chief Financial Officer Kaiser Ng is leaving and named Justin Yek, formerly vice president of finance at payments company Airwallex, as his successor. The announcement came nine days after hackers stole about $6.3 billion in cryptocurrency from the platform. Co-CEO Yi He said Ng was pursuing a new venture under a transition plan that had been in place for six months. Binance has pledged to cover customers’ losses [7][2][9][8]. Binance said hackers compromised employees’ computers months before the Oct. 29 theft. The FBI attributed the theft to North Korean hacking group TraderTraitor, an allegation North Korea denied [8][11][12][2]. U.S. regulators contacted banks to assess possible effects on the wider financial system [4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Withdrawal rush: After Binance unfroze accounts, customers withdrew a net $3.6 billion in the first few hours [2].
  • Asset recovery: Third parties froze or intercepted about $2.85 billion in stolen assets, though recovery can be difficult [3].
  • Reserve restoration: Binance reported that it had replenished about 95% of affected assets with its own funds by Nov. 2, when it began restoring withdrawals [9].

Timeline

  • 29 October 2026: Binance says hackers compromised employees’ computers months before the theft [8]
  • 31 October 2026: FBI identifies North Korean hackers as responsible for the attack [5]
  • 2 November 2026: Binance reports replenishing about 95% of affected assets and beginning to restore withdrawals [9]
  • 7 November 2026: Yi He announces Ng’s departure and Justin Yek’s appointment [7]

Technical Details

  • Initial access: Binance said the attackers compromised several employees’ computers months before the theft, using malware concealed in software downloads and fake recruitment approaches aimed at engineers [8][11].
  • Cross-chain laundering: Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic said the attackers had swapped stolen tokens for $124 million in bitcoin [9].

Historical Background

In February 2025, Bybit suffered a $1.5 billion hack that was also attributed to North Korean hackers; the theft from Binance exceeded that amount [5]. Binance founder Changpeng Zhao warned in September 2025 that North Korean hackers used fake job interviews, malware and attempts to gain insider access to target cryptocurrency exchanges [5].
