Anthropic mathematician says 250,000-page Riemann proof remark was a joke

Category: AI

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge said his remark about a Claude-generated explanation of the Riemann hypothesis exceeding 250,000 pages was a joke. The release contains about 17.37 million lines of code in Lean, a proof-checking language. Alpöge acknowledged substantial redundancy and quality problems in the code and said he should have organized it before publication [23][11]. Alpöge said he worked with Anthropic’s Claude AI system for about six weeks before posting the claimed proof on Nov. 20. Anthropic has not disclosed which Claude version he used [24][14]. The Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative, which develops Lean’s mathematical library, expressed full confidence in the formal proof, while mathematicians continue working to understand its argument [9][2]. The announcement came a day after Anthropic’s stock-market debut, prompting questions about its timing. Alpöge called the timing “terrible” but rejected the suggestion that the announcement was intended to influence the market [14]. To qualify for the Clay Mathematics Institute’s prize, the proof must be published in a qualifying outlet and remain under scrutiny for at least two years [6].

Did you know: Anthropic said Claude had already improved a lower bound related to the Riemann zeta function from 41.6% to 67.2% in August, months before the current proof claim [23].

Key Points

  • Code packaging: The formalization comprises 175,187 Lean source files divided into 14 compressed archives, according to Alpöge’s later account [23][11].
  • Division of labor: Alpöge said he directed the research while Claude contributed mathematics beyond his own expertise [9].
  • Mathematical aims: Alpöge said the ideas developed in the proof could help establish more natural, unified mathematical frameworks, including an H¹ object on number fields [23].
