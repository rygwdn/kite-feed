SpaceX launches NASA Pandora satellite to study exoplanets

Category: Science

SpaceX is set to launch NASA’s Pandora satellite on a Falcon 9 rocket from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California on Sunday, Jan. 11, with a scheduled liftoff time of 8:19 a.m. ET [1][3]. Pandora is a space telescope mission designed to study exoplanets—worlds orbiting other stars—by observing their host stars and planetary signals [1][3]. SpaceX plans to livestream the launch, giving the public a front-row seat to a mission aimed at sharpening how scientists interpret exoplanet observations [1][3].

Historical Background

Astronomers commonly detect many exoplanets using the “transit” method, which looks for a small dip in starlight as a planet crosses in front of its star [common]. Interpreting those signals can be tricky because changes in a star’s own light can mimic or distort what looks like a planetary signature [common]. Pandora’s mission fits into this broader scientific push to improve how star behavior is accounted for when studying distant planets [1][3].
