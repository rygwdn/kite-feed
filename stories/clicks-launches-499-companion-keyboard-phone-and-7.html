Clicks launches $499 companion keyboard phone and $79 snap-on

Category: Technology

Clicks, best known for physical keyboard cases, is expanding into new hardware with the Clicks Communicator: an Android 16 phone meant to be carried alongside your main smartphone and used primarily for messaging and essential communication rather than content consumption [4][2][7][10]. The Communicator includes a full QWERTY keyboard and its own cellular connection so it can operate independently, positioning it as a purpose-built device for people who want fewer distractions while staying reachable [2][4][9]. Alongside the phone, Clicks also introduced a $79 snap-on keyboard accessory and a separate “Power Keyboard” product that combines a wireless-charging power bank with a slide-out keyboard [10][3][8].

Primary Source: View original article

Communicator is to a smartphone what a Kindle is to an iPad,

— Jeff Gadway (MacRumors)

Did you know: The Communicator includes a microSD slot that supports up to 2TB of additional storage, a feature that has become less common on many mainstream smartphones [4].

Key Points

  • Hardware specs: Clicks Communicator includes USB-C, a 3.5mm headphone jack, a 4,000mAh battery, 256GB storage, microSD expansion up to 2TB, a 50MP rear camera, a 24MP front camera, and both physical SIM and eSIM support [4].
  • Home screen focus: Clicks says the Communicator uses a custom Android launcher that surfaces a curated set of communication apps—such as Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack, and Gmail—directly on the home screen [4].
  • Battery keyboard: The Clicks Power Keyboard accessory has a 2,150mAh battery and can charge phones over MagSafe or Qi2, while connecting its slide-out keyboard to phones and other devices via Bluetooth [3].
  • Adjustable positioning: The Power Keyboard is designed with extendable positions to fit different phone sizes and can be used in landscape mode, aiming to make quick typing more practical on the go [3].
  • Pricing contrast: The Verge notes the Communicator’s $499 price lands in the range of some midrange smartphones, which may shape whether it’s seen as a niche “second device” or a broader alternative for minimalists [2].

Perspectives

Clicks Technology (company messaging): Clicks frames the Communicator as a complementary, purpose-built device optimized for communication in a distraction-heavy environment, comparing it to how a Kindle complements an iPad [4].

Sources: MacRumors

Clicks co-founder Michael Fisher (MrMobile): Fisher argues the “two-phone lifestyle” is becoming more common for work separation or more intentional tech use, and says a phone should help people take action rather than “feeding distraction” [4].

Sources: MacRumors

The Verge (editorial framing): The Verge presents the Communicator as a fun, gadget-forward “BlackBerry for your phone” concept, while emphasizing that it still requires paying for a separate cellular-connected device [2].

Sources: The Verge

PCMag (use-case angle): PCMag highlights the Power Keyboard’s versatility (charging plus pairing beyond phones) and describes the Communicator as aimed at reducing social-media doomscrolling by shifting focus back to communication [8].

Sources: PCMag

Technical Details

  • Qi2 and MagSafe charging: Qi2 is a newer wireless-charging standard influenced by Apple’s MagSafe alignment approach; Clicks says the Power Keyboard can top up a phone battery using MagSafe or Qi2 wireless charging [3][common].
  • ESIM plus physical SIM: The Communicator supports both eSIM and a physical SIM tray, giving users flexibility to switch carriers or manage multiple lines depending on region and plan [4].
  • Push-to-dictate button: Clicks says the Communicator includes a side “Prompt Key” that you press and hold to dictate a text message, blending voice input with a keyboard-first design [4].

Industry Impact

  • Digital wellbeing devices: By pitching a communication-first device meant to reduce distraction, Clicks is participating in a wider trend toward single-purpose or “intentional” gadgets that complement, rather than replace, flagship smartphones [4][2][8].
  • Accessory innovation: A power bank with a slide-out keyboard combines two common travel/work accessories into one product, which could encourage other accessory makers to experiment with more multifunction designs [3][common].
  • Typing-first workflows: If products like this gain traction, they could strengthen demand for mobile-first text workflows (messaging, email triage, quick notes) that benefit apps optimized for keyboard shortcuts and compact UI layouts [4][common].

Historical Background

Phones with physical QWERTY keyboards—especially BlackBerry devices—were once a standard tool for heavy email and messaging users before large touchscreens became dominant [common]. Clicks initially tapped into that nostalgia and utility by selling attachable keyboard cases for modern smartphones, and these new products extend the same idea into stand-alone and accessory formats [1][2][3][10].

Q&A

Q: Will the Communicator’s 5G/LTE support work on all US carriers equally well?

A: Sources say it has global 5G and LTE support and includes both a physical SIM tray and eSIM, but they do not list specific carrier certification details [4].

Q: Can the Power Keyboard pair with devices other than phones?

A: Yes; it can connect over Bluetooth, and PCMag notes it can pair with tablets and smart TVs as well as charge a phone [3][8].

Q: How does Clicks position the Communicator compared with reading-focused devices?

A: Clicks’ marketing compares it to a Kindle as a complementary device to a more general-purpose smartphone, emphasizing purpose-built focus [4].

Action Items

  • Sanity-check whether a second phone fits your routine: If you’re considering the Communicator, list the specific apps it emphasizes (for example, Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack, Gmail) and verify those cover your real communication needs before adding another cellular line [4].
  • Plan for wireless-charging compatibility: If you’re interested in the Power Keyboard, confirm your phone supports MagSafe-style alignment or Qi2 so you can actually use the built-in 2,150mAh wireless power feature as intended [3].
