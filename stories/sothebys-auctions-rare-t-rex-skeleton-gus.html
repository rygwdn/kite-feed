Sotheby’s auctions rare T. rex skeleton Gus

Category: Science

One of the largest and most complete Tyrannosaurus rex skeletons found to date, nicknamed Gus, is scheduled for auction at Sotheby’s in New York on July 14 with an estimate of $20 million to $30 million, and it could sell for more [10][2][6][8]. The specimen is thought to be about 67 million years old, stands about 3.8 metres tall and was found on private ranch land in Harding County, South Dakota, within the Hell Creek Formation [10][5][8]. The fossil was excavated over three years starting in 2021 with permission from ranch owner Gary Licking [10]. Paleontologists warn that a sale into private hands could put the specimen beyond scientific access, while auctioneers argue commercial fossil hunters should be rewarded for locating scientifically important dinosaurs and removing them from the ground [5][10][2][6]. Bids had reached $19 million before the sale, according to Lifo and Il Giornale [8][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Specimen scale: Gus measures about 11.5 to 11.6 metres long and Sotheby’s describes the fossil as the remains of a large, robust adult [8][4][10].
  • Record benchmark: A stegosaurus called Apex set the current fossil auction record in 2024 when it sold at Sotheby’s for $44.6 million, 11 times its listing price [10].
  • Buyer shift: Sotheby’s 1997 natural history auction drew mainly museum buyers for the T. rex Sue, while the Gus auction also targets wealthy private collectors [2][6].
  • Chance discovery: Heavy rains exposed bone fragments on Gary Licking’s land, leading to a multi-year excavation that uncovered one of the largest T. rex specimens [5].

Timeline

  • 1902: One early T. rex skeleton was found in Hell Creek [5].
  • 2020: Stan, a nearly complete T. rex skeleton discovered near the Licking ranch, sold at auction for $31.8 million [5].
  • 2021: Excavation of Gus began on the South Dakota ranch [10].
  • 2024: Apex set the fossil auction record at Sotheby’s [10].
  • 14 July 2026: Sotheby’s scheduled Gus for auction in New York [10][8].

Technical Details

  • Completeness metrics: Sotheby’s says Gus includes 183 fossilized bone elements, about 61% of the total bone count but 75% to 80% of the animal’s skeletal mass [8][4].
  • Pathology evidence: The skull is described as exceptionally preserved, and the bones show bite marks and traces of trauma that may record injuries during the animal’s life [8].

Industry Impact

  • Natural history auctions: High-end fossil sales have moved from Sue’s $8 million museum purchase in 1997 to Stan’s $31.8 million auction in 2020 and Apex’s $44.6 million sale in 2024 [2][6][5][10].
  • Museum competition: Museums that dominated Sotheby’s early natural history auctions now compete with private wealth for major dinosaur specimens carrying eight-figure estimates [2][6].

Historical Background

The Hell Creek Formation stretches across Montana, Wyoming and the Dakotas and is a major source of Tyrannosaurus rex fossils; fossils unearthed there formed the basis for the species name [5].
