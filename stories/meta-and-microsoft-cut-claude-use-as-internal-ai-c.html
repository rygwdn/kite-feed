Meta and Microsoft cut Claude use as internal AI costs rise

Category: AI

Meta and Microsoft are reducing employees’ use of Anthropic’s Claude and steering staff toward in-house or lower-cost AI tools, according to The Information [1][14][16][12]. Microsoft has cut its planned internal spending on Claude by more than a third from an earlier projection of at least $1 billion this year; at Meta, the number of Claude Code users fell from about 60,000 earlier this year to roughly 30,000 [16][14]. The companies aim to control costs and increase use of their own products. Microsoft has directed employees toward GitHub Copilot, while Meta has promoted Muse Code and MetaCode; Microsoft engineers remain free to choose other models [14][16]. The pullback concerns internal use, while demand for Anthropic models through Microsoft’s external enterprise platform has been growing, according to the report [16]. Anthropic’s IPO filing says its two largest customers together account for about 25% of revenue but does not name them; their identities have not been confirmed [16].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Meta alternatives: MetaCode has more than 30,000 internal users, while Muse Code has more than 6,000 [16].
  • Claude spending: Despite the drop in Claude Code users, Meta spent more than $105 million on the service over a recent 28-day period [16].
  • Cost forecasting: Only 11% of nearly 400 surveyed businesses could accurately forecast their AI spending, according to figures cited by The Wall Street Journal [14].
  • Adoption gap: In July, 43.5% of U.S. businesses had paid subscriptions or token accounts with Anthropic, while AI spending varied sharply by company size [14].

Timeline

  • May 2026: Microsoft began reducing its reliance on Claude Code to limit employees’ token consumption [16].
  • 2026: Meta cut roughly one-tenth of its 78,000-person workforce during spring restructuring [16].
  • August 2026: Muse Code began external testing as Meta expanded its internal coding alternatives [16].

Industry Impact

  • Enterprise AI budgeting: Only 11% of nearly 400 surveyed businesses could accurately forecast their AI spending [14].
  • Uneven spending: The top 1% of U.S. businesses spent a median $7,400 per employee on AI in July, compared with $11.95 per employee at the median company [14].
