Venezuela quake toll nears 2,000 as toddler rescued

Category: World

Venezuela’s official death toll from the June 24 twin earthquakes rose to 1,943, with 10,571 people injured, National Assembly President Jorge Rodríguez said Tuesday [8][13][9][144]. The back-to-back magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 quakes hit northern coastal areas including La Guaira, where tens of thousands remain unaccounted for and preliminary NASA-linked satellite analysis estimated about 58,870 buildings were damaged or destroyed [13][8][14]. A Jordanian rescue team pulled a young child, identified as Klieber Morán, alive from the Los Corales Garden 1 building in La Guaira early Tuesday after six days under rubble, Venezuelan officials said; Jordanian civil defence said he received first aid, was taken to hospital and had good vital signs [5][12][13]. Elsewhere in Macuto, Ecuadorian and U.S. teams halted a rescue attempt after more than 40 hours when they stopped receiving responses from a mother and three children trapped beneath a nine-story building [9][3][41]. UN agencies warned that survivors in La Guaira face widespread food shortages, collapsed basic services and severed communications, while the World Health Organization said damaged hospitals and displaced people living without adequate sanitation have raised the risk of outbreaks such as measles, dengue, yellow fever and malaria [13][90][91][137].

Key Points

  • Rescues slowed: The government said official rescues fell from 5,380 people in the first two days after the quakes to four people found alive on Monday [142][97].
  • Scale dispute: Venezuelan officials listed 855 damaged buildings and 189 total collapses, while a NASA-based satellite assessment indicated tens of thousands of damaged or destroyed structures [8][148].
  • Shelter conditions: A UNHCR rapid assessment found nearly four in 10 displaced people were in streets, public spaces, churches, schools or improvised shelters below minimum protection standards [137][100].
  • Aid mobilization: Delcy Rodríguez said 3,681 rescuers from 30 countries had joined the search and that more than 1,000 tons of supplies and 27 aircraft had been provided by the international community [144].
  • Hospital damage: WHO data from 21 health facilities found three in critical condition and six structurally damaged or only partly functional across Caracas, La Guaira, Miranda and Falcón [104][100].

Historical Background

The earthquakes were described by several reports as among the strongest to hit Venezuela in more than a century [15][69]. La Guaira also suffered catastrophic destruction in 1999 when torrential rains triggered landslides and floods that killed thousands; official casualty figures from that disaster were never firmly established [common][112].

Humanitarian Impact

The UN migration agency estimated up to 6.8 million people could be affected and need shelter, water, sanitation, health care and essential relief items [8]. UNICEF estimated about 1.8 million people, including 680,000 children, need humanitarian assistance [69][53].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: La Guaira has collapsed buildings, damaged services and continuing search operations, with thousands missing and aftershocks reported [13][124]. Avoid disaster zones in northern coastal Venezuela unless part of an authorized relief mission.
  • Health: WHO and UN agencies warned of disease risks from damaged health facilities, poor sanitation and disrupted water systems [90][91][137]. Carry essential medicines, water purification supplies and hygiene items if already in affected areas.
  • Logistics: UNHCR said connectivity was largely severed and basic services had collapsed in La Guaira [137][100]. Confirm communications and transport routes before movement.

Action Items

  • Donate through established relief organizations: Use appeals from groups already operating or coordinating aid, such as UNICEF, the Disasters Emergency Committee, Caritas or national Red Cross partners cited in relief coverage [55][131][134].
  • Prioritize requested supplies: If contributing goods through local collection drives, check whether organizers are still accepting items because some donation sites paused intake after reaching logistics capacity [134].
