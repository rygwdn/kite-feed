Trump says Putin pauses Kyiv strikes until Feb. 1

Category: World

Ukraine reported a quieter night in and around Kyiv after President Trump said he asked Russian President Vladimir Putin to pause strikes on the capital and other cities during an extreme cold spell, with the Kremlin saying attacks on Kyiv would be halted until February 1 [1][3][7][8][17]. Several outlets said the pause was framed as weather-related, aimed at easing pressure on Ukraine’s heating and power systems during one of winter’s coldest stretches [1][2][5][17]. Ukrainian officials and independent reporting cautioned that details remain unclear and that Russia continued launching missiles and drones elsewhere in Ukraine, highlighting how limited and fragile any temporary arrangement could be [9][14][16]. Even so, a short reduction in strikes on Kyiv could offer civilians a small respite and create modest diplomatic breathing room as talks continue [2][8][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Semafor reported Ukraine’s weekend temperatures were forecast to reach -24°C (-11°F), a level where energy outages can become life-threatening quickly [5].

Key Points

  • Abu Dhabi talks: The Hindu reported the next round of trilateral Russia-Ukraine-US talks was set for Sunday, February 1, in Abu Dhabi [8].
  • Scope disputes: PBS said the timing and scope of the reported halt were unclear, reflecting uncertainty over what targets and areas the pause covers [9].
  • Overnight attacks: RFE/RL reported Russia launched a ballistic missile and more than 100 drones overnight, with none hitting Kyiv [16].
  • Cold-driven urgency: Semafor said forecasts called for temperatures as low as -24°C (-11°F) this weekend, while prior attacks left millions without heating [5].
  • Moratorium mismatch: Japan Today reported both Russia and Ukraine said they halted energy-infrastructure strikes, but differed on the moratorium’s timeframe [13].

Perspectives

United States (President Trump): Trump said he personally appealed to Putin to pause strikes because of extreme cold, and asserted Putin agreed to halt attacks on Kyiv and other cities for about a week [1][4][5].

Russia (Kremlin): The Kremlin said Trump requested a pause in strikes on Kyiv until February 1, and it agreed to that specific timeframe [3][7][8].

Ukraine (President Zelenskyy): Zelenskyy said he would wait to see whether Russia complies and signaled Ukraine would respond in kind, while also stressing there was no formal truce [2][4][6].

Sources: The Guardian

Technical Details

  • Energy infrastructure strikes: Attacks on power generation and grid assets can quickly reduce heating and electricity in winter, which multiple outlets linked to the urgency of the proposed pause [17][5].
  • Long-range drone raids: RFE/RL said Russia launched more than 100 drones overnight, showing how mass drone attacks remain a key feature of the conflict even amid talk of a pause [16].
  • Ballistic missiles: RFE/RL reported a ballistic missile was launched overnight, highlighting that high-speed missile threats remain alongside drones [16].

Industry Impact

  • Energy utilities: Reports of halted or debated strikes on energy infrastructure underline how utilities and repair crews remain central to civilian resilience, with heating outages still affecting Kyiv residents [17][13].
  • Winter logistics: The Straits Times reported Russia focused attacks on logistics while pausing Kyiv strikes, suggesting continued pressure on supply routes even if the capital sees fewer impacts [6].

Historical Background

Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in February 2022 and has included repeated long-range attacks on Ukrainian cities and energy infrastructure, particularly during winter months when heating needs rise [common]. In late January 2026, several reports described renewed pressure on Kyiv’s heating and power as cold weather intensified, adding urgency to any short pause in strikes [17][5].
