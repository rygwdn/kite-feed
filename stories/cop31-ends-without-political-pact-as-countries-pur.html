COP31 ends without political pact as countries pursue voluntary climate initiatives

Category: Science

The COP31 climate summit in Antalya ended on Nov. 22 without an overarching political agreement after countries split over whether the final text should address fossil fuels and increase climate finance [1][8]. Governments also pursued several voluntary initiatives outside the main negotiations [5][1]. More than 80 countries backed a target to raise electricity’s share of global energy use to 35% by 2035, while the Utilities for Net Zero Alliance outlined plans for $156 billion in annual grid investment [1][8]. The pledge does not specify that the additional electricity must come from renewable or other low-carbon sources [3]. Delegates also agreed to continue climate-finance talks through 2028, without setting a new amount or baseline for increasing adaptation funding [15]. A regional Pacific roadmap for moving away from fossil fuels is expected by July 2027, ahead of COP32, scheduled for Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, the following year [1][15].

If we fail on climate, the inequities we see today will grow almost beyond imagining: widespread crop failures, people losing access to water, rising conflict over basic resources, mass migration and growing tensions between countries

— Katharine Hayhoe, climate scientist at The Nature Conservancy (PBS NewsHour)

Key Points

  • Energy roadmaps: Twenty-seven governments had published or committed to publish plans for transitioning away from fossil fuels, while eight more announced plans during COP31 [1][8].
  • Treaty proposal: Thirty countries said they were working on a proposal for an international treaty to limit fossil-fuel production [1][3].
  • Building efficiency: A voluntary buildings initiative backed by 24 countries aims to cut energy use per square metre by 25% by 2035 [15].
  • Geothermal target: The Global Geothermal Ambition 2050 plan aims to increase global geothermal electricity and heat supply to 12 times its 2025 level [15].
  • Methane: Governments agreed to intensify efforts to cut methane emissions, including from fossil-fuel production, and establish new international partnerships [15].

Perspectives

European Union and Ireland: Irish climate minister Darragh O’Brien said a pact omitting fossil fuels and a transition away from them was unacceptable; the EU and several other countries rejected the proposed text [9][15].

Saudi Arabia: Saudi Arabia defended carbon capture as a practical climate response, a position that would allow continued reliance on oil and gas, according to the account of the closing negotiations [9].

Timeline

  • 2023: COP28 decision called for transitioning away from fossil fuels in energy systems [8]
  • April 2026: Countries held the first conference on transitioning away from fossil fuels [1]
  • 22 November 2026: COP31 concluded after negotiations failed to produce an overarching political agreement [1][8]
  • July 2027: Tuvalu and Ireland are due to host the next transition-away conference [1]
  • 2027: Ethiopia is set to host COP32 in Addis Ababa [1][15]

Technical Details

  • Carbon-removal scale: An early draft called for carbon-dioxide capture and removal to reach at least 551 million tonnes by 2035, then 1.4 billion to 2.8 billion tonnes annually; projects operating or in development are forecast to fall short of even the lower target [3].
  • Capture-project record: A review cited in the COP31 coverage found that 88% of carbon capture and storage projects from 1972 to 2018 failed because of technical or financial complications [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Warming outlook: A Climate Action Tracker report said progress in slowing warming had essentially stalled over the previous five years; even if countries meet their stated plans, the world would reach about 2.6°C above pre-industrial levels by the century’s end [2].
  • Carbon-removal risks: Some proposed approaches could require extensive land for growing plants, raising concerns about land grabs and displacement, while ocean-based carbon storage may pose ecological risks [3].

Historical Background

The 2015 Paris Agreement set the goal of keeping global warming well below 2°C while pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5°C [6]. At COP28 in 2023, countries agreed for the first time to call for a transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems [8][5]. COP31 was the first climate summit since Copenhagen in 2009 to end without a final agreement, according to accounts of the negotiations [1][9].

Q&A

Q: What separate funding mechanism did delegates agree to for the Adaptation Fund?

A: The UN Adaptation Fund is to receive a direct share of proceeds from international carbon-credit trading under Article 6.4 of the Paris Agreement [15].
