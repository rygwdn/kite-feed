France protests enter sixth weekend as turnout falls and clashes flare

Category: World

Protesters in France demonstrated over living costs and public services for a sixth consecutive Saturday on Nov. 21, with the Interior Ministry counting 82,500 participants nationwide and organizers estimating 150,000. Both figures were down from the previous weekend and far below the 800,000 organizers said had joined the first large march on Oct. 17 [4][11][10]. Students, unions, left-wing groups and Yellow Vest activists marched over living costs, housing, education and public services. Protesters also opposed a new law expanding legal protections for police who use firearms [4][36]. Most marches were peaceful, but police used tear gas to disperse unauthorized gatherings in Lyon, and clashes and fires were reported in several cities. The Interior Ministry reported 144 arrests by 6 p.m. An AFP tally cited by French media counted at least 31 people seriously injured by security forces since the protests began; authorities have issued no official injury total [11][15][4][9]. Youth- and worker-led rallies also took place in Portugal and Spain [8][11]. French unions have called another public-sector strike and demonstrations for Dec. 1, demanding higher public-sector pay and renewed wage negotiations [20][21].

Key Points

  • Paris turnout: Police counted 21,500 participants in the capital, while organizers estimated 45,000 [15][11].
  • Police law: The Senate passed the law 205-110. It presumes officers acted lawfully when using firearms, though evidence can rebut that presumption. The left says it will challenge the measure before the Constitutional Council [36][2].
  • Housing pressure: In Portugal, average annual earnings in 2025 were 37% below the EU average, while house prices had more than doubled since 2017 [11][7].
  • Care-home protest: In Madrid, demonstrators demanded accountability and compensation for 7,291 care-home deaths during the first COVID-19 wave in 2020 and called for publicly run care homes [11].

Perspectives

French Interior Minister Laurent Nunez: After passage of the police firearms law, Nunez urged regional authorities to be “particularly vigilant regarding the groups most hostile to law enforcement” [4].

  • 1 December 2026: Unions scheduled another public-sector strike and mobilization [20][21]

Historical Background

The Yellow Vest movement began in 2018 in protest against rising fuel taxes and later broadened to address living costs and social inequality [11][4]. The current protest wave began in mid-September with high-school students demanding better learning conditions, before unions and other groups joined [4][15].
