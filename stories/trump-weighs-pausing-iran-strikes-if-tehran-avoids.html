Trump weighs pausing Iran strikes if Tehran avoids new provocations

Category: World

President Donald Trump has told senior advisers he may hold off on renewed attacks on Iran if neither Tehran nor Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthi movement provokes the United States, three people familiar with the discussions said. Trump has not made a final decision, and the U.S. military remains prepared to escalate. A third aircraft carrier is heading to the Middle East to replace one of the two operating there, though all three could remain in the region if needed [1][3][11]. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan have urged Trump not to resume strikes, while Israeli officials are pressing Washington to launch another campaign. U.S. intelligence assessments say sanctions and the naval blockade are pressuring Iran. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has argued that the administration’s economic campaign has weakened Iran enough to make major combat operations unnecessary. Trump has also said Washington no longer needs a deal, while Qatar continues trying to broker an interim agreement to restore traffic through the Strait of Hormuz [1][6][11]. Trump has publicly threatened further action even as he signals restraint, and Iran says it will respond immediately if the United States or Israel resumes bombing. The standoff is affecting fuel prices and commercial shipping, and tankers have been involved in recent incidents in the Strait of Hormuz [6][3][18].

Key Points

  • Supply restrictions: In addition to its months-long naval blockade, the U.S. military is seeking to stop weapons and other sanctioned goods from reaching Iran by land or through the Caspian Sea [1][11].
  • Weapons stocks: People briefed on U.S. inventories said stocks of long-range precision munitions used in earlier operations had not been replenished; a White House official disputed that the military lacked ammunition for its objectives [1][17].
  • Negotiating gaps: Iran is reportedly willing to discuss down-blending its highly enriched uranium under U.N. supervision but seeks sanctions relief and war reparations. Washington rejects those demands and Iran’s proposed tolls on some Strait of Hormuz traffic [11].
  • Public opposition: A Reuters/Ipsos poll shortly before the Nov. 3 midterms found that 55% of Americans said the war was not worth its cost, and Vice President JD Vance linked it to Republicans’ electoral losses [1][2].
  • Shipping hazards: Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said three tankers caught fire after striking mines in the Strait of Hormuz over two days, while the U.N. said October had the waterway’s highest number of commercial-ship incidents since February [3][18].

Industry Impact

  • Fertilizer trade: Before the conflict, a third of the world's fertilizers passed through the Strait of Hormuz, according to the U.N. Office for Project Services [3].

Historical Background

The framework for a broader peace settlement proposed in the June memorandum foundered over disputes including navigation through the Strait of Hormuz [12][16].

Humanitarian Impact

The U.N. Office for Project Services said fertilizer prices had risen 30% since the conflict disrupted traffic through the Strait of Hormuz and warned that missed planting seasons could leave 45 million people facing acute food insecurity [3].

International Reactions

  • 🇮🇷 Iran: Deputy U.N. ambassador Gholamhossein Darzi said Tehran had not closed the door to diplomacy [6].
  • 🇺🇳 United Nations: Jorge Moreira da Silva, head of the U.N. Office for Project Services, called for an exceptional, time-limited arrangement allowing five fertilizer vessels a day through the Strait of Hormuz [3].
  • 🇶🇦 Qatar: Its prime minister met Trump at the White House this week; diplomats said the interim proposal initially appeared on track, before Trump's later comments clouded prospects [6].

Q&A

Q: Will U.S.-Iran negotiations resume?

A: Trump redirected Vice President JD Vance, who had been weighing renewed talks, to the administration's domestic anti-fraud campaign; regional diplomats said Trump's intentions remained unclear [6].

Q: What military option might Trump consider if he orders more strikes?

A: Trump could consider striking Iran's underground nuclear facilities again, though the United States has already hit them and the effectiveness of further attacks is uncertain [1][11].
