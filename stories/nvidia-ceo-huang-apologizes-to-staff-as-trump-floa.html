Nvidia CEO Huang apologizes to staff as Trump floats pardon

Category: World

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologized to employees on Tuesday, acknowledged violating the company’s conduct rules and said he is in therapy. He returned as CEO a day earlier, 11 days after resigning over disclosures of affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a businesswoman whom U.S. intelligence suspects of having ties to Chinese intelligence. That relationship became entangled with an investigation into whether Nvidia chips were diverted to China through Singapore-based customer Megaspeed [12][75]. At a company-wide meeting at Nvidia’s Santa Clara headquarters, Huang said it was “not acceptable” that he had broken rules he helped write. He denied that pressure from President Donald Trump had secured his reinstatement, saying the board had evaluated the facts. Trump has said he personally convinced directors to reinstate Huang. Attorney General Todd Blanche declined on Tuesday to confirm whether federal authorities had investigated Huang and described Nvidia as a “good corporate citizen” [12][54][75]. Trump told the New York Post a pardon was “always an option” but likely unnecessary, and urged Huang to tell the truth when he appears before lawmakers. Huang is scheduled for closed-door House interviews on Dec. 1 and a public Senate hearing on Dec. 3 [4][myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com#1]. Nvidia shares fell about 6% Tuesday after rising more than 31% Monday. The company’s internal inquiry and government inquiries remain active [12][2].

Key Points

  • Board scrutiny: Governance experts said the board’s reversal after publicly rejecting Huang’s return raises questions about its independence and enforcement of Nvidia’s conduct rules [76][111].
  • Spending outlook: Brian Levitt, chief global market strategist at Invesco, said Nvidia’s substantial cash flow could allow many AI-industry investments to continue, though investment and earnings growth were unlikely to return to their first-half pace [12].
  • China shipments: Nvidia said it halted shipments to China of U.S.-licensed Hopper chips, including the H200; analysts said Huang’s return does not change export restrictions [2].
  • Other inquiries: California’s attorney general has opened an investigation into Nvidia’s chip exports and shareholder disclosures, while a reported SEC accounting inquiry adds another line of scrutiny [myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com#1][70].
  • Board oversight: Governance experts note that exchange rules require a majority-independent board, while one expert said long director tenures can undermine independence [1][7].
  • Export proposals: Lawmakers may seek to add export-control measures to the annual defense policy bill during the lame-duck session; proposals include restrictions on advanced chips and chipmaking tools, as well as location verification [118].
  • Supplier exposure: The earlier rebound lifted Taiwan’s TSMC and South Korea’s Samsung and SK Hynix, along with other chipmakers and equipment suppliers across Asia [92][124].
  • Market warning: Charles Schwab strategist Joe Mazzola said one strong session does not establish a market bottom, with investors also pricing in a possible Federal Reserve rate increase [84].
