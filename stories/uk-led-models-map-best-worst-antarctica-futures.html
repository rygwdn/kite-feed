UK-led models map best, worst Antarctica futures

Category: Science

New UK-led modeling focused on the Antarctic Peninsula sketches a wide range of futures, depending on how greenhouse-gas emissions unfold—from relatively modest winter sea-ice reductions in a lower-emissions world to far larger losses under higher-emissions pathways [3][1][2]. The message is straightforward: choices made now shape whether the region’s ice, ocean conditions, and ecosystems drift toward changes scientists say would be hard to reverse on human timescales [1][3]. In the higher-emissions scenarios described across the coverage, researchers project faster ice loss and warmer surrounding waters, with effects that don’t stay in Antarctica—rising seas and disrupted Southern Ocean food webs could ripple outward [1][3]. The more encouraging note in the lower-emissions scenario is that winter sea ice remains only slightly smaller than today, and the Peninsula’s contribution to global sea level stays limited to a few millimetres—evidence that cutting emissions can still meaningfully reduce risks, even in a rapidly changing polar region [3][2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Sea-ice spread: In the worst-case, higher-emissions scenario, modeled Antarctic Peninsula sea-ice coverage drops by about 20% [3][2].
  • Warming range: The scenarios highlighted span outcomes that roughly align with limiting warming to about 1.8°C, versus futures that reach about 3.6°C to 4.4°C [3].
  • Ecosystem risk: Big sea-ice losses could hit krill hard, with knock-on impacts for penguins and whales that depend on it [3].
  • Sea-level scale: In the lower-emissions scenario, the Peninsula adds only a few millimetres to global sea level [3].
  • Irreversibility concern: Under higher emissions, the authors say the resulting changes could be effectively irreversible on human timescales, because regrowing glaciers and restoring wildlife would be extremely difficult [3][1].
