Quebec parties pitch affordability plans on fourth campaign day

Category: Canada

Affordability and the economy were the focus on the fourth day of Quebec’s election campaign on Sunday, with major parties setting out pledges on housing, taxes and public finances as the province faces budget constraints [1][2][3][5]. The Quebec Liberals and Québec solidaire promised measures aimed at making housing more affordable, the Conservatives emphasized further tax cuts, and the Coalition Avenir Québec released its full fiscal plan [5][3]. The campaign day followed a pre-election report in which Quebec’s auditor general warned that the next government must eliminate a multi-billion-dollar deficit by 2029-2030 under the province’s balanced-budget law [5]. Parti Québécois Leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon focused on language policy, proposing to extend the Charter of the French Language, known as Bill 101, to CEGEPs and to children of temporary immigrants who stay in Quebec for more than one year [3].
