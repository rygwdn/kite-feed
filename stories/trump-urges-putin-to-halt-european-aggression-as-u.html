Trump urges Putin to halt European aggression as Ukraine war intensifies

Category: World

President Donald Trump said on Nov. 26 that he had urged Russian President Vladimir Putin during talks in China the previous week to pull back “all military aggression” across Europe. He said he hoped that could lead to a quick deal to end the war in Ukraine but did not specify which territories or actions he meant. His statement followed a Florida meeting with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte, after which they called on Russia and Ukraine to avoid steps that could widen the war [1][4][5]. At their China meeting, Trump greeted Putin as a “friend,” worrying officials in Kyiv and European capitals. Russian strikes hit Kyiv overnight, wounding seven people, while Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, warned of another possible mass attack. European concern has also grown after missiles landed in Poland [4][9][7]. Washington has delayed a review of the U.S. military presence in Europe beyond its provisional Dec. 1 deadline while it assesses options and European offers to replace some U.S. capabilities. The review will go to Trump, but officials have not set a date for a decision [3].

Key Points

  • Troop review: The review covers U.S. troop levels, bases and overflight rights; about 80,000 U.S. troops are stationed in Europe, and the Pentagon is preparing five options [3].
  • Baltic tensions: Estonia accused Russian security services of ordering an attempted arson attack on a defense company; Moscow denied involvement [7].
  • War’s reach: Ukraine said its drones struck Russian oil facilities and an airfield, while Ukraine’s prime minister said Russian attacks hit two foreign grain ships carrying more than 100,000 tonnes [9][17].
  • EU financing: EU leaders debated a proposed €45 billion loan to meet Ukraine’s needs in 2027; European Council President António Costa said 24 member states supported it [9].
  • Territorial deadlock: Trump envoy Jared Kushner said territory in eastern Ukraine claimed by Moscow remained a sticking point in discussions [6][2].

Perspectives

Historical Background

Trump has spoken of negotiating an end to the war since taking office in January 2025, but no peace agreement has been reached [32][22]. In October, he agreed with Putin on Russian diesel deliveries to the United States, easing economic pressure on Moscow amid rising fuel prices [22].

Crews were working to restore electricity in six Ukrainian regions after Russia launched ballistic missiles and 113 drones overnight [5].
