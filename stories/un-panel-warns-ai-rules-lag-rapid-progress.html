UN panel warns AI rules lag rapid progress

Category: AI

A preliminary report by the UN Independent International Scientific Panel on Artificial Intelligence warned Wednesday that AI is advancing faster than governments can regulate it or scientists can assess its effects, leaving policymakers without assurances that safeguards can prevent harm [75][76][77][78]. The report said the window to establish effective global governance remains open but “may not stay that way for long,” as AI systems move from answering prompts toward agents that can plan tasks, use tools and act with less human supervision [75][77]. The panel said AI could accelerate progress in healthcare, education, scientific research, agriculture and accessibility, while inadequate safeguards could deepen inequality, spread misinformation, threaten human rights, disrupt labour markets and concentrate powerful systems among a small number of governments and companies [75][77]. In Washington, the White House is accelerating plans for voluntary standards with major frontier AI companies, focused especially on cybersecurity capabilities, with an announcement possible as early as next week, the Financial Times reported [28][43][49]. Gizmodo, citing the FT, said the Commerce Department’s Center for AI Standards and Innovation and the National Security Agency would play central roles if the standards are formalized [43].
