Nvidia partners with Wall Street on $500 billion AI financing

Category: Business

Nvidia said it signed memorandums of understanding with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to create financing platforms aimed at mobilizing more than $500 billion in third-party capital for artificial intelligence infrastructure [5][8][17][51]. The capital would support data centers, power systems, chip manufacturing and other Nvidia-based computing infrastructure for AI developers, enterprises, governments and cloud providers [7][11][17][51]. The initiative seeks to make AI “compute” an investable asset class, with financing tied to Nvidia hardware and infrastructure rather than relying only on technology companies’ balance sheets [7][8][50][55]. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang told CNBC that the company approached only the six named firms and none rejected the proposal; he also said Nvidia may backstop up to $125 billion, or 25% of potential deals [8][17][27]. Nvidia did not disclose financial terms, how much each partner may commit or a timetable for deploying the planned funding [17][51][18]. Large technology companies have said AI spending will continue, with combined outlays expected to exceed $730 billion this year [8][11][17].

These financing platforms will help customers access scarce compute at scale and build the AI factories that will power every industry and country in the age of AI

— Jensen Huang (The Guardian)

Did you know: BlackRock’s Larry Fink compared the new compute-financing idea to the mortgage-backed securities market he entered in the 1970s [55].

Key Points

  • Debt focus: Executives said the program will focus on debt financing to give Nvidia’s largest customers access to computing capacity, with deals already being prepared for the program [18][53][57].
  • Collateral model: Bloomberg reported that future deals may use compute power as collateral through private offerings and special-purpose entities able to raise tens of billions of dollars at a time [18][58][53].
  • Capital intensity: Huang said AI data centers cost about $50 billion to $60 billion for each gigawatt of power consumption, while BlackRock’s Larry Fink cited estimates that the United States alone will need more than 70 gigawatts of AI data center capacity [14][36][40].
  • Market reaction: Nvidia shares fell after the announcement; Reuters cited a drop of more than 3% in afternoon trading and other reports cited declines of about 2.86% [11][32][58].

Perspectives

Nvidia: Huang said Nvidia hardware is widely adopted, flexible and transferable, allowing lenders to underwrite compute as a revenue-generating asset.

Timeline

  • 2024: BlackRock, Microsoft and MGX formed data center financing alliance [57].
  • June 2026: Nvidia planned $25 billion U.S. bond issuance [11].
  • 10 August 2026: Nvidia announced financing MOUs with six Wall Street firms [17].

Industry Impact

  • Cloud providers: Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Meta are already investing hundreds of billions of dollars in AI data centers, and the financing platforms target the same demand for Nvidia-based compute [14][36][45].
  • AI startups: Huang said the funds could support AI research labs and startups, widening access to infrastructure that would otherwise require large upfront capital outlays [14][45][52].
  • Private credit: Apollo’s Jim Zelter said AI infrastructure will require more than $8 trillion in capital, including private and public funding [15].
  • Data center finance: Goldman Sachs is the only bank in the partnership and may lead public debt deals while also distributing returns through its asset-management arm [18].

Historical Background

BlackRock, Microsoft and Abu Dhabi-backed MGX formed an AI infrastructure partnership in 2024 to finance data centers. Nvidia and xAI later joined the effort [57][59].

Business Angle

  • Nvidia may benefit if third-party financing turns GPU clusters into recurring infrastructure assets rather than one-time hardware purchases [7][50].
  • A backstop could support deal flow but also creates contingent exposure if projects fail to perform [8][17].
  • The program's contribution to near-term earnings is uncertain, as the financing platforms are still in early stages and final terms are pending [17][51].
  • Customer funding that returns to Nvidia as chip sales can increase near-term demand without proving end-user profitability [14][48][46].

Action Items

  • Review Nvidia exposure: Separate confirmed revenue from contingent financing announcements because Nvidia has not disclosed partner commitments, terms or deployment timing [17][51].
  • Track customer leverage: Monitor whether major AI customers use debt-backed compute structures, since reports identify circular financing and collateral-value risk as central concerns [48][58][54].
