U.S. loses measles elimination status as cases hit 5,412

Category: World

The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), the World Health Organization’s regional body for the Americas, withdrew the United States’ measles-elimination status on Nov. 17 after the same virus lineage circulated continuously in the country for at least 12 months. Mexico and Bolivia also lost the designation; Canada lost it in 2025 [3][21]. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recorded 5,412 confirmed U.S. cases and six deaths through Nov. 12; state health departments have reported 10 deaths this year. Since 2025, about 7,700 people have been infected and 13 have died [3][4][31]. The designation does not require zero cases: it means outbreaks have not produced sustained local transmission. Most infections have been among unvaccinated people or those whose vaccination status is unknown [31][9]. PAHO expects the U.S. to submit a corrective action plan in the coming weeks, outlining steps to strengthen vaccination, surveillance and outbreak response. To regain the designation, the country must then show that measles transmission has stopped for 12 months [4][45].

Key Points

  • Spread nationwide: PAHO’s review found outbreaks in 48 of the 52 U.S. states and territories it tracked from January 2025 through January 2026 [34].
  • Vaccination gap: Measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccination coverage among U.S. kindergarteners was 92.4% in the 2025-26 school year, below the roughly 95% threshold experts say is needed to prevent outbreaks [23][26].
  • Hidden hotspots: Computer simulations using data from 50,000 schools found that school-level vaccination estimates can reveal vulnerable clusters obscured by county averages [45].
  • Hospital burden: About one in five people with measles is hospitalized, often for complications such as pneumonia or brain swelling [45].
  • Adult infections: Adults aged 20 and older accounted for 41% of U.S. measles cases in 2026, up from 29% in 2025 [26].
  • Coverage threshold: Public-health authorities say communities need at least 95% vaccination coverage to prevent sustained transmission [37][21].
  • Infant protection: Former CDC infectious-disease center director Daniel Jernigan warned that families with babies younger than 1 may need to be more cautious in crowds and during air travel [16].
  • State outbreaks: Pennsylvania recorded 1,542 cases in 46 counties and Wisconsin 649 cases in 20 counties, according to figures reported in mid-November [1].

Industry Impact

  • Local health departments: The CDC says it has invested $100 million in state and local health departments to target communities where measles is spreading [29].

Scientific Significance

  • Vaccine efficacy: Two doses of the MMR vaccine reduce the risk of contracting measles by 97%; one dose is 93% effective [37].
  • Local protection: Los Angeles County reported seven measles cases this year, all linked to travel; officials credited 97.9% MMR coverage among kindergarteners with preventing further spread [24].

Historical Background

Before the latest sustained spread, U.S. cases were generally linked to travel and outbreaks were contained; the country came close to losing the designation after outbreaks in 2019 [24][9][31].

Humanitarian Impact

About 11% of U.S. measles patients have required hospitalization, and young children and infants face a heightened risk of severe complications [1][45].

International Reactions

  • 🇧🇴 Bolivia: Health Minister Marcela Flores said reported measles cases had dropped 83% this year, crediting vaccination, contact tracing, border controls and community surveillance [28].
  • 🇲🇽 Mexico: Mexican authorities said more than 40.9 million measles vaccine doses had been administered and coverage had reached 90% as the government worked to end the outbreak [28].
  • 🇨🇦 Canada: PAHO Director Jarbas Barbosa said Canada had recorded two consecutive months without new cases but must maintain that interruption in transmission for a full year to regain elimination status [13].

Q&A

Q: What can be done to protect infants too young for routine vaccination in outbreak areas?

A: In measles hot spots, infectious-disease expert Amesh Adalja recommended mobile vaccination clinics offering an early MMR dose to infants ages 6 to 11 months [28].

Q: What might happen after someone is exposed to measles?

A: People may need to locate decades-old vaccination records and could be asked to quarantine for 21 days after exposure [21].

Q: How could the United States regain elimination status?

A: PAHO expects a corrective plan detailing vaccination, surveillance and outbreak response, followed by evidence that sustained transmission has been interrupted [23][16].

Action Items

  • Check your measles vaccination status before travel or exposure. If you are unsure whether you are vaccinated, contact your primary care provider or local health department, as recommended in outbreak guidance [30].
