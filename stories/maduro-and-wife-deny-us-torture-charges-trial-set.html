Maduro and wife deny US torture charges; trial set for June 2027

Category: World

Former Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, pleaded not guilty Thursday to new U.S. charges accusing them of conspiring to torture political opponents. U.S. District Judge Alvin K. Hellerstein entered the pleas in Manhattan after Maduro called the allegations false and rejected the indictment; Flores told the court she was innocent. The judge scheduled their trial to begin June 14, 2027, two weeks later than previously scheduled [3][8][17][12]. The indictment alleges that Maduro directed Venezuelan officials to torture suspected opponents, while Flores helped develop the system and ordered abuse in specific cases. Prosecutors describe beatings, electric shocks and asphyxiation, and say the alleged victims included three U.S. citizens. The couple are not accused of personally carrying out torture [8][11][13]. The torture case expands an existing U.S. prosecution over alleged cocaine trafficking and firearms offenses. Maduro and Flores have asked the judge to dismiss the case, arguing that, as the former president and first lady of a sovereign country, they are protected by immunity. The judge has not yet ruled on that argument. A further pretrial hearing is scheduled for Dec. 17 [7][8][17][23].

Key Points

  • Alleged incidents: The indictment cites a 2014 episode in which prosecutors say Maduro ordered the kidnapping of military personnel for questioning and urged officials to “squeeze them harder”; Flores allegedly backed his plan [11][12].
  • Potential sentence: If convicted on all charges, each defendant would face a mandatory minimum sentence of 50 years in prison [11][14].
  • International law: Torture can be prosecuted outside the country where it occurred and regardless of victims’ nationality, although U.S. prosecutions of foreign officials are rare [3][17].
  • Venezuelan leadership: Maduro’s former vice president, Delcy Rodriguez, has governed since his capture and increased cooperation with Washington, according to Reuters reporting carried by RTE [12][11].
  • Detention challenge: A judge recently rejected Flores’ request for release to home detention on health grounds, citing a high risk of flight [15][2].

Perspectives

Maduro's lawyers: They contend the U.S. seizure of Maduro by force was illegal [7].

Q&A

Q: Why was the trial date moved?

A: Both sides sought to move the start from June 1 to June 14. Maduro's lawyer called the resulting schedule demanding given the new charges but said he hoped the defense could meet it [8][10].
