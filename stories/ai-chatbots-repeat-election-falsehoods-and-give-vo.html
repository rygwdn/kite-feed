AI chatbots repeat election falsehoods and give voters incorrect guidance

Category: AI

As Americans vote in the Nov. 3, 2026, midterm elections, tests show that AI chatbots can provide incorrect voting instructions, repeat false political claims and recommend candidates in response to some prompts [3][5]. An October study by Innovating for the Public Good found that 13.9% of chatbot answers to questions about voting procedures were outdated or inaccurate, while 5.9% could lead voters to miss a deadline, vote on the wrong day or skip a required step [3]. In Texas, a likely AI-generated video falsely accused Democratic Senate nominee James Talarico of misconduct. The video drew more than 850,000 views in its first day, and Google’s Gemini and AI Overview and Microsoft’s Copilot repeated the claim before fact-checkers debunked it [5]. Companies have set up election monitoring operations, but separate research found differences in performance among systems, and researchers say persuasive chatbot conversations can shift political preferences [8][1].

Did you know: In a separate study, about a quarter of people who believed a conspiracy theory stopped believing it after roughly eight minutes of AI conversation tailored to their reasons for believing it [1].

Key Points

  • Model differences: In the Innovating for the Public Good test, Claude gave wrong or outdated voting information about one-third of the time, Copilot one-quarter of the time and ChatGPT one-tenth of the time [3].
  • Candidate nudges: In NPR’s test, four of five chatbots offered candidate lists aligned with users’ stated preferences after two or three prompts; Google Gemini did not [3].
  • Persuasion study: A study of more than 2,300 Americans found that a chatbot advocating for Kamala Harris moved likely Trump voters 3.9 points toward her on a 100-point scale; a pro-Trump bot moved likely Harris voters 1.51 points toward him [1].
  • Hidden ads: By early October, researchers had identified 220 AI-generated political ads worth more than $137 million, while analysts said the count likely understated their prevalence because they are difficult to detect [14].

Perspectives

Tim Chambers, principal at Dewey Square Group, which conducted the election chatbot research: He said the number of errors raised questions about how many more remained undiscovered.

Sources: NPR

Microsoft: The company said it had improved Copilot’s handling of election questions and continued to test and refine its responses to make them more reliable.

Sources: NPR

Sen. Mark Warner, Democrat of Virginia: He said technology companies have a responsibility to protect election integrity as more people use AI and social media for important information. He and other lawmakers urged firms to rely on highly authoritative sources rather than social media for answers to election questions.

Sources: Nextgov

Ian Marlow, founder of cybersecurity firm Fitech: He advised voters to use AI to collect facts rather than ask it for opinions, noting that systems from different companies may reflect different viewpoints.

Technical Details

  • Generative engine optimization: Campaigns and political influence operations are creating material designed to get chatbots to retrieve and repeat it, a strategy researchers describe as generative engine optimization [3].
  • API testing caveat: Anthropic said some safeguards may not have been active when researchers tested Claude through its application programming interface; the research group said tests using Claude’s web interface produced comparable results [3].

Industry Impact

  • Election safeguards: The Brennan Center found ChatGPT had significantly improved protections against realistic election-disinformation images since May, while Google Gemini and Meta AI had become more willing to generate convincing fake election images [8].
  • Deepfake policy: Microsoft said malicious deepfakes of candidates and election officials were disallowed on its platforms and that targeted individuals could report them [8].

Historical Background

Russia-linked influence efforts have sought to make false material appear to come from trusted domestic voices. They used a false persona to place articles on independent websites in 2016, recruited unwitting freelance journalists in 2020 and paid prominent right-leaning U.S. influencers in 2024 [5]. The group Storm-1516, which researchers linked to the Talarico video, had also circulated a similar false accusation against Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz during the previous election cycle [5].

Q&A

Q: How many Americans say they use AI to research this election?

A: A Gallup finding cited by Gizmodo showed that 8% of Americans said they were using AI to find information about the 2026 elections; among that group, 78% asked about candidates’ positions and 71% checked claims they had heard or read [2].

Action Items

  • Microsoft said it was working to point voters to accurate information directly from state and local election officials [8].
