Israeli air strikes across Gaza kill dozens

Category: World

Israeli air strikes hit multiple locations across the Gaza Strip on Saturday, with hospitals and local emergency services reporting dozens of Palestinian deaths, including children, even as a ceasefire agreement remains in place [7][3][13][16]. Reports described strikes on an apartment building in Gaza City and a tent area in Khan Younis, among other sites [10][15][3]. Israel’s military confirmed carrying out strikes and said it targeted Hamas commanders or acted in response to what it described as a Hamas ceasefire violation, while Hamas described the attacks as a renewed violation of the truce and urged mediators to press Israel to stop [1][7][8][12]. The escalation came as attention focused on the Rafah border crossing with Egypt, which several outlets said was due to reopen as part of the ceasefire arrangements, offering a potential pathway for increased movement and aid if implemented as planned [1][13][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Rafah is Gaza’s southernmost border crossing to Egypt and has frequently served as a key gateway for aid and evacuations during the war [common].

Key Points

  • Police station hit: Palestinian civil defence said a strike hit the Sheikh Radwan police station in Gaza City, killing police officers and detainees, as responders searched debris for additional victims [14][5].
  • Family shelter strike: Gaza’s health ministry said a strike on a tent shelter in southern Gaza killed members of a displaced family, including a child and an elderly person [2].
  • Ceasefire strain: Several reports described Saturday as among the deadliest days since an October ceasefire/truce framework, underscoring how violence has continued despite the agreement moving through phases [4][16][8].
  • Targets disputed: Israel said it was striking Hamas-linked targets, while Palestinian health and rescue officials emphasized civilian casualties, including women and children, arriving at hospitals [1][9][3].
  • Border focus: The strikes came just ahead of the expected reopening of the Rafah crossing, a step tied in some reports to ceasefire implementation and humanitarian access [13][5][1].

Historical Background

Israel and Hamas have fought a war in Gaza since Hamas’s Oct. 7, 2023 attack on Israel and Israel’s subsequent military campaign in the territory [common]. Multiple outlets describe an October ceasefire/truce framework intended to halt fighting, but also report recurring accusations by both sides of violations and continued violence [7][8][16]. The Rafah crossing on Gaza’s border with Egypt has been a critical pressure point for civilian movement and humanitarian access during the conflict [common], and several reports tied its planned reopening to the ceasefire arrangements [13][5].

Q&A

Q: Which specific locations were reported as struck across Gaza on Saturday?

A: Reports cited strikes on an apartment building in Gaza City, a police station in Gaza City, and a tent area in Khan Younis, among other locations [10][14][3].

Q: What did mediators reportedly do or plan to do next regarding the ceasefire?

A: One report noted Hamas urged the United States and other mediating countries to push Israel to stop the strikes, but the articles provided did not detail a specific new mediator action beyond that appeal [12].

Q: Why does the Rafah crossing matter in this moment?

A: Several outlets highlighted Rafah’s expected reopening as part of ceasefire arrangements, suggesting it could affect cross-border movement and aid flows if it proceeds as described [13][5][1].
