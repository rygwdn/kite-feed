NASA plans 2027 lunar docking tests before 2028 astronaut landing

Category: Science

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency remains on track for Artemis III, a two-week mission planned for next summer to test docking between its Orion capsule and lunar landers built by SpaceX and Blue Origin. The crew will orbit Earth rather than travel to the Moon; Artemis IV, scheduled for 2028, is intended to return astronauts to the lunar surface [2][1]. The docking tests will require three launches in quick succession. Orion is scheduled to dock first with Blue Origin’s lander, then with SpaceX’s Starship. NASA has not yet chosen which company will provide the Artemis IV lander, and officials say they will keep both options open as long as possible [2][1].

It's about our own history. It's a playbook this agency knows really well, the same playbook that went from Apollo 7 splashdown to Apollo 11 in just nine months.

— Jared Isaacman, NASA administrator (The Weather Channel)

Key Points

  • New space race: Isaacman said the competition to land astronauts on the Moon, now against China rather than the Soviet Union, will be “much closer than it ever needed to be” because of past delays [2].
  • Lander orbits: Blue Origin’s Blue Ghost is slated to operate about 270 miles above Earth, with SpaceX’s Starship in a slightly lower orbit [2][1].
  • South pole plans: NASA plans a lunar base near the Moon’s south pole, and officials say its first launch should take place by the end of 2026 [2].

Perspectives

Jared Isaacman, NASA administrator: NASA is accelerating work by reducing what Isaacman called endless meetings and bureaucratic inertia while bolstering its workforce. He said SpaceX, Blue Origin and other companies are working around the clock to meet deadlines [2].

Sources: The Weather Channel

Technical Details

  • Spacesuit testing: NASA plans a suit for the initial moonwalkers that is lighter and less complex than the Axiom Space suit still being developed for longer excursions. Axiom plans to test the newer “sortie suits” on the International Space Station next year [2].
  • Robotic preparations: NASA plans to send more landers, rovers and orbiters before the crew arrives, and officials expect the lunar vehicle to be waiting for the first Artemis moonwalkers [2].

Historical Background

The Apollo program sent 24 astronauts to the Moon between 1968 and 1972, 12 of whom landed on its surface [2].
