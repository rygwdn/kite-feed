UN-backed report warns overshooting 1.5C could permanently damage Earth's ice

Category: Science

A report by 80 scientists convened by the International Cryosphere Climate Initiative and the U.N. Environment Programme warns that even a temporary rise above the Paris Agreement’s 1.5C warming limit could cause lasting losses to glaciers, sea ice and polar ice sheets [1][3][5]. Earth has already warmed about 1.3C from preindustrial levels. The authors say any period above 1.5C should be as brief as possible before temperatures fall much further, and identify a long-term target below 1C by 2150 [1][3]. Under current policies, warming is projected to reach about 2.8C, a level the report links to near-total thaw of near-surface permafrost and likely severe disruption to ocean circulation [1][5]. Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets would continue losing ice even if temperatures later returned to 1.5C, while many ice systems could take centuries or millennia to recover [1][3]. The report was released ahead of the COP31 climate conference in Turkey [4][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Snow and ice respond rapidly to every fraction of a degree of warming.

— James Kirkham, glaciologist at the International Cryosphere Climate Initiative and lead author of the report (The Guardian)

Key Points

  • Mountain glaciers: Under current policies, glaciers in the Rockies, Alps, Iceland and Scandinavia could retain less than 10% of their mass; limiting warming to 1.5C would leave about 20% to 35% [1][5].
  • Himalayan water: The Hindu Kush Himalayas, a water source for nearly one-quarter of humanity, are projected to retain 41% of their glacier mass under current policies, compared with 83% if warming is limited to 1.5C [1][5].
  • Recent losses: Switzerland has lost 20% of its glacier ice in five years, while Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets are melting about six times faster than in the 1990s [1][3].
  • Emissions cuts: A separate U.N. Environment Programme assessment published the previous month said emissions must fall 18% by 2030 and 45% by 2035 to give a good chance of limiting warming to 1.7C [1][5].

Perspectives

James Kirkham, the report’s lead author, said the cryosphere is one of the climate system’s most sensitive components and its loss has planetwide consequences, citing a deadly ice-and-rock avalanche on the Nepal-China border [1][3].

Sources: The Guardian

Bill Hare, a climate scientist and chief executive of Climate Analytics, warned that climate impacts could exceed society’s ability to adapt [1][3].

Sources: The Guardian

Technical Details

  • Permafrost-carbon feedback: The report says thawing permafrost could release as much CO₂ each year as the United States currently emits annually [4].
  • Sea-level rise: In a 2.8C overshoot scenario, sea levels could rise by as much as 5 centimeters a year by 2150, compared with the current rate of 0.45 centimeters a year [4].

Scientific Significance

  • Sea-ice recovery: Recent research cited by the report suggests sea ice recovers more slowly than previously thought [1][3].
  • Threshold for polar ice: The authors say a safe long-term warming limit for polar ice sheets lies much closer to 1C than 1.5C and may be lower. The report also says glaciers would retain only about 70% of their current volume over centuries even if long-term warming fell to 0.8C [1][3].
