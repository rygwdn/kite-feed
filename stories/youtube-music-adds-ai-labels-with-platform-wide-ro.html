YouTube Music adds AI labels, with platform-wide rollout planned

Category: Technology

YouTube Music began rolling out labels on Oct. 14 for tracks created primarily with generative AI. An “AI” marker appears beneath the song title on the player page; tapping it opens an explanation of the track’s AI use [3][2]. The policy covers substantial AI contributions, such as generating a composition or vocals, rather than every track that uses AI in production [7][6]. YouTube says it will identify tracks using its own detection tools and information from music partners. It plans to bring the labels to the main YouTube app but has not given a date [5][2]. The labels disclose AI use but do not offer a setting to remove those songs from YouTube Music recommendations [2]. Deezer plans a worldwide filter that, when enabled, will hide fully AI-generated tracks, albums and related artists from search results. Deezer said the option is due before the end of 2026 [9]. YouTube Music’s rollout began Oct. 14, while the broader YouTube expansion remains undated [3][2].

Key Points

  • Upload volume: Deezer said it receives nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks daily; they account for 1% to 3% of streams on the service [9][4].
  • Royalty fraud: Prosecutors said musician Michael Smith used AI-generated songs and automated streams to collect more than $8 million in fraudulent royalties; the scheme involved hundreds of thousands of songs and billions of plays [9].
  • Different policies: Spotify’s AI Persona badges identify artist profiles depicting photorealistic AI-generated people, rather than indicating how a particular song was made [9].
  • Listener research: A Deezer/Ipsos blind test found 97% of participants could not distinguish two AI-generated songs from a human-made track; 80% of respondents wanted fully AI-generated music labeled [9].

Perspectives

Music industry groups: RIAA and IFPI leaders called for a two-tier system distinguishing AI-generated songs from AI-assisted songs, saying the categories should be clear and scalable [5].

  • 14 October 2026: YouTube Music began rolling out AI track labels [3][5]

Technical Details

  • Partner metadata: YouTube’s partner workflow supports “Fully Gen AI,” “Partly Gen AI” and “No Gen AI” declarations, distinguishing fully synthetic tracks from work that mixes generated elements with human performance [7][13].
  • Detection limits: YouTube combines partner disclosures with its own detection tools and may use additional signals when partners leave AI information blank; the announcement included no accuracy or catalog-coverage figures [13][2].

Industry Impact

  • AI creation tools: YouTube has experimented with Dream Track, which lets select creators generate soundtracks featuring AI-generated versions of participating artists’ voices [7].
  • Music royalties: Deezer said up to 85% of streams of fully AI-generated tracks were fraudulent in 2025, and platforms including Deezer and Tidal have responded by withholding royalties in specified cases [9][7].

Q&A

Q: Will the labels identify minor AI assistance, such as pitch correction?

A: YouTube’s partner guidance places tracks edited with tools such as Auto-Tune in the “No Gen AI” category; the labels target substantial use of generative AI [7].

Q: Does the absence of an AI label guarantee a track was made without AI?

A: No. YouTube gave no detection-accuracy or catalog-coverage figures, and the absence of a label is not proof that a song was made without AI [2].
