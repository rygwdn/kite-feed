Fossil studies reveal Arctic animals and dinosaur collagen

Category: Science

Recent paleontology reports described fossil records that preserve biological or environmental information: ancient penguin bones tied to climate evidence, a northern Norwegian cave deposit with Arctic animal remains from roughly 85,000 to 71,000 years ago, and an Edmontosaurus fossil that researchers say retains evidence of original collagen [1][2][3]. The Norwegian cave study centered on Arne Qvamgrotta near Kjøpsvik inside the Arctic Circle, where more than 6,000 bone fragments helped identify 46 vertebrate taxa, including polar bear, walrus, bowhead whale, reindeer, seabirds, freshwater fish and a collared lemming not previously recorded in Scandinavia [2]. The dinosaur study, led by the University of Liverpool, examined an Edmontosaurus hip bone from the Hell Creek Formation and reported remnants of collagen in the 66-million-year-old fossil [3].

Key Points

  • Cave preservation: The Norwegian deposit survived because a limestone cave preserved layered material that glaciers usually destroy in Arctic settings [2].
  • Lemming range: ScienceBlog said the collared lemming is extinct in Fennoscandia but still survives farther east, after correcting a headline that described the species as extinct in Europe [2].
  • Molecular debate: The collagen finding is part of a roughly 30-year scientific debate over whether original proteins can persist in Mesozoic fossils [3].

Perspectives

University of Liverpool-led researchers: The research team said the discovery could reveal molecular clues about dinosaur biology, evolution and how ancient proteins survived for so long.
