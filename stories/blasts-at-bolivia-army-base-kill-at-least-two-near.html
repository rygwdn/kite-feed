Blasts at Bolivia army base kill at least two near La Paz

Category: World

Two explosions struck an army barracks in Viacha, Bolivia, on Friday afternoon, killing at least two people and injuring dozens, authorities said. Local health officials gave higher estimates, with Viacha health director Rita Nebraska estimating 10 to 15 deaths and 62 injuries [1][3][5][7][10][11][63]. The blasts occurred around 2:30 p.m. local time at a military facility about 30 kilometers from La Paz, in an area used to store pyrotechnic material or fireworks. Officials said the cause remained under investigation [1][5][7][10][47][70]. Defence Minister Ernesto Justiniano said pyrotechnic material was stored at the base and reported 81 wounded, including two people in serious condition. Earlier police and health reports put the number of injured at 59 to 62 [1][7][45][22][10]. Police spokesman Roger Roca said fireworks had been stored at the barracks and that investigators had not established what triggered the explosion. Reports also cited seven missing people, while some ministry-linked accounts said 14 were missing [5][7][52][73][8][13][33]. The blasts damaged nearby homes, mainly shattering windows, and footage showed black smoke rising from the area as firefighters, ambulances, medical teams and police responded [1][3][10][47][35][60]. Firefighters warned that a heat source remained at the site, prompting Nebraska to urge residents to stay at least 150 meters away. President Rodrigo Paz expressed solidarity with victims’ families and said a technical and transparent investigation would take place [5][7][10][70].

Key Points

  • Civilian injuries: Justiniano said 52 of the injured were civilians; the rest were military personnel [3][45][28][30][37].
  • Hospital transfers: Nebraska said 14 wounded people were transferred to hospitals in La Paz, including a girl with third-degree burns [3][5][10][44][70].
  • Local mourning: Viacha authorities declared three days of mourning after the explosions [7][31][46][35][63].
  • Search effort: Emergency workers searched rubble for missing people after the explosions [7][31][46][47].
