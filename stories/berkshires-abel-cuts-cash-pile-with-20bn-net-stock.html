Berkshire’s Abel cuts cash pile with $20bn net stock buying

Category: Business

Berkshire Hathaway under Chief Executive Greg Abel bought about $23.5 billion of stocks in the second quarter and sold $3.7 billion, making nearly $20 billion of net purchases and ending a 14-quarter run as a net seller of equities [7][10][14]. Berkshire also invested $10 billion in Alphabet, Google’s parent company, and repurchased about $4.5 billion of its own shares [6][1][10][12]. The spending reduced Berkshire’s cash holdings to $365.5 billion from nearly $400 billion at the end of March, according to the company’s second-quarter report cited by AP and other accounts [6][1][12]. Abel became CEO in January after Warren Buffett retired from the role following six decades leading the company; Buffett remains chairman [6][1][14]. Berkshire reported second-quarter operating income of about $13 billion, up roughly 16% from a year earlier, as gains at BNSF Railway, Berkshire Hathaway Energy and manufacturing, service and retailing businesses offset weaker insurance results [3][7][14]. Net income more than doubled to $25.67 billion, helped by investment gains, while Geico’s underwriting profit fell 45%, which CFRA Research analyst Cathy Seifert called a concern [6][1][10].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Buyback threshold: Berkshire says it repurchases stock only when Abel and Buffett believe the shares trade below intrinsic value, and it does not set a fixed buyback target [1][13].
  • Alphabet ranking: After the Alphabet purchase, the Google parent joined Apple, American Express, Coca-Cola and Bank of America among Berkshire’s five largest stock holdings by market value [10][12].
  • Segment mix: Manufacturing, service and retailing profit rose 24% year over year to $4.47 billion, while energy profit rose 27% and insurance profit fell 13% [14].

Perspectives

CFRA Research analyst Cathy Seifert: Seifert said investors should be encouraged by the buyback but singled out Geico’s 45% drop in underwriting profit and its weak comparison with other large auto insurers as a concern.

Sources: ABC News

Yonhap, citing The Wall Street Journal: Berkshire officials said Abel still views public and private market asset prices as broadly high, suggesting patience on large acquisitions.

Sources: Yonhap News Agency

Shareholders cited by Portfolio: Portfolio said shareholders had long pressed Berkshire to put more of Buffett’s unusually large cash reserve to use beyond Treasury bills.

Sources: Portfolio

Timeline

  • 2018: Berkshire began a $78 billion multiyear buyback period [1]
  • January 2026: Abel became CEO after Buffett stepped down [6][1]
  • March 2026: Abel said Berkshire resumed repurchases after a long pause [1]
  • June 2026: Berkshire bought about $10 billion of Alphabet shares [10][14]
  • July 2026: Berkshire completed the Taylor Morrison acquisition after the quarter ended [7][1]
  • 8 August 2026: Berkshire reported second-quarter earnings and a lower cash balance [6][7]

Technical Details

  • Treasury payables adjustment: Business Insider calculated Berkshire’s cash and Treasury bills at $365 billion at the end of June after excluding Treasury payables, compared with $380 billion at the end of March on the same basis [7].
  • Mark-to-market earnings: Berkshire’s net income included gains from changes in the value of stock investments, helping lift second-quarter net income to $25.67 billion, while operating income was about $13 billion [1][10][14].

Historical Background

Buffett built Berkshire Hathaway from a textile business into a conglomerate spanning insurance, railroads, energy, manufacturing and retail [6][1]. During Buffett’s final years as CEO, Berkshire’s cash pile reached record levels as the company sold stocks and found few large bargains or acquisitions [7][11]. From 2018 through 2024, Berkshire spent $78 billion repurchasing its own stock [1].

Business Angle

  • Berkshire says it repurchases stock only when Abel and Buffett believe shares trade below intrinsic value, and it does not set a fixed buyback target [1][13][common].
  • Geico’s underwriting profit fell 45%; insurance float has historically been central to Berkshire’s capital base [6][common].
  • Alphabet became a top-five Berkshire holding by market value, increasing exposure to a technology platform whose economics differ from Berkshire’s railroad, insurance and consumer-brand assets [10][12].
  • Berkshire still held more than $360 billion in cash after the quarter [6][14].
