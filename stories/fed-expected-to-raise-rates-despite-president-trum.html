Fed expected to raise rates despite President Trump’s demands

Category: Business

The Federal Reserve is widely expected to raise its benchmark interest rate by a quarter percentage point on Wednesday, Sept. 16, its first increase since July 2023. The move would lift the federal funds target range from 3.50%-3.75% to 3.75%-4%, as policymakers respond to inflation that remained at 3.4% year over year in August, well above the Fed’s 2% target [1][2][16][10]. President Trump has called for lower borrowing costs or no change, while financial markets have priced in roughly a 90% probability of a quarter-point increase [2][16][32]. Warsh signaled at the Fed’s Jackson Hole conference in late August that inflation had not yet come under sufficient control. Renewed fighting involving Iran has pushed oil and gas prices higher, complicating the inflation outlook [1][2]. The Fed’s two-day meeting concludes Wednesday, with Warsh scheduled to hold a news conference at 2 p.m. EDT; investors will focus on whether officials signal additional increases after September [2][61].

Key Points

  • Bond-market risk: If the Fed surprises markets by holding rates steady, it could accelerate the bond-market sell-off that has pushed yields higher [52].
  • Market reaction: Goldman Sachs found that stocks typically dip immediately after the start of a hiking cycle and tend to bottom two to three months later, though the historical pattern does not predict this meeting’s outcome [11].

Perspectives

Industry Impact

  • Housing finance: Mortgage rates have risen with the 10-year Treasury yield, increasing the cost of home financing even before any change in the Fed’s overnight rate [16][23].
  • Government finance: Higher Treasury yields raise scrutiny of the cost of servicing the roughly $40 trillion US national debt [52][31].
  • Energy-sensitive businesses: Brent crude moved toward $110 a barrel and US diesel prices approached $6.27 a gallon, increasing transport and operating costs for businesses [31].
  • Digital assets: Bitcoin fell nearly 3% to about $75,800 before the decision, while several other digital tokens fell roughly 10% [46].

Historical Background

The Fed’s tightening cycle began in 2022 and eventually took the federal funds target range to 5.25%-5.50% in July 2023 [62].

Business Angle

  • Hawkish hike: If the Fed raises rates and signals more tightening, higher terminal-rate expectations could put additional pressure on high-valuation growth sectors [43].
  • Dovish hike: If officials raise rates but suggest policy is already restrictive, stocks could initially fall and then recover as short-term yields and the dollar ease [43].
  • Hawkish hold: A decision to leave rates unchanged could produce a brief rally that fades if officials present the pause as temporary [43].

International Reactions

  • 🇪🇺 European Central Bank: The ECB raised eurozone interest rates by 25 basis points the previous week, according to market coverage of the global central-bank decisions [31][61].
  • 🇬🇧 Bank of England: Markets expected the Bank of England to hold its benchmark rate on Thursday as the UK economy struggled with weak growth [31].
  • 🇯🇵 Bank of Japan: Investors were also preparing for a possible Japanese rate increase, which could affect international capital flows alongside the Fed decision [13].

Action Items
