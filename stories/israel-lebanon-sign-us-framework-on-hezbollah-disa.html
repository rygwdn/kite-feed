Israel, Lebanon sign U.S. framework on Hezbollah disarmament

Category: World

Israel, Lebanon and the United States signed a trilateral framework agreement at the State Department in Washington on June 26 after several days of U.S.-mediated talks on ending fighting between Israel and Hezbollah and opening a path toward a wider Israel-Lebanon settlement [3][21][11][57]. Lebanese Ambassador Nada Hamadeh and Israeli Ambassador Yechiel Leiter signed the document with U.S. officials present, and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio described it as an initial step rather than a finished agreement [10][20][36]. The 14-point framework says Israel and Lebanon affirm each state’s right to live in peace and intend to conclude the state of war between them through direct bilateral negotiations with U.S. mediation [21][25][47]. It calls for the Lebanese Armed Forces to restore effective sovereign authority over Lebanese territory pending verified disarmament of non-state armed groups and dismantlement of associated infrastructure, and Rubio said the United States would facilitate implementation through a trilateral Military Coordination Group for Lebanon [21][5][40]. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the Israel Defense Forces would withdraw from two areas in southern Lebanon and hand them to the Lebanese army, while keeping Israeli forces in a security zone as long as Hezbollah remains armed [22][44][34]. Hezbollah was not part of the talks and rejected the framework. Later on June 26, supporters protested in Beirut, blocked roads with burning tires and prompted Lebanese army checkpoints across parts of the capital [21][64][65][70].

Key Points

  • U.S. funding: Rubio said Washington would commit an immediate $100 million in humanitarian assistance coordinated with the U.N. and more than $30 million to improve Lebanese Armed Forces capabilities [3][40].
  • Legal pledges: The framework includes commitments to stop hostile or adverse actions in international political or legal forums and to work toward the release of detainees and return of remains [21][25].
  • No timetable: Israeli officials told Ynet the framework has no fixed implementation timetable, though its two pilot zones sit north and south of the Litani River [26][22].
  • Iran track: Washington feared continued Israel-Hezbollah fighting could undermine its Iran agreement, which BBC said included a commitment to end fighting on all fronts, including Lebanon [21][46].

Perspectives

Historical Background

Israel and Lebanon have never had a lasting peace treaty, and their border area has repeatedly been shaped by wars involving Israel, Lebanese armed groups and outside powers [common]. U.N. Security Council Resolution 1701, adopted after the 2006 Israel-Hezbollah war, called for no armed groups in southern Lebanon other than the Lebanese state and U.N. peacekeepers [common].

Humanitarian Impact

Israeli air and ground attacks have killed more than 4,000 people in Lebanon and displaced more than one million since the March escalation, leaving affected communities needing shelter, return access and reconstruction [38][56].
