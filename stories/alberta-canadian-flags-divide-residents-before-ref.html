Alberta, Canadian flags divide residents before referendum vote

Category: Canada

Displays of Alberta and Canadian flags have become visible signs of division in parts of Alberta before an October vote on whether to hold a referendum on the province's independence, according to The Canadian Press and Le Devoir [1][3][4]. In Airdrie, north of Calgary, music teacher Sarah Batchelor said a neighbour's Alberta flag bothered her because separatists have widely adopted it; she responded by putting a Canadian flag in her garden [1]. The Walrus published an opinion essay by Dónal Gill arguing that opposition to Alberta separatists and their referendum push conflicts with democratic principles, while noting that Canada’s political system relies mainly on representative government rather than direct democracy [2]. The essay traced that tension to Confederation-era choices that placed sovereignty in the Crown rather than in a U.S.-style popular founding myth [2].

Historical Background

The Walrus essay says Canadian leaders at Confederation deliberately avoided the U.S. model of popular sovereignty; it quotes Thomas D’Arcy McGee saying a united Canada would help resist absorption into the American experiment in self-government [2]. The essay also cites Sir John A. Macdonald using the American Civil War as evidence for the “superiority of our institutions,” a point Gill connects to Canada’s constitutional-monarchy structure [2].
