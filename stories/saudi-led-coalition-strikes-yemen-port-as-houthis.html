Saudi-led coalition strikes Yemen port as Houthis target Riyadh with missiles

Category: World

The Saudi-led coalition struck the Houthi-held Red Sea port of Hodeidah and targets in Sanaa, Saada and Taiz on Friday, saying it was disrupting weapons smuggling and missile operations. Early Saturday, the coalition said it intercepted and destroyed three ballistic missiles headed for Riyadh after authorities issued an air-threat alert and witnesses heard two explosions. Authorities later lifted the alert [5][39][35][37]. The Houthis said they were attacking targets across Saudi Arabia and claimed their strikes on a power station in Al-Shuqaiq and Jizan port caused extensive damage. The coalition said it intercepted two ballistic missiles near Jizan, with debris falling in Al-Shuqaiq [4][34][12]. The exchange follows the collapse of a four-year truce in July and a Houthi offensive that has driven Saudi-backed government forces from areas near the Bab al-Mandab strait. Hodeidah is a key entry point for food and other supplies to Houthi-controlled northern Yemen. The Houthis have also advanced toward Aden, the government’s interim seat [5][17][6].

Key Points

  • Strike targets: The coalition said it destroyed seven military sites in Sanaa, including six workshops used to assemble missile and drone launch structures and a major supply depot [13][30][31].
  • Casualty claims: The Houthis reported that coalition strikes killed 41 people over 24 hours, while Saudi-backed forces said 193 Houthi fighters were killed or wounded. The figures could not be independently confirmed [5][34].
  • Port operations: Yemen’s information minister said Hodeidah remained fully operational and goods continued to flow after the strikes [17][24].
  • Civilian boat allegation: The Houthis accused Saudi forces of striking a civilian boat near the Bab al-Mandab strait on Friday, killing 19 people [4][5].

Perspectives

Saudi-led coalition: It described the operation as an effort to neutralize military capabilities and infrastructure it said were used to smuggle weapons and threaten civilians in Yemen and Saudi Arabia [31].
