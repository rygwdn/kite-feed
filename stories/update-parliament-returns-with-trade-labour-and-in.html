Update: Parliament returns with trade, labour and investment agenda

Category: Canada

Canada’s House of Commons returns Sept. 21 after a three-month break, with the Liberal government preparing legislation on federal project approvals, supply chains and the Canada Labour Code. The fall agenda includes the U.S. trade dispute, closer Canada-European Union ties and affordability. Prime Minister Mark Carney identified those issues while defending his international travel and domestic priorities [1][2][4]. The government is expected to introduce a bill changing how Section 107 of the Canada Labour Code can be used during strikes and lockouts. Government House Leader Steven MacKinnon said the aim is to help parties reach collective agreements rather than prevent strikes [2]. Parliament will also consider Bill C-10, which would create an independent commissioner to monitor implementation of modern treaties, although critics have questioned whether the proposed office would have enough authority [3].

Key Points

  • EU relationship: European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen opened the possibility of Canada becoming an EU “associate member,” while Carney has advocated closer formal ties [1]
  • Investment target: Carney is seeking $500 billion in private investment by the end of the decade [4]
  • Treaty oversight: Bill C-10 is at third reading and would go to the Senate if it passes, with passage largely assured because the Liberals hold a majority [3]
  • Affordability risk: McGill professor Daniel Béland said the Liberals could face attacks from Conservatives if they leave an opening on the economy and cost of living [1]
