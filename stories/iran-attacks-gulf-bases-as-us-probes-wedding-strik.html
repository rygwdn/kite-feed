Iran attacks Gulf bases as US probes wedding strike

Category: World

Iran launched missiles and drones toward U.S. military targets in Kuwait and said it also struck Al Minhad Air Base in the United Arab Emirates after accusing Washington of hitting a wedding gathering in southern Iran earlier in the week [2][3][11][21][22]. Kuwait said it intercepted a missile-and-drone attack and called the strikes a grave breach of international law, while the UAE reported no such attack on Al Minhad [11][17][31]. U.S. Vice President JD Vance said Washington was investigating Iranian claims that a U.S. strike hit a home during a wedding in Kuhestak, in Sirik County, while saying he was “extremely skeptical” of Iranian state media accounts [9][8][18][37]. Iranian state television said a 22-year-old woman died in hospital after the strike, raising the reported death toll to five, and Iranian officials said nearly 70 people were wounded [2][3][18][33]. The U.S. military said it was aware of the reports and “never targets civilians” [2][9][24]. Weapons experts reviewing verified images and video in a Reuters analysis said the blast at the wedding likely resulted from a direct hit by a U.S. munition rather than a ricochet or misdirected Iranian air-defense missile [4][7][50][39][55]. The New York Times reported, based on a weapons expert and its visual analysis, that the bomb that hit the residential area was American [30][24][47].

Key Points

  • Target area: U.S. forces had carried out strikes in southern Iran targeting IRGC air-defense sites, radar systems and communications facilities, including a communications tower near the wedding site, according to accounts citing U.S. officials and satellite imagery [39][34][45].
  • Named victims: The locally named dead included Zarkhatoon Taheri, 50, Kolsum Mallahi Nazhdnia, 43, Mohammad Mallahi, 16, and Amirali Karimi, 4, before Iranian state media reported a fifth death in hospital [9][45].
  • Wider deaths: Iran’s Health Ministry spokesman Hossein Kermanpour said U.S. attacks from 8:30 p.m. on August 30 through 8:30 p.m. on September 2 killed 18 people and injured 142, without separating military and civilian casualties [12].
  • Hormuz pressure: Iran has maintained pressure on the Strait of Hormuz, while Washington has pressed a counter-blockade of Iranian ports [11][17][31].
  • Political strain: The Globe and Mail and Reuters-linked accounts said President Trump’s administration faces growing domestic pressure to end a war affecting his approval ratings before November’s midterm elections [5][50][33].
