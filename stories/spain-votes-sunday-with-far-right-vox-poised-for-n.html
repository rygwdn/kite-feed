Spain votes Sunday with far-right Vox poised for national government role

Category: World

Spain votes on Nov. 29 in a snap parliamentary election that could end Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez’s eight years in office and give far-right Vox its first role in a national government since Francisco Franco’s dictatorship ended in 1975. Pre-election polls put the conservative People’s Party (PP) near 31%, the Socialists (PSOE) around 29% to 30% and Vox at roughly 17% to 18%. The PP is expected to need Vox’s support to reach the 176 seats required for a majority in the 350-seat Congress [3][9][13]. Sánchez called the early vote after parliament rejected housing measures on Oct. 2, amid anger over high rents and evictions. The campaign has also focused on corruption investigations involving people close to Sánchez, two fatal train crashes and migration, including the summer influx into the North African enclave of Ceuta [3][5][6]. The PP and Vox have signaled changes to climate policy. Polling stations open at 9 a.m. local time and close at 8 p.m. If no bloc secures a majority, parties will have to negotiate support for a government [13][10][15].

Key Points

  • Youth support: Support for Vox has risen to 23.2% among voters aged 18 to 24, slightly above the Socialists’ 22%, according to Spain’s November polling [17].
  • Housing pledges: PP leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo has promised one million new homes over four years, while Sánchez has emphasized tighter housing-market regulation and restrictions on unauthorized tourist rentals [5].
  • Rail scrutiny: After two deadly train crashes this year, Feijóo has pledged a rapid audit of the rail network if elected; the transport minister resigned after the second crash [5][13].
  • Election mechanics: Voters choose party lists in 52 constituencies, and parties need at least 3% of a constituency’s vote to qualify for seats there [13].
  • Coalition tensions: The PP and Vox have governed together in four regions, where they have clashed over policy [27][42].

Perspectives

Pedro Sánchez, prime minister of Spain: Sánchez has defended legal immigration, arguing that people who come to Spain bring economic and social benefits [5].

Historical Background

Vox was founded in 2013 by former members of the PP’s more hard-line wing. Its breakthrough came after the 2017 Catalan independence referendum: it entered Andalusia’s regional parliament in 2018 and won 52 seats in Spain’s national parliament in a repeat election in November 2019 [35].

Q&A

Q: Would Vox necessarily take cabinet seats if the PP wins?

A: No. Feijóo has indicated he would prefer a minority government with Vox’s parliamentary backing, but has not ruled out a coalition arrangement like the PP-Vox government in Andalusia [38].
