Ottawa allocates $34 million for climate resilience projects

Category: Canada

The federal government announced $34 million in funding for 141 climate resilience projects in 128 communities across Canada, including work to reduce risks from heat, flooding and other climate hazards [2][3][4][5][1]. Environment Minister Julie Dabrusin announced the funding in Halifax on Wednesday, and the federal government said 13 of the projects are in Nova Scotia [2][5]. Halifax will receive nearly $800,000 for cooling measures in parks to help protect residents from extreme heat [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Summer hazards: The funding follows another summer in which Canadian cities and towns faced extreme heat, wildfires, flooding and tornadoes [2][1].
  • Local partners: The announcement included Shannon Miedema, MP, and Federation of Canadian Municipalities leaders Tim Tierney and Carole Saab alongside Environment Minister Julie Dabrusin [5].
  • Risk focus: The federal government described the projects as measures to help protect communities from climate risks, not disaster-response funding after a single event [5].

Perspectives

Environment Minister Julie Dabrusin said: "In Canada, the climate is changing. Our government is investing to make these communities more resilient, so that people are protected from floods, extreme heat and other risks."

Sources: La Presse

Federation of Canadian Municipalities: FCM president Tim Tierney and CEO Carole Saab joined the announcement with Dabrusin.
