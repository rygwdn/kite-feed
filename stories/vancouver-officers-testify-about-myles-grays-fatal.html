Vancouver officers testify about Myles Gray’s fatal 2015 arrest

Category: Canada

Const. Beau Spencer testified Thursday at a British Columbia Police Complaint Commissioner hearing examining whether seven Vancouver officers committed misconduct during the 2015 arrest that ended in Myles Gray’s death [1][4]. Spencer said the prolonged struggle led him to consider “excited delirium,” which he had been trained to treat as a medical emergency [1]. The hearing follows a 2023 coroner’s inquest that ruled Gray’s death a homicide, a finding that does not assign legal blame; no charges have been laid [1][3]. The officers are respondents in the current proceeding after an earlier disciplinary process dismissed misconduct allegations. The discipline authority criticized that process as “strangely lopsided” [1][3].

Key Points

  • Note-taking: Claire Hatcher, Spencer’s lawyer, said union representatives had told officers not to make notes or write on computers while the independent investigation was underway [1].
  • Memory: Const. Eric Birzneck said his memories had been jogged over time, with “pieces of the puzzle” coming together afterward [3].
  • Inquest findings: The inquest heard that Gray had a fractured eye socket, crushed voice box and ruptured testicles; a paramedic said the bruising was so severe he initially thought Gray was not white [3].

Perspectives

Const. Eric Birzneck: Birzneck said he believed Gray could lawfully be arrested and had no lawful right to repel the officers’ use of force. He also denied shaping his testimony to protect another officer [3].

Sources: The Canadian Press via Lethbridge Herald

Claire Hatcher, lawyer for Const. Beau Spencer: Hatcher argued that Spencer’s role should be assessed separately because he arrived after the fight began. She said his use of force was limited to three or four punches to help handcuff Gray [1].
