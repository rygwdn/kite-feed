Israeli strikes kill 11 in southern Lebanon

Category: World

Israeli airstrikes in southern Lebanon on Saturday killed at least 11 people, including three children, in the deadliest attacks since a June truce reduced fighting between Israel and Hezbollah [2][3][5][6][19][17]. Lebanon's Health Ministry and state media said seven people were killed when a strike hit a house on the edge of Ansar, while four others were killed in Deir al-Zahrani; the attacks wounded 19 people in total [2][3][8][10][12][19]. Israel said it struck Hezbollah infrastructure after what it described as an attack on Israeli forces in southern Lebanon, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office accused Hezbollah of violating the ceasefire and wounding three Israeli soldiers [2][5][8][9][50]. Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam condemned the strikes, saying the people killed in Ansar were not military infrastructure or military targets, while Hezbollah said the attacks would receive an appropriate response [3][6][10][5][17]. The strikes followed a U.S.-mediated framework under which Israeli troops would remain in southern Lebanon until Hezbollah disarms and the Lebanese army takes control, terms Hezbollah has rejected as surrender [3][8][12][24]. Israel and Lebanon were expected to hold an eighth round of U.S.-sponsored direct negotiations early next month in Rome, and Lebanese President Joseph Aoun said Saturday's strikes were a clear message aimed at those talks and U.S. efforts to implement the framework agreement [10][17][33][38].

Key Points

  • Wider strikes: Lebanon's National News Agency reported additional Israeli strikes across the south, including the Ali al-Taher hill north of the Litani River, and one valley strike near Ansar was described as Israel's deepest strike into Lebanon in two months [8][12][50][24].
  • Second village: Deir al-Zahrani lies about 18 to 20 kilometers from Ansar, and health authorities reported 17 wounded there, with some accounts saying the wounded included a child and 11 women [14][48][18][33].
  • Conflict toll: Reuters-based accounts said at least 4,300 people have been killed in Lebanon in the latest round of hostilities since March 2 [8][24][27][50].
  • Talks track: Lebanon and Israel had completed several rounds of talks before the next planned U.S.-sponsored meeting [25][40][14].

Historical Background

The current truce followed a halt in major fighting between Israel and Hezbollah, and Israel and Lebanon announced a separate framework agreement. The framework called for an Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon in exchange for Hezbollah's disarmament and the Lebanese army taking control, terms Hezbollah was not party to [3][8][12].

Humanitarian Impact

Among the dead in Ansar were children and women, and among the wounded in Deir al-Zahrani were women and a child [2][19][8][18]. Rescue workers searched rubble in Ansar after the strike destroyed a building [18][19][48].
