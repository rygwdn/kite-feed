Kim calls for wider tactical nuclear deployment as North Korea expands production

Category: World

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un called for deploying tactical nuclear weapons across multiple domains and said the country was systematically expanding nuclear-material production, state media reported Thursday. He spoke during a Wednesday inspection of nuclear-material production and weapons research sites, including a newly built production base and a nuclear-related equipment facility. State media did not identify their locations [21][12][22]. Kim said tactical weapons should serve different missions, citing what he described as threats from South Korea and the United States. He again declared North Korea’s nuclear status irreversible and warned that attempts to deny it would be treated as a declaration of war. Pyongyang enshrined its nuclear status in its constitution in 2023 [1][21]. The inspection followed President Donald Trump’s unsuccessful bid to meet Kim during a visit to China for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit last week. North Korea did not publicly respond; Trump later said the timing had not been right. South Korea’s defense ministry expressed grave concern over Kim’s stated intention to expand tactical weapons [5][7][21].

Primary Source: View original article

Our possession of nuclear weapons is an absolutely irreversible choice as long as malicious forces exist that use nuclear weapons as a means of achieving hegemony

— Kim Jong Un, North Korean leader (Reuters)

Key Points

  • Facility clues: Images released by North Korean state media showed equipment analysts identified as uranium-enrichment centrifuges and several apparent warhead designs. Analysts said the pictured equipment could be intended for a naval reactor program [2][14].
  • Production estimate: Weapons experts cited by NDTV estimated that North Korea’s annual fissile-material output has risen from about six bombs’ worth during Trump’s first term to roughly 12–15 bombs’ worth [6].
  • Watchdog concern: International Atomic Energy Agency Director General Rafael Grossi said North Korea had expanded its original uranium-enrichment facility at Yongbyon, calling the development a serious concern [5][7].
  • Monitoring limits: The IAEA has lacked access to North Korea since 2009 and monitors its activities using satellite imagery and open-source intelligence [22].
  • Possible reach: South Korean analyst Shin Jong-woo said Kim’s reference to “multiple domains” could signal targets beyond the Korean Peninsula, including the U.S. mainland [9].

Perspectives

North Korean leadership: Kim framed the buildup as a response to alleged nuclear threats from the United States and its allies, and said Pyongyang would not seek other countries’ approval for its nuclear status [3].

Sources: Deutsche Welle

South Korean Defense Ministry: The ministry called on Pyongyang to stop justifying its nuclear program and comply with United Nations Security Council resolutions; it said Seoul would bolster deterrence with Washington [21].

Lim Eul-chul, Kyungnam University professor: Lim said the visit did not necessarily shut the door to U.S. dialogue, but signaled that North Korea would seek talks between states treated as nuclear powers [9].

President Donald Trump: Trump said Kim had not ignored his meeting overture, described the summit schedule as too busy, and said contact could resume, though the two had not corresponded recently [7].
