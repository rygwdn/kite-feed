Italy caps pupils with limited Italian, bans face veils during school activities

Category: World

Italy’s cabinet adopted a decree Sept. 24 that bans burqas and niqabs during school activities and caps at 30% the share of students in specified first-year classes who lack adequate Italian. The government says the measures are intended to support language learning and integration. The decree took effect immediately but must be ratified by Parliament within 60 days [4][2][1][50]. The cap does not apply to all foreign-born pupils. It applies to defined groups of non-Italian citizens, with exemptions based on birth, preschool attendance or prior years in Italy’s education system. When a child has serious integration difficulties, social services can require parents to attend free Italian classes; parents who fail to comply may be fined up to 1,000 euros [9][33][1].

Key Points

  • Class placement: Territorial education offices must distribute among classes pupils who do not meet the exemption criteria [33].
  • Coverage boundaries: The face-covering rule applies to extracurricular and school-family activities, with health or security exceptions; a face-visible headscarf is not prohibited [51][9][37].
