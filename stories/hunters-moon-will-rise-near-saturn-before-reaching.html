Hunter’s Moon will rise near Saturn before reaching full phase

Category: Science

October’s Hunter’s Moon will appear nearly full as it rises on Sunday, Oct. 25, and Monday, Oct. 26, reaching full phase just after midnight Eastern time on Monday. Saturn will appear nearby in the evening sky. On Monday night, the Moon will move above the Pleiades star cluster [1][7]. Skywatchers can look toward the eastern horizon shortly before local moonrise, ideally from a spot with an unobstructed view. This full moon is not a supermoon, but the next four full moons, beginning with November’s Beaver Moon on Nov. 24, are expected to be supermoons [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Viewing conditions: In the San Francisco Bay Area, clear skies were forecast for Saturday night, while clouds could make Sunday viewing harder [2].
  • Moon illusion: The Moon can seem larger near the horizon because of a visual illusion, not a change in its actual size [1].
  • Orion rising: In Mexico, Orion’s Belt—Alnitak, Alnilam and Mintaka—can help observers locate the constellation, which begins rising in the east around 10:30 p.m. [5].
  • Moon names: Other names for October’s full moon include Falling Leaves Moon, Drying Rice Moon and Freezing Moon [1].
