AI coverage turns to terminology, utility and energy

Category: AI

Recent AI coverage focused on practical vocabulary, business use and infrastructure costs rather than model launches. TechCrunch published a glossary it called a "living document" for readers encountering terms such as LLMs, RAG, RLHF, AGI and AI agents in product meetings, pitches and panels [3]. TechCentral argued that companies should judge AI by utility rather than possibility, citing Palantir CEO Alex Karp’s warnings about practices such as "tokenmaxxing" and about giving corporate information to companies that run LLMs [5]. Several articles focused on energy demand. N1info reported that the European Commission had planned to require companies such as Amazon, Microsoft and Google to power data centres only with new, local renewable energy, but a newer draft would allow companies to cover nighttime electricity gaps with solar-energy certificates generated elsewhere in Europe during the day [6]. The Nation published an editorial citing a UN report on AI’s water and land-resource demands and arguing that the industry’s expansion relies on ecological costs, financial uncertainty and precarious labour [7]. The Nation also cited leaked reports concerning OpenAI financial losses, while TechCentral separately said business adoption requires task success rates much closer to 100% than criminal uses such as AI-generated phishing emails [5][7].

Key Points

  • AI definitions: TechCrunch noted that OpenAI’s charter defines AGI as systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work, while Google DeepMind frames AGI as AI at least as capable as humans at most cognitive tasks [3].
  • Enterprise risk: TechCentral described customer-facing service as an area where companies had already weakened service quality before large language models entered mainstream business use [5].
  • Regional grids: N1info said planned and expanding Balkan data centres could strain local power systems that still rely heavily on thermal power plants [6].
