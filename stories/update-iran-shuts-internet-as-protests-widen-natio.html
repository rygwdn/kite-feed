Update: Iran shuts internet as protests widen nationwide

Category: World

In the latest development, Iranian authorities imposed a nationwide internet blackout as protests over soaring living costs and a collapsing currency continued and spread across the country [1][2][19][17]. Rights groups and media reports said the crackdown has involved mass arrests and deadly force in some areas, while the government has acknowledged the demonstrations and signaled tougher measures [5][1][24][30]. Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei blamed the unrest on foreign influence and told President Trump to focus on problems in the United States [1][28][27][30]. President Trump warned the U.S. would “get involved” if Iran’s leadership killed protesters, while Iran’s U.N. ambassador alleged the U.S., in coordination with Israel, helped turn peaceful protests violent [32][35][36][41].

Did you know: One report estimated Iran’s Revolutionary Guard has influence over roughly 20% to 40% of the country’s economy, giving it an unusually broad role beyond security [15].

Key Points

  • Hospitals strained: Iranian medical staff described overwhelmed hospitals as injuries mounted amid continuing demonstrations [6].
  • Protest scale: Protests have been reported in more than 170 locations across 25 provinces, with some coverage estimating participation in the hundreds of thousands [13].
  • Zahedan clash: In Zahedan, a city with a large Baluch minority population, a march after Friday prayers was met with gunfire that reportedly wounded several people [1].
  • Opposition appeals: Exiled figure Reza Pahlavi urged supporters to keep mobilizing, including calls for protesters to seize city centres, while President Trump said he would not meet Pahlavi and questioned whether supporting him would be appropriate [1][39][37].
  • Regime calculus: Analysts said Iran’s leaders are projecting defiance publicly, but the internal cost-benefit calculus of repression may be more fraught than in past crises [9][4].

Timeline

  • December 28: Demonstrations began over rising prices and economic strain [1][10][16].
  • Friday: Authorities imposed a nationwide internet blackout as unrest expanded [1][19][17].
  • Friday: Khamenei said the regime would not back down [28][30].
  • Saturday: New rallies reported; Pahlavi urged plans to seize city centres [39][40].

Technical Details

  • Internet blackout: Multiple outlets reported Iran cut nationwide internet access and intermittent connectivity to limit communications and outside reporting during protests [1][19][17].
  • Casualty verification: Reported death and arrest totals vary widely across media and rights groups, reflecting constrained reporting conditions during the blackout and the lack of transparent official data [1][8][7].
  • Crowd-control tactics: Reports describe security forces using live rounds in some incidents, alongside other forceful crowd-control methods such as tear gas and water cannon [8][15].

Industry Impact

  • Aviation and travel: At least some flights were reported cancelled amid the unrest and communications shutdown, adding uncertainty for travelers and airlines operating to or within Iran [1].
  • Information access: The blackout disrupted phone calls and limited updates from Iranian news sites, complicating operations for international media, businesses with staff on the ground, and diaspora families trying to reach relatives [1].

Historical Background

Iran has faced repeated waves of nationwide protests in recent decades, often triggered by economic pressures and demands for political change, and authorities have at times restricted communications during unrest [common]. Several sources tie the current demonstrations to worsening economic conditions after last year’s conflict with Israel and the United States, alongside a sharp drop in the rial that made imported essentials harder to afford [1][15][10]. The same reporting notes that international sanctions and domestic economic management have compounded hardship, helping protests spread beyond major cities [15][10].

Q&A

Q: How long has the internet outage lasted, and how is it being tracked?

A: A monitoring update cited in reporting said the outage had been in place for about 36 hours, based on external internet connectivity tracking during the shutdown [43].

Q: Why do some analysts argue these protests are more than “food riots”?

A: Commentary described the movement as deeper and more structured than narrow price protests, with broader political demands and a difficult path to compromise [3].

Q: What explanation has the U.S. given in response to Iranian claims of foreign instigation?

A: U.S. officials dismissed Iran’s foreign-minister-level claims as “delusional,” rejecting allegations that Washington and Israel are fueling the protests [42][43].
