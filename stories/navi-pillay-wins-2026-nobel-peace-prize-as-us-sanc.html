Navi Pillay wins 2026 Nobel Peace Prize as U.S. sanctions ICC

Category: World

South African jurist and former U.N. human rights chief Navi Pillay won the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize on Friday for her efforts to promote peace and international law, the Norwegian Nobel Committee said [66]. The 85-year-old has served on South Africa’s High Court, the Rwanda tribunal, the International Criminal Court and the International Court of Justice. She also led a U.N. commission whose 2025 report concluded that Israel committed genocide in Gaza. Israel rejected that finding [5][25]. The committee said Pillay’s work to secure prosecutions for war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide was especially important as international legal institutions faced political attacks [64][62]. Hours after the Nobel announcement, the Trump administration imposed new sanctions on the ICC. President Trump later criticized the award and said Pillay “very obviously, hates Israel” [4][32][29]. Pillay learned of her award while attending a legal forum in Nuremberg, Germany, and dedicated it to survivors of international crimes [7][57].

Historical Background

Pillay defended anti-apartheid activists, including Nelson Mandela, early in her legal career; in 1973, she won a case establishing that political prisoners on Robben Island had a right to legal representation [57]. She joined the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda in 1995, where her work helped shape legal recognition of sexual violence as genocide and crimes against humanity [64][51].
