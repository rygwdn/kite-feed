Creators pull back on AI as web referrals fall

Category: AI

A developer of an I Ching app says they removed built-in AI features, arguing that the product worked better when it stayed focused on clear, predictable behavior rather than AI-generated interpretation [1]. At the same time, a Chartbeat report highlighted by PCMag says traffic to news sites from Google Search fell sharply over the past year—33% worldwide and 38% in the US—alongside a drop in social referrals, trends the article links to the rise of AI-powered answers and shifting distribution [2]. Separately, a Forbes report carried via Google News says a new Chrome update removes Google’s on-device AI component, signaling that even big platforms are still iterating on what AI belongs “on device” versus elsewhere [3]. Taken together, the pieces point to an AI moment that’s becoming more selective: some creators are simplifying products, while publishers and platforms adapt to changing discovery patterns [1][2][3].

Did you know: Chartbeat is an analytics company widely used by newsrooms to track real-time reader engagement and traffic sources [2].

Key Points

  • Product philosophy: The I Ching app’s author frames the change as a conscious design choice—preferring a tool that supports reflection without outsourcing meaning-making to generated text [1].
  • Publisher pressure: PCMag, citing Chartbeat, describes the referral declines as a business challenge for publishers that rely on search and social distribution to monetize reporting [2].
  • Browser changes: Forbes reports that a Chrome update deletes Google’s AI on the device, a reminder that AI features in consumer software can be added, moved, or removed as products evolve [3].
  • User experience shift: Across the stories, the practical question becomes where AI adds durable value—inside an app’s core workflow, inside a browser, or in search results that summarize the web [1][3][2].

Technical Details

  • On-device AI: On-device AI refers to models or AI components that run locally on a user’s hardware rather than in the cloud; Forbes’ Chrome item centers on Google removing an on-device AI component via an update [3].
  • Referral traffic: Referral traffic is the audience a site receives from sources like search engines and social platforms; PCMag highlights Chartbeat data showing declines from both channels for news sites [2].
  • AI feature scoping: Feature scoping is a product decision about what capabilities belong in an app; the I Ching app author describes removing AI to keep the experience simpler and more consistent [1].

Industry Impact

  • Independent apps: The I Ching app change shows a path where small developers differentiate by offering simpler, more predictable experiences rather than competing on generative features [1].
  • Digital publishing: If AI answers and changing discovery continue to reduce search and social referrals, publishers may need to rebalance toward direct traffic strategies and new monetization formats, as the Chartbeat-reported declines underscore [2].
  • Browser ecosystem: A Chrome update that removes an on-device AI component suggests AI capabilities in browsers may be modular and adjustable, affecting extension makers, developers, and user expectations about default AI tooling [3].
