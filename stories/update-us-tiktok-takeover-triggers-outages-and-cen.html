Update: US TikTok takeover triggers outages and censorship fears

Category: Technology

In the latest development after TikTok completed a deal to spin off its U.S. operations into a new American-run entity, the app has suffered a U.S. outage and ongoing glitches that have disrupted posting, view counts, and creator earnings displays [6][7][11][3]. TikTok has attributed the problems to a power outage at a U.S. data center that led to a “cascading systems failure,” saying it is making progress stabilizing service with its data center partner [7][6][11]. At the same time, some users and observers say the timing of errors affecting anti-ICE posts and messages referencing “Epstein” has fueled worries about content moderation shifting under the new ownership arrangement linked to President Trump’s deal to keep the app operating in the U.S. [2][12][10]. The bright spot: the turbulence is already energizing competition, with alternative short-form apps climbing app-store charts as users explore new places to post and be heard [10][8].

Key Points

  • Ownership details: ByteDance set up a separate American entity to run TikTok’s U.S. operations as a way to address U.S. concerns about data privacy and foreign control, and coverage of the deal has highlighted a joint-venture ownership roster that includes Oracle founder Larry Ellison and Dell Technologies’ Michael Dell [5][10].
  • User migration: UpScrolled, built by Palestinian Australian entrepreneur Issam Hijazi, has surged in downloads and reached No. 2 among free U.S. iPhone apps (behind ChatGPT), while other alternatives such as Skylight have also seen increased attention [10].
  • Censorship concerns: Researchers and experts told Ars Technica that users’ worries about a political “makeover” are understandable, arguing that repeated “bug” explanations do not fully resolve concerns about potential suppression of content critical of President Trump [2].
  • Creator impact: TikTok’s U.S. joint-venture account said creators may temporarily see ‘0’ views/likes and missing earnings due to display errors from server timeouts, while stating underlying engagement data remains safe [7].
  • User skepticism: Public figures amplified concerns, with Mashable reporting Billie Eilish sharing an Instagram story alleging TikTok was “silencing people,” alongside creators saying they struggled to upload anti-ICE posts [12].

Perspectives

TikTok (US operation): The company says a power outage at a U.S. data center triggered a cascading systems failure and that ongoing anomalies (including view/earnings displays) reflect technical timeouts, not lost engagement data [7][6].

Timeline

  • Last week: TikTok finalized U.S. operations spin-off into new entity [3].
  • Monday: TikTok reported data-center power outage and restoration work [7].
  • In a subsequent update: TikTok cited “cascading systems failure” causing multiple bugs [7].

Technical Details

  • Cascading systems failure: TikTok described the outage as a chain reaction triggered by a data-center power event, where downstream services experienced timeouts and failures that surfaced as app bugs [7][11].
  • Server timeouts: TikTok said server timeouts can make metrics like views/likes or earnings look missing even when underlying data remains intact [7].
  • Recommendation feed issues: Users have reported unusual “For You” behavior during the incident, alongside login and upload problems, highlighting how sensitive personalization systems are to infrastructure instability [7].

Industry Impact

  • Short-form video competition: App instability and user distrust can accelerate multi-homing (posting across platforms), giving newer entrants a chance to bootstrap network effects while users experiment with alternatives [10][8][common].
  • Cloud and datacenters: A single data-center power incident affecting TikTok and other apps it operates underscores how shared infrastructure dependencies can create correlated outages across products [7].
  • Creator economy tooling: Display glitches around views and earnings highlight why creators increasingly demand independent analytics, cross-posting tools, and diversified monetization streams to reduce platform risk [7][common].

Historical Background

U.S. officials and lawmakers have debated restricting or banning TikTok for years over national-security and data-access concerns tied to its China-based parent, ByteDance [5][3]. The latest U.S. restructuring created a separate American entity to run domestic operations, a move presented as a way to reduce foreign-control and privacy concerns [5].

Q&A

Q: What does a TikTok “U.S. spin-off” typically change for user data handling?

A: The reporting frames the restructuring as creating a separate American entity intended to address worries about Chinese government access to Americans’ data, but the practical impact depends on how data access, storage, and governance are implemented under the new structure [5][3].

Q: Why would an outage make recommendation feeds feel “off” even after logins work?

A: Recommendation systems depend on multiple backend services (ranking, feature stores, logging, and experimentation); if some components time out or lag after an incident, the app can load while personalization quality degrades [7][common].

Q: What kinds of claims have been cited as evidence of content suppression?

A: Articles describe reports of blocked or low-engagement posts about ICE activity and errors in direct messages mentioning “Epstein,” which TikTok has characterized as technical issues while critics say the pattern raises moderation questions [2][12].

Action Items

  • Verify whether TikTok metrics are delayed: If you see ‘0’ views/likes or missing earnings, wait and recheck later, since TikTok says these can be temporary display errors tied to server timeouts [7].
  • Download and test a backup platform: If TikTok performance affects your posting schedule, try alternatives such as UpScrolled or other emerging apps that have recently climbed in downloads, and compare reach and moderation policies firsthand [10].
  • Diversify content distribution: Cross-post short videos to at least one additional platform during instability periods to reduce reliance on a single app’s uptime and algorithm changes [8][common].
