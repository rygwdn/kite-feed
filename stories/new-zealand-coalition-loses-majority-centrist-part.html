New Zealand coalition loses majority; centrist party holds balance of power

Category: World

New Zealand’s governing National-led coalition lost its parliamentary majority in the final count of the Nov. 7 election, leaving it with 65 seats in a 130-seat Parliament, one short of the number needed to govern without additional support. The centrist Opportunity Party, which won seven seats, now holds the balance of power. The opposition Labour-led bloc has 58 seats and cannot form a majority even with Opportunity’s support [2][5][4]. The Electoral Commission’s count included 484,865 special votes cast overseas or outside voters’ electorates. National lost two seats compared with the preliminary tally; Labour gained two electorate seats and the Greens one. Prime Minister and National leader Christopher Luxon, who had ruled out working with Opportunity during the campaign, said formal talks would begin with his existing partners and Opportunity. Opportunity leader Qiulae Wong said her support depends on policy commitments and that she will not back a government with New Zealand First leader Winston Peters as foreign affairs minister [2][5][6]. Negotiations could extend into 2027. Luxon has warned that talks may be difficult, and failure to secure a governing or confidence-and-supply arrangement could lead to another election. A recount could still change the seat totals: applications are due by Dec. 3, and a recount could take a further five days [2][12].

Key Points

  • Vote shares: Labour received 29.88% of the party vote and National 28.35%, but National won more electorate seats; 2.8 million votes were cast [17][23].
  • Policy conditions: Opportunity’s stated priorities also include supermarket competition, political-donation limits, a 10-year cross-party health plan and ending bottom-trawling [2][20].
  • Narrow margins: Labour won Wairarapa from National by 81 votes and Hamilton East by 173, while the Greens took Wellington Bays from Labour by 1,610 [2].
  • Election readiness: Chief Electoral Officer Karl Le Quesne said the Electoral Commission is prepared to run another election if parties cannot form a government [5].
  • Voter turnout: Turnout was 77.2%, down from 78.2% in the 2023 election [14].

Perspectives
