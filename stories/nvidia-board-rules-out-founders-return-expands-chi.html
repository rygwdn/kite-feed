Nvidia board rules out founder’s return, expands chip-security oversight

Category: Technology

Nvidia’s board said it will not reinstate co-founder Jensen Huang as chief executive, rejecting his public offer to return hours after he made it. Huang resigned after the board began investigating his conduct and possible national-security implications involving the company’s business. Allegations about links to Chinese intelligence remain under investigation [19][15][65]. The board has created a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee to oversee security policies, export-control compliance and global trade risks [57][63]. Nvidia finance chief and interim CEO Colette Kress said the company would support employees through the transition [22]. The board is considering internal and external candidates for a permanent CEO and says the next leader must navigate national-security issues carefully [15][32]. Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter are scheduled to answer questions from three House committees in closed-door sessions on Dec. 1 and 2, respectively [16][63].

Key Points

  • Export controls: Kress said Nvidia is no longer shipping its Hopper data-center chips to China [47].
  • Compliance oversight: Nvidia has formed a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee to strengthen internal teams and work with outside experts on trade compliance. [77][91]
  • Capital commitments: Nvidia said it spent $28.1 billion on share repurchases in the quarter and had $227.3 billion remaining under its buyback authorization. [15]
  • CEO search: The board retained executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent chief executive [61][30].
  • Employee concerns: Nvidia developer technology director Nader Khalil warned the board that employees who value Huang’s leadership could leave, costing the company experienced staff [51].
  • SEC inquiry: The Wall Street Journal reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission was investigating whether Nvidia misled investors. [91]
  • Data center surge: Data-center revenue reached $107.6 billion, up 110% year over year and above analysts’ estimate of $101.85 billion. [95][57]
  • Product ramp: The new Vera Rubin platform generated nearly $20 billion in its first quarter of shipments, which CNBC described as Nvidia’s fastest product ramp. [134]
  • Petition limits: A campaign seeking Huang’s return had drawn more than 190,000 signatures, but signers’ listed affiliations were self-reported and not independently verified [41][30].
