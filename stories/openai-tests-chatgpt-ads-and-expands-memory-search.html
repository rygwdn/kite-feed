OpenAI tests ChatGPT ads and expands memory search

Category: Technology

OpenAI says it will begin testing advertising in ChatGPT for U.S. users on the Free and new Go tiers, while keeping higher-paid plans ad-free [1][3]. The company frames ads as a way to help fund broader access to more capable AI, and says ads will be separated from chatbot answers and that conversations will not be shared or sold to advertisers [1]. Separately, OpenAI has changed how ChatGPT can use a subscriber’s past conversations, enabling Plus and Pro users to ask questions that reference their full chat history without manually opening old threads [2].

Key Points

  • Ad placement: OpenAI says the ads are planned to appear at the bottom of queries, aiming to keep the main response area focused on the answer [3].
  • New Go tier: OpenAI introduced an $8 “ChatGPT Go” tier, positioned as one of the lower-cost plans alongside the free option [3].
  • Recent backlash: In December, users posted screenshots alleging irrelevant product-like suggestions in responses, and OpenAI characterized them as poorly timed “suggestions” rather than ads [1].
  • Pro user frustration: Some ChatGPT Pro subscribers paying $200 per month also complained that the experience felt off during the period when users suspected ads were appearing [1].
  • Ad experiments: Earlier reporting described internal ad concept tests, including layout and disclosure ideas intended to create “a new type of digital ads” that users would tolerate [1].

Technical Details

  • Full chat history search: Plus and Pro subscribers can ask questions that reference their entire ChatGPT chat history, including older conversations, without manually opening past threads [2].
  • Answer–ad separation: OpenAI says it will keep a separation between ChatGPT’s answers and advertising, emphasizing that responses should be “objectively useful” rather than ad-driven [1].
  • Ad disclosures and layout: OpenAI has been testing ad layouts and disclosure approaches internally to reduce user drop-off, according to reporting cited by Mashable [1].

Industry Impact

  • Digital advertising: Testing ads inside a chat interface signals continued experimentation with “conversational” ad formats and disclosure patterns, which could influence how other AI assistants monetize without disrupting responses [1].
  • Subscription packaging: The addition of an $8 Go tier broadens the pricing ladder and may encourage other AI products to add mid-tier plans that balance cost, features, and monetization [3][common].
  • User privacy expectations: OpenAI’s promise not to share or sell conversations to advertisers sets a benchmark users may begin to demand from other AI services that introduce ad-supported experiences [1][common].

Scientific Significance

  • Human-computer interaction: The move highlights a classic HCI challenge for AI assistants: preserving perceived neutrality and user trust while introducing monetization elements like ads and disclosures [1][common].
  • Personalization trade-offs: Searching across a user’s full chat history can improve continuity and usefulness, but it also makes privacy controls, data retention choices, and transparency more central to safe product design [2][common].

Historical Background

ChatGPT has long used a freemium approach, offering a free plan alongside paid tiers that add features or capacity [3]. As AI systems scaled, industry discussion has increasingly focused on funding high compute costs through subscriptions, enterprise offerings, and (for consumer products) advertising [common].
