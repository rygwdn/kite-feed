Australia's Northern Star rejects Gold Fields' $27 billion bid

Category: Business

Northern Star Resources, Australia’s largest gold miner, said Monday, Sept. 28, that it had rejected an unsolicited takeover proposal from South African rival Gold Fields. The offer valued Northern Star at A$38.7 billion ($27.1 billion), with Gold Fields proposing A$7.25 in cash plus 0.3125 of a new Gold Fields share for each Northern Star share [4][9]. Northern Star’s board called the proposal opportunistic and said it undervalued the company [9].

Primary Source: View original article

We see this combination as creating a stronger platform that can deliver value sooner

— Mike Fraser, Gold Fields chief executive (PerthNow)

Key Points

  • Premium dispute: Northern Star said the implied A$27 per share represented a 14%–22% control premium to its prior close, below the 30% minimum it said was customary for Australian takeovers [9].
  • Share response: Northern Star shares rose more than 9% on Monday, reaching an intraday high of A$24.64, up 11% [1][9].
  • Projected savings: Gold Fields chief executive Mike Fraser said a combination of the miners could generate about US$4 billion to US$5 billion in cost savings [11].

Perspectives

Elliott Investment Management: The activist investor, which holds about 5.6% of Northern Star, had sought board changes and a strategic review that could include a sale; partner John Pike said the board should engage with a genuine buyer and seek to maximize shareholder value [9].

Sources: Yonhap News Agency

Gold Fields: Fraser said the company was disappointed Northern Star’s board would not engage with its proposal, but Gold Fields remained willing to hold discussions [6][11].

Sources: Moneyweb

Business Angle

  • Northern Star argued that the proposal would expose investors to offshore jurisdictional risk [10].
