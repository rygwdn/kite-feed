Update: Australia grants visas to seven Iranian delegates

Category: World

Australia has granted humanitarian visas to two more people linked to Iran’s visiting women’s football delegation, bringing the total allowed to stay to seven, Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said [2][8][11][7]. The group was in Australia for an Asian Cup tournament when fears for their safety rose after Iranian state media criticised them for not singing the national anthem and later labelled the women “traitors” [6][7][15][9]. Australian officials said some members had been sheltered in a “safe house,” but later moved them after one member changed her mind about staying in Australia and shared the location with the Iranian embassy [13][9][1]. The visas offer immediate protection for those who remain, but other players and staff have returned—or plan to return—to Iran, underscoring that people are making different choices amid pressure and uncertainty [1][13][10].

I made them the same offer that I made the five players the night before.

— Tony Burke (Al-Monitor (Reuters))

Key Points

  • Who received visas: Reuters and other outlets said the five initial humanitarian visas included captain Zahra Ghanbari and players Mona Hamoudi, Fatemeh Pasandideh, Atefeh Ramezanizadeh and Zahra Sarbali [17][10][12].
  • Security relocation: Burke said officials moved the group after one delegation member reconsidered staying—after speaking with teammates planning to return to Iran—and then disclosed the safe-house location to the Iranian embassy [9][13][1].
  • Activist involvement: The BBC reported that activists helped contact the women as they tried to evade minders while seeking protection in Australia [14].
  • Public flashpoints: Footage described by Michael West showed players leaving under police guard, with protesters trying to block the team bus and demonstrators gathering at an airport [10].

Perspectives

Australian Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke: Burke said the newly issued visas came with the same terms as the five granted the night before. He added that officials relocated those seeking protection after a delegation member changed her mind and passed the safe-house location to Iran’s embassy [2][8][9].
