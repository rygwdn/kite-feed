Anthropic retracts IPO pricing draft as investors press for lower valuation

Category: AI

Anthropic, the developer of the Claude AI models, withdrew pricing details for its initial public offering (IPO) that it had emailed to investment banks on Monday, Nov. 9, saying the figures were inaccurate and it would send an updated filing. The draft described about 867 million shares at $70 to $80 each, implying proceeds of up to $69.3 billion. The company did not identify the errors, and those terms remain unconfirmed [19][24][33]. Investors were reconsidering the company’s valuation after a sharp selloff in AI-related stocks. Some prospective buyers were discussing valuations of $1.1 trillion to $1.3 trillion, below the roughly $1.5 trillion previously seen as the high end and the $2 trillion discussed earlier [16][58]. Anthropic’s latest filing reported nearly $17 billion in revenue for the quarter ended Sept. 30, though quarterly growth had slowed [19]. Anthropic was still expected to begin marketing the offering on Tuesday, Nov. 10, with pricing and a Nasdaq debut targeted for the following week. The company has not announced a change to that timetable [19][5]. Its listing plans also include a separate proposed $26 billion private placement from seven technology companies, though it is unclear whether those terms will change [19][3].

Key Points

  • Share allocation: The withdrawn draft allocated about 843.3 million shares to Anthropic and 23.3 million to existing shareholders, so the maximum proceeds of $69.3 billion included both company and shareholder sales [19][3].
  • Tech investors: The draft separately named Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC for a proposed $26 billion private placement at the IPO price; whether the correction affects that commitment remains unclear [19][20].
  • Revenue growth: Revenue rose to $16.96 billion in the third quarter from $11.56 billion in the second, a 47% sequential increase, down from 144% growth in the prior quarter [26].
  • Investor backdrop: The Nasdaq 100 fell 14% over the period described by Investing.com, while the VIX briefly topped 25, a level Renaissance Capital strategist Matt Kennedy said companies typically avoid when launching IPOs [26][9].
