Paramount Skydance clinches $110bn Warner Bros takeover deal

Category: Business

Paramount Skydance, led by David Ellison and backed by the Ellison family, has agreed to buy Warner Bros. Discovery in a deal valued at about $110–$111 billion, ending a months-long contest for one of Hollywood’s biggest collections of studios, TV networks, and streaming assets [4][5][3][1]. If the deal closes, it would unite major Warner properties with Paramount’s portfolio, putting a wide range of film, TV, and streaming brands and franchises under one roof [3][1]. The turning point came when Netflix dropped out, saying the numbers no longer worked at the price needed to match Paramount Skydance’s offer. That cleared the way for the Ellison-led group to move toward closing, pending approvals [4][5][11][14][10].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Offer terms: Paramount Skydance offered $31 per share for Warner Bros. Discovery, a price Netflix chose not to match [12][13].
  • What’s in the combined portfolio: Firstpost says the combined company would include major TV brands such as CNN, CBS, HBO, and Nickelodeon, plus franchises including Harry Potter, Game of Thrones, the DC Universe, Mission: Impossible, and SpongeBob SquarePants [1].
  • Bidding arc: Coverage describes a roughly three-month fight between Netflix and Paramount Skydance for control of Warner Bros. Discovery [14][6].
  • Inside messaging: After the deal became official, David Ellison sent employees a memo meant to steady staff and lay out what comes next for the combined company [2].
  • Strategic framing: The Financial Times says the effort, dubbed “Project Warrior,” reflects the Ellison father-and-son team’s view that more scale is necessary to compete with tech giants reshaping Hollywood [8].

Perspectives

David Ellison: In a memo to staff after the takeover became official, Ellison emphasized integration and the company’s next phase.

Sources: Business Insider

Financial Times analysis: FT frames the push as a survival play—building enough scale to compete with deep-pocketed tech rivals that increasingly control entertainment distribution.

Sources: Financial Times

Deal observers: The NYT says winning the bid is just the start, with the biggest risks in executing a complex integration and getting two large media empires to run as one.

Historical Background

Warner Bros. Discovery was created in 2022 through the merger of WarnerMedia and Discovery, bringing a major Hollywood studio and a large TV portfolio under one corporate parent [common]. These talks come amid a broader shift in entertainment, where streaming scale and global distribution increasingly determine which companies can fund premium content and keep audiences [3][8].
