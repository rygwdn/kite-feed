Monash study finds heat shifts honeybee behavior patterns

Category: Science

New research reported by Monash University suggests rising temperatures and expanding urban conditions can change how European honeybees behave, while the studied native Australian bees showed little to no behavioral shift under the same temperature changes [1]. A separate report highlights that prolonged extreme summer heat can overwhelm honeybees’ ability to regulate hive temperature, which researchers link to hive stress and population declines [3]. Together, the articles point to a hopeful throughline for conservation: some native pollinators may prove more heat-tolerant in certain contexts, while managed honeybee colonies may need targeted help during heat extremes [1][3].
