Ex-Cambridge professor Jason Arday found dead at 41

Category: World

Jason Arday, the British sociologist who resigned last week from the University of Cambridge after weeks of plagiarism allegations and scrutiny of his academic record, was found dead Friday at age 41 in Battersea, south London [10][16][15][14]. The Metropolitan Police said officers were called by the London Ambulance Service after a 41-year-old man was found unresponsive. He was pronounced dead at the scene, and police said the death was being treated as unexpected but was not believed to be suspicious [16][15][42][54]. Arday became Cambridge’s youngest Black professor in 2023, when he was appointed professor of sociology of education. He later faced allegations that parts of his 2015 PhD thesis had been plagiarised, along with questions about claims regarding his achievements [10][14][7][1]. He denied plagiarism, and Liverpool John Moores University, which awarded his doctorate, had not upheld plagiarism allegations. Cambridge had announced a new investigation into his qualifications and honorary appointments shortly before his resignation [1][14][31][25]. In a statement issued through Simon & Schuster U.K., Arday’s family said they were “in shock” and described him as a father, partner, brother, uncle and son who had faced sustained abuse and misinformation [10][15][16]. Cambridge Vice-Chancellor Deborah Prentice said the university was “desperately saddened” and offered sympathy to Arday’s family and friends [7][10][42].

  • Resignation wording: In his resignation statement, Arday said leaving Cambridge should not be mistaken for acceptance of the narratives around him and described the scrutiny as beyond scholarly disagreement [7][18][19].
  • Arday denied plagiarism but admitted errors in his early work, explaining that his autism led him to rely on mimicry to process information [29][19].
