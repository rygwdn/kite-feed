B.C. premier orders bridge study, SOGI removal and office return

Category: Canada

British Columbia Premier Lorne Doerkson on Wednesday ordered a design process for a bridge to replace the George Massey Tunnel, the removal of SOGI 123 school materials on sexual orientation and gender identity, and a phased return to the office for provincial employees. The orders follow mandate letters calling for the reversal of several policies of the previous NDP government [4][10][1]. The notice of intent asks the tunnel’s current contractor to develop the bridge option. Doerkson said cabinet would review the costs before deciding and that he hoped to make a final choice before Christmas [4]. Doerkson said SOGI 123 resources would be removed from government websites immediately, with school districts asked to remove them from schools [4][10]. He also directed ministries to plan a phased return to the office, saying employees able to work from government offices would return five days a week [4]. Other mandate-letter priorities include publicly funded treatment through another provider when waits exceed medically recommended limits, and an independent review of provincial finances [1][11].

Our responsibility is to ensure taxpayers get the best value for their money.

— Premier Lorne Doerkson (CBC News)

Key Points

  • Health changes: The health minister was also directed to conduct a 100-day system review, recruit doctors and nurses, and keep rural emergency departments and maternity wards open [10].
  • Energy targets: The new environment minister is directed to repeal CleanBC and electric-vehicle sales targets, while the energy minister is directed to triple natural-gas production by 2035 [6][10].
  • Mining permits: The mandate calls for fixed approval timelines for mines and exploration permits; Association for Mineral Exploration CEO Todd Stone said the sector welcomed the push to speed up permitting [9].

Perspectives

BC Government Employees Union (BCGEU): President Paul Finch said the union was disappointed it was not consulted, urged the government to compare productivity under remote and office work, and argued that added office costs should be considered before implementation.

Sources: CHEK News

B.C. NDP interim leader Jennifer Whiteside: Whiteside said the mandate letters lacked clarity and warned that British Columbians would not want the previous government’s work on housing, health care, affordability and climate resilience undone.

Sources: Vancouver Sun

Thomas Green, David Suzuki Foundation: Green argued that expanding gas production while cutting climate measures would benefit a small number of mostly foreign companies, and said eliminating industrial carbon pricing could expose B.C. gas to higher costs in European markets.

Sources: Canada’s National Observer

Association for Mineral Exploration, B.C.: CEO Todd Stone said the sector wants a framework to measure permitting progress, adding that the industry had made only modest progress under the previous government.

Sources: Vancouver Sun

Timeline

  • 12 November 2026: Doerkson said office-based workers should return, with possible rural exceptions [8]
  • 20 November 2026: Doerkson and his cabinet were sworn into office [1]
  • 25 November 2026: Mandate letters directed a 2027 increase in the basic personal tax exemption to $32,000 from $13,216 [1]

Industry Impact

  • Public service workplaces: The return-to-office plan affects roughly 36,000 civil servants, with the government asking ministries to assess office capacity and accommodations as they prepare timelines [8].
  • Mineral exploration: B.C. exploration companies invested a record $751 million in projects in 2025, and the industry association said it wants measurable progress on permitting reforms [9].

Historical Background

The previous B.C. NDP government planned to replace the George Massey Tunnel with another tunnel, rather than a bridge [4]. The SOGI 123 anti-bullying resource was introduced by the government of former B.C. premier Christy Clark [3].
