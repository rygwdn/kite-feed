Manitoba seeks youth social media and chatbot ban

Category: Canada

Manitoba Premier Wab Kinew says his government plans to ban children from using social media and artificial intelligence chatbots, which would make Manitoba the first province in Canada to pursue such a measure. In announcing the move, he pointed to Australia as a model [2][5][6][4]. He unveiled the plan at the Manitoba NDP’s spring fundraising dinner but did not give a timeline for introducing legislation, and reports differ on the age threshold, with some saying no age was specified [2][6]. Kinew said the proposal is meant to protect children from online harms, arguing that social media platforms and AI chatbots can be addictive and can worsen anxiety, depression, and childhood development outcomes [4][5]. The announcement has already sparked a broader Canadian debate. Coverage in Ontario has asked whether other provinces should follow Manitoba’s lead, while some young people quoted in related reporting said broad bans are not always the right answer [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Australia’s social media restrictions for children have quickly become an international reference point, and Manitoba is already pointing to them as it discusses its own proposal [2].

Key Points

  • Australia model: Kinew explicitly compared Manitoba’s proposal with Australia’s approach [2][7].
  • First province: If the law passes, Manitoba would become the first Canadian province to bar children from social media and AI chatbots [2][6][5].
  • Details pending: Key parts of the policy are still unsettled in public reporting, including the exact age cutoff and when the government will introduce legislation [2][6][1].
  • Debate widens: Ontario-focused coverage has already framed the proposal as a possible template for other provinces, suggesting a Manitoba initiative could shape digital-policy debates across Canada [1].
