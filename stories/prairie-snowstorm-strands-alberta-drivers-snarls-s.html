Prairie snowstorm strands Alberta drivers, snarls Saskatchewan roads

Category: Canada

A late-April snowstorm swept across parts of Alberta and Saskatchewan on April 24, bringing heavy snow and strong winds that stranded drivers, closed highways and made travel hazardous across the Prairies [1][4][5][2][3]. In Alberta, motorists were stranded overnight on highways north of Edmonton as RCMP and emergency crews worked to reach them with food, fuel and medicine, while Calgary cancelled its Parade of Wonders because of the weather [1][5]. In Saskatchewan, the same system made roads treacherous and contributed to dozens of collisions in Saskatoon. Forecasters said some northern areas had already seen more than a metre of snow over a series of April storms, with gradual melting expected over the following week [2][3]. The storm was an abrupt swing after warmer spring conditions, underscoring how quickly Prairie weather can turn even late in the season and why officials urged people to avoid unnecessary travel until conditions improved [3][5][1].

Did you know: Saskatchewan was hit by its fourth snowfall of April, even after earlier high temperatures in the province [3].

Key Points

  • Collision spike: Saskatoon police reported 52 collisions from Friday morning to evening as snowfall made roads slick across the city [2].
  • Northern totals: Some parts of northern Saskatchewan recorded more than a metre of snow during April's repeated storms, with melting expected to unfold gradually over about a week [3].
  • Wind damage: High winds toppled trees in parts of the Prairies as the storm brought blowing snow, reduced visibility and difficult driving conditions [1][4].
  • Rescue effort: The Royal Canadian Mounted Police said crews were working to reach stranded motorists in Alberta and deliver supplies including food, fuel and medicine [5].
