House passes bill aimed at making companies cover AI data-center costs

Category: AI

The U.S. House passed the bipartisan Ratepayer Protection Act, 417-3, on Sept. 16. The bill directs state utility regulators to consider standards that would require large data centers to cover the added costs of power generation, transmission and local grid upgrades built to serve them. It addresses concerns that rapidly expanding AI infrastructure could raise electricity bills for households and small businesses [6][4][13]. The bill would not require states to adopt the standards; it would require regulators to evaluate them. It now moves to the Senate, where Republican leaders had not committed to scheduling a vote before lawmakers leave Washington to campaign for the November midterm elections [10][9]. Republican Rep. Gabe Evans of Colorado and Democratic Rep. Kathy Castor of Florida introduced the legislation. President Trump has continued to support data-center development as important to U.S. AI leadership [7][13].

Thou shalt not pass on costs to rate payer

— Rep. Gabe Evans (Colorado Public Radio)

Key Points

  • House procedure: House GOP leaders fast-tracked the bill under a procedure requiring a two-thirds majority for passage [6].
  • Review deadlines: State regulators would begin reviewing the proposed standards within one year and decide whether to adopt them within two years [11].
  • Financial safeguards: The bill also calls for assurances that data-center developers can meet their obligations and will not leave unfinished projects or infrastructure costs behind [8].
  • Public opposition: A University of Massachusetts Amherst poll cited in the reporting found that 11% of Americans supported building an AI data center in their community [7].

Perspectives

Rep. Gabe Evans: Evans said the proposal should establish a federal standard for recovering generation and transmission costs from data centers, while allowing state-specific approaches because a single solution would not fit every utility system [8].

Sources: Colorado Public Radio

President Donald Trump: Trump has argued that data centers make people and states wealthy and described them as “the oil of the next 20, 25 years,” while opposing mandatory regulation of their energy needs [7][14].

Sources: The News International, RTS

House lawmakers: House GOP leaders fast-tracked the bill, while several Republicans facing tough reelection bids signed on as cosponsors in recent weeks [6].

Sources: CBS News

Technical Details

  • 100-megawatt classification: The bill identifies facilities with maximum demand of 100 megawatts or more at a site or campus as “large-load customers,” a threshold that determines which projects face regulatory review [11][7].
  • Cost allocation: The proposed standards would assign incremental generation, transmission and distribution-upgrade costs to the high-load customer rather than spreading them across the wider rate base [11][8].
  • Scope of covered customers: The proposed framework would address large electricity consumers, including data centers, rather than being limited to AI facilities [13].

Historical Background

President Trump's Ratepayer Protection Pledge was presented as a voluntary, nonbinding commitment under which large technology companies would avoid shifting data-center energy costs to the public [8][7]. It was backed by Google, Microsoft, Amazon and OpenAI [7][8].

Business Angle

  • Data-center developers could face higher capital requirements if states make them fund generation and transmission expansions [8].
  • State-by-state implementation may create uneven economics for new AI infrastructure projects across the United States [10][4].
