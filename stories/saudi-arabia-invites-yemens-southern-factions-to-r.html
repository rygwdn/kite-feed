Saudi Arabia invites Yemen’s southern factions to Riyadh talks

Category: World

Saudi Arabia has called on Yemen’s southern political and armed factions to attend talks in Riyadh, as fighting and political moves by southern separatists raise fears of a broader split in the anti-Houthi camp [1][4][10][16][13][12][6]. The appeal follows Saudi-led airstrikes against UAE-backed separatist forces in the south that killed people, according to multiple reports, amid escalating confrontation between partners who once fought together against the Houthis [2][9][11][7]. At the same time, the UAE said it had withdrawn all its troops from Yemen and urged restraint, while the Southern Transitional Council (STC) announced steps toward an independent south, injecting fresh uncertainty into Yemen’s already fragmented conflict [3][5][18][17][15].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Yemen is widely described as the Arabian Peninsula’s poorest country [10].

Key Points

  • Independence roadmap: The STC said it is launching a two-year transition toward independence—pointing to 2028 as the target—and warned it could move faster if attacks continue [15][7].
  • UAE de-escalation line: The UAE said it was engaging “with restraint, coordination, and a deliberate commitment to de-escalation” as the southern crisis accelerates [17][19].
  • Hadramout flashpoint: In Hadramout province, a Saudi-backed governor described “peaceful” efforts to retake military bases that separatists had abandoned, highlighting how local control of facilities is part of the current standoff [11].
  • Alliance fracture: Several outlets frame the clashes as exposing a widening rift inside the anti-Houthi coalition, with Saudi Arabia and the UAE backing rival Yemeni forces despite years of alignment against the Houthis [2][8][4].
  • Constitution step: The STC announced a constitution for what it describes as an independent southern state and called on other factions to accept the move, though reports note uncertainty over whether it can be implemented [3][5].

Perspectives

Saudi Arabia: Riyadh is urging a broad conference in Riyadh to bring together southern factions and discuss “just solutions” for the southern issue, presenting talks as a path to de-escalation [10].

Technical Details

  • Southern Transitional Council (STC): The STC is a UAE-backed separatist movement in southern Yemen that says it is pursuing independence and has now announced a draft constitutional framework to underpin that goal [3][5].
  • Anti-Houthi alliance: Multiple reports describe the anti-Houthi coalition as fractured in government-held areas, with Saudi Arabia and the UAE backing rival Yemeni partners despite sharing opposition to the Houthis [2][8][19].
  • Troop withdrawal: The UAE says it has withdrawn all its troops from Yemen, a move it casts as part of a restrained approach amid the current escalation [18][17].

Historical Background

Yemen existed as two states—North Yemen and South Yemen—before unifying in 1990, and the idea of a separate southern state has resurfaced at different moments since then [7][common]. Since Yemen’s civil war intensified after the Houthi takeover of Sanaa in 2014 and the Saudi-led intervention in 2015, Saudi Arabia and the UAE have backed different Yemeni factions even while opposing the Houthis [common][4][6].

Q&A

Q: What practical steps would be required for the STC’s constitution to take effect on the ground?

A: Reports note it is unclear whether the STC’s constitution and independence declaration can be implemented, implying that acceptance by other factions and control of institutions and territory would be decisive [3].

Q: Which parts of southern Yemen are at the center of the current standoff?

A: Coverage points to fighting and political contestation in the south, including references to developments in Hadramout province and activity around bases there [11][19].

Q: How are Saudi and UAE positions being coordinated—if at all—during the escalation?

A: The UAE says it is acting with coordination and a deliberate commitment to de-escalation, while Saudi Arabia is pushing for a broad Riyadh dialogue forum for southern factions [17][13].
