B.C. wildfire prompts evacuation orders for nearly 200 properties

Category: Canada

Officials on Saturday expanded evacuation orders for areas south of the Pear Lake wildfire, British Columbia’s largest active wildfire, with orders from the Squamish-Lillooet and Thompson-Nicola regional districts together covering nearly 200 properties [3]. The affected areas include properties near Highway 99 in the Marble Canyon area and west of the Highway 97 junction, while the Pear Lake fire has burned more than 1,600 square kilometres [3][2][5]. The B.C. Wildfire Service warned that unstable weather could bring high winds and dry lightning across the southern and central Interior on Saturday, conditions that can accelerate fire growth and complicate suppression work [1][2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Regional orders: The Thompson-Nicola Regional District ordered evacuations for two additional Highway 99 properties west of Cache Creek, raising the number of evacuated properties in the Bonaparte Plateau and Blue Sky Country areas to 81 [2][5].
  • First Nation: The Ts'kw'aylaxw First Nation issued evacuation orders for several of its reserves as regional governments expanded their own orders and alerts [3].
  • Return conditions: Evacuation orders remained in place for properties west of Clinton, and Summerland residents faced a boil-water notice during phased returns [3].

Perspectives

Thompson-Nicola Regional District: The district expanded its evacuation order along Highway 99 west of Cache Creek and added four properties to an evacuation alert farther south.

Sources: The Canadian Press via Lethbridge Herald

Squamish-Lillooet Regional District: The district expanded an evacuation order south of the fire to include properties near Highway 99 in the Marble Canyon area. Updated orders and alerts cover more than 100 properties in the Pavilion and Marble Lake areas.

Sources: CBC News

Humanitarian Impact

More than 1,500 Bonaparte Plateau properties remain under alert and could be ordered to evacuate if conditions worsen [3][2][5].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Evacuation orders and alerts have expanded because of the Pear Lake wildfire. Avoid travel into evacuation-order zones and check the relevant regional district notice before driving Highway 99 in the affected area [3][2][5].
  • Weather: Delay non-essential trips through fire-affected Interior routes during thunderstorm warnings, and keep fuel, water and communication backups ready [1][2][5].

Action Items

  • Check your evacuation status: Use the Thompson-Nicola, Squamish-Lillooet and Ts'kw'aylaxw First Nation emergency notices to confirm whether your property is under an order or alert before travelling [3].
  • Prepare to leave quickly: If you are in an alert area, gather medications, identification, pets, chargers and essential documents so you can leave immediately if an order follows.
