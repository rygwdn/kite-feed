CES 2026 opens with AI-first gadgets, TVs, robotics

Category: Technology

CES 2026 is underway in Las Vegas from January 6–9, bringing global brands, startups, and media together to preview consumer tech slated to roll out through 2026, with artificial intelligence positioned as a default feature across categories [7][10][14]. Early coverage describes a show floor full of “AI-infused” consumer gadgetry, with reporters tracking announcements and hands-on demos throughout the week [11][4][8]. The tone from multiple outlets is that CES is again acting as a fast-moving snapshot of where home tech, displays, chips, and robotics are headed next [2][15][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: CES includes both the main show and preview events like CES Unveiled, which lets exhibitors show products before the exhibit halls open [18].

Key Points

  • Tri-fold phone: Samsung previewed a Galaxy Z TriFold concept that folds three ways, expanding from a 6.5-inch closed form to a 10-inch opened display aimed at tablet-like multitasking and media use [1].
  • Micro RGB TVs: TV makers highlighted “Micro RGB” LCD sets that use red, green, and blue LEDs (rather than white backlights) to improve brightness and color accuracy; Engadget notes contrast still differs from OLED and Micro LED because pixels aren’t individually switchable [2][3].
  • Vision AI features: Samsung outlined “Vision AI Companion” ideas for TVs, including on-screen Q&A, content recommendations, recipe suggestions from what’s shown, and picture/sound personalization modes [1].
  • Robot and XR push: Preview coverage points to more domestic robots and robotics-for-manufacturing demos, alongside talk of extended reality as a training environment for robots and “physical AI” systems [10][7].
  • Playful storage: PC-focused CES coverage also surfaced whimsical designs in practical gear, like SanDisk portable SSDs styled as oversized Crayola crayons and other novelty-themed drives shown at a Pepcom showcase [15].

Technical Details

  • Micro RGB backlighting: Micro RGB TVs use separate red, green, and blue LEDs in the backlight, aiming for brighter output and improved color accuracy compared with white-LED Mini LED designs [2][3].
  • Local dimming limits: Because Micro RGB is still a backlit LCD approach, Engadget notes it can’t fully match OLED or Micro LED contrast where individual pixels can be switched off [2].
  • Tri-fold form factor: A tri-fold phone adds an extra hinge/fold compared with book-style foldables, letting a pocketable device open into a larger, tablet-like screen for multitasking and media [1].

Industry Impact

  • TV manufacturing: Micro RGB’s emergence as a CES talking point signals new component and tuning demands (RGB LED backlight design, processing, calibration), potentially shifting supplier priorities within the LCD TV ecosystem [2][3].
  • Smart home UX: TV-centric assistants and automation updates show how living-room screens are being positioned as control hubs for recommendations and household routines, which could increase platform lock-in among device ecosystems [1].
  • Robotics pipeline: The emphasis on domestic robots, manufacturing robotics, and XR training environments hints at broader experimentation in “physical AI,” potentially expanding partnerships between consumer brands, industrial firms, and AI platform providers [10][7].

Historical Background

CES (the Consumer Electronics Show) is an annual Las Vegas trade show where companies preview products and platform shifts for the year ahead [common]. The 2026 event follows a 2025 show that drew more than 141,000 attendees from over 150 countries, regions, and territories, setting expectations for similar scale this year [10].
