NASA awards $600 million for 2028 Moon landings

Category: Science

NASA awarded nearly $600 million to three private companies for four robotic Moon landings planned for late 2028 under the Commercial Lunar Payload Services program [3]. All four landers will carry the same three science instruments, a repeated payload design that Science Blog said supports NASA’s Moon Base Program [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Apollo 13 set the human distance-from-Earth record by accident in 1970 after an explosion forced an emergency loop around the Moon [2].

Key Points

  • Contract split: Astrobotic took two missions under the award, while Firefly Aerospace and Intuitive Machines each received one late-2028 delivery [3].
  • Payload strategy: NASA assigned the same three science instruments to all four landers rather than distributing different payload sets across the missions [3].
  • Booked missions: The newly assigned flights bring NASA’s booked commercial lunar surface deliveries to 17 across multiple providers [3].
  • Crewed context: Artemis II flew its crewed lunar flyby in April 2026, marking the first time astronauts traveled beyond low Earth orbit since Apollo [2].
