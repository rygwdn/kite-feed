AI-designed lung drug shows early anti-aging signals

Category: AI

Rentosertib, a drug candidate developed with artificial intelligence by Insilico Medicine, was associated with lower estimates of biological age in a Nature Biotechnology study [2][1][6]. The drug was designed to treat idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, a rare chronic lung disease that scars and stiffens the lungs over time; researchers also examined whether the same biology overlaps with aging-related processes [2][1][6]. The 12-week phase 2a trial enrolled 42 patients assigned to three dosing groups and a placebo group. Researchers tracked lung function and blood-based biological age measures [1][6]. Six proteomic aging-clock models estimated lower biological age among patients who received rentosertib, while placebo or untreated patients saw biological age increase or remain unchanged [1][6]. Digital Trends reported that the data do not show whether the change reflects a direct anti-aging effect or a byproduct of improved lung health [6].

Primary Source: View original article

Technical Details

  • Split-dose response: The 30 mg twice-daily regimen produced a stronger signal than the 60 mg once-daily regimen, suggesting the dosing schedule may affect the measured biomarker response [6].

Scientific Significance

  • Evidence level: The finding comes from an early biomarker analysis in a 42-person phase 2a trial; it does not show that the drug slows mortality, morbidity or real-world aging outcomes [1][6].
