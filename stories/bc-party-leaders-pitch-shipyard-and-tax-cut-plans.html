B.C. party leaders pitch shipyard and tax-cut plans before Oct. 24 vote

Category: Canada

British Columbia’s three major party leaders presented competing economic plans to business representatives in Vancouver on Oct. 13, ahead of the province’s Oct. 24 election. NDP Leader David Eby promoted public investment and job supports, interim Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson argued for lower taxes and faster approvals, and Green Leader Emily Lowan called for a different industrial strategy [3][4]. A Greater Vancouver Board of Trade survey found nearly three-quarters of members were very concerned about provincial finances, and almost four in five said the economy was on the wrong track [3]. A Research Co. poll released Tuesday put the Conservatives at 44% among decided likely voters, the NDP at 42% and the Greens at 8% [3]. The leaders were scheduled to debate Wednesday, Oct. 14, before the Oct. 24 vote [4][3].

Key Points

  • Shipyard proposal: Eby said an NDP government would work with Ottawa on a shipyard in B.C. to build ferries and other vessels, such as icebreakers [4][3].
  • Tax commitments: Doerkson promised to permanently cancel a proposed expansion of the provincial sales tax, exempt machinery, equipment and software from PST for three years, and establish a 120-day approval process for capital projects over $250 million [4].
  • Green proposals: Lowan proposed an industrial strategy to reduce reliance on natural-resource extraction and a tax on the ultra-wealthy to help pay down the deficit and fund universal child care [4][3].
  • Business concerns: The Board of Trade survey found 8% of respondents had already decided to move outside B.C. or reduce investment, while 23% were considering doing so [3].

Perspectives

David Eby: Eby warned that the province could not afford what he called the “chaos” and division of a Conservative government during a period of tariffs and trade wars [3].
