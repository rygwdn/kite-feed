Ontario liquor retailer adds police at 40-plus stores to deter theft

Category: Canada

The Liquor Control Board of Ontario (LCBO), the province’s liquor retailer, will deploy paid-duty police officers at more than 40 stores under a new campaign called Operation Secure Stores. The officers will work with local police in Toronto, Durham, Peel and York regions, while the LCBO adds guards and covert security personnel at stores it identifies as high-risk [2][5][4]. The LCBO says organized crime groups account for about 70 per cent of its theft-related losses and that existing security measures led to more than 2,000 arrests in the past year [1][3]. The operation will focus on repeat offenders and improve information-sharing with police; third-party guards will receive refresher training on preventing theft, handling tense situations and gathering evidence [4][5].

Key Points

  • Store network: The LCBO operates more than 690 stores carrying high-value goods, according to Global News [2].
  • Guard authority: The LCBO says its plainclothes security personnel can intervene in thefts and make arrests on site [5].
  • Seasonal rationale: The LCBO said the security push is intended to protect customers, staff and products as the holiday season approaches [5].
  • Earlier probe: Months earlier, Toronto police released the results of a widespread retail-theft investigation that led to hundreds of arrests. Video shown at the news conference showed masked people entering a Toronto LCBO, filling bags and suitcases, and leaving with them [6].

Perspectives

LCBO: Senior Director of Resource Protection Al Almeida said the campaign uses data analysis to direct security resources to stores where they are most needed [4].
