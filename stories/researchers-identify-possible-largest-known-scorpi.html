Researchers identify possible largest known scorpion, over one meter long

Category: Science

Researchers have identified Praearcturus gigas, a scorpion known from a fossil found in the United Kingdom dating to about 415 million years ago, as a candidate for the largest known scorpion. They estimated that the animal exceeded one meter in length. A new analysis combining CT scans with traditional fossil tracings resolved decades of uncertainty over its classification [1][2][4]. Paleontologist Richie Howard and colleagues infer that it hunted both on land and in water; terrestrial ecosystems were still developing at the time [1][3]. The researchers suggest that the absence of other large predators may have enabled its large size, while later competition may have contributed to its decline [1][4].

Primary Source: View original article

If this is the case, then Praearcturus is an example of an animal that likely returned to the water after its ancestors moved onto land.

— Greg Edgecombe, paleontologist at the Natural History Museum in the UK (ScienceAlert)

Key Points

  • Large pincers: Its pincers measured about 16 centimeters, longer than the bodies of many scorpion species alive today [1][4].
  • Possible sound: Ridge patterns on a limb may indicate stridulation—sound production by rubbing body parts together, a behavior used by some modern scorpions [1][2].
  • Fossil comparison: The team compared the British fossils with Canadian specimens of the ancient scorpion Eramoscorpius to place P. gigas within the scorpion family tree [1][4].

Technical Details

  • Aquatic adaptation: Fossils from Wales preserve flap-like epimera resembling structures in lobsters and crabs. Researchers cite these features as evidence that P. gigas could forage in water as well as on land [1][2].
