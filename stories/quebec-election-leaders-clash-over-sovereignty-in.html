Quebec election leaders clash over sovereignty in final debate

Category: Canada

The leaders of Quebec’s five main parties met in Montreal on Sept. 23 for the campaign’s final televised debate, moderated by Radio-Canada’s Patrice Roy. They debated the province’s future, the economy, taxes, public services, child care and health care. Immigration and the U.S. trade dispute also featured in the discussion [6][1][7]. Quebecers are due to vote Oct. 5 [6]. Sovereignty was a central point of confrontation. Parti Québécois Leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon reaffirmed that he would hold a referendum during a first PQ term, after U.S. President Donald Trump leaves office. Liberal Leader Charles Milliard said the election had become a referendum campaign, while St-Pierre Plamondon urged Quebecers not to fear a referendum [10][2].

Primary Source: View original article

It’s (former Quebec premier) Jacques Parizeau who said this, but don’t be afraid of your democracy, don’t be afraid of going to the polls, don’t be afraid to think about our future,

— Paul St-Pierre Plamondon (The Canadian Press)

Key Points

  • Immigration limits: The parties proposed annual immigration caps of 45,000 to 70,000 [6].
  • Debate-day protests: Supporters and protesters, including striking paramedics, pro-Palestinian activists and environmental groups, gathered outside the venue [12].
  • Referendum opposition: Conservative Leader Éric Duhaime said he would seek to block a sovereignty vote if elected and said 70 per cent of Quebecers oppose one [10][11].

Perspectives

Parti Québécois: St-Pierre Plamondon ruled out an alliance with another party after rivals said his ideas resembled those of Conservative Leader Éric Duhaime [1].

Historical Background

The 1995 Quebec referendum asked voters whether Quebec should become sovereign; the proposal was defeated [common].
