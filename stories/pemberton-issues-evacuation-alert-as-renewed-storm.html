Pemberton issues evacuation alert as renewed storms threaten B.C. communities

Category: Canada

Pemberton issued an evacuation alert for properties along Airport Road as another storm brought heavy rain to southwest British Columbia, where forecasters warned of flooding and landslides [2][1]. The village said high river flows and significant precipitation could cause flooding. Forecasts called for 150 to 200 millimetres of rain on western and central Vancouver Island, with some areas potentially exceeding 250 millimetres; flood watches also cover parts of the South Coast, the Central Coast and the Fraser Valley [2][5]. The provincial transportation ministry warned drivers to expect pooling water, debris and road closures, particularly on Highway 99 through the Sea-to-Sky corridor, Highway 1 in the Fraser Valley and Fraser Canyon, and the Malahat [7]. Forecasters expected the heaviest rain through Wednesday, easing Thursday, with another system reaching Vancouver Island on Friday [3][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Snowmelt risk: The River Forecast Centre said warm temperatures through Wednesday could melt shallow snowpacks at higher elevations, adding water to rivers already affected by rainfall [2].
  • Road response: The province said contractors were clearing debris from roads and drainage infrastructure, while transportation crews planned to increase patrols during the storm [7].
  • Wider weather: A high-pressure ridge was forecast to push temperatures in Alberta and Saskatchewan 7 to 10 degrees above seasonal levels, with parts of the territories 10 to 15 degrees warmer [3].
  • Alert levels: Environment Canada’s orange warnings indicate severe weather likely to cause significant damage or disruption; yellow warnings cover other parts of Vancouver Island and B.C. [5][2].

Perspectives

Environment Canada meteorologist Michael Gismondi described the rainfall as fairly typical for the season and said he did not expect catastrophic impacts, while warning that flooding remained possible [1].
