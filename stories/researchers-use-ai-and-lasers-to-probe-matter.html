Researchers use AI and lasers to probe matter

Category: Science

Researchers at GSI/FAIR and international collaborators developed a machine-learning model to study how heavy elements form during violent astrophysical events, including neutron star mergers [1]. The team incorporated a deep-learning neural network into hydrodynamic simulations to model energy released during r-process nucleosynthesis, according to findings published in Physical Review D [1]. A separate materials-science report said scientists used lasers to make atoms vibrate trillions of times per second in research on materials for computing [3]. The work centers on physically squeezing or stretching a grid of atoms, a method that can change how a material conducts electricity or stores magnetic information [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Element origins: The r-process produces many elements heavier than iron when atomic nuclei rapidly absorb neutrons that later transform into protons [1].
  • Computing barrier: Detailed simulations of heavy-element formation remain computationally challenging because astrophysical events such as supernovae and neutron star mergers involve extreme energy and neutron-rich conditions [1].

Technical Details

  • Neural-network hydrodynamics: The GSI/FAIR-led team embedded a deep learning neural network directly into hydrodynamic simulations to estimate r-process energy release rather than treating the nuclear-energy component outside the flow model [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Astrophysics modeling: The machine learning model gives researchers a way to include r-process heating in simulations of neutron star mergers, linking nuclear reactions more directly to the hydrodynamic evolution of merger material [1].
  • Evidence limits: The provided reports describe simulation and laboratory methods but do not provide device-performance results, independent replication, or quantitative error estimates for the new approaches [1][3].
