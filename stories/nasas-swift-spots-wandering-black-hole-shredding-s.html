NASA’s Swift spots wandering black hole shredding star

Category: Science

NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory observed a rare supermassive black hole far from the center of a distant galaxy tearing apart and consuming a star. Astronomers described the object as an apparent “orphan” black hole because it sits outside the galactic core where such objects are usually found [4][6]. The flare, a tidal disruption event named 2025abcr, revealed a black hole estimated at about a million times the Sun’s mass [4][7]. NASA said astronomers had not previously detected an event of this kind so far from a galaxy’s center; ScienceDaily reported the object lay more than 30,000 light-years from the host galaxy’s core, while Phys.org described it as tens of thousands of light-years from the center [4][6][1]. Robert Stein of the University of Maryland and NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center said the discovery validates a technique for finding otherwise hidden supermassive black holes that wander away from galactic cores [4][6].

Primary Source: View original article

With this discovery, which is one of just a couple that have been confirmed so far, we’ve validated a new technique and can use it to hunt for more.

— Robert Stein (NASA)

Key Points

  • Observed flare: The event briefly outshone its entire host galaxy in ultraviolet light, and ScienceDaily described the flare as blazing like 10 billion suns [6].
  • Research team: Stein led the study, which was published Monday in The Astrophysical Journal Letters, with Phys.org identifying University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill astronomers among the observing team [4][1].

Perspectives

Science: Science reported the object was a stripped-down black hole likely ejected or displaced after a galaxy merger, emphasizing its value for studying galaxy growth.

Sources: Science

New Scientist: New Scientist described the detection as the best example yet of an enormous wandering black hole, based on light from a star being ripped apart far from its galaxy’s center.

Sources: New Scientist

Technical Details

  • Tidal disruption signature: A tidal disruption event occurs when a star passes close enough for a black hole’s gravity to tear it apart; the stellar debris heats as it spirals inward, producing a distant, detectable glow [4][7].
  • Multi-observatory confirmation: The Zwicky Transient Facility at Palomar Observatory first flagged the flare, then Swift's Ultraviolet/Optical Telescope and X-ray Telescope captured the event, providing multi-wavelength data that confirmed the tidal disruption [4].
  • Survey triage: Infobae reported that advanced algorithms helped identify the extreme flare, which researchers then confirmed using ground- and space-based observatories [7].
