Ebola death toll in Congo exceeds 5,700 as response lags

Category: Science

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has surpassed 11,700 confirmed cases and 5,700 deaths as of Nov. 19, making it the country’s largest and deadliest. Cases have also been reported in Uganda, Kenya and Nigeria [1][3]. The Bundibugyo strain is driving the outbreak, and no approved vaccine or targeted treatment is available for it. Health workers say early cases can be difficult to identify because initial symptoms resemble those of other illnesses [1][2]. Late detection, population movement and mistrust of response teams have hindered containment, while attacks on health workers and burial teams have further complicated the response [1][3]. On Nov. 17, health workers in Butembo, North Kivu, escalated a month-long strike over unpaid wages and bonuses, halting even minimum service at treatment centers [6].

Coming early, before there are complications, gives patients more chances of survival

— Vivuya Kavira Mwenga, nurse at the Doctors Without Borders Ebola treatment center in Beni (EL PAÍS)

Did you know: The current outbreak crossed 10,000 confirmed cases in 163 days, 51 days faster than the 2014-16 West African epidemic [1].

Key Points

  • Treatment outcomes: Six Ebola treatment centers supported by ALIMA have received 1,243 patients, nearly 70% of whom recovered, according to Le Potentiel [5].
  • Children at risk: Children accounted for 28% of confirmed cases but 35% of deaths; by Nov. 4, 1,737 of the 2,959 people under 18 with confirmed infections had died [3].
  • Cases reach Europe: France confirmed a case in June in a doctor returning from a humanitarian mission in Congo; a British doctor who tested positive in Congo in November was flown to London for treatment and recovered [1].
  • Funding shortfall: International Rescue Committee response director Katie Thomas-Canfield said cuts in U.S., U.K. and German aid weakened surveillance and community relationships before the outbreak grew [1].

Perspectives

Doctors Without Borders (MSF): International president Dr. Javid Abdelmoneim said resources committed to the response were not effectively reaching patients, frontline staff and communities. He called the situation after six months unacceptable [3].

Historical Background

The 2014-16 West African Ebola epidemic caused 28,600 cases and 11,300 deaths [2]. The current epidemic is the Democratic Republic of the Congo’s 17th Ebola outbreak [3].

Humanitarian Impact

The U.N. reported 260 attacks on health workers in the outbreak’s first six months, and many deaths have occurred outside treatment centers [1][3].
