U.S. envoy condemns settler siege of Qusra homes

Category: World

U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee condemned Israeli settlers who surrounded Palestinian homes in Qusra in the occupied West Bank, calling the siege a “horrific act of terror,” after residents said settlers cut water and electricity, blocked access and trapped families inside for days [3][2][11][15][18]. The standoff involved three Palestinian families in the Ras al-Ain area of Qusra, including one in a home owned by Palestinian American Loui Ridi in Ohio, and the UN human rights office said about 15 people, including at least two children, were confined in the homes [10][11][38][37]. Israeli forces moved into Qusra after the U.S. rebuke, dismantled two unauthorized outposts near Qusra and Jalud, detained one Israeli and said troops would protect residents and maintain security [7][12][61][56]. Village officials and residents said settlers remained nearby after forces removed tents and equipment, while Israeli soldiers took positions in Palestinian homes and told residents operations could continue until Sunday [4][25][7][15]. An Israeli government spokesman told the BBC the settlers’ action was “deplorable” and “unacceptable” and said Israel would investigate, arrest and prosecute those responsible [15]. The Palestinian Foreign Ministry accused Israeli authorities of using settler violence to advance forced displacement and annexation, while OHCHR said the families faced a risk of forced displacement from their homes and land [9][11][38].

Historical Background

Israel captured the West Bank in the 1967 Middle East war, and the territory is the main land component of a potential future Palestinian state [18][common]. More than 500,000 Israelis live in West Bank settlements among about three million Palestinians, and settlements are considered illegal under international law [36][15]. Israel evacuated four settlements in the northern West Bank in 2005, but Israeli settlement activity has expanded elsewhere since 1967 [38].
