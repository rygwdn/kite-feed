Trump says Iran ceasefire is over but talks will continue

Category: World

President Trump said the United States agreed to continue talks with Iran after Tehran asked to keep negotiations going, but he said Washington told Iran the ceasefire was over [1][2][4][5][9][18][26][23]. His Truth Social statement followed renewed U.S.-Iran exchanges this week, including attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, U.S. strikes on Iranian sites and Iranian strikes on U.S. military facilities in Gulf states [6][10][17][28][12][44]. U.S. officials said Washington wants Iran to publicly pledge to stop attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz, keep all lanes open to shipping and impose no tolls [3][16][22][24][27]. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran had “kept its word” on the memorandum of understanding and accused Washington of violating it, while Iranian officials denied Trump’s claim that Tehran had sought renewed talks [4][20][22][62]. Qatari mediators were in Tehran on Friday, and Iranian state media said Araghchi would travel to Oman on Saturday for talks on the Strait of Hormuz [5][26][13][45].

Historical Background

Washington and Tehran signed a memorandum of understanding in mid-June intended to turn a months-long ceasefire into a more durable settlement [5][25]. The Strait of Hormuz has long been a strategic chokepoint because tankers use it to move oil and gas from Gulf producers to global markets [common].
