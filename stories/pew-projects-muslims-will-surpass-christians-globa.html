Pew projects Muslims will surpass Christians globally by 2035

Category: World

Muslims are projected to become the world’s largest religious group by 2035, reaching 2.57 billion people compared with 2.52 billion Christians, according to new projections from the Pew Research Center. Both populations are expected to grow, but the Muslim population is projected to increase faster, mainly because Muslim women had an average of 3.1 children in 2020-2025, compared with 2.5 among Christian women [24][19][2]. Pew estimates there were 2.02 billion Muslims and 2.27 billion Christians in 2020. If current trends continue, the Muslim population will exceed 3.6 billion by 2070, while the Christian population will remain just below 3 billion. The world population is projected to grow 29% over the same period [24][19]. The report models demographic patterns across 201 countries and territories, including age, fertility, mortality, migration and religious switching. Its projections do not account for events such as wars, epidemics or natural disasters [24]. By 2070, sub-Saharan Africa is expected to be home to more than half of the world’s Christians, while Pakistan is projected to have the largest Muslim population [11][24]. Pew’s researchers say the projected growth is largely taking place in countries where each faith is already predominant, rather than reflecting a direct shift from one religion to the other [19][7].

I don't think this is a situation in which the growth of Muslims is coming at the expense of Christians

Key Points

  • Switching patterns: From 2020 to 2070, Pew projects religious switching will result in net losses of 8 million Muslims and 105 million Christians; movement directly between Islam and Christianity is expected to roughly balance [19].
  • African growth: Pew projects sub-Saharan Africa’s Muslim population will rise from about 369 million in 2020 to 980 million in 2070, increasing its share of the world’s Muslims from 18% to 27% [23].
  • South Asia: Pakistan’s Muslim population is projected to grow from 227 million in 2020 to 461 million in 2070, while India is projected to have about 334 million Muslims [19][13].
  • Unaffiliated share: People with no religious affiliation are projected to account for 17% of the global population in 2070, down from 24% in 2020, largely because China’s population is expected to decline [24][10].
  • Other faiths: Hindus and Jews are projected to grow in number but decline as a share of the global population, while Buddhists are projected to decline in both number and share [24][11].

Perspectives

Stephanie Kramer, Pew Research Center researcher: Kramer said India’s projected religious change is driven mainly by longstanding age and fertility differences; she said the fertility gap has narrowed and is likely to keep shrinking [13].

Gina Zurlo, Harvard Divinity School senior researcher: Zurlo said interreligious education and personal relationships across faiths can help counter misunderstanding as populations change [7].

Technical Details

  • Projection coverage: The model covers 201 countries and territories representing 99.98% of the world’s population, using 2020 census and survey data to estimate the size and age structure of religious groups [24].
  • Cohort model: Researchers projected populations by age and sex in five-year steps, accounting for births, deaths, migration and religious switching [24].
  • Scenario limits: The model assumes recent demographic patterns continue and excludes possible effects of wars, natural disasters, epidemics and future economic or social changes [24].

Q&A

Q: Would Muslims still overtake Christians if fertility rates were equal?

A: Yes. In a hypothetical scenario where religious groups within each country had the same fertility rates, Pew’s projection still has Muslims surpassing Christians, but in 2040 rather than 2035 [23].
