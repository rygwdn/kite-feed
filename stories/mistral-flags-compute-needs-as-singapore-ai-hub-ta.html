Mistral flags compute needs as Singapore AI hub targets 200 staff

Category: AI

French AI company Mistral says access to data centers and chips will be essential to expanding its Singapore-based Asia-Pacific operation, which opened as a regional headquarters and research hub on Nov. 20. The company plans to grow its Asia-Pacific workforce to 200 by 2027, with most hires expected in Singapore [8][12][4]. Geoff Soon, Mistral’s vice president of revenue for Asia-Pacific, said the company needs more digital infrastructure to run experiments and develop longer-running AI agents. About 70% of its Singapore team is expected to be forward-deployed engineers and scientists who work directly with customers to develop and implement AI, rather than focus on sales [8][6][4]. The company is targeting public agencies, high-tech manufacturing, robotics and regulated industries such as financial services. New U.S. licensing rules for some advanced AI chip exports to Singapore took effect on Nov. 19. Mistral says it will seek a roughly even split of local and international hires and plans to announce details of new financial-sector partnerships later [12][8].

Key Points

  • Local competition: Singapore’s national AI research program built its SEA-LION model on Alibaba’s Qwen model, while OCBC uses open models including DeepSeek in more than 30 internal banking tools [4][13].
  • Manufacturing research: Mistral’s Singapore hub will also explore physical AI and engineering simulations for manufacturing environments [2].
  • Sovereignty scope: Mistral’s open-weight approach is intended to give organisations control over data, model customisation, compute infrastructure and operational oversight [5][10].

Perspectives

Marjorie Janiewicz, Mistral’s chief revenue officer, says the company aims to compete with Chinese open-model providers by combining its model with deployment technology, workflow tools and engineers who help customers put systems into production [4].

Sources: Fortune

Singapore’s Home Team Science and Technology Agency (HTX) says its collaboration with Mistral on Phoenix models and embodied AI has grounded development in operational settings and can support its public-safety work [5].

Sources: TechNode Global

Timeline

  • 2024: Singapore’s Ministry of Defence first approached Mistral about working together [4][13].
  • May 2025: HTX signed an AI development agreement with Mistral and Microsoft for public-safety applications [2].
  • November 2025: Mistral and HTX agreed to deepen work on video, embodied AI and cybersecurity [4][13].
  • 19 November 2026: U.S. licensing rules for some advanced AI chip exports to Singapore took effect [12].

Technical Details

  • Robostral Navigate: The 8-billion-parameter robotics model was trained entirely in simulation and uses a single RGB camera to navigate unfamiliar environments [5][10].
  • Forward-deployed engineering: Scientists build models, while engineers develop agent capabilities and communicate directly with customers rather than limiting their work to model fine-tuning [6][8].

Business Angle

  • About 70% of Mistral’s Singapore team is expected to be forward-deployed engineers and scientists working directly with customers [4][8].
  • Mistral had about 40 staff in Singapore in May and had previously planned to have 100 there by year-end [8].
  • Mistral’s Singapore collaborations include Singtel, NCS and ST Engineering, as well as HTX and DSO National Laboratories [2].

Q&A

Q: What kinds of applications could the Singapore hub develop?

A: Mistral aims to tailor public-sector models to Singapore’s linguistic diversity and code-switching, help robots navigate settings such as multi-floor buildings and security screening, and use production-line images to identify manufacturing defects [12].
