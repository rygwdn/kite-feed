US envoy Kushner meets Hamas before Netanyahu talks

Category: World

U.S. envoy Jared Kushner met Hamas political chief Khalil al-Hayya in Egypt on Sunday in a rare direct contact over President Trump’s U.S.-backed Gaza road map, before planned talks with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Monday [2][5][8][9][14]. The plan centers on Hamas disarming in Gaza and Israel withdrawing from the territory, where about 2 million people live and large areas remain under Israeli military control more than 10 months after a ceasefire ended major military operations [2][5][14][31]. The Egypt meeting lasted more than two hours, according to a regional official and a Hamas official cited by the Associated Press, and included officials from Egypt, Qatar and Turkey [2][5][14][20]. Netanyahu rejected the 15-point U.S.-backed plan last week, while Hamas later urged mediators and Trump’s Board of Peace to compel Israel to approve the road map and begin setting a timetable for implementation [8][4][27][36].

Key Points

  • Implementation demands: A source told CBS News the talks sought concrete, verifiable steps to maintain the ceasefire, transfer governing duties to the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza, keep Hamas out of future governance, enable Israeli withdrawals and deploy an international stabilizing force [9].
  • Israeli politics: The BBC reported Netanyahu faces an October election and leads a coalition that includes ministers favoring a harsher line on Gaza, while many Israelis doubt Hamas is ready to disarm [8].
  • U.S. message: Sources cited by AFP said Kushner pressed Hamas to take “concrete, verifiable steps” and relayed that “Gaza can never again be a source of terror for Israel” [27][33][38].

Perspectives

Hamas reaffirmed its commitment to disarm and demilitarize Gaza during the direct talks, according to a source cited by Axios [7].

Historical Background

Hamas has governed the Gaza Strip since 2007, after taking control of the territory from forces loyal to the Palestinian Authority [common]. The October 7, 2023 Hamas-led attack on Israel triggered the Gaza war, and the United States has long designated Hamas a terrorist organization [27][common]. Kushner, Steve Witkoff and Hamas leaders met in Egypt last year in talks that helped secure the ceasefire; next steps are stalled over disarmament [2][5][20].
