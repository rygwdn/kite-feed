Trump threatens tax on U.S. companies building AI data centers abroad

Category: AI

President Donald Trump threatened Friday to impose a “tremendous tax” on U.S. technology companies that build AI data centers abroad, saying domestic computing capacity was necessary to stay ahead of China. He offered no details on how the tax would work and said he had not yet discussed the proposal with his administration [3][4][17]. Google has announced a €13 billion investment in AI infrastructure in Finland, including three data centers. Meta has begun construction in Alberta, Canada, while Anthropic agreed to lease a proposed facility in Nova Scotia [2]. The push to keep projects in the United States faces local resistance over data centers’ power and water demands and a more restrictive outlook for developers after Democrats’ midterm gains. Google and Meta say they are offering communities greater transparency, environmental commitments and increased financial benefits as they seek project approvals [11][4].

Did you know: Cloud computing still accounts for more U.S. data-center use than AI, even as AI drives additional growth in the sector [11].

Key Points

  • Finland project: Construction on two of Google’s planned Finnish data centers was halted last month over concerns about clearing more than 300 hectares of forest [2].
  • Investment scale: UBS economists estimate technology companies will spend as much as $1.8 trillion next year on AI development, mostly on data centers [11].
  • Croatian proposal: A proposed U.S.-backed AI center in Topusko, Croatia, is designed for one gigawatt of capacity, with construction planned for 2027 and operations for 2029 [27].
  • Energy legislation: The Senate bill would shorten approval timelines for major energy projects and require data centers to cover the costs of their energy use and grid upgrades. It now goes to the House [16][11].

Perspectives

President Donald Trump: Trump argues that data centers can generate their own electricity and bring down local power costs [7].

Timeline

  • 3 November 2026: Voters approved 40 of 41 data-center restriction measures across 10 states [7][2].
  • 16 November 2026: The administration issued rules restricting remote access to advanced U.S. chips and servers [15].
  • 19 November 2026: The Senate approved a bipartisan bill to accelerate energy-project permitting [16].
  • 20 November 2026: Trump threatened to impose the tax at a White House briefing [3].

Industry Impact

  • Data-center operators: U.S. operators Equinix and Digital Realty already have facilities abroad that could be affected by a policy targeting overseas construction [13][27].
  • Construction labor: The buildout has boosted wages for some skilled contractors, particularly electricians, who are in short supply [11].

Business Angle

  • AI valuations: Since Nov. 4, the S&P 500 has fallen about 9% as investors reassessed AI valuations, though other factors contributed. Anthropic’s record-setting IPO did not ease concerns about AI stocks [3].

Q&A

Q: What legal step would be needed to impose a federal tax?

A: A federal tax generally requires legislation passed by Congress. The proposal has not been described as being in effect [13][7].
