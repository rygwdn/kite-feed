Nvidia touts liquid cooling to cut AI water use

Category: AI

Nvidia announced a warm-water, liquid-cooled AI data center design that it says can sharply reduce facility water use. An executive said the system eliminates virtually all water use inside the data center [1][6]. The company says the design uses a closed-loop coolant system that can be filled once and recirculated for the life of a facility, cutting on-site water use by up to 100% in favorable climates [1][5]. TechCrunch reported that Nvidia’s framing counts what happens inside the data center while excluding water tied to electricity generation and chip manufacturing [1]. The Verge and Gizmodo cited scrutiny of AI data centers’ water and energy needs [6][5]. TechCrunch said it asked Nvidia to clarify the scope of its water-use claims and would update its article if the company responded [1].

Key Points

  • Coolant mix: Gizmodo reported that Nvidia’s liquid coolant is about three-quarters water and one-quarter propylene glycol, similar to automotive antifreeze [5].
  • Energy claim: Nvidia said fully liquid-cooled AI infrastructure can reduce cooling energy consumption and said cooling is a major part of data center energy use at hyperscale [5].
  • Cost link: Gizmodo said hotter-running coolant can reduce the need for large amounts of water and fans, lowering operating costs and on-site resource use [5].

Perspectives

Nvidia: Nvidia highlighted its Rubin-generation reference design for fully liquid-cooled AI data centers and said it had eliminated “massive amounts of power usage and pretty much all water usage.”

Sources: The Verge

Gizmodo: Gizmodo framed Nvidia’s design as addressing one part of AI’s environmental footprint, while saying it remains “a long way from 100% sustainable.”

Sources: Gizmodo

Technical Details

  • The design uses a liquid coolant that absorbs heat from chips, carries it outside the racks and then returns, so the facility does not need a continuous supply of fresh water for chip cooling [1][5].
  • High-temperature operation: Nvidia’s coolant enters racks at 45°C and leaves at 55°C, temperatures high enough to remove heat without relying as heavily on evaporative cooling [1].
  • Water accounting boundary: TechCrunch said facility-level water-use metrics can exclude upstream water embedded in electricity generation and chip fabrication, which may double or triple a data center’s total water footprint [1].

Industry Impact

  • Hyperscale data centers: Nvidia says its fully liquid-cooled design can reduce cooling energy consumption at hyperscale, which could lower energy costs if proven at scale [5].
  • Energy sourcing: Gizmodo cited a Wall Street Journal report that Microsoft signed a Chevron deal for a natural gas-powered data center in West Texas, noting that water-saving facility designs can still be paired with fossil-fuel power choices [5].
  • Local permitting: Public opposition often centers on water and energy, so cooling systems that reduce on-site resource use could affect permitting for new AI data centers [6].
