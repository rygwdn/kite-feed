President Trump backs Irish reunification during Dublin visit

Category: World

President Donald Trump said Saturday during a meeting with Irish Taoiseach Micheál Martin in Dublin that he would “love to see” the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland unified. He called reunification “a fantastic thing,” said it would eventually happen and “may as well happen now.” Trump acknowledged that the United Kingdom would have a say and later described Irish unity as a question with “no good answer” before repeating that he considered it “a very cool thing” [2][4][5][49]. The remarks departed from the United States’ longstanding public neutrality on Northern Ireland’s constitutional status and drew immediate criticism from the UK government and pro-British unionist politicians. Downing Street said Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s position remained unchanged and that a border poll was off the table, while Democratic Unionist Party leader Gavin Robinson said the region’s future should be decided by its people [1][6][36][13]. Trump made the comments at the start of a two-day visit that included meetings with Irish leaders and attendance at the Irish Open at his golf resort in County Clare [8][10].

  • Unionist opposition: DUP leader Gavin Robinson said unionists were not interested in joining the Irish Republic [36].

Perspectives

Irish government: Martin did not endorse or reject Trump’s remarks when reporters asked for his response, and his spokesperson said the comments would not change government policy [21].
