Studies link interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS to ancient star system

Category: Science

Astronomers using the European Southern Observatory’s Very Large Telescope said interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS probably formed in the cold outer regions of an ancient planetary system. The finding is based on the first detailed chemical-isotope measurements of a comet from outside the solar system [5][6][7][17]. The study, published in Nature Astronomy, focused on carbon and nitrogen isotope ratios in cyanide gas around the comet. Several accounts said the object may be far older than the Sun, possibly more than twice its age [4][12][16][17]. 3I/ATLAS is the third confirmed interstellar object detected, after 1I/ʻOumuamua and 2I/Borisov. Researchers said its brightness and active gas cloud made measurements possible that were not available for the earlier visitors [5][13][17]. The findings point to formation around an old, low-metallicity star, one born when the universe contained fewer heavy elements than it does today [4][5][16]. A separate James Webb Space Telescope analysis reached related conclusions from elevated carbon isotopes, deuterium and water measurements in the comet’s icy material [9][11][12][16]. The ESO team observed 3I/ATLAS between December 6 and 26, 2025, after it had passed closest to the Sun [5].

3I/ATLAS is a really exciting opportunity to probe the composition of another planetary system, one that formed long before our Sun and solar system even existed

— Rosemary Dorsey (Popular Science)

Key Points

  • Instrument: The observations used the UVES spectrograph on ESO’s Very Large Telescope in Chile’s Atacama Desert [5][6][9][11].
  • Different chemistry: Researchers found nitrogen and carbon isotope ratios unlike those seen in local comets. Scimex reported that both elements tended to be lighter than in solar-system comets [4][6][12].

Perspectives

Cyrielle Opitom: Opitom and colleagues said interstellar objects let astronomers study material from other protoplanetary disks and analyze volatile composition when those objects become active and sublimate.

Sources: Sci.News

Scimex: Scimex highlighted the work of an international team that included a New Zealand scientist and described the measurements as clues to how the building blocks of planets form around distant stars.

Sources: Scimex

NASA analysis: Phys.org said detailed NASA observations ruled out speculation that 3I/ATLAS was an alien spacecraft and that later NASA analysis instead pointed to ancient extrasolar origins.

Sources: Phys.org

Timeline

  • July 2025: 3I/ATLAS appeared extremely bright during its approach to Earth [4]
  • 6 December 2025: The ESO team observed the comet after perihelion [5]
  • 6 July 2026: The Nature Astronomy study on isotope ratios was published [17]

Scientific Significance

  • Scientific meaning: The findings give researchers a direct compositional sample from a planetary system older than the Sun, instead of relying only on remote observations of distant disks or stars [4][5][7].
  • Independent evidence: The VLT isotope results and separate James Webb Space Telescope analysis both reported results consistent with an ancient, chemically distinct origin for 3I/ATLAS [9][11][12][16].
  • Limits: The sources describe the comet’s likely birth environment, not a named parent star or exact planetary system, so the origin remains inferred from chemical signatures rather than directly traced [5][6][16].
