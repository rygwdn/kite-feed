U.S. strikes Iran missile site as Gulf attacks widen

Category: World

U.S. fighter jets struck an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) missile-launch site on Iran’s Qeshm Island on Nov. 17, after Iran said it had sent drones against a U.S.-operated radar system at Bahrain’s Sheikh Isa Air Base. Washington described the strike as self-defense; Bahrain and U.S. Central Command said the drones were intercepted without damage or injuries. Iran’s deputy governor for Hormozgan province said the U.S. strike caused no damage to homes, businesses or infrastructure [30][4][1][20]. The exchanges widened across the Gulf on Nov. 18: Kuwait said its air defenses were countering drone threats, Bahrain reported further interceptions, and a Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed a Houthi cruise missile headed toward Riyadh. Iran’s army said it had targeted U.S. military systems at Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait. Separately, a United Arab Emirates-owned vessel was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz; its operator said one crew member was missing and 11 were injured [12][15][24]. The war began with U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran on Feb. 28. The current exchanges coincide with a U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports and efforts to restrict or escort shipping through Hormuz. Qatar and Oman continued diplomatic contacts with Tehran, including talks on maritime security and reducing tensions, but no agreement has been announced [12][13][20].

Key Points

  • Attack pattern: Iran’s Tuesday drone operation was its third reported attack on U.S. military targets in Bahrain in five days [4][26].
  • Carrier deployment: The USS Theodore Roosevelt joined the USS George Washington and USS George H.W. Bush, bringing three U.S. carrier strike groups to the region [4][26].
  • War-duration pledge: Vice President JD Vance said the administration would not let the conflict become a two-year commitment, while reiterating concern over Iran’s nuclear program [2].
  • Negotiating gap: The Financial Times reported that Washington believes its blockade and ability to move oil through Hormuz strengthen its position, while Tehran believes it can withstand economic pressure and keep energy prices elevated [22].
  • Iranian warning: IRGC commander Maj. Gen. Ahmad Vahidi warned that any renewed U.S. or Israeli aggression, “no matter how minor,” would draw “devastating and painful strikes” [3].

Perspectives

Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps: The Guards said the Bahrain radar attack was retaliation for repeated violations of Iranian airspace by U.S. military drones and warned Bahrain over its alliance with Washington [1][13].

Industry Impact

  • Oil exports: Iraq resumed pumping oil to Turkey at 120,000 barrels per day and plans to raise pipeline capacity to 500,000 barrels per day, avoiding Hormuz for those shipments [2].
  • Commercial shipping: Tankers moving through the strait under U.S. military protection have helped return oil shipments from the Gulf to roughly prewar levels [4].

Humanitarian Impact

The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency said the crew of a tanker attacked north of Oman’s Khasab was unaccounted for. The vessel, first attacked on Nov. 16, reportedly sustained a second strike and caught fire again [20].

Business Angle

  • Brent crude futures were up 0.4% at $92.76 a barrel, above prewar levels but below the April wartime peak of $126.41 [13].

  • 🇹🇷 Turkey: Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan called for Hormuz to reopen and urged a swift, positive conclusion to U.S.-Iran talks [20].
  • 🇰🇼 Kuwait: Kuwait denounced the attacks as a serious breach of international law and said it reserved the right to take necessary steps to defend its sovereignty and facilities [24].
