US and Iran pause strikes as Hormuz talks advance

Category: World

The United States held off on attacks against Iran for a third night, and Tehran said it had halted retaliatory operations after nearly two weeks of U.S. bombing and Iranian strikes on countries hosting U.S. forces [5][9][14]. U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz said President Trump paused the campaign to give negotiations time, while a senior Iranian official told Reuters that Iran would keep its attacks halted as long as Washington maintained its pause [6][14][26][31]. The halt followed 13 consecutive nights of U.S. strikes, with no U.S. attacks reported on Saturday or Sunday after the Pentagon suspended the campaign late Friday [14][18][25][32]. Diplomatic activity focused on Oman-led talks in Tehran over management and reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, where disputes over shipping access reignited U.S.-Iran tensions earlier in July [42][23][20][4]. President Trump has kept military options available, and Waltz said additional U.S. military assets were moving into the region [42][25]. The pause coincided with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s expected White House visit on Tuesday, after reports that he had pressed Washington to continue strikes against Iran [9][17].

Key Points

  • Hormuz proposals: Iran has proposed charging transit fees and jointly managing the Strait of Hormuz with Oman, while Oman reportedly suggested separate Iranian and Omani corridors with different passage rules [15].
  • Regional spillover: The conflict broadened over the weekend as Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthis attacked Saudi oil installations and Iran accused Ukraine of targeting an Iranian vessel in the Caspian Sea [12][51].
  • Commodity spillover: Chicago soybean oil futures fell the most in a month as lower crude prices pulled grain and oilseed markets down [77].
  • Market breadth: U.S. stock-index futures rose as oil fell, with Dow futures up 337 points, S&P 500 futures up 0.80% and Nasdaq futures up 1.28% in Sunday evening trading [69][64].
  • Red Sea risk: Yemen’s Houthis claimed attacks against Saudi targets, while Bloomberg reported ship-tracking data showed flows remained constrained and The National reported damage to several tankers [76][97][98].
  • Blockade enforcement: U.S. Central Command said on July 25 that its blockade had redirected 12 commercial vessels, disabled two noncompliant vessels and boarded two others [28].
  • U.S. constraints: Reuters reported that U.S. advisers warned preselected targets were largely exhausted and raised concerns about depleting the arsenal, while Waltz rejected claims that U.S. munitions were insufficient [26][52][42][25].

Business Angle

  • Lower oil prices help energy-intensive companies if shipping routes reopen enough to restore dependable supply, not merely because futures prices fell for a session [55][105][common].
  • Companies with high fuel exposure remain vulnerable because Hormuz uncertainty can reprice oil quickly even when physical flows continue [21][common].
  • Airlines, chemical producers and logistics firms still need to manage fuel hedging carefully because Red Sea and Hormuz disruptions have not fully cleared [76][97][common].
