China weighs approving Alibaba, ByteDance Nvidia chip purchases

Category: Technology

China has signaled it may allow Alibaba, ByteDance and other domestic companies to buy Nvidia’s RTX Pro 5500 workstation GPU, according to a report by The Information citing two people familiar with the matter. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology recently asked companies how many chips they planned to buy and how they would use them. It told some firms it intended to approve the purchases [1][2][3][6][12][10]. Reuters has not independently verified the report, and China had not publicly confirmed any approval by the time of publication. The timing of any approval, permitted quantities and conditions remain unclear [1][11]. The RTX Pro 5500 is a workstation graphics card based on Nvidia’s Blackwell design, with 84GB of memory, rather than a data-center AI accelerator [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Possible order size: ByteDance was reported to be considering an order of about 1 million cards, though any purchase depends on Chinese approval [18][15].
  • Supply schedule: Nvidia reportedly planned to begin shipments to China in late December at roughly 500,000 units per quarter, and told customers to place orders by September 30 to secure supply [18].
  • Export question: Some industry executives believe the RTX Pro 5500 may not be subject to U.S. export restrictions [10].

Perspectives

Nvidia: A company spokesperson said U.S. firms face constraints from both U.S. export controls, which the spokesperson described as outdated, and China’s own limits on U.S. products [10][9].

Historical Background

Earlier in 2026, the United States cleared Nvidia to sell H200 chips to Chinese companies [1].

Q&A

Q: What price was reported for the card in China?

A: South Korea’s Yonhap reported that the card was expected to cost 85,000 to 90,000 yuan per unit in China [18].
