Disney elevates parks chief Josh D’Amaro to CEO role

Category: Business

The Walt Disney Company has chosen Josh D’Amaro, chairman of Disney Experiences, as its next chief executive, ending years of speculation over who would succeed longtime leader Bob Iger [9][17][15]. D’Amaro, a nearly three-decade Disney veteran who has overseen theme parks, cruise lines, and consumer products worldwide, will take over on March 18 while Iger stays on as a senior advisor through year-end to support the transition [6][5][13]. Disney’s board, led by chairman and former Morgan Stanley chief James Gorman, said D’Amaro was unanimously selected after beating out more than 100 internal and external contenders, including entertainment co-chair Dana Walden, citing his record of turning the Experiences division into the company’s primary profit engine and his vision for Disney’s future [1][businessinsider.com#5][13]. The move signals a strategic bet that Disney’s physical experiences can anchor its finances as the company works to revitalize its film studio, navigate cord-cutting in TV networks, and grow streaming platforms like Disney+, Hulu, and an evolving ESPN offering [6][10][14].

Did you know: Josh D’Amaro originally studied sculpture and once envisioned becoming a professional artist before switching to business and eventually joining Disney in 1998 [12][businessinsider.com#5].

Key Points

  • Succession process: Disney’s CEO search spanned multiple years and considered more than 100 candidates, culminating in a unanimous board vote for D’Amaro after a final contest primarily between him and entertainment co-chair Dana Walden [1][businessinsider.com#5][17].
  • Experiences division strength: Under D’Amaro, Disney’s Experiences unit—including parks, cruises, and products—has delivered record profits and recently generated over 70% of company operating income despite accounting for less than half of total revenue [5][businessinsider.com#6][13].
  • Leadership style: D’Amaro has described admitting “I don’t know” as a key leadership tool, crediting openness to uncertainty with helping him rise from aspiring sculptor to the top of a nearly $200 billion entertainment company [12][13].
  • Activist investor skepticism: Activist investor Nelson Peltz argued at a Florida event that D’Amaro’s limited direct entertainment and movie experience could justify Iger remaining influential, suggesting Iger might claim he needs to guide the new CEO [3].
  • Operational track record: D’Amaro has overseen global parks in Florida, California, France, Shanghai, Hong Kong, and Tokyo, managing workforces totaling about 185,000 employees and stewarding a roughly $60 billion long-term expansion program for resorts and cruise ships [11][13].

Perspectives

Disney board and James Gorman: Gorman wrote to employees that D’Amaro showed a strong vision for Disney’s future and a deep understanding of what makes the company unique, calling him the right person to take the reins after leading the most profitable division [businessinsider.com#5][13].

Sources: Business Insider

Bob Iger: Iger publicly described D’Amaro as an exceptional leader and the right person to become Disney’s next CEO, while agreeing to stay on as a senior advisor through the end of the year to support a smoother handoff than in the previous CEO transition [6][13].

Sources: Business Insider

Nelson Peltz and Trian Partners: Nelson Peltz contended that D’Amaro’s lack of entertainment and movie-business background could give Iger a reason to remain deeply involved, adding that he hoped D’Amaro would get the opportunity to run the company independently [3].

Sources: Business Insider

Industry analysts and investors: Early commentary from analysts highlighted that choosing the parks chief underscores the importance of in-person experiences to Disney’s earnings base, while also noting investors will watch closely to see how D’Amaro handles streaming economics and studio output after the turmoil of the prior succession attempt [13][10][14].

Sources: Fortune

Timeline

  • 2020: Bob Iger steps down as Disney CEO, succeeded by parks chief Bob Chapek [20][13].
  • 2022: Disney’s board removes Chapek amid company pressures and brings Iger back as CEO [20][17].
  • Monday: Disney’s board votes unanimously to appoint Josh D’Amaro as next chief executive [businessinsider.com#6][13].
  • Tuesday: Disney publicly announces D’Amaro’s promotion and Iger’s planned transition to senior advisor [15][9].
  • March 18: D’Amaro is scheduled to formally assume the CEO role from Iger [6][15].

Technical Details

  • Disney Experiences division: Disney Experiences encompasses theme parks, cruise lines, and consumer products, and under D’Amaro’s leadership this segment became the company’s primary profit driver, contributing a clear majority of operating income from less than half of total revenue [5][13].
  • Streaming portfolio: As CEO, D’Amaro will oversee further growth of subscription platforms Disney+ and Hulu and guide ESPN’s evolution toward more streaming-centric distribution, a shift intended to counteract revenue declines from traditional TV networks affected by cord-cutting [6][10][14].

Industry Impact

  • Media and streaming: By choosing a CEO rooted in physical experiences rather than content or pure streaming, Disney signals to competitors that theme parks and destination experiences remain central to its monetization strategy, potentially encouraging other media groups to double down on live events and experiential spin-offs to complement digital platforms [10][16].
  • Theme parks and tourism: D’Amaro’s promotion and track record of record park profits and large-scale expansion could support continued investment in new attractions and cruise capacity, beneficial for regional tourism economies and suppliers that depend on Disney’s steady capital spending [businessinsider.com#6][13].
  • Corporate governance: The more methodical, board-led process that elevated D’Amaro after the abrupt end of the prior CEO’s tenure may reinforce best-practice discussions across corporate America about building deeper succession benches and avoiding last-minute leadership scrambles at iconic brands [17][21].

Historical Background

Bob Iger first led Disney as CEO from 2005 to 2020 before stepping down and handing the role to another parks executive, Bob Chapek, whose troubled tenure ended with Iger’s return to the top job in 2022 [13][20]. That earlier handoff made this succession one of the most scrutinized in corporate America and shaped the board’s determination to run a longer, more deliberate process this time that resolved an internal contest among senior lieutenants and aimed to avoid the instability seen during Chapek’s short leadership [17][21].

Q&A

Q: How has D’Amaro’s background in running global parks shaped his approach to managing a workforce of over 180,000 employees?

A: Reporting notes that D’Amaro has overseen theme parks across the United States, Europe, and Asia, collectively employing about 185,000 people, and has emphasized on-the-ground engagement and collaboration with creative and operational teams when making decisions [11][businessinsider.com#6].

Q: What specific challenges will D’Amaro inherit in Disney’s ESPN business?

A: Sources describe ESPN as being in the midst of a pivot from cable to streaming distribution, requiring careful renegotiation of sports rights, the design of direct-to-consumer offerings, and management of declining traditional TV revenues while preventing subscriber erosion during the transition [6][10][14].

Q: How did Disney communicate the leadership change internally to employees?

A: James Gorman sent a memo to staff explaining that D’Amaro’s inspiring leadership, innovation, strategic-growth focus, and passion for Disney’s people underpinned the board’s choice, while also noting his collaborations with major entertainment figures and his use of technology to enhance immersive storytelling [businessinsider.com#5].
