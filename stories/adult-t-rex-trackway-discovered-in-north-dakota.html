Adult T. rex trackway discovered in North Dakota

Category: Science

Researchers at the Denver Museum of Nature and Science and Liverpool John Moores University have identified the first known trackway made by an adult Tyrannosaurus rex. The four footprints were found in 2025 on U.S. Forest Service land near Marmarth in southwestern North Dakota’s Hell Creek Formation and date to about 66.5 million years ago [2][6][3]. The trackway stretches about 23 feet and contains two left and two right three-toed prints, each roughly 3 feet long. The spacing indicates a stride of about 13 feet and a walking speed of approximately 3.5 to 4.5 mph, according to analyses described in the Journal of Vertebrate Paleontology [2][4]. Researchers said the sequence provides evidence of movement that fossil bones alone cannot show [2].

Key Points

  • Earlier evidence: Before this discovery, researchers had confirmed only two isolated adult T. rex footprints, one in New Mexico and one in Montana [6].
  • Fossil hunter: High school teacher and amateur fossil hunter Kent Hups found the trackway while volunteering on one of Tyler Lyson’s excavation projects [6].
  • Site context: The discovery was made in the badlands near the Montana border [6].
