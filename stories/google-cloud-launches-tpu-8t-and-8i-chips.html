Google Cloud launches TPU 8t and 8i chips

Category: Technology

Google used its Cloud Next 2026 event to introduce two separate eighth-generation AI chips: TPU 8t for training large models and TPU 8i for inference. The move marks a shift away from a single general-purpose TPU design [1][2][7][5]. Google executives tied the change to growing enterprise AI adoption and the rise of so-called AI agents. Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian said nearly 75% of Google Cloud customers already use AI in production, and that the challenge has shifted from experimentation to scaling across organizations [1]. Across the coverage, Google said the split design should shorten model training times and lower the cost of serving AI applications at scale, while strengthening Google Cloud's position against Nvidia even as Google continues to offer Nvidia hardware in its cloud [2][4][7]. The launch also fits into a broader product push at the event, where Google paired the new chips with AI software and agent-management tools meant to make enterprise AI systems easier to deploy and manage [3][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Performance gains: Google said TPU 8t delivers 2.8 times the performance of the seventh-generation Ironwood TPU at the same price, while TPU 8i delivers an 80% performance improvement over Ironwood [8][2].
  • Availability timing: Coverage of Google's announcement said both new chips should become available later in 2026 [8][2].
  • Compute pairing: The Register reported that Google is pairing the TPU strategy with Arm-based Axion CPU cores rather than x86 in parts of its cloud AI infrastructure [4].
  • Workspace tie-in: Google also announced Workspace Intelligence, which it said will provide personalized context across Workspace apps, showing how the chip launch fits into a broader enterprise AI stack [6].

  • 2026: Google said both chips will become available later this year [8][2]

Technical Details

  • Split accelerator design: Unlike earlier TPU generations, which handled both model building and serving, Google split the workloads between TPU 8t for training and TPU 8i for inference, reflecting different bottlenecks in compute efficiency, latency, and utilization [2][5].
  • Price-normalized uplift: Google's claimed 2.8x gain for TPU 8t is framed against Ironwood at the same price, rather than as raw peak speed alone, making cost-adjusted throughput a key comparison metric for cloud buyers [8].

Industry Impact

  • Cloud competition: The launch puts more pressure on rival cloud providers and accelerator vendors by offering distinct chips for training and inference instead of a single general-purpose design [2][7].
  • Enterprise AI software: Google tied the hardware release to agent-management and Workspace products, signaling that infrastructure sales increasingly depend on bundled software and orchestration tools [3][6].
  • CPU ecosystem: The Register said Google is combining TPUs with Arm-based Axion cores, which could expand Arm's role in AI data-center platforms at the expense of x86 in some deployments [4].

Historical Background

Google has developed custom Tensor Processing Units for years as an alternative to relying entirely on third-party accelerators [common]. The company announced its seventh-generation Ironwood TPU in 2025, and several reports describe TPU 8t and TPU 8i as its direct successors, with a new split between training and inference roles [2][8]. The broader backdrop is the rapid buildout of AI infrastructure across cloud providers as demand for model training and serving surged after the generative AI boom of the early 2020s [common].

Action Items

  • Assess cloud AI costs: Teams running large AI workloads can compare whether separate training and inference hardware could cut model-serving bills or speed development cycles once the chips become available later in 2026 [2][8].
  • Review stack fit: Enterprise buyers considering Google Cloud can weigh the new TPUs alongside Google's agent-management and Workspace tools to see whether an integrated platform fits their deployment plans [3][6].
