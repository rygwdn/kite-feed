IMF says AI investment sustains 3% global growth despite energy shock

Category: AI

IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said on Oct. 12 in Bangkok that investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure is helping keep global growth near 3% despite an energy-supply shock [15][1][5]. She estimated the investment boom could add 0.5 to 1 percentage point to growth in advanced Asian economies and 0.2 to 0.8 percentage points in emerging Asian economies, where suppliers of AI hardware and data-center infrastructure are benefiting [15][11][18]. The IMF forecast 3% global growth for 2026 in July and planned to release updated projections on Tuesday, Oct. 13 [15][8]. Georgieva also warned that AI could affect about 40% of jobs worldwide over time by augmenting work, reducing job quality or eliminating roles. She described the technology as a “slow-moving tsunami” with consequences for education and government tax revenues as economies shift from labor toward capital [15][3]. An initial IMF study found that 10% of U.S. jobs already require AI skills, while routine work is declining and lower-paid jobs in construction and data centers are growing [15][8]. World Bank President Ajay Banga argued that smaller, task-specific AI tools could raise productivity in developing economies, while Singapore President Tharman Shanmugaratnam warned that demand for graduates entering their first jobs is falling [15][11]. The IMF and World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok run through Oct. 18 [19][22].

It is a transformative technology. It so completely changes how we work, how we live

— Kristalina Georgieva, IMF managing director (RTHK)

Key Points

  • Uneven impact: Tharman warned AI could widen differences in growth and job creation between advanced and developing economies [15][aawsad.com#1].
  • Small AI: Banga cited an Indian agriculture service using a 3G phone image to identify crop disease and order treatment, and an Indonesian clinic network using AI-assisted skin assessments [11].
  • Trade surge: The WTO raised its 2026 merchandise-trade growth forecast to 3.9%; AI-related semiconductors and servers accounted for 47% of trade growth in the first half, with their trade turnover up 67% from a year earlier [12].
  • Uneven job exposure: The IMF estimated AI could affect 60% of jobs in advanced economies, compared with 23% in low-income countries [11].

Perspectives

International Monetary Fund: Georgieva said broad adoption will depend on reliable electricity and digital infrastructure, a workforce prepared to use AI, and government safeguards against malicious actors [19].

Sources: La Razón

World Bank: Banga presented small AI applications as a potential source of job creation in emerging economies, not just a productivity tool [15][11].

Sources: RTHK

Timeline

  • July 2026: IMF forecast global growth of 3% for 2026 [15][8]
  • 12 October 2026: IMF and World Bank annual meetings opened in Bangkok [15][19]
  • 13 October 2026: IMF scheduled the release of updated global and country economic projections [15][19]

Industry Impact

  • Asian technology supply chain: The IMF identified the United States, China and India as leading AI supply-chain producers, while many infrastructure firms are based in Asia. It also cited Thailand as part of that ecosystem [15][11].
  • Private-sector employment: Banga said private businesses create 75% to 85% of jobs in every country and that conditions for private investment are important to job creation [11].

Q&A

Q: Which five sectors did the World Bank identify as potential sources of domestic job creation?

A: It listed infrastructure, smallholder agriculture, primary health care, tourism and higher-value manufacturing [11].
