OpenAI plans $300 doughnut-shaped ChatGPT speaker for 2027

Category: Technology

Bloomberg reported that OpenAI is developing its first consumer hardware device: a screenless smart speaker shaped like a doughnut, roughly the size of a hockey puck, with a launch expected in 2027 [21][2][11][19]. The battery-powered device is designed to be carried around a home with one hand, positioned as an "AI-first" computer for daily tasks, and priced around $300 to $400, according to people familiar with the project cited by Bloomberg [21][10][13][20]. The product is being designed with Jony Ive's LoveFrom studio and is expected to feature premium materials and components, including high-quality metal, speaker grilles, microphones, lights, a camera system and other sensors [4][6][15][42]. Bloomberg reported that it would work similarly to ChatGPT's voice mode but use more advanced models that can learn about a user over time, while moving parts would signal when the device is responding and make interactions feel more lifelike [21][2][11][16]. OpenAI has not officially announced the product name, final price or release date, and CNET said an OpenAI representative did not immediately respond to a request for comment [2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Legal dispute: Apple sued OpenAI in July 2026, alleging trade-secret theft tied partly to metal-finishing techniques; OpenAI denied the claims and asked a court to dismiss the case [15][25][41].
  • Device lineup: OpenAI is reportedly developing several AI-powered consumer products beyond the speaker, including smart glasses, making the speaker an entry point into hardware [12][7].
  • Home controls: The speaker is expected to handle standard smart-speaker tasks such as smart-home control, media playback and answering questions, while adding personalized ChatGPT-style voice interactions [7][11].

Perspectives

Gizmodo: Gizmodo questioned whether an anthropomorphized hockey puck with moving parts would persuade consumers to adopt AI hardware, expressing doubt that the design novelty would translate into practical daily value.

Sources: Gizmodo

Mashable: Mashable likened the device to a cross between a HomePod and a Furby, highlighting its combination of smart-speaker functions and animated physical feedback.

Sources: Mashable

Fast Company: Fast Company said OpenAI faces a difficult smart-speaker market because consumers mostly use existing speakers for music, weather, timers and smart-light controls.

Sources: Fast Company

Timeline

  • 2019: Jony Ive left Apple after leading product design [7]
  • May 2025: OpenAI acquired Ive's io device startup for $6.5 billion [6]
  • July 2026: Apple sued OpenAI over alleged hardware trade secrets [25]
  • 6 August 2026: Bloomberg published new device details from unnamed sources [21]
  • 2027: OpenAI speaker launch is expected during 2027 [21][2]

Industry Impact

  • Smart speakers: Reports citing Bloomberg say Amazon and Google dominate the existing smart-speaker category that OpenAI wants to enter [12][29].
  • Consumer AI devices: OpenAI's reported speaker would enter a hardware category that has already seen AI-first devices struggle, with Meduza noting that two similar devices released in 2025 failed [32].
  • Apple ecosystem: Apple is developing its own home hub with a display and square form factor, while OpenAI's reported device would be screenless and circular [15].

Business Angle

  • At $300 to $400, the speaker would cost more than many mainstream smart speakers and compete with lower-priced smart-home accessories [2][4].
  • A direct hardware channel could reduce OpenAI's dependence on phone platforms for user access, but only if consumers adopt the device as a daily interface [27][21].
  • Mechanical parts, sensors and premium metal materials may raise build costs and repair risks compared with simpler stationary smart speakers [4][15].
  • Apple's lawsuit could delay or complicate the product roadmap if courts accept claims tied to disputed hardware techniques [25][37].
