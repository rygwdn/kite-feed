Google launches Gemini avatars for enterprises in 97 languages

Category: Technology

Google launched Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar on Sept. 24, a feature that generates visual personas that speak and respond with synchronized lip movements and facial expressions. The feature is generally available to Gemini Enterprise customers for customer-service agents and interactive walkthroughs, and Google says it supports 97 languages [8][10][2]. The system combines live speech and video generation and processes audio and visual inputs together, including camera feeds and screen shares. Google is offering the feature on the web, mobile devices and interactive kiosks; the company says it is intended for enterprise use rather than as a consumer product [10][7][4][2].

Primary Source: View original article

As enterprise voice AI evolves beyond basic speed and cost metrics, our priority has shifted to making each interaction even higher quality.

— Fabien Blanc-paques (Google Cloud Blog)

Key Points

  • Background tasks: Agents can make tool and API calls while continuing to speak with users, allowing backend work to continue without pausing the conversation [10][8].
  • Custom avatars: Organizations can select preset avatars or create their own; custom-avatar access is limited to an allowlist [1][10].

Perspectives

Google product manager Fabien Blanc-paques described Live Avatar as adding interactive visuals to Gemini Live’s native speech-to-speech foundation [10].

Sources: Google Cloud Blog

The Register: The Register flagged uncertainty about how human-like computer avatars may affect people, while noting the feature can generate cartoon characters as well as realistic human simulations [5].

Sources: The Register

Slashdot: Android Police argued that voice-only AI conversations can still feel like talking to a phone representative or someone from tech support, rather than a real conversation [1].

Technical Details

  • Agent Development Kit: Android Headlines says developers can use Google's Agent Development Kit to define agent memory and stream audio directly without a conventional speech-to-text setup [7].
