Houthis strike Yemen camps, killing up to 58 troops

Category: World

Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthis launched ballistic missile and drone attacks Thursday on Saudi-backed government forces in Marib and Hadramawt, the deadliest escalation between the sides since the 2022 truce, according to military and medical sources [5][21][45][26]. Death tolls varied: Yemeni officials and government sources reported at least 30 troops killed, medical sources cited by AFP put the toll at at least 38 dead and 29 wounded, and a later military source cited by AFP said at least 58 government troops were killed and dozens wounded [4][7][10][21][45]. The strikes hit camps in al-Ruwaik in Marib and in al-Abr and al-Wadiah in Hadramawt near the Saudi border, with one missile hitting troops during a morning formation and killing or wounding at least 45 personnel, military sources said [7][14][33][45]. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the operation targeted Saudi-backed military build-ups with ballistic missiles and drones, while Yemen’s Defense Ministry said its forces would respond “at the appropriate place and time” [4][15][11][41]. Houthi attacks also hit Saudi Arabia’s Najran region, where the Saudi-led coalition said 11 civilians were wounded: seven Saudis, one Yemeni, two Egyptians and one Pakistani, including a four-year-old child with second-degree burns [69][79][70][87]. Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki accused the Houthis of indiscriminate shelling against civilian objects and said the coalition would continue measures to protect civilians [67][69][95][79].

Key Points

  • Toll dispute: Houthi officials claimed “hundreds” of Saudi-aligned fighters were killed or wounded, but Reuters and other reports said the account could not be independently verified [13][28][41].
  • Regional threat: A senior Saudi official said Saudi, U.S. and regional intelligence indicated the Houthis and Iraqi militias under IRGC supervision could soon target Saudi energy infrastructure, ports and airports [86][64][66][93].
  • Security pact: Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan were due to sign a defense agreement in Jeddah on Friday, with Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif already in the kingdom and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan expected to arrive [21][67][80][86].
  • Maritime pressure: The Houthis declared a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea last month and have claimed attacks on Saudi-linked tankers and shipping routes [13][14][73][31].

Perspectives

Houthi movement: Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree said the group used ballistic missiles and drones against camps in Marib and Hadramawt, claiming the operation killed or wounded “hundreds” of Saudi-backed fighters, destroyed camps, weapons depots and vehicles, and warning Riyadh against further escalation.
