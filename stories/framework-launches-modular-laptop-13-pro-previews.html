Framework launches modular Laptop 13 Pro, previews eGPU kit

Category: Technology

Framework on April 22 unveiled the Laptop 13 Pro, a redesigned version of its repairable 13-inch notebook built around Intel Core Ultra Series 3 "Panther Lake" chips. It also announced quality-of-life updates for the Laptop 16 and previewed an OCuLink development kit for external GPUs [2][14][5][1]. The new 13 Pro keeps much of Framework’s upgrade-friendly philosophy. Reviews and company materials say many parts remain compatible with earlier Laptop 13 generations, including motherboard swaps, though the larger 74Wh battery requires the new bottom cover and some components no longer interchange across every model [2][14][17][10]. Framework opened preorders at $1,199 for the DIY Edition and $1,499 for prebuilt configurations, with first shipments expected in June [4][22][20]. This refresh is less about repairability for its own sake and more about making it feel like a premium mainstream option. Coverage describes the Laptop 13 Pro as adding a new aluminum chassis, haptic trackpad, larger battery, higher-end display, touch support, and options such as factory-loaded Ubuntu. Ars Technica said that makes it Framework’s first officially Ubuntu Certified system [13][6][8][11]. On the 16-inch model, Framework added a cheaper Ryzen AI 5 340 option, new all-in-one keyboard and trackpad modules to address usability complaints, and a 10Gb Ethernet card. It also showed a prototype OCuLink board that uses native PCIe lanes for high-throughput peripherals such as external GPUs [1][15][19].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Framework says you can install the new Laptop 13 Pro motherboard into the original Framework Laptop 13 from 2021, preserving an upgrade path all the way back to the company’s first model [2].

Key Points

  • Linux focus: Framework said the Laptop 13 Pro is its first prebuilt laptop that can ship with Linux from the factory, and Ars Technica reported that the system is officially Ubuntu Certified [13][6][20].
  • Display upgrade: Hands-on reports said the new model brings Framework’s first touchscreen and a brighter panel [14][17].
  • Laptop 16 pricing: The refreshed Laptop 16 now starts at $1,249 for DIY and $1,599 prebuilt with a Ryzen AI 5 340 option, down from $1,499 and $1,799 respectively [15].
  • Port expansion: Framework is also responding to customer requests with a 10Gb Ethernet expansion card and a wireless keyboard accessory announced alongside the laptops [1][22].

Perspectives

Framework: Founder and CEO Nirav Patel said the Laptop 13 Pro reflects years of user feedback and called it the company’s effort to build the ultimate portable machine for developers and power users.

Timeline

  • 22 April 2026: Company announces Laptop 13 Pro and Laptop 16 updates [2][1]
  • June 2026: First Laptop 13 Pro shipments are scheduled to begin [4][22]

Technical Details

  • LPCAMM2 memory: Framework’s product page says the Laptop 13 Pro uses LPCAMM2 memory, a modular laptop memory format meant to deliver LPDDR-class efficiency while remaining replaceable, unlike the soldered memory common in thin notebooks [5].
  • OCuLink: The OCuLink Dev Kit uses an OCuLink connection that Engadget said links directly to the mainboard over native PCIe lanes, giving eGPUs and other peripherals higher-throughput access than typical USB-based external connections [1][19].

Industry Impact

  • Repairable PCs: The launch gives the right-to-repair laptop segment a more premium flagship, with multiple outlets emphasizing that Framework is trying to bring modular notebooks closer to mainstream expectations for build quality and battery life [3][10][11].
  • Linux computing: Official Ubuntu certification and preloaded Linux options could make Linux laptops more accessible to buyers who want vendor-supported hardware rather than a self-installation project [13][6].
  • External graphics: Framework’s OCuLink prototype suggests a path for thinner modular laptops to tap desktop-class graphics externally through direct PCIe connectivity, potentially broadening eGPU use cases beyond Thunderbolt-style docks [1][19][9].

Historical Background

Framework launched its original Laptop 13 in 2021 and built its brand around modular, repairable notebooks with motherboards and parts that owners could replace instead of discarding the whole machine [2][14]. Over the following years, it shipped multiple Intel and AMD mainboard updates while mostly preserving the same 13-inch chassis and cross-generation compatibility [14]. The Laptop 13 Pro is the company’s first broad redesign of that platform, showing how Framework is trying to pair long-term repairability with the premium fit and finish buyers expect from the wider laptop market [14][10].

Action Items

  • Check upgrade compatibility: If you already own a Framework Laptop 13, review which new parts fit your generation before ordering. Reports say the larger 74Wh battery needs the new bottom cover, even though many other parts remain interchangeable [2][14].
  • Compare prebuilt options: If you want Linux out of the box, look at the prebuilt Laptop 13 Pro configurations that can ship with Ubuntu instead of adding Windows separately [13][4].
  • Assess external GPU needs: Laptop 16 owners interested in desktop graphics should keep an eye on the OCuLink Dev Kit, which Framework says is aimed at high-throughput peripherals such as external GPUs [1].
