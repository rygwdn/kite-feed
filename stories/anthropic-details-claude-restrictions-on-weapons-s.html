Anthropic details Claude restrictions on weapons, surveillance and abuse

Category: AI

Anthropic’s revised Claude usage policy sets out restrictions on deceptive campaigns, election interference, weapons-related software and surveillance, along with a new rule against sustained, needless cruelty toward its models. The changes take effect Nov. 12 [72][45]. The company says it has seen attempts to use Claude to build guidance and control software for weapons and to run fake-account networks and fabricated news sites. The clarified rules prohibit software that enables weapons and the arming of drones or other autonomous vehicles. They also prohibit tracking people without consent and using Claude to recommend whom law enforcement should investigate, arrest or charge [72][66][71]. Anthropic also added requirements for Claude-connected hardware capable of causing injury: a qualified operator must be able to monitor the equipment and stop it if necessary [72]. Its policy says Claude’s ability to end persistently abusive conversations remains the primary enforcement method. Ordinary frustration, disagreement, dark creative themes and model testing are exempt [29][15].

Key Points

  • Law-enforcement limits: Claude cannot recommend which people police should investigate, arrest or charge, and tracking people without their consent is prohibited [16].
  • Conversation controls: When Claude ends an abusive exchange on Claude.ai or Claude Code, the thread is locked, but the user can start a separate conversation [52].
  • Election safeguards: The revised rules specifically prohibit false or misleading election information, impersonating candidates or election officials, and efforts to suppress voter turnout [66][1].
  • Targeting exception: Anthropic removed its blanket ban on personalized political targeting because it also covered legitimate work such as nonprofits translating voter information; deceptive targeting and misuse of personal data remain prohibited [16][20].
  • Election safeguards: The policy bars using Claude to deceive voters, impersonate candidates or election officials, or discourage people from voting [20][1].
  • Civic outreach: Anthropic narrowed its election restrictions to deception and disruption, allowing legitimate civic initiatives such as translating voter instructions into other languages [71].
  • Surveillance limits: The policy also prohibits doxxing and building or improving surveillance tools, in addition to non-consensual tracking [72].
