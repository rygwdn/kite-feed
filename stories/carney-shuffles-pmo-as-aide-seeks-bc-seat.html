Carney shuffles PMO as aide seeks B.C. seat

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney is reorganizing senior staff in his office. Scott Gilmore, his senior adviser on foreign, defence and security policy, is set to become principal secretary on July 22 [1][2]. Gilmore replaces Tom Pitfield, who was named to the Senate last week in Carney’s first group of appointments to the upper chamber [1][2]. Maia Johnson will take on a newly created chief operating officer role in the Prime Minister’s Office while remaining senior adviser on Canada-U.S. relations [1][2]. Braeden Caley, Carney’s deputy chief of staff, said Sunday evening he was leaving the role and considering a run for the Liberal nomination in British Columbia [1][2][7]. The only vacant B.C. federal seat is North Vancouver—Capilano, previously held by former Liberal minister Jonathan Wilkinson, who resigned after his April appointment as ambassador to the European Union [7].

I am stepping away as Deputy Chief of Staff to the Prime Minister, with immediate effect, in consideration of seeking a nomination to stand for Parliament in my home province of British Columbia.

Key Points

  • Caley résumé: Before joining Carney’s office, Caley spent six years as Liberal Party communications director and served as national campaign director during the 2025 election [7].
  • Office structure: A senior government official said Johnson’s COO job is a new PMO position and described the change as reflecting Carney’s business background [1][2].
  • Media report: La Presse and The Canadian Press said The Globe and Mail first reported the Johnson and Gilmore appointments on Monday [1][2].
