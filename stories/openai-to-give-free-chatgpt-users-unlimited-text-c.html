OpenAI to give free ChatGPT users unlimited text chats

Category: Technology

OpenAI announced a ChatGPT update on Aug. 6 that makes GPT-5.6 Luna the default model for Free and Go users this week in place of GPT-5.5 Instant, and adds unlimited text-only chats for those tiers next week [20][15][4][3][18]. Free and Go users will also get a “Think” button for harder prompts, while OpenAI said separate limits will still apply to files, images, voice and image generation [20][15][4][14]. Plus and Pro subscribers can use an updated GPT-5.6 Sol in ChatGPT now, with a reasoning slider that lets users choose how much processing effort the model applies to a response [20][9][7][3]. OpenAI said internal evaluations found factual errors were 62% less common with GPT-5.6 Luna and 68% less common with GPT-5.6 Sol than with GPT-5.5 Instant; the Sol update applies to general ChatGPT conversations, while the versions used in Codex and ChatGPT Work remain unchanged [20][15][12][21].

Key Points

  • User scale: ChatGPT recently crossed 1 billion weekly users, so the access change applies to a large consumer service [15][30][24][27].
  • Tier boundaries: ChatGPT Plus costs $20 per month and Pro costs $200 per month, while the Go plan costs $8 per month [14][27].
  • Reasoning control: BleepingComputer observed a slider ranging from Instant to High, with high-reasoning responses potentially taking several minutes for research, coding, writing, planning and complex decisions [7].
  • API economics: OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna API prices by 80% in late July, to $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens [27][30][23].

Perspectives

Apfeltalk: Apfeltalk cautioned that AI systems still do not truly think, understand or feel, and said hallucinations remain part of how such systems can behave despite OpenAI’s reliability claims.

Huxiu: Huxiu argued that OpenAI is making low-cost text chat free to protect user entry points and gather interaction data, while keeping higher-compute work behind paid or metered features.

  • May 2026: GPT-5.5 Instant became the default Free and Go model [4].
  • July 2026: GPT-5.6 models became widely available in ChatGPT and Codex [9].
  • 6 August 2026: OpenAI announced upgraded Sol and broader Luna access [20].
  • 6 August 2026: Updated GPT-5.6 Sol reached Plus and Pro users [15].
  • August 2026: GPT-5.6 Luna becomes Free and Go default this week [3].
  • August 2026: Unlimited text chats and Think button arrive next week [15].

Technical Details

  • Context window: Digital Trends reported GPT-5.6 Luna has a 1,050,000-token context window and a knowledge base cutoff of Feb. 16, 2026 [12].
  • Modality limits: Digital Trends said Luna supports text and media input but does not support audio or video, while free-account media-upload limits still apply [12].

Industry Impact

  • Consumer AI access: Free and Go users gain unlimited text-only ChatGPT sessions, removing text-chat caps that previously produced daily rate-limit messages [14][18][28].
  • AI pricing pressure: CnYES reported OpenAI cut Luna API prices by 80% and Terra prices by 20% in late July, while Sol API pricing stayed at $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output tokens [27].
  • App developers: Huxiu argued that cheaper or free basic chat increases pressure on thin AI wrapper apps, especially those built around low-cost API access rather than differentiated user value [31].

Business Angle

  • The access change gives Free and Go users unlimited text chats; investors should track whether usage converts into Plus, Pro, Go, enterprise or API revenue [15][14][common].
  • Limits on image generation, voice and file tools keep caps on higher-compute features [4][14][common].
  • OpenAI’s lower Luna API prices give customers cheaper lightweight text inference than before [27][30][common].

Action Items

  • Use text-only mode to avoid remaining limits: Keep prompts and replies text-only if you want unlimited Free or Go usage; file uploads, image work, voice and image generation still have separate caps [4][14].
  • Adjust reasoning for task difficulty: Use the Think button on harder questions when it rolls out to Free and Go users, or use the reasoning slider on Plus and Pro for tasks such as research, planning, coding and writing [20][7].
