Anthropic pitches bill setting high bar for blocking risky AI models

Category: AI

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant, has privately presented U.S. lawmakers with a draft bill to regulate frontier AI systems. It would allow the government to stop a model’s release or use only if the model posed a foreseeable, imminent risk of causing more than $1 billion in damage or killing or seriously injuring more than 50 people. A federal judge would have to approve the intervention [2][3][17]. The specified risks involve biological weapons, a model evading its developer’s controls or cyberattacks [17][1]. The proposal would require frontier developers to test their models, disclose findings and undergo independent evaluations. It would give the Commerce Department a role in assessing risks while barring other federal agencies and officials from suspending a model’s development, deployment or internal use [17][1]. Congress has not passed a federal AI law, and U.S. policy has relied largely on voluntary industry commitments and executive orders. Congress is out of session until after the midterm elections next month [17][23].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Anthropic has already granted the nonprofit evaluator METR employee-level access, including access to internal systems and hardware, to assess Claude models [27].

Key Points

  • Assessment deadline: Independent evaluators would register with the Commerce Department and, if they identify a specified imminent risk, report it within 72 hours of completing an assessment. Labs could pay evaluators, but payment could not depend on the evaluation’s outcome [17][1][25].
  • State laws: The bill would displace some state requirements covering risk disclosures, third-party assessments and reporting serious safety incidents [27][17].
  • Safety measures: The draft also calls for frontier labs to maintain safety frameworks, publish transparency reports for new frontier models and protect whistleblowers [27].
  • Rival proposal: Charlie Bullock of the Institute for Law & AI called Anthropic’s draft “pretty reasonable” but said it was weaker than the competing FRONTIER Act introduced in the House this summer [16][17].

Perspectives

Charlie Bullock, Institute for Law & AI: Bullock said Anthropic’s proposal went further than those from other leading AI labs [16].

Sources: PUNCH

President Donald Trump: Trump has dismissed calls to slow AI development as “negative forces,” while Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei recently called for the industry to slow its pace [17][23].

Sources: Channel NewsAsia

Timeline

  • February 2026: The Trump administration barred Anthropic from the Pentagon [17][23].
  • June 2026: Washington forced Anthropic to suspend its most advanced models for nearly three weeks [17][23].
  • 16 October 2026: Anthropic representatives privately pitched lawmakers on the draft bill [2][17].

Historical Background

In June, Anthropic argued that, without a strong federal law, states should retain full authority to regulate AI [17][23]. The company says the draft draws on recommendations in its Advanced AI Framework [17][1].
