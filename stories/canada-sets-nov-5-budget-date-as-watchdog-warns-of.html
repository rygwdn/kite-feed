Canada sets Nov. 5 budget date as watchdog warns of deficits

Category: Canada

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne said the Liberal government will table its 2026 federal budget on Nov. 5. It will be the second budget under Prime Minister Mark Carney and the second since the government shifted budget announcements to the fall [1][3][5]. The budget will set out Ottawa’s fiscal plan as trade tensions with the United States weigh on growth and federal spending commitments outpace some revenue gains [2][5]. The Parliamentary Budget Officer (PBO) now forecasts real economic growth of 0.9% this year and 1.5% in 2027, down from its June projections, and a $63.3-billion deficit in fiscal 2026-27, rising to $70.2 billion the following year [2][3]. The spring update had projected a $65.3-billion deficit for the current fiscal year [1]. The government has pledged to balance its operating budget by 2027, a target the PBO expects Ottawa to miss [3][2].

Key Points

  • Inflation outlook: Annual inflation reached 3.1% in September, while the PBO expects it to average 2.8% in 2026 and 2.4% in 2027 as higher energy prices persist [3].
  • Interest costs: The PBO projects federal public-debt charges will climb from $25.3 billion in 2021-22 to $83 billion in 2030-31 [2].
  • Fiscal anchors: The watchdog forecasts the deficit-to-GDP ratio will rise in 2027-28 and 2029-30, and says Ottawa’s fiscal anchors have imposed limited discipline on spending and borrowing [2].
  • Tax relief: Ottawa stopped collecting the excise tax on gasoline and diesel on April 20 and plans to restore it gradually beginning in February 2027 [4].

Perspectives

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne’s office: Spokesperson John Fragos said the government’s assumptions differ from the PBO’s and the budget will explain its plan to balance the operating budget a year ahead of the watchdog’s forecast [2].
