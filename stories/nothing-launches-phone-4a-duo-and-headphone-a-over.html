Nothing launches Phone 4a duo and Headphone (a) over-ears

Category: Technology

Nothing has unveiled two new midrange smartphones — the Phone 4a and the Phone 4a Pro — alongside its new Headphone (a) over-ear headphones. The company is leaning into playful colors (including pink) while pushing more premium hardware into lower price tiers [4][21][16][22]. The Phone 4a Pro moves to a slimmer metal unibody look and keeps the company’s Glyph-style lighting interface in a brighter “Glyph Matrix” version. The standard Phone 4a, meanwhile, aims for practical upgrades: sturdier protection and a camera setup that tries to outclass typical entry-level phones [2][3][14]. On specs and pricing, sources peg the Phone 4a Pro at $499, with a 6.83-inch AMOLED display (144Hz), peak brightness listed as 5,000 nits, and a 5,080mAh battery [4]. The Phone 4a adds IP64 resistance, Gorilla Glass 7i, and a brighter 6.78-inch display. It also includes a periscope telephoto camera that Nothing says reaches 3.5x optical zoom, plus software-driven “ultra zoom” modes [3][10]. Availability is uneven: multiple outlets say only the Phone 4a Pro will be sold in the US, while the Phone 4a is expected to skip the US entirely [16][8][13]. On the audio side, the $199 Nothing Headphone (a) is chasing value with a bold battery claim — up to 135 hours per charge — plus app support and multiple color options [21][25][23][22]. The throughline across early impressions is straightforward: brighter displays, better durability, longer battery life, and more capable cameras keep trickling down into cheaper devices, giving buyers more fun options without defaulting to the priciest phones [5][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Nothing says the Headphone (a) lasts 135 hours on a charge — nearly triple the 50-hour figure Engadget cites for Sony’s WH-CH520 as a battery-life comparison point [21].

Key Points

  • Design specifics: Hands-ons say the Phone 4a Pro’s redesign isn’t just about losing transparency. It also shifts to a thin full-metal build (7.95mm) and a reshaped, more conventional oblong camera housing — while keeping an updated Glyph Matrix lighting panel [2][14][6].
  • Canada availability: MobileSyrup says the Phone 4a Pro (and Headphone (a)) will be sold in Canada, widening Nothing’s reach beyond its strongest early markets [7].
  • Workday audio: A ZDNET review calls the Headphone (a) a strong value pick for work, pointing to its distinctive design and long battery life as practical all-day strengths [26].

Perspectives

Engadget (hands-on view): Engadget says the Phone 4a Pro feels unusually premium for a midrange device thanks to its thin full-metal build, but notes the new look can feel less distinctive — and even a bit like other mainstream phones [2].

Sources: Engadget

Android Police (design reaction): Android Police argues the Phone 4a Pro’s design choices make it a better fit for people who never liked Nothing’s earlier aesthetic, framing the shift as a deliberate move to broaden its appeal [6].

Sources: Android Police

WIRED (market availability): WIRED notes that Nothing is offering two midrange phones, but says only one will be sold in the US, making the uneven regional rollout a central part of the story [16].

Sources: WIRED

Digital Trends (buyer comparison): Digital Trends contrasts the Phone 4a Pro’s flashy hardware with the Pixel 10a, arguing that first impressions may favor Nothing — but that Google’s midranger can still look like the more practical choice after a closer look [9].

Sources: Digital Trends

Timeline

  • January 2026: Nothing says it will skip a 2026 flagship Phone 4 [4].
  • 5 March 2026: Nothing announces Phone 4a and 4a Pro lineup [4][15][16].
  • 5 March 2026: Nothing unveils Headphone (a) and touts a five-day-plus battery-life claim [21][23][25].

Action Items

  • Choose between 4a and 4a Pro based on where you live: If you’re in the US, look for the Phone 4a Pro (not the 4a), since multiple outlets say only the Pro model will be sold in the US [16][8][13].
  • Sanity-check zoom expectations: If long-range photos matter to you, compare each model’s periscope zoom claims and look for sample shots in hands-ons before buying [3][10].
  • Plan for fewer charges: If you want maximum time between charges, consider the Headphone (a) and its extreme multi-day battery pitch — and confirm whether its fast-charge behavior fits your routine [21][23][25].
