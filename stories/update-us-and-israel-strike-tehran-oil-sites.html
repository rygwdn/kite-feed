Update: US and Israel strike Tehran oil sites

Category: World

In the latest development in the week-old US–Israel–Iran war, Israel and the United States widened their strikes in and around Tehran, hitting fuel and oil storage facilities. Large fires were reported at sites including the Shahran depot, with additional blasts reported near Tehran’s airport and other energy-related locations [9][10][13][7][2]. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Israel would keep fighting, cast the expanded target set as part of an effort to undermine Iran’s governing system, and promised “many surprises” in the next phase [9][6][2][5][14]. Iran, meanwhile, kept up missile and drone attacks that Gulf states say struck or threatened their territories. Reported incidents affected Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, the UAE, and Bahrain; several countries said they intercepted incoming fire and activated emergency responses. Kuwait’s national oil company said it made a precautionary cut to crude production [12][30][31][32][15]. Iran’s President Masoud Pezeshkian publicly rejected President Trump’s demand for “unconditional surrender” and issued an unusual apology to neighboring Gulf states for Iranian strikes. Other Iranian officials, however, signaled more attacks ahead and warned that areas used by “the enemy” could face heavier strikes—underscoring apparent tensions inside Tehran after the killing of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei that preceded the conflict [17][16][20][22][21][27]. Even as the fighting intensified, a few diplomatic and protective signals stood out. Saudi Arabia, according to sources cited by Reuters partners, told Iran it prefers a diplomatic settlement but warned it could retaliate if strikes on the kingdom and its energy sector continue [24][26][25]. Gulf governments also emphasized civil defense, air-defense activity, and infrastructure protection as the conflict’s impact spreads beyond Israel and Iran [30][15][12].

Key Points

  • Gulf infrastructure: Bahrain said a desalination plant was hit as the conflict spilled across the region, underscoring the risk to essential services—not just military targets [12].
  • Baghdad incident: Live conflict updates said air defenses intercepted a rocket attack over the U.S. embassy in Baghdad, adding to security concerns in Iraq [3].
  • Airspace claim: Netanyahu said Israeli and U.S. strikes had given them “almost complete control” of Tehran’s airspace, as Israel urged Iranians to treat the escalation as a “moment of truth” [14].
  • Strike scope: Live updates and officials cited in coverage said U.S.-Israeli strikes hit five oil-related sites in or near Tehran in the latest wave [1][8].
  • US escalation rhetoric: Reuters-partner reporting quoted Trump rejecting a negotiated settlement and suggesting the campaign could make talks irrelevant if Iran’s military is destroyed and potential leaders are killed [36].

Perspectives

Government of Israel (Netanyahu): Netanyahu said Israel would continue the war “with all our force,” claimed Israeli and U.S. pilots had near-complete control of Tehran’s airspace, and again framed the wider target set as a push to topple Iran’s ruling system—while promising “many surprises” ahead.

Sources: The Hindu, Le Monde

Government of Iran (Pezeshkian): Pezeshkian dismissed Trump’s demand for “unconditional surrender” as a “dream,” issued a rare apology to Gulf neighbours, and said Iran would stop striking them unless attacks on Iran came from their territory.

Sources: The Guardian, The Hindu

United States (President Trump): Trump said the war would end only when Iran’s leaders “cry uncle,” and separately vowed the U.S. would hit Iran “very hard,” saying he has no timetable for when the fighting will stop.

Sources: Al Jazeera

Saudi Arabia (official message via sources): Saudi Arabia told Iran it wants a diplomatic settlement but warned that continued attacks on the kingdom and its energy sector could push Riyadh to retaliate. Sources said the message was delivered before Pezeshkian’s televised apology to Gulf neighbours.

Sources: Al-Monitor

Industry Impact

  • Refining risk: Live updates said Iran’s Guards targeted Israel’s Haifa refinery, showing how the conflict has widened from storage sites to energy-processing facilities [3].

Historical Background

Iran’s political system has long centered power in the office of the supreme leader, a role held by Ayatollah Ali Khamenei since 1989 [common]. His killing has raised an abrupt succession question, with reporting pointing to interim decision-making structures operating as the war unfolds [28].
