Update: Iranian protests spread as leader vows crackdown

Category: World

Protests driven by anger over Iran’s struggling economy widened into Tehran and multiple other cities as clashes intensified in western regions of the country [1][3][4][6]. Rights groups reported additional deaths in the west during confrontations with security forces, while some outlets put the overall toll in the low-to-mid teens, including members of the security forces [2][4][1][7][10]. Iran’s Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei publicly backed a tougher response to what he called “rioters,” as the movement entered about a week of unrest [7][10][9]. The unrest also unfolded amid ongoing international tension around Iran’s nuclear program, which ABC News noted remains a backdrop to the country’s heightened political pressure [6].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: The reports described the current unrest as the most significant protest wave since Iran’s 2022–2023 movement, according to activists cited by Al-Monitor [4].

Key Points

  • How it began: Local media described the initial spark as a Dec 28 strike by shopkeepers over economic concerns, which then widened into broader demonstrations [3].
  • Scale compared: Al-Monitor said these are the most significant protests since the 2022–2023 movement that ended with hundreds killed and thousands arrested, according to activists [4].
  • Rights claims: Two rights groups cited by Al-Monitor accused the Revolutionary Guards of opening fire on demonstrators during Saturday’s clashes in western Iran [4].
  • Ilam reports: Hengaw reported four additional protesters shot dead in Malekshahi County in Iran’s Ilam province [5].
  • US messaging: Japan Today reported President Donald Trump threatened to come to the aid of protesters, and Khamenei responded that he would not yield [8].

  • Shopkeeper strikes: A shopkeeper strike typically uses business closures to pressure authorities by disrupting local commerce without requiring mass gatherings, and local media linked this tactic to the protests’ origin [3][common].

Historical Background

Iran has experienced repeated waves of nationwide protests in recent years, including a major 2022–2023 movement that authorities ultimately suppressed after large-scale casualties and arrests, according to activists cited by Al-Monitor [4]. Several of the latest reports frame the current unrest as rooted in cost-of-living pressures and wider political frustrations, echoing prior episodes where economic strain has spilled into street protests [1][4][6].
