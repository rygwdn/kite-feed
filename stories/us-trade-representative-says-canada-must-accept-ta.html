U.S. trade representative says Canada must accept tariffs

Category: Canada

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said Canada should expect higher U.S. tariffs to be part of any new arrangement with Washington. He framed that as a condition for reaching a deal — and as a way to support reshoring American jobs and industries [1][4][2]. The remarks land as Canada and the United States head into a period of negotiation and review of the Canada-U.S.-Mexico trade pact (CUSMA/USMCA) [2][3]. For Canada, the message adds near-term uncertainty for exporters and for supply chains that cross the border. Canadian business groups also pointed to a recent U.S. Supreme Court decision affecting tariffs as a modest bright spot, even as CUSMA concerns persist ahead of the formal review [3].

Key Points

  • Market access: Greer also said any deal with Canada would need to give Americans more market access, adding another demand alongside tariffs [4].
  • Upcoming meeting: Le Devoir reported Greer is expected to meet soon with Canadian minister Dominic LeBlanc, with CUSMA renegotiation in the background [2].
  • Court constraint: Business groups said the Supreme Court decision matters because it narrows the scope for broad ‘global’ tariff actions, which could push future U.S. tariff moves toward more targeted measures — though uncertainty remains [3].

Historical Background

Canada-U.S. trade has been governed by NAFTA since 1994 and by its successor agreement (CUSMA/USMCA) since 2020 [common]. CUSMA includes a built-in review mechanism that can reopen negotiations and, for businesses, raise policy risk as review deadlines approach [3][2].
