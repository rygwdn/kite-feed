NASA targets early April launch for Artemis II

Category: Science

NASA is aiming to launch Artemis II as soon as April 1 from Kennedy Space Center in Florida, with a six-day launch window opening that day [1][3][6]. The mission would send four astronauts on a crewed flight around the Moon — the first crewed lunar mission since Apollo 17 in 1972 [1][6][9]. NASA says it has addressed the technical issues that pushed back earlier launch opportunities, and the mission has been cleared to move toward the next attempt as preparations continue into late March [6][2][3].

Primary Source: View original article

We are on track for a launch as early as April 1, and we are working toward that date.

— Lori Glaze (The Guardian)

Key Points

  • Crew lineup: Artemis II’s crew is listed as Reid Wiseman (commander), Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Canadian astronaut Jeremy Hansen [8].
  • Review milestone: Space.com reports NASA has completed the Artemis II flight readiness review before setting the new target [3].
  • Near-term operations: Scientific American reports NASA plans to roll the rocket back out later this month as it works toward the April 1 target [2].
  • Public update: Space.com notes NASA held a mission update on March 12 and streamed it live to share the latest readiness information [4].

Perspectives

NASA (mission leadership): NASA says the corrective work is complete, and teams are moving through final preparations for the next Artemis II attempt [3].
