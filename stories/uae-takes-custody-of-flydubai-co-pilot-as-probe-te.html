UAE takes custody of Flydubai co-pilot as probe tests Iran claims

Category: World

The United Arab Emirates took custody of the Flydubai co-pilot accused of stabbing the captain and trying to bring down Flight FZ1073 as investigators examined the attack on the Dubai-to-Tel Aviv flight. The aircraft, carrying 174 people, plunged nearly 14,000 feet in less than 30 seconds over Saudi Arabia. The injured captain managed to open the cockpit door, passengers restrained the co-pilot and off-duty pilots landed the plane in Tabuk on Sept. 30 [13][21][14][35]. The UAE opened an investigation into whether the incident involved terrorism, prior planning or outside direction, asserting jurisdiction because the aircraft is registered there. Saudi Arabia, Israel, Oman and the United States are also assisting. The suspect, whom Israeli officials identify as an Omani national, has not been publicly named. Investigators have not established a motive [21][16][66]. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the suspect’s background indicated radical Islamist indoctrination but stressed that conclusions were not final and it was too early to determine whether Iran was involved [17][44]. President Donald Trump said Iran was likely linked to the attack, without offering details, and warned it would be “hit very hard” if responsible. Flydubai suspended service to Israel during the investigation; the suspect is now in UAE custody [15][6][19][35][77].

Key Points

  • Weapon questions: Investigators are trying to establish what weapon was used. Netanyahu raised the possibility of an emergency crash axe normally kept in the cockpit, while passengers described a knife [24][50].
  • Early assessment: Two Israeli security officials said their initial assessment was that the co-pilot likely acted alone. Mental-health concerns had not been ruled out [21][12].
  • Passenger awards: Israel announced heroism honors for five passengers who helped overpower the co-pilot and prevent the aircraft from crashing [107][111].
  • Captain recovering: India’s embassy said Captain Smit Machchhar was recovering well after being transferred to the UAE for further treatment [10][35][100].

Industry Impact

  • Crew screening: Former Israeli national security adviser Eyal Hulata said screening has traditionally focused more on passengers and baggage than on crew. He predicted crew members would face stricter checks after the incident [140].
  • Flight operations: El Al announced special flights to bring Israelis home from Dubai [19][105].

Historical Background

Israeli rules reportedly bar airlines from assigning crew members from countries without diplomatic relations with Israel to flights serving the country. Reports said enforcement was largely left to foreign airlines and that dozens of Omani pilots had flown to Tel Aviv despite the restriction [132][83].
