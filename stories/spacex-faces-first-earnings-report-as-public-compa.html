SpaceX faces first earnings report as public company after 50% slide

Category: Business

SpaceX is scheduled to release its first quarterly results as a public company after the U.S. market closes on Tuesday, Aug. 4, less than two months after its June IPO [7][14][1]. Shares traded around $110 on Monday, about 19% below the $135 offer price and 51% below the June 16 intraday high of $225.64. The debut was the largest IPO in U.S. history [7][10][17]. Investors are watching whether revenue from Starlink and new AI infrastructure contracts can support spending on Starship, satellites and data-center ambitions while the company remains loss-making [5][18][14]. Analysts cited by AFP expect a second-quarter loss of $1.9 billion on revenue of $6.8 billion [14]. The earnings call precedes the first expiration of SpaceX’s post-IPO lockup, which will let employees and early investors sell some shares later this week [5][6][24]. Reports citing the lockup terms said up to about 912 million shares could become eligible for sale, a supply increase investors will watch two days after the results [12][16][24].

Did you know: Fast Company compared SpaceX’s post-IPO decline with Facebook’s 2012 debut, when the stock fell 54% and ended that year down 32% [10].

Key Points

  • AI spending: Reuters figures cited by Biznis.rs put expected second-quarter AI capital spending above $10 billion; Fast Company said SpaceX is spending about $40 billion a year on AI infrastructure and its rocket program [18][10].
  • Analyst split: Morgan Stanley reportedly kept a $300 price target while estimating second-quarter revenue of $6.75 billion, 12 million consumer users and an adjusted loss of $0.35 per share [12].
  • Starship status: The July Starship test reached orbit, but some landing thrusters failed to ignite normally, according to Asiae’s account of the 13th test flight [17].
  • Index exposure: SpaceX joined the Nasdaq 100 in July, adding the stock to funds and retirement accounts that track the index [14].

Perspectives

Morgan Stanley: The bank reportedly called the reporting window SpaceX’s most dangerous moment since listing, but still assigned more than half of its $300-per-share valuation model to enterprise AI.

Sources: EDigest

Analysts cited by Reuters: Some expect AI business revenue to nearly triple in the second quarter, but capital spending in that segment will exceed $10 billion, according to Reuters.

Sources: Biznis.rs

SpaceX investors: Investors are expected to question Elon Musk on Starship’s test schedule, satellite-network plans, orbital data-center ideas and unconfirmed talk of a possible Tesla combination.

Sources: ABC News

Timeline

  • 12 June 2026: SpaceX listed publicly at $135 per share [7][10].
  • 16 June 2026: Shares touched an intraday high of $225.64 [7][10].
  • July 2026: SpaceX entered the Nasdaq 100 index [14].
  • 24 July 2026: Starship lifted off from Starbase for a test flight [5][9].
  • 4 August 2026: SpaceX was scheduled to report second-quarter results after the market close [7][1].
  • 6 August 2026: First insider lockup release scheduled for Thursday [6][24].

Technical Details

  • Staggered lockup release: SpaceX structured the lockup to avoid a single rush of sales, but the Financial Times said the staged approach leaves investors with 12 months of uncertainty over insider selling [4].
  • Enterprise AI valuation: Morgan Stanley’s reported model assigned $152 per share to enterprise AI, $128 to network and Starlink operations, $12 to X and Grok AI, and $8 to traditional space operations [12].

Industry Impact

  • Satellite internet: Analysts cited by Biznis.rs estimated Starlink’s second-quarter revenue at about $3.82 billion and operating profit at about $1.42 billion [18].
  • AI infrastructure: SpaceX has signed AI computing infrastructure contracts with Anthropic, Google and Reflection AI, according to Biznis.rs [18].
  • Market cap: SpaceX has shed over $500 billion in market value since its peak, making it one of the largest losses of market value in a debut month [10].

Historical Background

Elon Musk founded SpaceX in 2002 with the goal of lowering launch costs and developing reusable rockets [common]. NASA selected SpaceX’s Starship system in 2021 as the lunar lander for its Artemis program, tying part of the company’s rocket-development timeline to U.S. human spaceflight plans [common].

Business Angle

  • Analysts are watching whether Starlink and AI contracts can produce recurring cash after heavy capital spending [18][10].
  • Short interest in SpaceX has surged to over 219 million shares, more than a third of the free float, showing short sellers have built large positions ahead of the report [19].
  • Musk is expected to emulate Tesla's earnings call style, focusing on long-term vision rather than near-term financial metrics [12].
  • Morgan Stanley’s reported valuation gives more weight to enterprise AI than to traditional space operations, making execution in a newer business line central to the stock case [12].

Action Items

  • Check index-fund exposure: Review whether Nasdaq 100-tracking funds in your portfolio added SpaceX after its July index inclusion [14].
  • Separate earnings from lockup effects: Compare the Aug. 4 operating results with trading after the Aug. 6 lockup release before treating price moves as a judgment on the business [7][6].
