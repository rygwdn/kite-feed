US payrolls beat forecasts, raise Fed rate concerns

Category: Business

U.S. nonfarm payrolls rose by 162,000 in August, above expectations of 55,000 cited by Anadolu and 56,000 cited by Tőzsdefórum, while unemployment held at 4.1% and the number of unemployed people was about 7 million [2][3][7][9][10][11]. The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the data Friday, and several accounts described the report as a rebound from weaker summer hiring [9][10][11]. Major U.S. stock indexes fell Friday as investors weighed the chance of another Fed rate hike. President Trump criticized the market reaction, the Fed and U.S. trade partners from the Oval Office [3][7]. Jonathan Golub of Seaport Research Partners told Bloomberg the stronger jobs report reinforced the case for a Fed rate increase despite President Trump's calls for lower borrowing costs [13].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: A CNBC item listed by Google News said women accounted for almost all of August's job gains [4].

Key Points

  • Wages rose: Average hourly earnings increased by 10 cents, or 0.3%, to $37.75, leaving pay up 3.1% from a year earlier [9][2].
  • Labor supply: The labor-force participation rate rose to 61.6% from 61.4%, while involuntary part-time employment fell by 414,000 to 4.4 million [11].
  • Stocks retreated: The S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,718.13, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.30% to 26,505.44, and the Dow lost 0.54% to 53,398.15 [7].
  • Sector split: Food services and drinking places added 59,000 jobs, local government education added 42,000, manufacturing added 16,000, and information lost 23,000 [9].

Perspectives

President Donald Trump: Trump cited the jobs number in support of his economic message but objected to the view that stronger hiring could add to inflation pressure.

Sources: ABC News

Jonathan Golub, Seaport Research Partners: Golub said the payroll report strengthened the argument for a Fed hike and said longer-term bond yields worried him because AI investment and government borrowing compete for capital.

Sources: Bloomberg

MarketWatch: MarketWatch framed the jobs report as favorable for bonds and argued that Wall Street commentary overstated how strong the labor market felt for working Americans.

Sources: MarketWatch

Timeline

  • June 2026: June payroll growth was revised to 31,000 jobs, up from an earlier estimate of 20,000 [11].
  • July 2026: July payrolls were revised up by 44,000 to a gain of 21,000, reversing an initially reported drop of 21,000 [2][11].
  • 5 September 2026: U.S. stocks fell after the August jobs report [7].
  • 11 September 2026: The August CPI report is scheduled for release [12].

Business Angle

  • Rate-sensitive businesses face valuation risk if stronger labor data gives the Fed more reason to keep policy tight or raise rates [13][common].
  • The reported wage increase is manageable for companies that can raise prices or improve productivity, but it pressures firms with thin margins and weak pricing power [2][common].
  • The information-sector job loss contrasts with overall hiring strength and gives investors a reason to check whether tech-related cost cuts are company-specific or sector-wide [9][common].
