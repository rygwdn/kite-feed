International Observe the Moon Night draws global skywatchers

Category: Science

International Observe the Moon Night was scheduled for Saturday, Sept. 19, 2026, inviting people worldwide to observe Earth’s nearest celestial neighbor with telescopes and other activities [2][3]. The annual event has been held since 2010 and is scheduled near the Moon’s first-quarter phase, when sunlight creates long shadows that make craters, ridges and mountain peaks easier to see [2][3]. In September 2026, the Moon reached its first-quarter phase on Sept. 18, leaving a waxing gibbous Moon visible during the event the following evening [2]. Western Nevada College’s Jack C. Davis Observatory scheduled a free viewing session at 6:30 p.m., led by observatory director Thomas Herring, who was also slated to discuss lunar history, statistics and NASA’s Artemis effort to return astronauts to the Moon [3].

Did you know: An illuminated lunar mountain peak or crater rim can appear as an isolated speck of light beyond the terminator on the otherwise dark side of the Moon [2].

Key Points

  • Seasonal timing: International Observe the Moon Night is held each fall in the Northern Hemisphere [2]
  • Reference appearance: An EarthSky community photo showed a waxing gibbous Moon at 65% luminance, close to how the Moon was expected to appear on Sept. 19 [2]
  • Lunar distance: The Moon is nearly 240,000 miles from Earth [3]

Timeline

  • 2010: International Observe the Moon Night began as an annual event [2]
  • 18 September 2026: The Moon reached its first-quarter phase before the global observing night [2]
  • 19 September 2026: The worldwide observing event and Nevada telescope program were scheduled for this date [2][3]

Action Items
