UK team proposes lunar satellite to study early universe

Category: Science

A UK-led research team is proposing a suitcase-sized satellite called CosmoCube to orbit the moon and search for the 21cm radio signal from neutral hydrogen atoms in the early universe [3][2][5]. The spacecraft would gather data while passing over the far side of the moon, using the moon as a shield from FM radio and other Earth-based technological interference that hampers observations from or near Earth [3][4]. The mission concept targets the poorly observed interval before the first stars appeared, about 150 million years after the big bang, and could look back roughly 13.5 billion years [2][4]. Prof Eloy de Lera Acedo of the University of Cambridge’s Cavendish Laboratory said the 21cm signal would let scientists track the physical temperature of gas in the early universe [3][5].

The main thing we track by looking at this signal is what’s the physical temperature of the gas during the early universe

— Prof Eloy de Lera Acedo (The Guardian)

Key Points

  • Signal behavior: Redshift has stretched older 21cm signals to longer wavelengths, allowing researchers to compare their strength over time with the cosmic microwave background [3][5].

Scientific Significance

  • Limits of proposal: The articles describe CosmoCube as a proposed mission concept rather than an operating spacecraft, so observations depend on launching the satellite and obtaining enough clean far-side observing time [3][4].
