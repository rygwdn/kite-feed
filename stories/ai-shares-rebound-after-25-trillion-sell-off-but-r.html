AI shares rebound after $2.5 trillion sell-off, but risks persist

Category: AI

U.S. technology shares rebounded Friday after a Thursday sell-off wiped roughly $2.5 trillion from Wall Street market value, as investors priced in the prospect that Democratic control of Congress could bring tighter scrutiny of AI and data-center expansion [12][10]. Democrats have outlined proposals on data-center electricity costs, independent testing of advanced AI systems and companies’ liability for harm, while President Donald Trump supports AI development [28][36]. Thursday’s losses were concentrated in technology: the Nasdaq fell about 5.5% and the S&P 500 dropped 4.4%. Friday’s Nasdaq gain of 1.1% and S&P 500 rise of 0.3% recovered only part of those losses [17][12]. Chipmakers and Asian technology markets also fell sharply, with South Korea’s Kospi and Japan’s Nikkei each closing down about 6% Friday [12]. Investors face political uncertainty, high borrowing costs, heavy infrastructure spending and doubts about when AI investments will generate sufficient returns [16][13]. Economist Bruno Cavalier of Oddo BHF said data-center spending contributed 30% of U.S. growth in 2025 and 2026 [10]. The incoming Congress’s actions from January 2027 and the market reception of Anthropic’s planned initial public offering remain uncertain [28][21].

Key Points

  • Chip losses: The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell more than 12% in one session, while South Korea’s Kospi briefly halted trading for 20 minutes after a steep drop [2][16].
  • Buildout exposure: UBS strategists estimate that data-center delays could put nearly one-third of U.S. computing capacity projected to come online in 2028 at risk [16].
  • Debt pressure: AI-computing provider CoreWeave fell 20% over the week and stood more than 75% below its June 2025 peak; Nebius was down 58% from its June high [16].
  • IPO uncertainty: Anthropic has reportedly sought to raise as much as $100 billion at a valuation of up to $2 trillion, while prediction markets lowered the odds of an IPO announcement by year-end [10][21].
  • Market mechanics: Trend-following funds and long-only managers deepened Thursday’s decline as they cut positions, while Friday’s U.S. rebound was smaller than the losses in Asian markets [21][12].

  • Semiconductors: Memory suppliers Sandisk, Micron and Western Digital each lost between 17% and 22% on November 5 [13].

  • AI financing: OpenAI has pushed its IPO plans to next year [10].

  • 🇯🇵 Japan: Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said her government was monitoring steep declines in AI and chip shares and would focus on measures to build a strong economy [12].
  • 🇰🇷 South Korea: The finance and economy minister convened a meeting on the market impact, and officials said they would prepare measures to prevent excessive reactions in Korean stocks [12].

Q&A

Q: What near-term indicators could clarify the outlook?

A: U.S. inflation data is due Tuesday, November 10, followed by Nvidia’s results the next week. The inflation report could inform the interest-rate outlook, while Nvidia’s results could indicate the strength of chip demand [19].

Q: How could a prolonged market downturn affect households?

A: A sustained decline could reduce household wealth and spending: 43% of U.S. household wealth is exposed to the stock market, and the richest 10% of Americans account for half of consumer spending [10].
