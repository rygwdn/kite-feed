Maduro seeks dismissal of U.S. drug charges

Category: World

Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, asked a U.S. federal judge in Manhattan on Wednesday to dismiss drug trafficking charges, arguing that they have immunity as Venezuela’s head of state and first lady [2][8][1][10]. Maduro’s lawyer, Barry Pollack, filed the request before U.S. District Judge Alvin Hellerstein, who had set Wednesday as the deadline for the immunity motion [6][36][48]. The Manhattan federal charges were the basis for the U.S. military raid in Caracas on January 3 that captured Maduro. He has been held in a Brooklyn federal jail, has pleaded not guilty and is scheduled for trial on June 1, 2027, if the dismissal effort fails [6][7][9][13][44]. Flores also faces drug trafficking charges, and both she and Maduro have filed immunity arguments tied to their status under Venezuelan law [8][1][32]. Maduro’s lawyers wrote that no U.S. court has presided over a criminal trial of a foreign leader recognized by his own country as a sitting head of state when charges were brought, and said only Venezuelan courts can prosecute them [8][1][10]. Prosecutors have until October 2 to respond, and Hellerstein has scheduled oral arguments on the dismissal motions for November 17 [18][24][51][57].

Did you know: Lawyers cited Manuel Noriega’s case as the closest precedent because U.S. troops captured him in Panama in 1989 before he faced a U.S. drug trial [57][47].

Key Points

  • Two motions: Pollack sought dismissal of the full indictment on sovereign-immunity grounds and filed a fallback motion to dismiss only the narcoterrorism conspiracy count [16][17][19][27].
  • Specific charges: Spanish- and Portuguese-language reports said Maduro is accused of narcoterrorism conspiracy, cocaine importation and possession of automatic weapons [16][17][26][50].
  • Legal precedent: Several reports cited the 1990 Manuel Noriega case, in which a Miami federal judge rejected the former Panamanian leader’s head-of-state immunity claim partly because he had never formally held the presidency [47][51][57].

Perspectives
