Ring ends Flock Safety camera-link deal after ad backlash

Category: Technology

Amazon-owned Ring called off a planned partnership with surveillance-technology company Flock Safety after a Super Bowl commercial drew privacy backlash over neighborhood-scale monitoring [2][1]. The ad promoted a feature branded “Search Party,” pitched as a way to find lost pets by tapping nearby Ring doorbells. Critics said the same kind of camera-linking could enable broader searching and tracking than many users expect [2][4]. The blowback has revived a familiar set of questions: where smart-home video ends up, who can get access to it, and how tools sold as safety and convenience can slide into wider surveillance [3].

Key Points

  • What the canceled tie-up would have done: Coverage described a Ring integration with Flock Safety that would have linked camera networks across neighborhoods, sharpening worries about cross-platform, community-scale searching [2][1].
  • How the backlash was framed: Futurism cast the commercial as a Super Bowl marketing misfire and said the controversy helped push Ring to cancel the partnership [4].
  • The bigger access question: The Guardian focused on what smart-doorbell cameras collect — and whether law enforcement can view footage without users realizing it [3].
  • Other security-news context: Wired noted the Ring/Flock cancellation in a broader surveillance roundup that also highlighted Meta’s reported plans to add face recognition to its smart glasses [1].
