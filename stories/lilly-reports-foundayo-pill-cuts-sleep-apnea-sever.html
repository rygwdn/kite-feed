Lilly reports Foundayo pill cuts sleep apnea severity in trials

Category: Science

Eli Lilly said Nov. 9 that its daily obesity pill Foundayo reduced the severity of obstructive sleep apnea in two 52-week phase 3 trials involving more than 700 adults with overweight or obesity and moderate-to-severe disease. Severity was 36% to 44% lower than with placebo among participants not using positive airway pressure (PAP) therapy and 26% to 28% lower among those continuing PAP. Participants lost about 10% to 11% of their body weight [5][1][6]. Lilly plans to seek U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval for Foundayo to treat sleep apnea by the end of 2026. The pill is already approved for chronic weight management, but not sleep apnea. The company’s injectable drug Zepbound is approved for sleep apnea in people with obesity [3][6].

There are no current oral treatments for sleep apnea. It’s usually [treated with] a sleep apnea mask or oral device, which can be very hard for people to use. This could be a great alternative or even an add-on for them

— Michael Grandner, director of the sleep and health research program at the University of Arizona (Healio)

Key Points

  • Remission: Among participants not using PAP, 31.5% of those taking Foundayo met criteria for remission or improvement to mild sleep apnea [6].
  • Study setting: The two trials ran at 66 centers across North America, South America, Europe and Asia, and participants received diet and lifestyle counseling [5].
  • Side effects: Gastrointestinal problems, including nausea, diarrhea, constipation and vomiting, were the most common side effects; 12% of participants not using PAP stopped Foundayo because of side effects, compared with 3.8% on placebo [6].

Perspectives

Daniel Drucker, an endocrinologist at the Lunenfeld-Tanenbaum Research Institute, said questions about overall and cardiovascular safety arise whenever researchers modify the GLP-1 drug structure. He was not involved in the trial and previously consulted for Lilly [3].

Sources: Scientific American

Vaishnavi Kundel, an associate professor of medicine at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, said sleep apnea is strongly and independently associated with cardiovascular disease, including hypertension, diabetes, heart attack and stroke [5].

Timeline

  • December 2024: FDA approved Zepbound as the first medication for obstructive sleep apnea [6]
  • April 2026: FDA approved Foundayo for chronic weight management [3][5]
  • 9 November 2026: Lilly reported results from its two phase 3 sleep-apnea trials [1][5]
  • December 2026: Lilly plans to submit Foundayo sleep-apnea data to the FDA by year-end [6][3]

Technical Details

  • Apnea-hypopnea index: The primary endpoint measured the number of times per hour that breathing stopped or became restricted. Foundayo reduced this measure by 18 to 21 events per hour in participants not using PAP, compared with four to five with placebo [5].
  • Hypoxic burden: This trial measure captures how far oxygen levels fall during sleep and how long they remain low. Lilly reported reductions of 55.9% without PAP and 45.8% with PAP, while the trial report summarized reductions of 72% versus placebo in both groups [6][5].

Scientific Significance

  • Clinical results: The trials showed improvements in respiratory events and cardiometabolic measures. Systolic blood pressure fell by 7 to 8 mm Hg with Foundayo (orforglipron), compared with 1 to 3 mm Hg with placebo [5].
  • Evidence limits: The studies lasted 52 weeks and excluded people with diabetes or recent incretin-based treatment [5].
