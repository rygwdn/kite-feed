Cyclone Arnab hits India, nearly 100,000 evacuated across two states

Category: World

Cyclone Arnab crossed India’s southeastern coast early Wednesday, forcing nearly 100,000 people from vulnerable areas in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha. The India Meteorological Department classified it as a “very severe cyclonic storm,” with winds of 125-135 kph and gusts reaching 150 kph. It later weakened to a severe cyclonic storm as it moved inland. No casualties were reported in the initial updates as officials assessed damage [2][3][12]. Heavy rain and winds disrupted power and transport. The weather department warned of flooding, damage to homes and crops, and a storm surge of 1.5 to 2.5 metres above normal tide in low-lying areas [2][12]. Sri Lanka closed schools over flood and landslide risks, while Nepal warned that Arnab could bring heavy rain, snowstorms and avalanches to Himalayan areas from Thursday night through Saturday. Nepal’s disaster authority urged tourists to avoid high-altitude travel [5][6][13].

Key Points

  • Emergency response: Andhra Pradesh officials said they established more than 2,000 shelters across 20 districts, secured over 30,000 fishing boats and deployed nearly 1,100 medical teams [3][12].
  • Medical evacuations: More than 4,700 pregnant women in areas at risk were transferred to hospitals before the storm [6][16].
  • Transport disruption: Before landfall, authorities cancelled at least 12 trains and suspended rail services on 53 southern-coast routes [13][21].
  • Coastal exposure: Andhra Pradesh officials said more than 12 million people live along the cyclone’s expected path [3][10].

Perspectives

Nara Chandrababu Naidu, chief minister of Andhra Pradesh, said officials had been instructed to monitor the storm continuously [7][6].

Sources: Dawn, RFI

Rajeshwari, a resident evacuated near Kakinada, said her home floods, forcing her family to stand in water. She said the government provided a bus to take them to a shelter [2][19].

Timeline

  • 26 October 2026: Arnab formed over the Bay of Bengal, India’s weather office said [13].
  • 27 October 2026: Authorities ordered evacuations and closed schools before landfall [6][13].
  • 28 October 2026: The storm crossed Andhra Pradesh’s coast and weakened to a severe cyclonic storm; officials expected it to weaken further as it moved inland [3].

Industry Impact

  • Agriculture: Konaseema district, identified as the worst-hit area in early assessments, suffered extensive damage to banana and coconut plantations [12].
  • Electricity and roads: Officials said about 6,000 electricity poles were uprooted in Konaseema. Fallen trees blocked roads as crews worked to restore power and clear routes [12].

Historical Background

A super cyclone that struck Odisha in October 1999 killed nearly 10,000 people and remains one of India’s deadliest natural disasters [2]. Cyclones are a recurring threat in the Bay of Bengal and the northern Indian Ocean [5].

International Reactions

  • 🇱🇰 Sri Lanka: Sri Lanka closed schools, warned of flash floods and mudslides, and placed its military on alert [6][5].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: People already in high-altitude areas should remain in safe locations during the warning period [6].

Q&A

Q: Which travel services did officials expect the storm to affect most?

A: India’s civil aviation ministry said flights to Andhra Pradesh and Odisha were expected to be among the most affected [2].
