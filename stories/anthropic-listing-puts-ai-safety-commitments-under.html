Anthropic listing puts AI safety commitments under shareholder scrutiny

Category: AI

Anthropic, the San Francisco-based developer of the Claude models, began trading on Nasdaq on Nov. 19 after a record $81.3 billion IPO that made it the first frontier AI lab to go public [12][33]. An interview on Ticker News focused on whether a company identified with AI safety can meet Wall Street’s earnings expectations [15]. Anthropic’s co-founder and chief executive, Dario Amodei, has warned about risks from misaligned AI and called for regulation, while cheaper open-source models could challenge demand for paid systems such as Claude [25][6]. Trading in Anthropic options began Monday, Nov. 23. About 500,000 contracts had changed hands by 3 p.m. Eastern, with calls and puts traded in roughly equal numbers. Options prices implied daily share moves of about 6%, according to Reuters [14]. Reuters reported that weekly options contracts, popular with retail traders, could begin trading in the coming days [14].

Key Points

  • Enterprise uptake: Anthropic reported 11,900 customers spending at least $100,000 annually on its products as of Sept. 30, up from 1,500 at the end of 2025; its net revenue retention rate was 900% [2].
  • Internal adoption: Claude generated about 90% of additions to Anthropic’s codebase during September 2026, and average daily code output per engineer in the third quarter was 45 times its 2024 average [2].
  • Financial results: Anthropic reported $33.2 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2026, up 1,206% year over year, and $5.6 billion in adjusted non-GAAP profit, compared with a $2.9 billion adjusted loss a year earlier [2].

Technical Details

  • AI-assisted model development: An early-November assessment by independent research firm METR found that Anthropic’s models were improving on certain benchmark tests almost twice as fast as a year earlier. The report said added computing power alone did not explain the acceleration and pointed to AI’s role in helping Anthropic improve its models [30].

Industry Impact

  • AI investment products: Issuers launched at least 14 leveraged and inverse funds tracking Anthropic after its IPO, adding products that can amplify short-term exposure to the company’s shares [42].
  • AI infrastructure demand: Anthropic’s appetite for computing capacity is one factor behind hyperscalers’ data-center investment, with potential knock-on effects for chip suppliers, data-center equipment firms, utilities and energy companies [11].

Historical Background

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by seven former OpenAI employees who left over concerns about AI safety and alignment [25]. The company is registered as a Public Benefit Corporation, a structure that requires it to weigh public benefit alongside profit [25].
