Ottawa plans $6-billion boost to expand CSIS foreign reach

Category: Canada

Canada’s latest federal budget sets aside $6 billion over 10 years for the Canadian Security Intelligence Service (CSIS), and a senior national-security official says the government plans to change the law to let the agency collect more intelligence abroad [25][2]. Prime Minister Mark Carney’s budget, tabled Nov. 5, includes measures to strengthen Canada’s sovereignty and security [6]. Dominic Rochon, secretary to cabinet on national security and intelligence, said the initial expansion would include foreign human-intelligence gathering. He said the changes could eventually lead to a separate foreign-intelligence agency, but that would require a broader parliamentary debate and oversight arrangements [25]. CSIS has limited authority to gather intelligence overseas, and Canada relies heavily on intelligence shared by partners in the Five Eyes alliance. Rochon said foreign collection would initially account for about 5% to 10% of intelligence gathering. The government would first expand CSIS’s powers rather than immediately create a new agency [25][2].

Primary Source: View original article

We have been a net importer of intelligence. There is no secret about that.

— Dominic Rochon, secretary to cabinet on national security and intelligence (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Funding details: The increase is scheduled to begin in 2027-28 and includes $579.5 million in ongoing funding. CSIS currently has an annual budget of about $980 million, The Maple reported [3].
  • Legal oversight: Rochon said federal judges have no jurisdiction abroad. Overseas work by the intelligence agency CSE requires ministerial approval and review by an intelligence commissioner, while CSIS needs judicial approval for many activities in Canada [25].
  • Budget rationale: The budget says the additional resources are intended to help CSIS counter terrorism, foreign interference and espionage [3][2].

Perspectives

Stephanie Carvin, a Carleton University international-relations professor and former CSIS analyst, said building a foreign human-intelligence capability within CSIS first was preferable to immediately creating a separate agency, which could cannibalize CSIS and other agencies [25].

Sources: The Globe and Mail

Intelligence researcher Wesley Wark said serious discussions were under way about foreign human-intelligence collection. He considered a decision to establish such a capability more likely than not [2].

Sources: Global News

Historical Background

Canada does not currently have a dedicated international spy service comparable to the CIA or MI6 [25][2].

Q&A

Q: Has Ottawa said how much of the CSIS increase will go toward foreign collection?

A: No detailed breakdown has been released. The budget gives only broad descriptions of how CSIS could use the funding, and Finance Canada referred questions back to that language [2][3].
