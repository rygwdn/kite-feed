Canada restores sanctions on 11 Iranian entities over nuclear activities

Category: Canada

Canada announced Nov. 10 that it is reimposing sanctions on 11 Iranian entities it says support nuclear and ballistic-missile activities or finance violence abroad. The measures bar Canadians from financial dealings with the listed organizations, including banks, energy and insurance companies, and a tanker operator [4][1][3][7]. Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand said Iran’s repeated failure to cooperate with the International Atomic Energy Agency had heightened concerns about its nuclear activities. Canada removed the entities from its sanctions list in 2016 after Iran made progress under the 2015 nuclear agreement. Ottawa says the new restrictions align with Group of Seven efforts to press Tehran to cooperate with the agency and return to negotiations. Anand announced the measures during the G7 foreign ministers’ meeting in Paris [4][8].

Canada’s position is unequivocal: Iran must never be allowed to obtain a nuclear weapon. We will continue to work with our G7 partners and the broader international community toward a comprehensive, verifiable and durable agreement that addresses Iran’s nuclear program and strengthens regional and international security.

— Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand (Government of Canada)

Key Points

  • Named targets: The list includes National Iranian Oil Co., National Iranian Tanker Co., Bank Mellat, Bank Melli Iran and the Iranian Insurance Co. [7][8]
  • Sanctions record: Canada says it has sanctioned more than 500 Iranian individuals and entities since 2010; the government counts 25 rounds covering 237 individuals and 276 entities under several laws and regulations [4].
  • Treaty obligations: Ottawa says Iran is bound by the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons not to develop or acquire nuclear weapons [1][6].
  • Financial networks: Canada says the restrictions are intended to disrupt financial networks supporting Iran’s nuclear program and limit access to sensitive technology and funding [4].

Perspectives

Iranian government: Iran maintains that its nuclear program is exclusively peaceful, according to Asharq Al-Awsat [8].

Sources: Asharq Al-Awsat

Timeline

  • 2012: Canada cut ties, closed its Tehran embassy and expelled Iranian diplomats [1].
  • 2015: Iran and world powers reached the nuclear agreement cited by Ottawa [4].
  • 2016: Canada delisted the 11 entities after Iran made progress under the agreement [4][3].
  • 10 November 2026: Anita Anand announced the sanctions during the G7 meeting in Paris [4][8].

Historical Background

Canada severed diplomatic ties with Iran in 2012 over its nuclear program, human-rights record and position on the Syrian civil war. Ottawa also designated the country a state sponsor of terrorism [1][3]. The nuclear agreement established measures intended to keep Iran’s program exclusively peaceful [4].
