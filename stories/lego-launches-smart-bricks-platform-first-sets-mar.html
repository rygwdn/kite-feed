Lego launches Smart Bricks platform, first sets March 1

Category: Technology

Lego has unveiled its Smart Play system at CES 2026, centered on “Smart Bricks” that hide a tiny computer inside a standard-looking 2x4 brick to add lights, sound, and sensor-driven interactions to physical builds [2][4][13][12]. The first wave is Star Wars-themed and is set to ship on March 1, with some outlets noting preorders begin January 9 [4][14][12]. Lego says the goal is to make creations feel more alive without relying on screens, and multiple reports emphasize the company avoided cameras and generative AI to keep play more predictable and child-friendly [3][4][14][7]. The announcement has also sparked debate: some play experts and commentators welcome the new creative possibilities, while others worry it could steer Lego toward more guided, “on-rails” experiences if tools for tinkering aren’t offered [7][9][10].

Everything that we do is driven with an appetite for innovation

Did you know: Lego reported 2024 as a record year with $10.8 billion in revenue, and said the first half of 2025 continued to grow [14].

Key Points

  • Tiny custom chip: Smart Bricks use a custom 4.1mm ASIC, described as smaller than a single Lego stud, integrating processing with sensors, RGB LEDs, and a small speaker [2][4][13].
  • Tag-based triggers: Interactions are triggered via NFC “Smart Tags” embedded in new tiles and minifigures, enabling builds to react when figures are placed or moved [3][4][11].
  • Mesh networking: The bricks can form a Bluetooth mesh network to sense each other’s relative position and orientation, expanding what multi-brick builds can do together [4].
  • Charging design: Reports describe wireless charging via a pad that can charge multiple bricks at once, helping keep the brick sealed and durable [4][3][2].
  • Set lineup details: Early set details reported include a Luke Skywalker X-Wing priced at $100 (548 pieces), a Darth Vader TIE Fighter at $69–$70 (piece counts vary by outlet), and a 962-piece Emperor’s Throne Room set at $160 [14][4].

Perspectives

The Lego Group: The company frames Smart Play as a way to engage “digital native” kids while minimizing screen time, and says it deliberately avoided cameras and generative AI to keep behavior predictable and focused on physical play [14][3][4].

Sources: Fast Company

Play experts (as cited by BBC): Some play experts expressed unease about adding more tech into children’s play, questioning whether it shifts the balance away from open-ended, imaginative building [7].

Sources: BBC

Hands-on reviewers: A hands-on take argues that the “unknown” and surprising behaviors are part of the fun, positioning Smart Bricks as a new kind of play experience rather than a strict replacement for classic sets [9].

Sources: Gizmodo

Skeptical tech commentators: A more critical view warns that without a developer kit or open tooling, Smart Play could encourage pre-scripted, “fun on rails” play rather than empowering deeper experimentation [10].

Sources: The Register

Timeline

  • 2024: Lego quietly piloted Smart Play technology in a Lego City set [4].
  • Monday: Lego announced Smart Play and demonstrated scenarios at CES 2026 [14].
  • January 9: Preorders are scheduled to open for the first Smart Play sets [12].
  • March 1: First Smart Brick Star Wars sets are slated to ship [12][4][14].

Technical Details

  • Custom ASIC: A custom application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) concentrates computing and control logic in a very small package; Lego’s chip is reported at 4.1mm to fit entirely inside a 2x4 brick [2][4].
  • NFC smart tags: Near-field communication (NFC) tags in tiles/minifigures let a brick identify what’s placed nearby and trigger programmed effects like sounds or lights [3][11][4].
  • Bluetooth mesh: Bluetooth mesh allows many devices to relay messages across a group, which outlets say Smart Bricks use to coordinate with nearby bricks for spatial/interaction awareness [4].

Industry Impact

  • Toy safety design: By avoiding cameras and describing the microphone as a virtual button rather than a recording tool, Lego appears to be aiming for simpler privacy and safety expectations in connected toys [4][3].
  • Kid-friendly UX: Demonstrations described effects like alarms, sirens, animal sounds, and tire squeals, showing how embedded sensors can translate physical actions into immediate feedback without a screen [14].
  • Maker ecosystem tension: The launch highlights a split in the wider smart-toy market between curated experiences and open-ended hacking; some coverage praises the surprise factor, while other coverage questions whether Lego will provide developer tools [9][10].

Historical Background

Lego has previously experimented with interactive elements (from motors and sensors to app-connected sets), but Smart Play is positioned as a deeper shift because the computing hardware sits inside a standard-looking brick rather than an external hub [14][2]. Lego describes Smart Bricks as its biggest building-system change since the minifigure debuted in 1978, highlighting how rare platform-level changes are for the company’s core brick ecosystem [7][4].

Q&A

Q: Will Lego publish an official developer kit or programming tools for Smart Bricks?

A: Some commentary notes that no developer kit was mentioned in early coverage, leaving openness unclear for now [10].

Q: How will firmware updates work for the Smart Brick platform?

A: Smart Brick firmware can be updated from a smartphone app, according to one report [13].

Q: What did Lego’s earlier Smart Play pilot include, and how did it perform?

A: Lego said it quietly piloted the technology in a 2024 Lego City set, but the articles here don’t provide detailed performance results [4].

Action Items

  • Compare Smart Play set options before preorders open: Check the announced Star Wars Smart Play sets, pricing, and preorder timing (reported as January 9) to pick the build size and budget that fits your household [12][14].
  • Plan for charging and play space: If you buy in, consider where a multi-brick wireless charging pad can live, since reports describe pad-based charging for multiple bricks [4][2].
