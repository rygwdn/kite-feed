Student kills classmate and himself at Philippine school

Category: World

A student opened fire Tuesday at Ateneo de Zamboanga University's junior high school in Zamboanga City, southern Philippines, killing a fellow student before taking his own life, officials said [1][2][4][5][10][9][15][30][57][53]. Zamboanga Mayor Khymer Adan Olaso said the suspect appeared to be livestreaming the attack with a body-worn camera, and police said the situation was under control after hundreds of students were evacuated [2][4][5][12][22][30][57]. Police accounts differed in some early details, with regional police and Reuters-based reports describing the gunman as a Grade 9 student who brought a pistol and a rifle onto campus, while some AP accounts described him as carrying two pistols [1][9][10][15][22]. Officials said the victim was a male student one grade ahead of the shooter, and two other people were wounded, though Ateneo president Ernald Andal said there were no additional fatalities and no further reported injuries at the time of his statement [2][4][5][9][10][33][57]. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. ordered law enforcement agencies to investigate and identify measures to prevent violence in schools [10][15][18][24]. The shooting was the Philippines' second fatal school attack in less than two months, after a June attack at a public high school in Tacloban City killed at least three students and injured about 20 others [9][13][15][18][53].

Key Points

  • Classroom sequence: Olaso said the suspect first fired at a teacher, apparently missed, then moved to another room and shot the student who died [3][5][30][53][57].
  • Police appeal: National police chief Gen. Jose Melencio Nartatez Jr. urged the public not to share unconfirmed reports, photos or videos while investigators establish the circumstances [4][5][12][26][32].
  • Campus response: The school coordinated with parents and guardians to pick up children and said it had ensured the safety of those who remained on campus [1][5][26].
  • Gun rules: School shootings are rare in the Philippines, where gun ownership rules include background checks and psychological evaluations, while illegal firearms remain in circulation [9][13][18][58].
