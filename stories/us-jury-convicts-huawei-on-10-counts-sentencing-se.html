US jury convicts Huawei on 10 counts; sentencing set for 2027

Category: World

A federal jury in Brooklyn found Chinese telecommunications company Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 criminal counts, including bank fraud, sanctions violations and conspiracy to steal trade secrets, while acquitting it of racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice [8][5][11]. The verdict followed a trial lasting more than two months over allegations that Huawei misled banks about its Iran business and obtained technology from U.S. companies [8][39]. Prosecutors said Huawei concealed its Iran operations to preserve access to international banking and dollar-clearing services and pursued technology from competitors. The company argued that the government assembled isolated incidents into an unsupported corporate conspiracy. It has rejected the convictions and says it will appeal [28][8]. U.S. restrictions have already limited Huawei’s access to U.S. technology and telecom markets. The company continues to operate internationally and has expanded into chips [36][5]. U.S. District Judge Ann Donnelly is scheduled to sentence Huawei on September 14, 2027; potential penalties include fines, forfeiture and restitution [8][28].

Key Points

  • Prosecutors said Huawei ran an internal program offering bonuses to employees who obtained industrial secrets from competitors [11].
  • Banking dispute: Huawei’s defense argued that HSBC and other banks knew about its Iran business and competed for the company’s account [30][8].
  • Defense case: Huawei lawyer Brian Heberlig pointed to $110 billion in research-and-development investment and $11.4 billion in technology licensing payments over two decades [42].
  • Trial scale: Prosecutors called more than 60 witnesses during the trial, while Huawei’s defense called 15 [8][5].
  • 5G restrictions: Before the verdict, Washington had urged countries not to use Huawei infrastructure for new 5G networks [11][50].

Perspectives

U.S. prosecutors: Brooklyn U.S. Attorney Joseph Nocella described the verdict as accountability for what prosecutors called years of sanctions evasion, lies to banks and theft from U.S. technology companies [8].

Huawei Chief Financial Officer Meng Wanzhou later returned to China under a deferred prosecution agreement, and the charges against her were dropped [9].
