Apple teases week of launches led by budget MacBook

Category: Technology

Apple CEO Tim Cook has teased “a big week ahead,” and multiple outlets expect a run of product announcements starting Monday, March 2, capped by in-person “Special Apple Experience” hands-on sessions for media on Wednesday, March 4 in New York, London and Shanghai [8][10][11][2]. Most reporting points to a new low-cost MacBook, an iPhone 17e, and refreshed iPads and Macs, with Apple widely expected to share much of the news through press releases rather than a traditional keynote [10][11][7]. The buyer-friendly angle: the rumored entry-level MacBook could bring macOS to more students, families, and budget shoppers by taking aim at Chromebooks and inexpensive Windows laptops—while also adding some personality if Apple follows through on “playful” color options [2][10][4][13]. Exactly how Apple lands a lower price is still disputed, but the most common claim is that it will use an iPhone-class A-series chip (A18 Pro or A19 Pro/A19 depending on the source) and accept trade-offs such as fewer ports and scaled-back display and hardware features compared with a MacBook Air [2][10][6]. On phones and tablets, expected updates include an iPhone 17e that follows last year’s iPhone 16e rebrand away from “SE,” plus new iPads that could bring an A18 chip to the base iPad and an M4 chip to the iPad Air. Key details—like whether the 17e keeps a notch or shifts to Dynamic Island—are still unsettled across reports [5][7][10].

A big week ahead

Key Points

  • Pricing debate: Some reports put the low-cost MacBook as low as $599 [13]. Others suggest a more likely $699–$799 range [17], and Digital Trends warns component costs could push it closer to MacBook Air pricing than many buyers hope [16].
  • Spec trade-off mix: Macworld says the budget MacBook could still feel “Mac-like,” with an aluminum build and even a rumored “Ultra Retina XDR OLED” display, while saving money elsewhere—simpler audio hardware, more basic display sensors, and slower charging. That spread hints at how unsettled the final spec sheet remains [2].
  • iPhone 17e extras: Beyond the notch-vs–Dynamic Island question, Macworld expects the iPhone 17e to run on an A19 chip and add MagSafe compatibility [5].
  • Naming curiosity: Macworld says readers are debating whether Apple revives “iBook,” uses “MacBook SE,” or simply calls it “MacBook,” a sign that Apple’s positioning is still an open question [3].

Perspectives

Ars Technica: Ars expects Apple to space announcements across several days via press releases, with the March 4 “special experience” serving mainly as a media hands-on capstone rather than a standard presentation.

Sources: Ars Technica

ZDNET: ZDNET argues that if Apple really lands at $599, a cheaper MacBook could chip away at what it calls the budget advantage held by Windows laptops and Chromebooks.

Sources: ZDNET

Engadget: Engadget frames a low-cost MacBook as a way to pull in Windows users, and says Apple could cut costs by using a mobile-class processor instead of an M-series chip.

Sources: Engadget

Digital Trends: Digital Trends warns that rising component costs could undercut the “affordable MacBook” pitch if the final price ends up too close to an M4 MacBook Air.

Sources: Digital Trends

Macworld: Macworld says “playful colors” could be a real part of the appeal, signaling a more fun and approachable Mac design alongside the lower price.

Sources: Macworld

Timeline

  • June 2025: Analyst Ming-Chi Kuo predicts Apple will build a low-cost MacBook [2].
  • 2 March 2026: Apple’s teased launch week begins Monday, according to multiple reports [8][10][11].
  • 4 March 2026: Apple holds “Special Apple Experience” hands-on sessions in three cities [2][10].

Technical Details

  • A-series Mac architecture: Reports say the budget MacBook may use an A18 Pro or A19 Pro/A19 (iPhone-class) SoC rather than an M-series chip—a shift that would shape thermal design and help Apple separate it from the MacBook Air [2][10][3].
  • I/O and storage constraints: Rumors point to fewer ports and slower SSD speeds as additional ways to differentiate the entry model from the MacBook Air [2][10].

Historical Background

Apple previously sold a thin 12-inch MacBook built around low-power Intel Core M chips, a model some reports say the new low-cost MacBook could echo in spirit [10]. Apple also tested demand for cheaper Macs by selling the older M1 MacBook Air through retailers like Walmart for about $700, later dropping to $650, before that design started to feel dated [17].

Action Items

  • Decide whether to wait before buying a MacBook Air: If you’re shopping in the $700–$1,000 range, consider holding off until Apple’s March 2–4 announcements clarify the new entry-level MacBook’s price and trade-offs [10][2].
  • Make a quick must-have list: Before launch week, write down your deal-breakers—backlit keyboard, display features (True Tone), and the ports you need—since those are among the rumored areas where Apple could cut costs [10].
