Study finds AI chatbots link to risky retail sites

Category: AI

A study by brand-protection firm Podqi found that ChatGPT, Google Gemini and Perplexity included risky shopping websites in answers to questions about 151 brands, including Lululemon, Birkenstock and Le Creuset. Across 6,795 answers collected during two testing periods in September, risky sites appeared in 15% of Perplexity answers, 8% of ChatGPT answers and 4% of Gemini answers [2][6]. Podqi classified fake stores, counterfeit sellers, transferred brand domains and inactive websites as risky. Its test used prompts the firm designed, not real user conversations. A link could count even if a chatbot mentioned it without recommending it or warned users about it, and the study measured exposure rather than confirmed fraud [2][1]. Podqi CEO Trevor West advised shoppers to verify a retailer through the brand’s official website before sharing payment or personal information [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Domain audit: Podqi flagged 718 of 4,437 reviewed domains: 351 were fake sites and 367 were inactive domains that could later be repurposed [5][6].
  • Recommendation pattern: Among answers that included risky sites, 57% recommended them for shopping, 34% mentioned them without explanation and 9% warned users [6].
  • Regional queries: Questions about overseas delivery or official sellers by country produced risky sites in 16% of cases, with some chatbots inventing regional storefronts for brands that had none [6][2].
  • Smaller brands: Podqi found that smaller brands had roughly twice the exposure to risky links as larger companies [2][1].

Perspectives

Google: Spokesperson Jessica Levis Boord said Gemini links to sources so users can check answers, and that Google continues testing and improving its models [2].

Sources: The Verge

OpenAI: Spokesperson Taya Christianson said the company is improving safeguards to make potentially deceptive websites less likely to appear in shopping results [2].

Sources: The Verge

Perplexity: Spokesperson Jesse Dwyer defended the company’s performance on research benchmarks, while acknowledging that it does not claim to be 100% accurate [2].

Sources: The Verge

Action Items

  • Before buying, review a retailer’s contact and return policies; an AI chatbot’s confident wording is not proof that a store is legitimate [1].
