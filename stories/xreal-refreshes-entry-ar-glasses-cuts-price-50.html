Xreal refreshes entry AR glasses, cuts price $50

Category: Technology

At CES 2026, Xreal introduced the Xreal 1S, a refreshed version of its entry-level “personal cinema”/AR-style glasses that plug into compatible devices over USB-C to show a large virtual screen [3][9]. Multiple outlets say the 1S raises display specs (including a move to 1200p per eye, a slightly wider field of view, and higher brightness) while lowering the price to about $449–$450, making the “big screen anywhere” idea a bit more approachable [3][13][8][9]. Xreal also announced a companion hub/battery accessory called Neo aimed at smoothing connectivity and adding power for on-the-go use, including support for Switch play in hands-on demos [2][6][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Mashable says the 1S comes in a cobalt blue color option, unlike other Xreal models it describes as black [13].

Key Points

  • Real 3D mode: AppleInsider says the 1S includes a “Real 3D” view mode that can convert on-screen content into 3D, positioning it as an entertainment-first feature rather than a full spatial-computing OS [6][13].
  • Neo hub design: Xreal’s Neo accessory combines a video hub with a built-in power bank (reported as 10,000 mAh) and DisplayPort connectivity, and AppleInsider describes it as MagSafe-oriented hardware meant to keep devices running while feeding video to the glasses [6].
  • Console compatibility: CNET’s hands-on notes that playing on the glasses can feel great, but connecting a Nintendo Switch can be awkward, which is why the $99 Neo mini-dock/battery concept focuses on being a passthrough converter for Switch use [2][7].
  • Android XR lead-in: 9to5Google frames the 1S launch as a bridge to Xreal’s upcoming Android XR device, “Project Aura,” suggesting the company is building an ecosystem of simpler tethered displays alongside future XR products [8].
  • Work and play: ZDNET’s review-like coverage emphasizes using the glasses as a large virtual desktop (it describes a roughly 170-inch workspace) and highlights 3D as an unexpectedly strong part of the experience for the price tier [10].

Perspectives

Engadget: Sees the 1S as a modest but welcome spec bump paired with a lower price, reinforcing the “personal cinema” framing for people curious about AR-style glasses without stepping up to a full headset [3].

Sources: Engadget

CNET: Reports that the viewing experience for Switch gaming can be very enjoyable, but setup and cabling can still feel fiddly—making accessories like Neo feel practically motivated [7].

Sources: CNET

TechRadar: Focuses on simplicity and “pure entertainment value,” portraying the product less as futuristic computing and more as an easy way to put a big virtual screen in front of you [5].

Sources: TechRadar

AppleInsider: Contrasts Xreal’s tethered, single-window approach with headset-style “spatial computing,” and spotlights the Real 3D mode plus the Neo hub’s power/video features as the practical story [6].

Sources: AppleInsider

Technical Details

  • USB-C video output: The 1S works by connecting over USB-C to compatible devices and displaying that device’s output as a floating virtual screen in front of the wearer [3][9].
  • Micro-OLED displays: Android Authority describes the glasses using micro-OLED displays (with 1200p per eye on the 1S) to create a virtual screen effect while keeping the hardware sunglasses-like [9].
  • 3DoF spatial anchoring: Mashable reports the 1S supports native 3DoF spatial anchoring, which can keep the virtual screen “pinned” in place as you move your head [13].

Industry Impact

  • Portable gaming: Hands-on coverage positions tethered display glasses as a new “big screen” option for handheld and console play, with Neo-like docks aiming to simplify portable Switch connections and power needs [7][2].
  • XR product strategy: By pairing incremental hardware refreshes (1S) with ecosystem accessories (Neo) and teasing an Android XR device (Project Aura), Xreal appears to be pursuing a multi-tier pipeline that could pressure competitors to differentiate on comfort, compatibility, and software rather than only on display specs [8][11].

Scientific Significance

  • Perceived screen size: Engadget describes how the glasses use near-eye displays to create the perception of a very large screen (with an “average” view it says is about 171 inches, and potentially much larger depending on focus), illustrating a core perceptual trick behind near-eye optics [3].
  • Depth conversion: Reports of a mode that converts 2D content into 3D (Real 3D) reflect a growing consumer push for software-driven depth effects on ordinary media, though sources present it as a feature rather than a validated measurement claim [6][13].

Historical Background

So-called “personal cinema” glasses have long relied on tethering to phones, laptops, or consoles, trading standalone computing power for lighter designs and simpler setup compared with full VR headsets [common]. Xreal launched the Xreal One in late 2024 and later released a higher-end One Pro, and several CES 2026 write-ups describe the 1S as a mid-generation refresh that sits below the One Pro while improving the entry option [3][13][11].

Q&A

Q: What does the 16:10 aspect ratio change mean for movies and work apps?

A: Engadget notes the 1S shifts to a 16:10 aspect ratio (from 16:9 previously), which can show more vertical space in productivity apps while potentially adding letterboxing or cropping depending on video content format [3].

Q: Where does the 1S sit relative to other Xreal models?

A: Mashable and Ubergizmo describe the 1S as priced below the One Pro and as part of an expanded One-series lineup following the One (late 2024) and One Pro (2025) releases [13][11].

Q: Is Neo mainly a battery, or mainly a video hub?

A: AppleInsider describes Neo as both: a hub with DisplayPort connectivity plus a built-in 10,000 mAh power bank intended to keep a connected setup running longer [6].

Action Items

  • Check device compatibility before buying: Confirm your phone, tablet, laptop, or console supports USB-C video output (the connection method described for the 1S) so you don’t end up with a cable-and-adapter surprise [3][9].
  • Plan for Switch connectivity: If you want to use a Nintendo Switch, consider a passthrough-style dock/battery accessory like Xreal’s Neo concept, since CNET notes the connection process can otherwise feel awkward [2][7].
