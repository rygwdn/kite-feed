Carney joins premiers for Churchill Falls energy deal

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney is expected in St. John’s on Monday, Aug. 17, for an announcement with Newfoundland and Labrador Premier Tony Wakeham and Quebec Premier Christine Fréchette on energy security and economic development linked to Churchill Falls [1][3][5]. Newfoundland and Labrador’s government said Wakeham would welcome Fréchette to the afternoon event, while Quebec Energy Minister Bernard Drainville and Hydro-Québec president and CEO Claudine Bouchard are also scheduled to attend [3][5][6]. La Presse reported that the provinces have reached an agreement that would raise the planned output from new hydroelectric works in Labrador and include wind projects, giving both Hydro-Québec and Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro more energy than the December 2024 agreement-in-principle had anticipated [1]. Radio-Canada confirmed Fréchette was travelling to Newfoundland to sign an agreement, CBC reported; Radio-Canada also reported that each province would receive more electricity than under the earlier memorandum of understanding [3][5]. The negotiations centre on sharing energy from the Churchill River in Labrador, where the two provinces have been trying to replace the existing Churchill Falls arrangement and expand generating capacity [4][5][6]. La Presse reported that Ottawa played a facilitator role in the talks, with Carney’s presence confirmed by his office on Sunday evening [1].
