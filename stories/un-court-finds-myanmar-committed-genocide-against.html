UN court finds Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya, orders reparations

Category: World

The International Court of Justice (ICJ), the United Nations’ top court, ruled 9-6 on Nov. 19 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during the military’s 2017 campaign in northern Rakhine state. It is the first time the court has held a state responsible for genocide. Judges rejected Myanmar’s defense that the campaign was a legitimate response to insurgent attacks [4][12][38]. The court found that security forces killed thousands, used widespread sexual violence, subjected children to extreme violence and attacked people fleeing to Bangladesh. It ordered Myanmar to facilitate refugees’ safe, voluntary and dignified return, restore lost property or pay compensation, investigate and punish suspected perpetrators, and provide reparations. Compensation amounts will be settled later [8][30]. The ruling is final and binding, but the ICJ cannot enforce it; it can refer noncompliance to the U.N. Security Council. The Gambia brought the case in 2019. The judgment is being closely watched for its legal reasoning in South Africa’s separate genocide case against Israel, which remains pending [9][21][49].

Key Points

  • Scale of violence: The court estimated that about 10,000 Rohingya were killed and found roughly 815,000 people had been targeted; evidence included the burning or bulldozing of hundreds of villages [12][23].
  • Intent finding: Judges cited anti-Rohingya hate speech, discriminatory restrictions and the pattern of violence as evidence that the campaign was intended to destroy a substantial part of the group [12][23].
  • Birth prevention: The court found that sexual violence and other acts, including measures affecting Rohingya people’s ability to have children, supported its finding of genocidal intent [8][41].
  • State obligations: The judgment also found Myanmar had failed to prevent genocide and to punish it, including where civilians acted alongside security forces [30][41].
  • Evidence obstruction: The court said Myanmar’s military obscured crimes by burning or disposing of bodies and destroying sites, while authorities denied investigators free access to parts of Rakhine State [12][23].

  • November 2019: The Gambia brought its genocide case against Myanmar to the ICJ [20][21].
  • January 2020: The ICJ ordered Myanmar to protect Rohingya and preserve evidence [20].
  • 19 November 2026: The ICJ ruled Myanmar responsible for genocide by a 9-6 vote [4][38].

Historical Background

The Rohingya faced decades of discrimination in Myanmar, including denial of citizenship and restrictions on movement and access to services [15][23]. In August 2017, after attacks by Rohingya militants on security posts, Myanmar’s military began so-called clearance operations in northern Rakhine; more than 700,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh [20][12].

Humanitarian Impact

About 1.2 million Rohingya refugees live in Bangladesh, mainly in camps around Cox’s Bazar, where reports describe overcrowding, fire risks, infectious disease, monsoon landslides, inadequate sanitation and limits on work [15][27].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇳 United Nations: Secretary-General António Guterres welcomed the judgment as a demonstration of international justice and called on Myanmar to comply with it [7][41].
  • 🇬🇧 United Kingdom: Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband announced the expulsion of Myanmar’s defence attaché, summoned its chargé d’affaires and called for an urgent U.N. Security Council session [38][48].
  • 🇬🇲 The Gambia: The government welcomed the ruling, saying its 2019 case was brought with support from the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation because preventing and punishing genocide is a shared obligation of convention members [48].
