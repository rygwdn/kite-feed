Mexico captures FBI-wanted cartel leader in raid that leaves 11 dead

Category: World

Mexican forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro,” in Guasave, Sinaloa, on Friday night, Nov. 20, in an operation that left four soldiers and seven suspected cartel gunmen dead, Mexico’s Defense Ministry said. Meza Flores, who was flown to Mexico City, is on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted list. U.S. authorities accuse him of trafficking drugs and illegally possessing firearms [1][4][2]. The raid triggered armed clashes, vehicle burnings and roadblocks across parts of Sinaloa. Videos showed baseball fans fleeing gunfire in Guasave, while local authorities urged residents to stay indoors. The government said U.S. intelligence helped locate Meza Flores; The New York Times reported that U.S. and Mexican authorities had shared intelligence for weeks [11][46][16]. Meza Flores was allied with Los Mayos, a faction fighting for control of the Sinaloa Cartel after its co-founder Ismael “El Mayo” Zambada was taken into U.S. custody in 2024. President Donald Trump has pressed Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum to intensify action against cartels, including by linking trade policy to security progress. Meza Flores is now in the custody of Mexican prosecutors, and the United States has sought his extradition [18][6][5].

Key Points

  • Other detainees: Authorities said eight people were arrested overall: Meza Flores and two senior associates were taken to Mexico City, while five others remained in Sinaloa [4][10].
  • Weapons seized: Security Minister Omar García Harfuch said authorities recovered a.50-caliber rifle, a grenade launcher and hand grenades, as well as drugs and cash [20][10].
  • Baseball suspended: The Mexican Pacific League canceled the remaining weekend games after gunfire disrupted the Guasave match [46][48].
  • Reward offer: The U.S. State Department had offered up to $5 million for information leading to Meza Flores’ arrest [7][4].
  • Additional allegations: When the FBI added Meza Flores to its most-wanted list in 2025, it also cited alleged kidnapping, extortion and torture; these remain allegations [5][31].

Perspectives

Mexican Security Minister Omar García Harfuch: He called the arrests an important step toward pacifying Sinaloa and said federal security forces would remain deployed to protect residents [28][43].
