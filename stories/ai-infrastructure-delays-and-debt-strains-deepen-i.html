AI infrastructure delays and debt strains deepen investor doubts

Category: AI

Investors are reassessing the AI infrastructure boom as borrowing costs rise, data-center projects stall and questions grow over whether future revenue can cover the cost of the buildout [33][39]. The buildout requires heavy upfront spending on computing capacity, while providers such as AI cloud company CoreWeave face credit concerns [33][39]. At least 47 large U.S. data-center projects have been delayed, canceled or blocked this year, while Bernstein estimates that only about 35% of planned projects are viable. More than 1,000 U.S. communities have tried to impose moratoriums or other restrictions on AI infrastructure, according to Financial Times findings reported by Yonhap [39]. A separate estimate puts U.S. AI infrastructure spending from 2025 through 2032 at $10.3 trillion, averaging 3.6% of gross domestic product annually [39]. Investors are pushing AI cloud company Nscale to lower its valuation ahead of a planned initial public offering, and two other infrastructure firms have postponed or paused listing plans [18][15][39].

Key Points

  • Debt structure: CoreWeave buys Nvidia chips and leases data-center capacity under contracts lasting more than 15 years, then rents computing capacity to customers. It remains responsible for lease costs even after customer agreements end [33].
  • Financial sensitivity: Columbia Business School professor Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh warned that a 10% decline in cash flow could wipe out equity value in the heavily debt-funded buildout [39].
  • Project delays: South Korean outlet EToday cited a Bernstein estimate that about 35% of planned U.S. data-center construction projects were considered feasible [22].
  • AI adopters: Infomed Momentum CEO Travis Prentice recommended focusing on companies using AI to improve profits, citing MetLife’s efficiency work and Visa’s fraud detection and security applications [32].
  • Power delays: Delays in power-grid approvals for Oracle’s planned 902-megawatt Wisconsin data center make operation before 2029 increasingly unlikely, data-center researcher Aterio said [33].
  • Funding pressure: My Bui, an economist at fund manager AMP, said higher government bond yields and wider corporate credit spreads are making it harder to borrow for and refinance AI infrastructure [43].

Industry Impact

  • Credit markets: Barclays data cited by Yonhap showed that spreads on riskier AI-linked corporate bonds had risen more than 50% in six months [33].
  • Data-center construction: Citing Financial Times reporting, EToday said at least 47 large U.S. data-center projects had been delayed, paused or withdrawn this year [22].
  • AI cloud providers: Executives expect consolidation to reduce the number of neocloud providers from hundreds to about seven to 10. One early provider’s CEO said asset managers and telecom or fiber-optic companies could become acquirers [39].
  • Corporate earnings: Overall earnings were still expected to rise 30%, driven by profits at large technology companies [43].
  • Data-center development: Power-approval delays and local restrictions are slowing planned capacity growth. Delayed grid approvals have pushed the expected start of Oracle’s Wisconsin project beyond its 2028 target [33][39].

Historical Background

As a share of GDP, projected AI infrastructure spending would be about 1.5 times the railroad boom’s peak and more than three times the internet boom’s peak [39].
