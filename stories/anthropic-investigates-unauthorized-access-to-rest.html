Anthropic investigates unauthorized access to restricted Mythos model

Category: AI

Anthropic is investigating reports that unauthorized users may have accessed Claude Mythos Preview, a tightly restricted AI model the company said it was limiting to select testers instead of releasing broadly [1][3][2]. Multiple outlets say a small Discord-linked group may have reached a preview environment through a third-party vendor setup, though Anthropic said it had not found evidence of unauthorized access to Mythos itself at the time of its statement [2][3][26]. The story drew outsized attention because Mythos was described as a model with significant cybersecurity capabilities. That raised concern among banks, intelligence agencies, and cyber-risk observers about how tools like this should be secured and governed [1][22][39].

We’re investigating a report claiming unauthorized access to Claude Mythos Preview through one of our third-party vendor environments.

— Anthropic spokesperson (Anthropic statement reported by Futurism)

Did you know: The group that reportedly accessed Mythos was described as members of a private Discord community focused on finding unreleased AI models [26].

Key Points

  • Discord trail: Reports say the users were part of a private Discord community focused on unreleased AI models. They used bots and searches of unsecured sites such as GitHub to hunt for clues [50].
  • Mixed assessments: The Register reported early analysis suggesting Mythos may be less exceptional than advertised, underscoring disagreement over how much practical danger the model currently poses [46].
  • Limited use: A source familiar with the matter said the Discord group had been using Mythos for non-cybersecurity purposes and was more interested in experimenting with unreleased models than in carrying out attacks [26].
  • Global concern: The New York Times reported that Mythos prompted emergency responses from central banks and intelligence agencies globally as governments weighed the risks of a model built for vulnerability discovery [1].
  • Partner testing: Anthropic limited Mythos preview access to companies including Amazon, Microsoft, Apple, Cisco and Mozilla. Mozilla said the model helped it find and patch 271 Firefox vulnerabilities [43].
  • China focus: South China Morning Post reported that Mythos has become part of a wider debate in China over AI competition, cybersecurity preparedness and US technology controls [45].

Technical Details

  • Hosting pattern inference: Reports say the group inferred Mythos's online location from Anthropic's earlier naming and storage conventions, then combined that with contractor-linked permissions to reach the preview environment [26][49][50].
  • Storage inference attack: The reported access path relied on predicting where Mythos was hosted based on Anthropic's prior storage patterns, showing how infrastructure naming and deployment conventions can leak enough metadata to help determined outsiders find restricted systems [26][14].
  • Cyber-capable coding output: Coverage around Mythos tied concern not just to general model scale but also to software-generation behavior, with cyber experts warning that Anthropic systems could produce vulnerable code or assist offensive cyber workflows if safeguards fail [13][1].
  • Zero-day capability claims: Mythos has been described as capable of identifying and exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities across major operating systems and browsers, a claim that helps explain both its restricted release and the scrutiny over whether those capabilities hold up in practice [49][46].
  • Third-party vendor environment: Anthropic's statement focused on a vendor environment rather than its core model systems. That distinction matters because preview access, evaluation tooling, and storage layers often sit outside the most hardened internal production boundary [26][16].
  • Vendor environment boundary: Anthropic's reported distinction between a third-party vendor environment and its own systems matters technically because it suggests the alleged exposure may have involved access-control segregation around model evaluation infrastructure rather than a direct breach of core internal systems [43][50].

Industry Impact

  • Enterprise AI access: Banks and government agencies were already seeking access to Mythos to protect their systems, showing demand for controlled-use cybersecurity models even before the access claims surfaced [43].
  • AI governance: The incident adds pressure on developers to secure contractor portals, model evaluation workflows and preview deployments, not just public-facing products [43][49][50].
  • Browser security: Mozilla said Mythos helped identify and patch 271 Firefox vulnerabilities, suggesting AI-assisted security testing could shorten discovery and remediation cycles for major software projects [43].
  • Cybersecurity vendors: The incident is likely to increase demand for stronger model-access controls, monitoring, and third-party risk management as companies integrate advanced AI into cyber workflows [10][38].
  • AI governance: The episode added momentum to calls for updated regulation tailored to highly capable AI systems, especially where model capabilities intersect with cyber risk [40][41][1].
  • Financial sector: Banks were singled out as institutions that cannot ignore Mythos, suggesting financial firms may speed up internal reviews of how frontier AI could affect fraud, defense, and operational resilience [1][22].

Historical Background

Since generative AI accelerated after the public release of ChatGPT in 2022, major labs have increasingly staged launches with API limits, preview programs, and safety testing instead of full public releases [common]. At the same time, governments have been building AI oversight frameworks, including the EU AI Act adopted in 2024 and the 2023 UK AI Safety Summit, both of which pushed frontier-model risk and governance into mainstream policy debates [common]. The Mythos episode sits at the intersection of those trends: tighter access controls for frontier models on one side, and growing pressure for outside oversight on the other [1][41].

Action Items

  • Review third-party AI access paths: Security teams can audit contractor portals, evaluation sandboxes and cloud storage naming patterns for exposed routes similar to those described in the reports [49][50].
  • Tighten model rollout controls: Separate evaluation infrastructure from production systems and avoid predictable storage or naming patterns, a weakness reports cited in explaining how users may have located Mythos [26][14].
  • Review third-party AI access paths: If your organization tests frontier AI, map every vendor environment, preview endpoint, and storage path used in evaluations, because the reported investigation centered on a third-party environment rather than confirmed direct access to the core model [26].
  • Harden secret model deployments: Developers handling restricted AI systems can separate vendor environments from core systems and tighten permission scopes for model evaluators and contractors [43][50].
