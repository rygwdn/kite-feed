Cambridge scholar transcribes Alan Turing’s unpublished short story

Category: Science

Sarah Dillon, a University of Cambridge literature professor, has transcribed and studied Alan Turing’s six-page handwritten short story “Pryce’s Buoy,” a previously unpublished draft now printed in full and analyzed in new research [1][2][5][6]. Dillon believes Turing likely wrote the unfinished story in late 1952 or early 1953, after his 1952 gross indecency arrest and before his death in June 1954 [1][2][6][5]. The story centers on a romance between two men. One character, Alec Pryce, is a gay scientist known for inventing “Pryce’s buoy” [1][6]. Dillon argues that Pryce is a stand-in for Turing and that the draft challenges the image of Turing as socially unaware, isolated and concerned only with logic and numbers [1][2][5][6]. The New York Times described the manuscript as presenting a different side of the mathematician in the year before his death [4].
