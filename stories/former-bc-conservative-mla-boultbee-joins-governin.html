Former B.C. Conservative MLA Boultbee joins governing NDP

Category: Canada

Amelia Boultbee, the MLA for Penticton-Summerland, joined British Columbia's governing New Democrats after she left the B.C. Conservative caucus and sat as an Independent [1][2][4]. Premier David Eby announced the move Friday. Boultbee said her former party had changed from the "big-tent" organization she believed she had joined [1][2]. Boultbee won Penticton-Summerland as a B.C. Conservative in fall 2024 by a few hundred votes, then left the caucus in 2025 after criticizing John Rustad's leadership [1][4]. She accused the Conservatives of offering "Trump-style populism" and said she was joining Eby's caucus to work on issues including housing affordability and health-care worker shortages [1][4].

What was promised to me and many others as a big-tent party gets smaller by the day.

Key Points

  • Riding impact: Boultbee described herself as a fourth-generation Pentictonite and said she wanted a stronger government voice for Penticton, Summerland and the South Okanagan [4][1].
  • Internal rupture: The Vancouver Sun reported that Boultbee had accused Rustad of intimidating MLAs, involvement in improper memberships during a leadership review and trying to block a caucus vote on his position [4].
  • NDP response: Eby praised Boultbee's legal training, political experience and willingness to defend human rights, saying those qualities would help the NDP caucus [1].
  • Conservative pushback: Rustad accused Boultbee of leaking internal caucus information and suggested she did not align with the B.C. Conservatives' views [4].

Perspectives

Boultbee described housing affordability and the shortage of health-care workers as generational challenges that require thoughtful and determined leadership, and said Eby's NDP is best positioned to meet them.
