Pope Leo urges Milei to protect poor as Argentina visit begins

Category: World

Pope Leo XIV urged Argentine President Javier Milei to put people before the economy and protect democracy as he opened a four-day visit to Argentina in Buenos Aires on Sunday, Nov. 8. It was the first papal trip to Argentina since 1987. “The economy must be at the service of the people, rather than people being at the service of the economy,” Leo said, calling for growth to benefit unemployed people, pensioners, struggling families and people with disabilities [4][5][9]. The pope also called on public officials to recognize limits on their power, listen to political opponents and use language carefully. The Catholic Church has raised concerns over Milei’s spending cuts to social services, pensions, disability assistance and public universities [13][34]. Milei has defended the cuts as necessary to stabilize Argentina’s economy and curb inflation. In his welcome speech, he emphasized life, liberty and property as constitutional principles and praised the late Pope Francis, whom he had previously attacked [9][4][13]. Leo’s schedule includes a visit to a disability-care home, public Masses and meetings in Argentina before he leaves for Peru on Wednesday, Nov. 11 [6][8].

Key Points

  • Youth and education: Leo said Argentina should ask why young people must leave to pursue their goals and urged stronger education, vocational training, universities and research [6][35].
  • Crime and corruption: The pope warned that organized crime, human trafficking, arms and drug trafficking, and corruption weaken social cohesion and divert resources from the common good [34].
  • Inflation trade-off: Annual inflation has fallen from more than 200% to about 33% under Milei, while households still face pressure from public-sector layoffs and business closures [13].
  • Church affiliation: A University of Buenos Aires poll found 57.7% of Argentines identified as Catholic, down from about 90% in the 1960s [5].
  • Milei’s defense: Milei said his government sought to avoid “punishing the Argentines of tomorrow in order to benefit the Argentines of today” [10].
