ULA Vulcan failure destroys 40 Amazon Leo satellites, delays deployment

Category: Technology

A Vulcan rocket operated by United Launch Alliance (ULA), a Boeing-Lockheed Martin venture, failed about 10 minutes after liftoff from Cape Canaveral, Florida, on Nov. 15, destroying all 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites aboard. ULA said the vehicle suffered an anomaly and did not reach orbit; it has not identified the cause. The failure was ULA’s first total mission loss since its formation in 2006 [1][10][9]. The mission was the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon purchased to carry more than 1,500 satellites. Before the flight, Amazon had deployed about 396 satellites toward a first-generation constellation of roughly 3,232 and planned to begin initial commercial service in 2026. A failure investigation is now delaying the next Vulcan flight, which Amazon had expected within weeks [1][9][21]. ULA says it will work with Amazon and other organizations to determine corrective action before returning to flight. Amazon’s next scheduled Leo launch is an Ariane 6 mission carrying 36 satellites, targeted for no earlier than Nov. 23 from French Guiana. Amazon has until July 30, 2029, to deploy and operate its first-generation fleet [1][8].

Key Points

  • Launch bottleneck: Amazon also has 24 launches booked on Blue Origin’s New Glenn rocket, which has been grounded since a May test explosion. Amazon has booked 24 Ariane 6 and 13 SpaceX Falcon 9 launches as well [1][10].
  • Satellites in storage: Amazon recently said it had manufactured its 1,000th Leo satellite, leaving more than half of its planned fleet on the ground awaiting launch [9].
  • Countdown delays: A nitrogen-plant problem scrubbed Friday’s attempt, network issues delayed Saturday’s, and an unexplained technical issue prompted a hold 20 seconds before liftoff Sunday. ULA has not said whether that final issue was linked to the failure [10].
  • Market gap: SpaceX’s Starlink network had more than 11,000 satellites and millions of customers, while Amazon was still deploying its planned Leo system, according to reporting before the failure [14][21].

Perspectives

United Launch Alliance: ULA said customers could follow an investigation using what it described as rigorous methodology. It pledged updates as it determines corrective action [3].

Timeline

  • 2006: Boeing and Lockheed Martin formed United Launch Alliance [14]
  • January 2024: Vulcan made its debut flight as ULA’s Atlas successor [25]
  • October 2024: A Vulcan booster nozzle problem occurred during its second flight [25][10]
  • February 2026: A later booster anomaly prompted further review and a national-security launch pause [7][10]
  • May 2026: Blue Origin’s New Glenn exploded during a ground test, leading to a grounding [1][9]
  • 15 November 2026: Vulcan failed during its Amazon Leo mission, losing 40 satellites [1][10]

Technical Details

  • First-stage overburn: Ars Technica reported that the twin methane-fueled BE-4 engines continued firing at least 30 seconds beyond the expected shutdown. Such an overburn could indicate that the first stage was compensating for underperformance, but ULA has not identified it as the cause of the failure [9].
  • Centaur telemetry: A telemetry-driven flight animation showed the Centaur V upper stage making unusual orientation adjustments early in its planned burn, after the first stage shut down and separated later than expected, according to flight coverage [10][18].

Industry Impact

  • U.S. national-security launches: Vulcan is contracted to fly roughly half of U.S. military national-security space missions in the coming years. The Space Force had not yet approved it to resume those launches after prior booster anomalies [9][7].
  • NASA lunar program: NASA selected a variant of ULA’s Centaur upper stage for future Space Launch System flights, according to reporting on the potential consequences of a Vulcan grounding [25].

Business Angle

  • Cadence target: Amazon had planned 20 or more launches in 2027 to deploy its fleet [6].

Q&A

Q: What happens if satellites miss Amazon’s earlier FCC deployment milestone?

A: Satellites that were not operational by the earlier milestone temporarily lose priority in key spectrum bands, although the FCC allowed Amazon to continue deploying satellites [1].
