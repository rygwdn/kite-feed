B.C. crews hold Saw Creek wildfire near Lytton

Category: Canada

British Columbia wildfire officials said Tuesday that the Saw Creek wildfire south of Lytton was being held after it triggered evacuations in and around the village [1][3][4][5][6]. The fire was still burning south of Lytton, and the status change followed a days-long response to a blaze discovered on Friday [6][2]. The Thompson-Nicola Regional District and the Village of Lytton lifted evacuation orders and alerts affecting about 200 properties, allowing residents to return home, while an evacuation order from the Lytton First Nation remained in effect [2]. The fire forced hundreds of residents from their homes, prompted a state of emergency, closed part of Highway 1 and destroyed at least one home in the regional district [2][5][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: The Saw Creek fire was discovered five years to the day after a wildfire destroyed most of the Village of Lytton on June 30, 2021 [5][2].

Key Points

  • Fire footprint: The Saw Creek blaze was last mapped at seven square kilometres after growing sixfold in one night [2][5].
  • Suppression effort: About 200 firefighters worked the fire, and officials cited cooler temperatures, light winds and ground-and-air operations for reduced fire activity [3][5][2].
  • Cause assessment: Officials believe the Saw Creek wildfire was human-caused, according to the Vancouver Sun’s account of fire service information [2].
