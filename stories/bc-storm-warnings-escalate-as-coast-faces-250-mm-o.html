B.C. storm warnings escalate as coast faces 250 mm of rain

Category: Canada

Environment Canada escalated rainfall warnings for southwest British Columbia as a new storm system was forecast to bring more than 250 millimetres of rain to parts of Vancouver Island. Heavy rain was expected Tuesday and Wednesday, with rain continuing later in the week. Orange warnings cover west and central Vancouver Island, Howe Sound and the Sea to Sky; the provincial River Forecast Centre issued a flood watch for much of southwest B.C. [2][5][7] The incoming storm follows weekend winds and rain that killed a driver in Surrey. Police also linked the storm to the death of a 78-year-old Vancouver pedestrian, and a canoeist is missing off northern Vancouver Island. Nearly 415,000 BC Hydro customers lost power at some point, and about 13,300 remained without electricity by late Monday afternoon. In Zeballos, evacuation orders and alerts remained in force for properties threatened by landslides. Officials expected to issue another update Friday, Nov. 13. [2][4][7]

Key Points

  • Flood risk: Forecast rainfall totals exceed 160 millimetres in Howe Sound and the Sea to Sky and 120 millimetres near Metro Vancouver’s mountains; officials warned of flooding and landslides. [2][5]
  • Search update: The Ma’amtagila Tribe Society said the missing canoeist’s vessel was found capsized; Environment Canada recorded nearby gusts of 88 kilometres an hour that Friday. [2][5]
  • Rescue account: North Vancouver resident Brittany Craig was swept into a creek while working on a water line. She clung to a log until firefighters pulled her out; a tree fell near the rescuers. [8]
  • Temperature swing: Meteorologist Rhythm Reet said an omega-block pattern could push temperatures 10 to 15 degrees above seasonal levels in parts of the North. Yellowknife was forecast to reach 3 C on Thursday, Nov. 12, compared with an average high near -7 C. [4]

Perspectives

BC Hydro: The utility said crews faced widespread damage and difficult access as they worked to restore power. It brought in additional crews, contractors and specialized equipment for repairs in the central interior. [6]
