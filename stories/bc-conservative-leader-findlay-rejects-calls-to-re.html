B.C. Conservative leader Findlay rejects calls to resign

Category: Canada

British Columbia Conservative Leader Kerry-Lynne Findlay said she would not resign after at least two caucus members asked her to step down during an emergency meeting Wednesday night. The pressure followed allegations that former party executive director Elaine Allan hired a private investigator to investigate current and former party figures, including legislators who had criticized Findlay. Party lawyer Bruce Hallsor said Allan hired the investigator, while the party said it was investigating how the inquiry was authorized and funded [1][5][8][10]. Findlay addressed the dispute Thursday at the Union of B.C. Municipalities meeting in Vancouver, saying she was “not leaving” and citing the 27,000 party members who elected her. Nine MLAs have left the Conservative caucus since she became leader at the end of May, citing concerns about her leadership [4][11][14].

Key Points

  • Investigation targets: A memo allegedly listed current MLAs Claire Rattée and Mandeep Dhaliwal; former MLAs Brennan Day, Rosalyn Bird, Áʼa:líya Warbus and Amelia Boultbee; former party officials; a communications firm; a journalist; and political staffers [10].
  • Party outreach: Party President Mauro Francis contacted people on the reported list to tell them they were under investigation [5].
  • Return invitation: Findlay said MLAs who left the party could discuss returning, but they had to do so immediately [14].
