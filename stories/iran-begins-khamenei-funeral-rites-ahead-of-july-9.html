Iran begins Khamenei funeral rites ahead of July 9 burial

Category: World

Iran began public mourning for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei on Friday after his body arrived at Tehran’s Grand Mosalla religious complex, where ceremonies are expected to draw millions before an official funeral on Saturday [1][9][40][49]. The program is due to continue through July 9, with events in Tehran, Qom and the Iraqi holy cities of Najaf and Karbala before Khamenei’s burial in Mashhad, his birthplace [1][9][15][10]. Khamenei, 86, was killed on February 28 in U.S.-Israeli strikes at the start of the war, and the funeral was delayed while the conflict continued [9][22][15][18]. Iranian officials expect crowds of up to about 20 million people, while authorities and organizers have framed the funeral as a large public display of support for the Islamic Republic after Khamenei was succeeded by his son, Mojtaba Khamenei, who has not appeared publicly since becoming supreme leader [9][5][6][49]. Iran warned the United States and Israel against any attack during the funeral period, while indirect U.S.-Iran talks were paused until after the commemorations [8][24]. Qatari and Pakistani mediators said the next meetings would be scheduled “at the earliest possible time” after the funeral events, and President Trump said Wednesday that talks on Iran’s denuclearization were making progress [24].

Key Points

  • City restrictions: Tehran ordered public and private offices to close from Saturday through Monday, restricted most private vehicles in the city center and planned partial or full airspace closures during the ceremonies [9].
  • Heat preparations: Workers installed cooling systems in the Grand Mosalla courtyard as mourners prepared to gather in temperatures expected to reach about 40C [11].
  • Hormuz warning: Iran’s military command told vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz to use routes designated by Iranian armed forces or face a “forceful response” [10][46].
  • Guard commander: Gen. Ahmad Vahidi, a senior Revolutionary Guard figure, appeared publicly for the first time in months at funeral-related events in Tehran [23][24][12].

  • Hospitality and transport: Iran arranged transport, accommodation and food for mourners, while hotels offered 50% discounts and buses and trains were diverted for funeral travel [20].

Historical Background

Ayatollah Ali Khamenei led Iran from 1989, after the death of Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, who became the Islamic Republic’s first supreme leader after the 1979 revolution [15][common]. Al Jazeera reported that Khamenei shaped Iran’s military and paramilitary apparatus during his decades in power [15]. Dawn reported that the funeral’s symbolism draws on Shia traditions of martyrdom and mourning during Muharram [20].
