Carney says Canada welcomes moves to restore free trade after U.S. midterms

Category: Canada

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Friday, Nov. 6, that Ottawa welcomes efforts by the incoming Democratic-controlled U.S. Congress to restore free trade. Democrats won control of both congressional chambers in the Nov. 3 midterms. Carney said he had not discussed proposed tariff legislation with Democrats and that Canada would continue negotiating with Washington [1][6]. The next Congress could challenge President Donald Trump’s tariffs, but Trump can veto legislation, and lawmakers would need two-thirds support in both chambers to override him. Canada suspended trade talks in August over U.S. demands that Carney said threatened Canadian sovereignty and French-language protections. The two governments have resumed discussions, but Carney says they have not reached a deal. Trump has also threatened tariffs on Canadian automobiles starting in January [1][6][8].

Primary Source: View original article

We welcome any moves to restore a free trading arrangement between Canada and the United States, which is in the interests of American families struggling with affordability

— Prime Minister Mark Carney (The Associated Press)

  • Senate proposal: Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer introduced legislation in September to repeal Trump’s tariffs, including duties on Canadian goods [6].
  • Tourism effects: Canadian return trips from the United States fell 25% in 2025; June 2026 trips remained 28.7% below June 2024 levels [4].
  • Trade concerns: Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand said tariffs on steel and aluminum are hitting Canadians hard and called for continued action to assert Canadian interests [1].
