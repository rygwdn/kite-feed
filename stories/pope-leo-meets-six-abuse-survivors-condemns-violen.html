Pope Leo meets six abuse survivors, condemns violence against women in Peru

Category: World

Pope Leo XIV met six survivors of abuse by Catholic clergy and church institutions in Lima on Thursday and urged the Church to pursue truth, justice and reparation. Leo spent nearly two decades as a missionary and bishop in Peru. The Vatican said he spoke individually with each survivor for more than an hour; some had belonged to groups linked to Sodalitium Christianae Vitae, a Peruvian lay Catholic movement dissolved in 2025 [34][7][10]. Earlier Thursday, Leo told Peru’s bishops to accompany communities facing poverty and injustice and strengthen abuse prevention and protection. In a public address, he condemned “machismo” and violence against women, called for women to be valued, protected and respected, and noted that poor, Indigenous and Afro-Peruvian women face additional marginalization [1][2][4]. Survivors and advocates pressed for remedies beyond listening, including financial help and a zero-tolerance approach to abusers. Leo is due to travel to Chiclayo on Friday, where he served as bishop from 2015 to 2023, before continuing to other parts of Peru [21][13][11].

We must respond as Jesus would, with truth, justice, forgiveness, making amends and an ever more resolute commitment to fostering a culture of prevention and protection for the weakest in light of such crimes

— Pope Leo XIV (ABC News)

Key Points

  • Femicide figures: Peru recorded 1,261 femicide victims from 2016 through 2024, and the annual number rose 44% over that period, according to the country’s statistics institute [2][3].
  • Vatican plan: The Vatican said in April 2026 it planned a Lima listening channel for abuse survivors and intended to use assets seized from Sodalitium to compensate them [7][21].
  • Youth gathering: A Lima stadium vigil drew about 52,000 young people, according to El País as cited by Mercopress; Leo urged them to cultivate their talents and not live only by others’ expectations [13].

Perspectives

Camila Bustamante, abuse survivor and journalist: She called the meeting an important milestone and said Leo appeared committed, while warning that survivors rarely find closure and urging them to keep working against abuse.

  • 12 November 2026: Leo met individually with six survivors at the Apostolic Nunciature in Lima [34]
  • 13 November 2026: Leo was due to travel to Chiclayo, where he had served as bishop [13][11]

Historical Background

Pope Francis dissolved Sodalitium Christianae Vitae, a lay Catholic movement founded in 1971, in 2025 after years of allegations of sexual, physical and psychological abuse and corruption [7][21]. The movement acknowledged that its leaders abused 19 minors and 10 adults between 1975 and 2002, according to La Libre [14].
