October skywatchers get Saturn opposition, Orionids and Jupiter occultation

Category: Science

October brings several notable skywatching events: Saturn reaches opposition on Oct. 4, the Moon passes in front of Jupiter for observers in North America on Oct. 6, and the Orionid meteor shower is active from Oct. 2 to Nov. 7, peaking overnight Oct. 21–22 [7][13][9][1]. Earth passes through debris left by Halley’s Comet, producing the Orionids [1]. At opposition, Saturn appears opposite the Sun in Earth’s sky. The Jupiter occultation occurs before dawn, while the Orionids are best observed later in the month [7][9][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • At its peak, the Orionids may produce 10 to 20 meteors per hour and are best viewed between midnight and dawn [29][1].
  • Saturn should be visible to the unaided eye, while a small telescope can reveal its rings [7][2].
  • The Jupiter occultation is centered on 10:16 UTC, or 6:16 a.m. EDT. The waning crescent Moon will be about 20% illuminated, and local disappearance and reappearance times vary along the viewing path [9].
