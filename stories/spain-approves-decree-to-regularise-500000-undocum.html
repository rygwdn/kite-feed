Spain approves decree to regularise 500,000 undocumented migrants

Category: World

Spain’s cabinet has approved a decree to regularise undocumented migrants and some asylum seekers, with the government saying the measure could cover around 500,000 people and is expected to take effect in April [6][7][9]. Eligibility described in the reports includes proving residence in Spain for at least five months before 31 December 2025, having no criminal record, and in some cases having applied for international protection by that date [3][1][6]. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez and his government argue the plan supports Spain’s economy and helps address labour needs alongside an ageing population, while critics on the right call it a “reward” for irregular migration and pledge to roll it back if they return to power [2][4][7].

Spain “lacks people” and must choose between being “a closed and poor nation” or “opening itself to the world to ensure prosperity.”

Did you know: Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez argued that migration accounted for 80% of Spain’s dynamic economic growth over the last six years [2].

Key Points

  • Who qualifies: The scheme is described as offering one-year residence and work permits, with beneficiaries able to work in any sector and anywhere in Spain, according to Spain’s migration minister [10][4].
  • Political pathway: Several outlets report the decree route lets the minority government act amid a fragmented parliament, putting immigration at the center of Spain’s domestic political divide [4][7].
  • Economic argument: Sánchez has framed migration as a major contributor to Spain’s recent growth, with one report citing his view that it accounted for 80% of the country’s dynamic economic growth in the past six years [2].
  • Opposition backlash: The opposition Popular Party’s Alberto Núñez Feijóo is reported to have vowed to repeal the measure if elected, while Vox leader Santiago Abascal called for mass deportations and used “invasion” rhetoric [4].
  • European context: Multiple reports describe Spain’s move as running against a broader European trend toward tougher migration policies, with one comparing it to approaches championed by President Trump in the US [7][6][5].

Timeline

  • April: Decree is expected to come into effect in April [6][4].

Technical Details

  • Regularisation decree: A government decree sets out an expedited pathway to legal residence and work authorisation for eligible undocumented migrants and certain asylum seekers [6][7].
  • Residence proof rule: Applicants must document at least five months’ residence in Spain before the end of 2025, according to several reports [3][6].
  • Criminal record check: Eligibility is limited to people without a criminal record, as described in reports of the criteria [3][6].

Industry Impact

  • Labour market: Officials argue the measure targets workforce shortages and an ageing population, potentially widening the pool of legally employable workers across sectors [4][9].
  • Asylum system: By including some asylum seekers and people who applied for international protection before a cut-off date, the decree could shift caseloads and incentives within Spain’s protection system [1][6].
  • Domestic politics: The plan has intensified party competition on immigration, with reported promises to repeal it and calls for deportations shaping Spain’s political agenda [4][7].

Historical Background

Spain has periodically used “regularisation” drives to bring undocumented residents into the formal labour market, a tool European governments sometimes use to reduce informality and improve access to rights and tax compliance [common]. The current plan is presented by Spanish officials and several outlets as a deliberate contrast to the tougher migration rhetoric and policy shifts gaining ground in parts of Europe and the United States [7][5].

Q&A

Q: How will Spain verify “five months’ residence” in practice?

A: The articles describe the requirement but do not spell out the documentation standards or verification method; those details are expected to come with implementation guidance when the decree takes effect [3][6].

Q: Will the permits be renewable after the initial period?

A: The reporting cited here mentions one-year residence and work permits but does not describe renewal rules, suggesting further regulations will clarify next steps [4][10].

Q: Does the plan apply to people with criminal convictions?

A: No—eligibility is described as limited to applicants who can show they have no criminal record [3][6].
