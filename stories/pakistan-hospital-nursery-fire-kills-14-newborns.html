Pakistan hospital nursery fire kills 14 newborns

Category: World

Pakistani officials said at least 14 newborns died early Wednesday after a fire broke out in a nursery at the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences, a major government hospital in Islamabad [4][9][6][7][18][25]. Officials said at least 15 babies were in the nursery and one was rescued; Geo TV put the toll at 15 and said 16 newborns were in the ward [4][7][13][10][22][28]. Rescue officials said the fire began at about 6:45 a.m. after an air-conditioning unit compressor apparently exploded inside the nursery in the gynecology ward. A preliminary account cited by Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry said investigators were checking where an electrical short circuit occurred [4][9][7][26]. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif ordered an immediate investigation, and the Islamabad district magistrate appointed a multiagency fact-finding committee to examine the cause, spread, fire-safety measures and emergency response within 24 hours [4][19][30].

Did you know: PIMS is centrally located in Islamabad and is the hospital where former Prime Minister Imran Khan was briefly treated the week before the fire [2][28].

Key Points

  • Emergency deployment: The Islamabad administration said six fire brigade vehicles and four ambulances took part in the rescue operation [25][42].
  • Oxygen risk: Health Minister Mustafa Kamal said many infants were in incubators or on oxygen, and that oxygen cylinders and pipelines helped the blaze spread rapidly [7][18][31][42].
  • Identification process: Police said some newborns were so badly burned that DNA testing would be needed to confirm identities before remains could be released to families [12][26][64].
  • Public hospitals: Accounts cited by Reuters described Pakistan’s subsidized state healthcare system as under-resourced and overburdened, with watchdogs frequently criticizing public facilities as mismanaged [2][22][28].

Historical Background

Fires in large buildings are frequent in Pakistan. Accounts cited by AFP point to weak safety laws, building codes and enforcement [6][16][27][36]. In Karachi, more than 65 people died in a shopping centre fire in January, at least 11 died in a mall fire in 2023, at least 16 workers died in a 2021 factory fire, and at least 250 labourers died in the 2012 Baldia Town garment factory fire, where the building had no fire escape [16][27].
