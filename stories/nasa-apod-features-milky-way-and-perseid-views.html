NASA APOD features Milky Way and Perseid views

Category: Science

NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day for August 16 featured a 16-image panorama from 2014 showing the Milky Way above Silex Spring in Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming [2]. NASA described Silex Spring as a roughly 10-meter-wide pool whose colors come from layers of bacteria, with steam rising from water heated by underground magma linked to the Yellowstone Hotspot [2]. The August 15 APOD highlighted the Perseid meteor shower from near Grisslehamn, Sweden, in a composite image taken near the shower’s August 12 peak [3]. NASA said the Perseids are produced when dust grains from periodic comet 109P/Swift-Tuttle vaporize in Earth’s atmosphere, and that this year’s peak coincided with a New Moon, leaving the meteors undimmed by bright moonlight [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Foreground contrast: NASA noted the Milky Way band in the Yellowstone image was unrelated to the hot spring and lay far in the distance, lit by billions of stars [2].
  • Meteor brightness: The Sweden composite recorded two bright Perseid meteors and a reflection in the water, with the meteors appearing almost as bright as Altair [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Meteor characteristics: The Perseids are known for bright, fast meteors, and the shower’s radiant lies in the constellation Perseus, linking the display to a specific sky location [3].
  • Geothermal setting: Artificial illumination highlights Silex Spring’s vivid colors, revealing layers of bacteria that thrive in the hot, mineral-rich water heated by magma beneath Yellowstone [2].

Historical Background

NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day presents a different astronomy-related image each day with a short explanation written by a professional astronomer [2][3]. The Yellowstone Hotspot last produced a supervolcanic eruption about 640,000 years ago, and NASA said another such eruption would affect a large part of North America [2].
