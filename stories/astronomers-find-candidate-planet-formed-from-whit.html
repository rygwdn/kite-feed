Astronomers find candidate planet formed from white dwarf debris

Category: Science

Astronomers say the white dwarf HS 0209+0832 may host a second-generation planet that formed from material expelled when its star died. In a study published Oct. 5 in Nature Astronomy, researchers describe the candidate as a gas giant roughly the size of Jupiter [1][2]. The planet has not been confirmed, and researchers are investigating how such worlds form and whether they are common. Lead author Jamie Williams plans to continue using Hubble over the next several years to study this potential population [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Chemical clue: Researchers reanalyzing Hubble observations from 1999 identified unusually strong niobium signatures, which they link to material expelled during the star’s death [1].
  • Supporting observations: Archived FUSE data also showed strong niobium signatures, while four months of TESS monitoring revealed recurring brightness changes consistent with a planet orbiting about 6 million kilometers from the white dwarf [1].
  • Atmospheric loss: Researchers think the white dwarf’s intense heat may be stripping gas from the nearby candidate, potentially producing a tail and a disk of material around the star [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Possible long-term fate: Williams said the candidate may survive as the white dwarf cools and eventually remain in its stable habitable zone for millions of years [1].
  • Possible formation process: Researchers propose that expelled, chemically enriched stellar material gathered into a planet. Material later shed by the suspected planet could account for niobium detected at the white dwarf [1].
