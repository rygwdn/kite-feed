Manitoba promises ER reopenings and middle-class tax cut before election

Category: Canada

Manitoba’s NDP government pledged to reopen Winnipeg’s Victoria Hospital emergency department by next summer and deliver a middle-class tax cut in a throne speech Tuesday that Premier Wab Kinew described as his last before the 2027 election [1][3][4]. The previous government closed Victoria’s emergency room, and reopening it was a 2023 campaign promise [3]. The province also plans to offer free shingles vaccinations to some seniors starting in July 2027 [3]. The speech also promised a new emergency room in Eriksdale by next fall and said details of the tax cut would come in next spring’s budget [1][3]. Kinew said balancing the budget remains a priority as the government pursues affordability measures [1].

The economic horse pulls the social cart.

— Premier Wab Kinew (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Vaccine eligibility: The free two-dose Shingrix program is planned for people aged 65 to 79 and immunocompromised people aged 18 and older, leaving out some people over 50 [3].
  • Crime unit: The new fraud-and-corruption unit will have five Crown attorneys and follows a recommendation from the inquiry into Winnipeg’s police headquarters construction [3].
  • Business measures: Manitoba plans to match the federal capital-investment tax deduction, which Kinew estimated would cost the province nine figures, and freeze Crown land lease increases for a fourth year [3][4].

Perspectives

Manitoba Progressive Conservative Party: Interim leader Konrad Narth called the speech a repeat of the 2023 campaign and said his party’s proposal to exempt the first $30,000 of income from provincial tax would offer more measurable savings [3].

Sources: CBC News

Manitoba Nurses Union: President Darlene Jackson said the speech included no dedicated funding to begin implementing nurse-to-patient ratios, and warned that opening more emergency departments without adding staff could shift nurses between facilities [1].
