Google and Samsung roll out Gemini task automation

Category: AI

Google and Samsung spotlighted a new kind of “do it for me” mobile AI: Gemini on Android that can carry out certain multi-step tasks inside popular apps, like requesting a ride or placing a food-delivery order [1][9][10]. Google says the feature is launching soon and, at first, will be limited to the newest flagship devices—Samsung’s Galaxy S26 lineup and Google’s Pixel 10—making them early showcases for Android’s latest AI capabilities [1][6][10]. Beyond automation, the companies leaned on safety and convenience upgrades tied to the S26 launch. PCMag reports that the Galaxy S26 Ultra adds an exclusive “Privacy Display” meant to reduce shoulder-surfing by making the screen harder to read from the side, while Google highlighted additions like call scam detection and updates to Circle to Search coming to Samsung’s newest devices [4][6][2]. Several outlets described the news as another step in the shift from chat-style assistants to action-taking agents on phones. The Verge and CNBC cast the rollout as a sign that Android is pushing assistant-like features deeper into real app workflows, and Samsung used the event to reinforce its broader “AI everywhere” message across new hardware [10][20][19][3].

Key Points

  • Design/material tradeoffs: Hands-ons point to small changes—slightly thinner and lighter, a switch to an aluminum frame (from prior titanium), and a curved-top S Pen. Samsung still skips built-in Qi2-style magnets for magnetic accessories [59][62][175].
  • Pricing context: PCMag reports the Galaxy S26 Ultra starts at $1,299.99, a reminder that the most feature-packed model is also the priciest option in the lineup [3].
  • Hands-free gestures: Samsung is adding head gestures—such as nodding to answer and shaking to reject calls—as part of the Buds4 generation feature set [129][90].
  • Event setting: PCMag’s hands-on reports put the S26 unveiling and early demos at Samsung’s Unpacked event in San Francisco [5][8].
  • Design tweak: Engadget says Samsung kept the “blade”/stem look but made it less angular and dropped the previous generation’s flashy lights, switching to a flatter metal panel with an engraved guide for pinch controls [53].
  • Camera software specifics: Several of the touted camera upgrades are software-led, including Samsung’s Object Aware Engine for improved selfie and portrait rendering and updated Super Steady video behavior. Coverage also highlighted the Ultra’s creator tools and low-light focus [60][29].
  • Canada pricing: In Canada, MobileSyrup lists Buds4 at C$249 and Buds4 Pro at C$329, with a March 11 launch and preorders starting Feb. 25 [64].
  • Multi-assistant strategy: Samsung is pushing a “choose-your-agent” approach—Galaxy AI alongside options like Gemini and Bixby, and with third parties such as Perplexity getting a bigger spotlight as part of the S26’s broader agentic pitch [24][106][35].
  • Privacy Display (how it’s positioned): Hands-on coverage describes the S26 Ultra’s Privacy Display as a built-in screen mode designed to narrow off-angle readability and help deter shoulder-surfing [4][5].
  • Deal mechanics (not just 'preorder is open'): The value of preorder deals depends on the promo type—trade-ins that favor existing Galaxy owners, limited-time storage upgrades, or retailer gift-card bundles that vary by region and seller [132][65][37].
  • Durability bump: Buds4 are rated IP54, while Buds4 Pro step up to IP57 for water and sweat resistance [31].
  • Unpacked extras: Alongside the S26 phones, Samsung used the event to push its broader “AI everywhere” strategy, including new Galaxy Buds4/Buds4 Pro and a Perplexity AI tie-in highlighted in Unpacked coverage [3][7][14].
  • Proactive UI features: Samsung’s new Now Nudge is drawing comparisons to Pixel-style contextual suggestions. Some features appear to depend on Samsung Keyboard, a detail that could influence how many people actually use it [139].
  • Battery ratings: Samsung lists up to 5 hours per charge for Buds4 (6 with ANC off) and up to 6 hours for Buds4 Pro (7 with ANC off), with up to 24–30 hours total including the case depending on ANC usage [31].

Historical Background

As year-to-year hardware gains have become more incremental, smartphone makers have increasingly leaned on “AI features” as the headline differentiator in flagship updates [85]. Google introduced real-time scam detection for calls on Pixel devices in 2024. Samsung’s S26 launch stands out for bringing that capability to Samsung’s Phone app, a sign that real-time fraud warnings may be moving toward a broader Android security baseline beyond Pixel [50].

Action Items

  • Compare total upgrade cost before preordering: If you’re planning to buy, check trade-in and gift-card or storage promos across Samsung, Amazon, and major carriers. Offers vary, and many “free phone” deals rely on bill credits spread across long installment terms [45][70][71].
  • Compare fit styles before ordering: Choose Buds4 if you prefer an open-fit style, or Buds4 Pro if you want an in-ear seal; both options are described in retailer and spec coverage [30][53].
  • Try task automation on supported phones: If you have (or upgrade to) a Galaxy S26 or Pixel 10, look for Gemini’s multi-step task automation in the Gemini app/Android AI features when it becomes available [1][6].
  • Check preorder bundles: Best Buy says My Best Buy Plus members can get a $20 gift card with Buds4 preorders and My Best Buy Total members can get a $30 gift card with Buds4 Pro preorders; Amazon also has a Buds4 Pro preorder bundle that includes a $30 gift card [32][90].
  • Enable anti-scam protections: On a Galaxy S26, check phone/security settings for the call scam detection options highlighted as part of the new Android AI features [6].
  • Turn on scam warnings if you want them: On a Galaxy S26, look in the Phone app settings for Scam Detection. Google says it’s off by default, so you’ll need to enable it if you want real-time alerts [58].
  • Try Privacy Display where it matters most: On an S26 Ultra, set Privacy Display to apply only to specific apps or notifications so you get extra privacy without permanently narrowing viewing angles [86][42].
  • Verify water-resistance needs: If you plan to work out in them, the Buds4 Pro’s IP57 rating offers more protection than the Buds4’s IP54 rating [31].
