Apple foldable iPhone prototypes draw praise before expected September launch

Category: Technology

Apple’s first foldable iPhone, described in reports as the iPhone Ultra or iPhone Fold, has drawn positive early feedback from people who used prototypes before an expected September launch, according to Bloomberg journalist Mark Gurman as cited by technology publications [1][5][10][16][31]. Testers praised the device’s pocket-friendly book-style design, hinge feel and durability, and iPad-like app layouts on the larger inner display [5][9][15][22][33]. The device is expected to have a roughly 5.5-inch outer display and a roughly 7.8-inch internal display, putting its open screen size slightly below an iPad mini and above current slab iPhones [5][10][13][15][22]. Reports say Apple made hardware trade-offs to keep the foldable thin: the phone is expected to omit a telephoto camera, rely on a dual rear camera system led by a 48-megapixel main lens, and use Touch ID in the side button rather than Face ID [6][10][11][32][30]. Apple is expected to introduce the foldable iPhone alongside the iPhone 18 Pro models at its annual fall event, with several reports pointing to an event around Sept. 8 or Sept. 9 [5][10][33]. Reports put pricing above $2,000, with higher storage configurations potentially reaching $2,500 [2][10][21][11][25].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: If Apple uses Touch ID as reported, it would be the first iPhone without Face ID since Apple introduced the technology with the iPhone X in 2017 [6].

Key Points

  • Display size: Cult of Mac estimated the foldable would offer roughly 29 square inches of screen area, compared with 17.9 square inches on the iPhone 17 Pro Max [3].
  • Hardware omissions: 9to5Mac said leaked dummy units show the device will also lack an Action button, MagSafe and a mute switch, in addition to a telephoto camera [6].
  • Biometric shift: Reports attribute the move from Face ID to side-button Touch ID to the thin foldable body and the difficulty of fitting Face ID components for both the inner and outer displays [6][30].
  • Market context: Samsung launched its Galaxy Z Fold 8 shortly before Apple’s expected reveal, and several reports compare Apple’s device with Samsung’s book-style foldable form factor [5][16][33].

Perspectives

9to5Mac: Chance Miller identified five likely omissions from the device, including a telephoto camera, Action button, Face ID, MagSafe and mute switch.

Sources: 9to5Mac

Android Headlines: The site said Apple’s iPad-ready app library could reduce the need for developers to optimize apps from scratch for the foldable screen.

Technical Details

  • Fold-state APIs: iFanr said Xcode 27 references parameters such as foldState and angleDegrees, suggesting Apple has prepared software hooks for fold angle and device state [23].
  • Thin chassis: Reports put the unfolded thickness at about 4.5mm and the folded thickness around 9mm to 9.5mm, limiting room for camera and authentication modules [10][26][23].
  • Hinge materials: MacDailyNews said the hinge is rumored to use Liquidmetal, an amorphous metal alloy valued for strength-to-weight ratio, elasticity and wear resistance [9].

Industry Impact

  • Foldable market: iFanr cited Bloomberg data saying foldables represented about 1.6% of global smartphone shipments in 2025, indicating Apple would enter a small but established category [23].
  • China demand: iFanr said China accounted for more than half of global foldable-phone shipments in 2025, with sales above 10 million units [23].
  • Developer ecosystem: iFanr said Apple used WWDC guidance to push developers toward layouts that adapt across device sizes, orientations and aspect ratios [23][24].

Historical Background

Commercial foldable phones have been available since Royole introduced the FlexPai in 2018, followed by foldable models from Samsung, Huawei and others [3]. Samsung released the first Galaxy Fold in 2019, and by 2026 its line had reached the Galaxy Z Fold 8 [23]. Apple stayed out of the category during earlier hardware cycles, when display creases, hinge durability and device thickness remained recurring foldable-phone issues [3].

Business Angle

  • The foldable's premium price point could raise iPhone average selling prices if demand extends beyond early adopters [2][25].
  • The reported hardware trade-offs could reduce the appeal to Pro Max buyers who expect Apple’s highest-end features in its most expensive phone [10][6].
  • Limited launch supply would cap near-term unit impact even if demand proves strong [5].

Action Items

  • Compare camera needs before buying: If optical zoom matters to you, compare the reported dual-camera foldable setup with Apple’s Pro models before placing a preorder [6][10].
  • Wait for independent reviews: ComputerBase noted that full reviews are not expected before the sales launch, so buyers can wait for battery, durability and camera testing rather than relying on prototype impressions [16].
