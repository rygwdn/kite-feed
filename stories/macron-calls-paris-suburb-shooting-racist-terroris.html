Macron calls Paris suburb shooting racist terrorism as prosecutors investigate

Category: World

A gunman killed one person and wounded five others, including a 12-year-old girl, in shootings across Trappes and La Verrière, west of Paris, on Friday. Police arrested the 39-year-old suspect in nearby Coignières less than 20 minutes after the shooting began, French officials said [30][4][1]. French President Emmanuel Macron called the attack a “terrorist and racist” act, saying victims were targeted because of their skin colour or perceived religion. France’s National Anti-Terrorist Prosecutor’s Office (PNAT) opened an investigation into murder linked to a terrorist enterprise. Investigators are examining whether victims were targeted because of their actual or perceived race, ethnicity, nationality or religion; the motive remains under investigation [30][2][12]. The suspect’s car contained a Sten-type submachine gun and a tear-gas canister. Investigators found three gas cylinders and wires connected to a timer in his apartment. A close family member was also taken into custody. Investigators are examining possible terrorist conspiracy and possession of a Category A weapon [4][33][1].

What happened today is, I am telling you, a turning point, because it is the first time we have had a terrorist act that was planned to kill our compatriots, our children, based on the colour of their skin or their religion

— French President Emmanuel Macron (Reuters)

Key Points

  • Police response: Five officers from France’s anti-crime brigade stopped the suspect; Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said the shooting lasted about 15 minutes [6][33].
  • Suspect’s record: Prosecutors said the suspect had four previous convictions that were automatically expunged under French law; he was not on a security-services list of serious threats [1].
  • Victim identified: The man killed was a French citizen born in 2000; Trappes Mayor Ali Rabeh said he was of African origin [14][30].
  • Protest security: Authorities planned to deploy about 70,000 police officers and gendarmes for Saturday’s demonstrations. Major Paris museums closed, and Art Basel Paris canceled its weekend program [6][3].
  • Medical condition: The 12-year-old girl was shot eight times; La Verrière Mayor Nicolas Dainville said she was seriously injured but in stable condition [1][6].

Perspectives

Ali Rabeh, mayor of Trappes: Rabeh described the attack as probably Islamophobic and racist, and urged politicians to condemn rhetoric that he said could ideologically arm people capable of such violence [30][2].

French Council of the Muslim Faith: The national Muslim umbrella body warned that hate speech, stigmatization and scapegoating could have consequences, and urged Muslims in France to remain vigilant [24].

French President Emmanuel Macron: Macron said the attack should not divide the country and called for respect and unity, warning that people would fear they could be targeted next [2].

Sources: The Guardian

Technical Details

  • Sten-type firearm: The Sten is a British World War II-era submachine gun; France’s Category A rules cover military-grade firearms or weapons capable of firing in bursts [2][1].
  • Apartment search: Prosecutors said they found three gas cylinders and electrical wires connected to a timer in the suspect’s apartment. The investigation also covers possible conspiracy with a terrorist group to prepare crimes against people [33][1].

Historical Background

Trappes drew national attention in 2014-16, when dozens of residents reportedly left to join jihadist groups in Syria and Iraq. Parliamentary inquiries and court records documented recruitment networks and attempted departures [15]. In 2021, Jordan Bardella, then head of the far-right National Rally, described the town as “a miniature Islamic republic” and called for the removal of Mayor Ali Rabeh [15]. Trappes is a working-class town of about 35,000 people, roughly one-third of whom are foreign-born, according to France’s statistics agency [15].
