Astronomers identify three black holes in ancient galaxy

Category: Science

An international team led by the Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics identified three actively accreting supermassive black holes in the galaxy J0148-4214, whose light has taken more than 12.5 billion years to reach Earth [2][3]. The system is seen as it was about 1.2 billion years after the Big Bang [2][3]. The team used the galaxy’s redshift, z=5.02, to determine its distance and lookback time [2][3]. It detected the black holes through signatures from fast-moving hydrogen in accretion disks, not by imaging the black holes directly [2][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Merger path: The two black holes nearest the galaxy’s center could spiral together within roughly 700 million years; the study describes the system as evidence of an approaching merger, not a direct observation of one [1].
  • Early growth: Researchers said the system may help explain how supermassive black holes reached large masses so early in cosmic history [2][3].
  • Mass scale: One summary of the finding put the largest black hole in J0148-4214 at about 80 million times the mass of the Sun [1].

Perspectives

Hannah Übler: Übler said the finding suggests early-universe processes could bring massive black holes together efficiently and prepare systems for mergers that future gravitational-wave observatories may detect.
