Scientists identify six deep-Earth structures using AI

Category: Science

Researchers used a machine-learning program to examine more than 2 million earthquake recordings collected over about 35 years and identified nearly 175,000 faint seismic signals tied to structures near the boundary between Earth’s mantle and liquid outer core [1][3]. The study reported six areas that likely host significant heterogeneities not previously documented [1][3]. The structures are near the mantle-core boundary, about 2,900 kilometers beneath the surface. Scientists cannot observe that region directly and instead infer conditions from how earthquake waves travel through the planet [1][3]. The researchers wrote that the six areas provide “clear priority targets for future exploration of Earth’s deep interior” [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Earth’s deepest human-made borehole, the Kola Superdeep Borehole, reached about 12.3 kilometers, far short of the roughly 2,900-kilometer depth of the mantle-core boundary [common].

Key Points

  • Data scale: The algorithm screened earthquake records from events of magnitude 6 or greater between 1990 and 2024, yielding 174,929 precursor signals [3].
  • Manual checks: Specialists manually reviewed and corrected examples from the system, then used the revised cases to improve the algorithm [3].
  • Mapping result: The large signal set enabled the most detailed map so far of seismic-scattering zones in the lower mantle, according to the study description [3].
  • Hazard relevance: ScienceAlert said understanding deep-Earth motion helps scientists study processes linked to earthquakes and other natural hazards [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Data reliability: The analysis uses a large observational dataset, but the reported structures remain indirect inferences from seismic-wave behavior rather than direct samples or images [1][3].
