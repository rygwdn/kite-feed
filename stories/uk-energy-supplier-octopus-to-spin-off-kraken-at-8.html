UK energy supplier Octopus to spin off Kraken at $8.65bn

Category: Business

UK energy supplier Octopus Energy agreed to sell a $1 billion stake in its software-and-AI unit Kraken Technologies, valuing the business at $8.65 billion, as a first step toward spinning it out as a separate company [5][6][3][7][4]. The stake sale is backed by investors including D1 Capital Partners, Fidelity, and Ontario Teachers’ Pension Plan, which will buy equity in the unit [7]. Octopus is positioning the move as a way to let Kraken grow independently while continuing to support utilities and energy retailers running customer and grid operations on the platform [6][5].

Technical Details

  • Spin-off (demerger): A spin-off (often called a demerger in the UK) separates a business into a standalone company, and Octopus is preparing to do this with Kraken after selling a minority stake [5][3].
  • Minority stake sale: Selling a minority stake brings in outside capital and external valuation signals; Octopus’s deal sells a $1bn stake and values Kraken at $8.65bn [5][7][6].
  • Utility software platform: Kraken is positioned as a technology platform used to run utility operations, which is why Octopus is treating it as a scalable tech business rather than only an in-house tool [6][5].

Industry Impact

  • Utility digitization: The deal spotlights rising investor appetite for “picks-and-shovels” utility technology—software platforms that can modernize how energy retailers manage customers and operations—rather than betting only on power generation assets [6][7].
  • Capital formation: A large, disclosed valuation and blue-chip shareholders may make it easier for other energy-tech units to raise growth capital or pursue separations, especially when strategic investors want exposure without buying an entire utility business [7][5].
  • Public-market comparables: With global equity markets ending 2025 strongly, dealmakers may be more willing to pursue spin-offs and stake sales that rely on healthy risk appetite and clear valuation benchmarks [8].
