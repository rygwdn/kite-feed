Spain train crash kills 68 as investigators probe control error

Category: World

A Renfe high-speed train struck a stationary Iryo train near Urda in central Spain on Oct. 28, killing at least 68 people and injuring 107, including 15 in serious condition. The two trains, travelling from Madrid toward Seville, carried a total of 544 passengers. It was Spain’s second deadly high-speed rail crash this year, following a January collision near Adamuz that killed 46 [1][guardian.com#2][8][18]. Transport Minister Óscar Puente said a computer failure disabled the LZB safety system on a 63-kilometre stretch at about 8:30 a.m., stopping six trains. He said Madrid’s control centre then authorized the Renfe train to resume at normal speed; it travelled about 25 kilometres before hitting the stopped Iryo train at 8:53 a.m. Puente called the instruction “inexplicable” and said the drivers were not at fault. Iryo said its train had not been authorized to restart and remained stopped after the signal failure, according to ABC News Australia. Investigators are examining the differing accounts [38][8][43]. Spain declared three days of national mourning, from Oct. 29 through 31. The Rail Accident Investigation Commission and judicial authorities are investigating. Rail services between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia were suspended [26][31][8].

  • Election campaign: The crash occurred about a month before Spain’s Nov. 29 snap election; the Popular Party and governing Socialists suspended campaign events, while Vox continued rallies [18][7].
  • Rail disruption: Adif set up assistance points at major stations, including Madrid-Atocha, Seville, Córdoba and Málaga [70].
  • Local response: Urda residents brought first-aid supplies, blankets and water, while the town’s sports hall became a survivor centre and its primary school received relatives [47][48].
  • Network use: Spain’s rail network carried 45 million travellers last year, including millions of tourists, according to NPR [18].

Historical Background

Spain’s dedicated high-speed rail network opened in 1992 and had no fatal crash on its high-speed lines until the Adamuz disaster in January 2026, according to the Associated Press and NPR [5][18]. In 2013, a high-speed train derailed near Santiago de Compostela, killing 80 people; an official inquiry cited speeding and a driver distracted by a call from a superior [74]. The January Adamuz collision remains under investigation; a preliminary Civil Guard report cited a possible defect in the track, rail or weld while keeping other explanations open [74].

Humanitarian Impact

Relatives sought information as rescue workers continued searching the wreckage [guardian.com#2][25]. The Civil Guard established offices in Madrid, Toledo, Córdoba and Seville for relatives to provide DNA samples to help identify victims; blood-donation points opened in Castilla-La Mancha and Madrid [45].
