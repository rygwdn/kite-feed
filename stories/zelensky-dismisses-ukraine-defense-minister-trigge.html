Zelensky dismisses Ukraine defense minister, triggering protests

Category: World

President Volodymyr Zelensky dismissed Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov in a wartime government reshuffle, prompting protests in Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities on July 16 [2][4][5][8][18]. Fedorov, 35, had held the post for about six months and was credited with pushing drone warfare, digitization and defense-ministry modernization, but his work brought him into conflict with Ukraine’s military establishment [3][9][20][52]. The dispute centered on Fedorov’s relationship with Commander-in-Chief Gen. Oleksandr Syrskyi; Fedorov said he had urged Zelensky to replace Syrskyi and Chief of the General Staff Andrii Hnatov, while Zelensky said the conflict between the Defense Ministry and General Staff had become systemic and required him to intervene [18][13][15][24]. Zelensky named acting SBU security chief Yevhen Khmara as acting defense minister and said Khmara’s tasks would include continuing defense-sector reforms, expanding long-range strikes on Russia and supplying the army [14][18][16]. The reshuffle also changed Ukraine’s civilian government: parliament accepted Prime Minister Yulia Svyrydenko’s resignation on July 14, then approved Naftogaz head Serhii Koretskyi as prime minister in a 289-1 vote, with 21 abstentions [12][28][8]. Zelensky nominated Koretskyi, citing his energy-sector record and the need to prepare Ukraine for another wartime winter, when Russian attacks on the power grid intensify [28].

Did you know: Fedorov said he had joined Zelensky’s team in 2019 and was the last remaining minister from Zelensky’s first government [15].

Key Points

  • Protests spread: Demonstrations were reported in Kyiv, Ivano-Frankivsk, Vinnytsia, Lutsk, Khmelnytskyi, Lviv, Uzhhorod, Dnipro, Ternopil and Odesa, with chants including “Bring back Fedorov,” “Shame,” and “Syrsky must go” [11].
  • Internal backlash: Ukrainian Air Force deputy commander Pavlo Yelizarov resigned in protest, and RFE/RL reported that two outside Defense Ministry advisers also quit after Fedorov’s removal [1][39].
  • Wartime attacks: Ukraine’s Emergency Service said Russian missiles killed two people and wounded five others, including a child, in Kyiv overnight as the political dispute unfolded [8][28].
  • Minister’s record: Fedorov said his team disrupted Russian use of Starlink terminals, supported strikes on Russian logistics in occupied Crimea and reformed procurement in ways he said saved billions of hryvnias [52][70].
