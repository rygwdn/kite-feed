Apple tests whether users understand health AI advice grounded in medical research

Category: AI

Apple’s health-AI study will examine whether people can understand and use medical chatbot answers, testing the gap between chatbots’ performance on clinical tests and consumers’ ability to interpret complex guidance. A February 2026 study cited by ABC News found that chatbots identified conditions correctly nearly 95% of the time in clinical cases, while everyday users reached the right answer less than 35% of the time when interpreting complex guidance [5]. Apple and clinical AI platform OpenEvidence launched the U.S. study on Nov. 15 to assess trust, comprehension, answer quality and effects on everyday health actions. OpenEvidence provides responses grounded in peer-reviewed medical literature. Participants will rate answers and report how they use the information, including whether it helps them prepare for doctor visits [2][9]. The project is a research study, not a consumer health feature, and Apple has not announced when it will publish results [3][13]. ABC News reported that 35% of U.S. adults, including about half of adults under 30, already use AI chatbots [5]. Harvard Medical School professor John Brownstein warned that unclear or incomplete responses can create false reassurance or delay care, and said researchers need to learn how people actually use these tools [5].

Did you know: OpenEvidence’s monthly U.S. clinician users rose from 355 in January 2024 to more than 703,000 in June 2026, according to Chief Healthcare Executive [8].

Key Points

  • Clinical sources: OpenEvidence draws from sources including The New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA, Nature, Cochrane and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network, with citations linked to answers [9][3].
  • Research access: Apple told ABC News it intends to make the research broadly available to support computer-assisted health communication [5].
  • Safety limits: The app’s safeguards can recommend follow-up with a provider, but the study has no live medical monitoring and staff will not contact participants about self-harm questions [3].
  • Study design: The project includes baseline and monthly surveys and collects demographic information to study communication needs. An independent review board oversees the research [9].
  • Eligibility limits: The study excludes people outside the United States and those without an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone 15 Pro or later, narrowing the population represented by its eventual results [2][7].
  • Engagement prompts: Participants with a paired Apple Watch may receive check-in notifications and suggested topics; participants who do not take part may be removed from the study [3].
  • Clinical-AI use: The reported number of U.S. clinicians using OpenEvidence each month grew from 355 in January 2024 to more than 703,000 in June 2026, according to Chief Healthcare Executive [8].
  • AI competition: OpenAI says users submit more than 300 million health-related questions weekly, while both OpenAI and Anthropic have introduced health-focused chatbot capabilities [4][22].
  • Onboarding information: Participants are asked for demographic details such as race, age and education level so researchers can examine how these factors relate to preferences and communication needs [3].

Perspectives

Robert M. Wachter, UCSF Department of Medicine chair: Wachter said patient and clinician AI tools are being built and tested separately, and argued that using versions of the same trustworthy tools could improve appointment conversations and follow-up [9].

Technical Details

  • Question processing: CNET reports that the iPhone first uses tools to remove identifying details; a secure study environment operated by contractor UST Global then separates queries from participant IDs and screens them again before OpenEvidence generates answers [3].
  • On-device classification: The study app uses on-device Apple Foundation Models to classify questions by category, according to Apple; CNET says those models are part of Apple Intelligence, though the app itself is not integrated with Siri [3][15].
