UAE says Flydubai co-pilot tried to seize plane with crash axe

Category: World

The United Arab Emirates said Saturday that a Flydubai co-pilot attacked the captain with a cockpit crash axe and tried to take control of Flight FZ1073. It described the incident as an attempted “terrorist act.” The Dubai-to-Tel Aviv flight made an emergency landing in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, on Sept. 30 after the captain was wounded and the aircraft descended sharply. Passengers and crew subdued the co-pilot, allowing other pilots aboard to land safely [19][36][64]. People familiar with the investigation identified the suspect as Omani national Hamam al-Hammami. People briefed on the matter said Oman had previously barred him from flying over concerns about extremist views and moved him to an administrative role at Oman Air. Oman has not publicly confirmed that account. Investigators are examining how Flydubai hired him and whether anyone else was involved [64][1][36]. The UAE is leading the investigation, reviewing physical and digital evidence and examining the suspect’s motives and connections. Flydubai suspended its Tel Aviv service, and Israeli carrier Arkia began repatriation flights from Dubai on Friday. The UAE prosecutor general said final findings would be released after the investigation was completed [19][27].

Key Points

  • Crew response: Four pilots were aboard. After the captain opened the cockpit door, passengers intervened and two pilots traveling in the cabin took control [21][22].
  • Background: A regional official said al-Hammami was born in the UAE to an Omani father and Syrian mother [64].
  • Social media evidence: Posts including cockpit footage and images of al-Qaeda figures appeared on a LinkedIn profile bearing the suspect’s name about nine hours after the incident. Investigators have not established who published them [47].
  • Investigation scope: Security agencies from four regional countries are working with U.S. and other Western counterparts to establish the suspect’s motive [36][64].

Perspectives

Israeli security officials: Two Israeli security officials said their initial assessment was that the co-pilot acted independently and that investigators had found no evidence of Iranian involvement. The inquiry continues.

Technical Details

  • Cockpit crash axe: The UAE prosecutor said the co-pilot used an axe kept in the cockpit for emergencies and then tried to take control of the aircraft [19].
  • Flight descent: Flight-tracking data cited by Ynetnews showed the aircraft dropped more than 14,000 feet in less than 30 seconds during the incident [57].

Industry Impact

  • Airline operations: El Al said the UAE was delaying the landing permits it needed to fly into Dubai. Arkia and Israir were listed for arrivals in Dubai late Friday and early Saturday [31].

Historical Background

Israel and the United Arab Emirates established diplomatic ties in 2020 through the Abraham Accords, and Dubai became a popular destination for Israeli travelers [31]. Israel and Oman do not have formal diplomatic relations; Israel says it has agreements with foreign carriers to prevent pilots who are citizens of countries without ties to Israel from flying into the country [64].
