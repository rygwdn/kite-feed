Block unveils Dessa speech-to-speech AI for live conversation

Category: Technology

Block, the financial technology company led by Jack Dorsey, unveiled Dessa, a speech-to-speech AI model trained from scratch on human conversations rather than text or pretrained models [3][5][10]. It can listen and speak simultaneously, handling interruptions and vocal cues such as laughter, pauses and changes in tone. Block says it can run locally on a MacBook Pro with an M4 Max chip or newer [3][1]. Dessa remains a research preview, with Block initially offering access to a small group of AI safety researchers. The company has not announced a public release date [5][10]. Public demonstrations are company-produced, and Block has not disclosed the model's size or benchmark results [10][1]. The lifelike speech has prompted concerns about deception and scam calls as researchers assess risks before broader access [10][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Training hardware: Block says it trained Dessa on a single NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 system; a separate report says the system contains 72 Blackwell GPUs [7][1].
  • Inference speed: Block says Dessa runs faster than real time, without noticeable pauses [3][1].
  • Access details: Block has not announced an API or pricing, or released model weights [3].
  • Conversation demo: Dessa can finish a sentence when a user forgets a movie title [1].

Perspectives

Block: The company says current voice systems discard emotional information by converting speech to text, and argues that detecting distress or pain in a voice could improve safety [5][2].

Sources: TechCrunch

Changpeng Zhao, Binance founder: Zhao said natural voice interaction might help children learn, noting that current AI voice systems had been a challenge for his GiggleAcademy education project [10].

Sources: CoinDesk

Justine Bateman, filmmaker: Bateman questioned the need for AI voices to sound human and warned that such realism could trick people into lowering their guard [10].

Sources: CoinDesk

Industry Impact

  • Small-business services: Square already uses voice AI for ordering; Block's on-device model could extend voice features to merchants and consumers, although the company has not announced a product deployment [5][8].

Historical Background

Block's AI lab includes former employees of Toronto-based deep-learning startup Dessa, which Square acquired in 2020 [10].
