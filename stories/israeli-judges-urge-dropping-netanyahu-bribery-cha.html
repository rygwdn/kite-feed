Israeli judges urge dropping Netanyahu bribery charge during coalition talks

Category: World

Jerusalem District Court judges on Wednesday urged prosecutors to drop the bribery charge in Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Case 4000, reconsider a second corruption case and seek an agreement in a third. Their comments were preliminary and did not constitute a ruling; Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara said she would review the recommendations [5][21][8]. Netanyahu, who denies wrongdoing, called for the trial to end immediately. In Case 4000, prosecutors allege he granted regulatory benefits to telecom company Bezeq in exchange for favorable coverage on the Walla news site. Judges said a conviction appeared unlikely, citing difficulties with key witness testimony; they also questioned whether the conduct alleged in Case 2000 constituted a crime [5][21]. Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister after an inconclusive October 27 election. His rival, former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, has the first opportunity to form a government, while coalition discussions remain unsettled [17][8]. The Knesset, Israel’s parliament, also gave preliminary approval to a bill barring indicted lawmakers from forming a government; it must pass a committee stage and three further votes to become law [2]. Eisenkot’s mandate to form a government expires December 6, with a possible two-week extension [32].

Key Points

  • Trial status: Netanyahu completed his testimony on June 24, and his defense team has since called witnesses [2].
  • Coalition shift: Eisenkot announced an agreement with Yisrael Beiteinu, led by Avigdor Lieberman, bringing his bloc closer to forming a government [32].
  • Alleged benefits: Bezeq’s former chairman Shaul Elovitch denied wrongdoing; prosecutors put the alleged regulatory benefits at about 1.8 billion shekels [5].

Perspectives

Gali Baharav-Miara, Israeli attorney general: She said prosecutors had already offered a plea agreement that Netanyahu’s side had not accepted, and that the case should conclude through a verdict or an appropriate agreement [21].

Q&A

Q: What would a plea agreement involve?

A: Middle East Monitor reported that the proposed arrangement would require Netanyahu to admit to charges in exchange for a presidential pardon and withdrawal from political life; he has rejected that option [21].
