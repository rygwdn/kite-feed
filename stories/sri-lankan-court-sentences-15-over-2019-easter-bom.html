Sri Lankan court sentences 15 over 2019 Easter bombings

Category: World

A Sri Lankan High Court on Tuesday convicted 15 men of helping organize the April 21, 2019, Easter Sunday bombings, which targeted three churches and three luxury hotels. The attacks killed 279 people, including 45 foreigners, and wounded about 574 others. The three-judge panel found 14 defendants guilty on all charges, including murder, conspiracy to commit terrorism, and possession of firearms and explosives. The 15th defendant was convicted on some charges. Nine other defendants were acquitted. [1][2][3][4][6][7][11] The court sentenced the defendants to prison terms of 200 to 260 years and ordered the seizure of their property and assets. The verdict followed a trial lasting about five years and stemmed from 23,270 charges filed against the suspects in 2021. One of the 25 people originally indicted died in custody before the case concluded. The bombers who carried out the attacks also died in the explosions. [3][6][7][14][30]

Did you know: The case involved about 2,300 witnesses and was heard over roughly 250 court days. [7][11]

Key Points

  • Defendants: The 25 men indicted in the case were members of Sri Lanka's Muslim minority. [6]
  • Security measures: Police and commandos heavily guarded the Colombo courthouse, while authorities transported the accused to the hearing in handcuffs on prison buses. [3][6][30]
  • Warning failures: In July, a former police chief and a former senior defense official received death sentences for failing to act promptly on intelligence warnings. [14][5]
  • Appeal rights: The convicted men can appeal the High Court ruling to Sri Lanka’s Supreme Court. [7]

Perspectives

Sri Lanka’s Roman Catholic Church: The church welcomed the convictions after years of waiting and continued to seek answers about whether those convicted represented the full conspiracy behind the attacks. [4][14].

Historical Background

Sri Lanka’s civil war ended in 2009, and the Easter bombings were the country’s deadliest attacks since the conflict ended. [6][32] Sri Lanka has not carried out an execution since 1976, and death sentences are typically commuted to life imprisonment. [14][24]
