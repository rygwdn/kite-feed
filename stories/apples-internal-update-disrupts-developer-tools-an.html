Apple's internal update disrupts developer tools and store systems

Category: Technology

Apple’s November 17 service outage affected more than consumer apps: developers reported trouble with App Store Connect, while Apple’s status listings also showed problems with account tools, music and podcast services, and school-management systems [21][14]. Staff at some stores could not complete purchases; a Philadelphia customer said both checkout and the Apple Store app’s self-checkout were unavailable at the Walnut Street location [49]. Apple said an internal update caused the disruption but did not specify what the update changed or why it affected several systems [10][43]. Users also reported iCloud sign-in failures and device-activation errors, which can block setup of new Apple devices [6][13]. The company’s public status page did not always match user reports: it initially showed services as available, then added and revised incident notices throughout the day [43][1]. Apple listed all services as available by Tuesday evening, though users had reported lingering problems earlier that afternoon [7][10].

Key Points

  • Outage reports: Reports to Downdetector began to spike around 11:40 a.m.; shortly before 1 p.m., roughly 2,000 users were reporting problems with Apple Support, Apple Pay and iCloud [7].
  • Hotel systems: Marriott operates 10,200 properties with more than 1.8 million rooms [80][83].
  • Store disruption: At Apple’s flagship Michigan Avenue store in Chicago, systems were unavailable for at least an hour and operations appeared to resume by 12:20 p.m. local time [41].
  • NSA operations: The NSA said scheduled maintenance temporarily degraded some internal systems but left mission-critical capabilities operational [67][85].

Perspectives

Curt Esser, Appleton-based IT consultant: Esser pointed to software patches and transitions between old and new systems as possible contributors, while saying the outages did not appear related; he also cautioned that AI tools can produce faulty solutions if they lack complete information [56].

Sources: WBAY

Tony Sabai, cybersecurity expert: Sabai said frequent upgrades and maintenance on older systems can increase the chance of widespread problems, and argued that firms may be doing more such work to identify vulnerabilities [49].

Dr. Ali Butt, a computer science professor at Virginia Tech, cautioned against assuming the outages were connected. He said localized technical failures are plausible and the complexity of systems can turn small problems into substantial disruptions [79].

Technical Details

  • Exchange gateway defect: Microsoft’s preliminary account described lock contention in an internal Windows process on some Microsoft 365 networking gateway servers. Newer machines with more cores worsened the defect, which tests focused on less powerful hardware failed to detect [75].
  • FAA network overload: The FAA pointed in part to a router upgrade in southern Arkansas that overwhelmed its telecommunications network. The root cause remained under investigation [73].

Industry Impact

  • Airlines: United Airlines’ separate technology outage lasted more than 90 minutes and delayed departing flights on Monday [80].

Historical Background

No common cause has been established for the November 10 disruption and the November 17 outage [5][41].
