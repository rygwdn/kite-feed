US consumer confidence jumps to 103.3, highest since January 2025

Category: Business

US consumer confidence rose 11.4 points in November to 103.3, its highest level since January 2025, the Conference Board said Tuesday. The increase exceeded forecasts and followed the Nov. 3 midterm elections. Confidence rose sharply among Democrats and independents after the election but edged down among Republicans. Respondents remained frustrated by high prices, while their assessments of jobs and the economy improved [2][3][6]. Reduced post-election uncertainty and falling gasoline prices were cited as possible reasons for the rebound. The national average gasoline price was $3.61 a gallon on Nov. 24, down from $4.30 a month earlier but still above $3.07 a year earlier. October inflation was reported at 4%, up from 3.7% in September [9][7]. Consumer spending had remained robust through two quarters despite weak sentiment, and economists say surveys have become less reliable measures of behavior [2][7]. The Federal Reserve was expected to raise its benchmark rate by a quarter percentage point at its next meeting, about two weeks away, as it continued efforts to curb inflation [6].

Key Points

  • Current conditions: The Conference Board’s Present Situation Index rose 13.7 points to 132.8, while its Expectations Index gained 9.9 points to 83.6 [15][1].
  • Job perceptions: The share saying jobs were plentiful reached 30.8%, up from 26.2%; those saying jobs were hard to get fell to 18.4% from 21.4% [2].
  • Vacation plans: The share of respondents planning a vacation in the next six months reached 44.9%, up 2.2 percentage points from October, with domestic travel intentions rising and overseas plans falling [1].
  • Uneven gains: Confidence increased across age groups, with the biggest rise among Gen Z respondents; consumers in higher-income households recorded the largest improvement [15][2].
  • Inflation expectations: Consumers’ expectations for inflation over the next 12 months fell for a second consecutive month but remained elevated at 5.7% [6].

Perspectives

Christopher Rupkey, chief economist at FWDBONDS, warned that trouble in artificial intelligence stock valuations could slow consumption through the wealth effect [2].

Timeline

  • November 2026: Conference Board survey fieldwork ran from Nov. 1 through Nov. 17 [15]
  • 3 November 2026: Democrats won control of Congress in the midterm elections [2][1]
  • 24 November 2026: Conference Board released November confidence index at 103.3 [2][3]

Historical Background

The confidence index rose for a second consecutive month in November after falling in September to its lowest level since April 2014, below its level during the pandemic [7][9].

Q&A

Q: What economic report was due next?

A: The Commerce Department was scheduled to release a separate inflation measure closely watched by the Federal Reserve on Nov. 25 [9][11].

Q: What does the survey say about the kinds of purchases consumers are considering?

A: Respondents reported increased plans to buy motor vehicles, major appliances and homes over the following six months [2].
