OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet for agent-led purchases with card safeguards

Category: Technology

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Nov. 13, allowing its “dots” AI agents to shop and pay for U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 or older with eligible U.S.-issued cards. Before each purchase, users review the items, price and card, then authorize payment with a biometric check such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan. The payment network supplies a transaction token instead of the user’s actual card details. Purchases are charged to the saved card, and users retain eligible card rewards and dispute protections [22][14][3]. The wallet adds checkout to ChatGPT’s product research and comparison functions. OpenAI says ChatGPT has 1.2 billion weekly users and is limiting the launch to learn from real transactions and improve compatibility with businesses, banks and payment networks [22][14]. Amazon blocks outside agents from shopping on its site, and United Airlines said it would block agents from its booking systems. OpenAI published a merchant guide and says it is working with payment and fraud-prevention providers. It is not charging a transaction fee at launch [3][9][14].

Key Points

  • Consumer concerns: An American Bankers Association survey cited by Gizmodo found that 77% of respondents were worried about allowing AI to make payments, while 61% said they were unlikely to let an agent manage bank balances autonomously in the next year [12].
  • Early demand: Uber reported that AI agents placed just four ride requests and 500 Uber Eats orders in the last week of October, out of about 320 million total rides and deliveries. DoorDash said it had received “maybe several hundred orders in total” from AI assistants [12].
  • Agent reliability: Visa tested 22 AI models as shoppers during November and found they failed 42.8% of the time, according to The Next Web [9].
  • Launch limits: Wallet works only in dots—not regular ChatGPT conversations, ChatGPT Work or standalone Codex sessions—and does not support virtual cards at launch [2].

Perspectives

United Airlines and Amazon: Both companies have restricted outside AI agents from their platforms. Reports attributed the restrictions to security concerns and said Amazon also seeks to direct shoppers to its own AI assistant [7][4][3].

Technical Details

  • Transaction token structure: After the user approves a purchase, the payment network generates transaction-specific substitute credentials containing a card number, security code and expiration date [22][14].
  • Optional merchant integrations: OpenAI says browser-based agent access requires neither a plugin nor a product feed. Businesses can separately build plugins for availability or cart management, or add WebMCP tools [22][17].

Industry Impact

  • Retail and e-commerce: Merchants may need to configure firewalls or content delivery networks and work with payment and risk providers so authorized agent purchases are not blocked. Product feeds can help provide current pricing and availability [22][29].
  • Payments: OpenAI’s launch includes American Express, Mastercard and Visa. The company is also working with payment processors Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe and fraud-prevention firms including Forter and Riskified [22][2].

Historical Background

Card networks had begun developing infrastructure for agent-led commerce: Mastercard's Agent Pay framework calls for agent registration and verification, while Visa has pursued developer integration through Visa Intelligent Commerce [21].

Business Angle

  • OpenAI plans controls for more autonomous purchasing under preset spending limits, categories or time windows, but they are not part of the current launch [22][3].

Q&A

Q: Does biometric approval guarantee that an order was placed?

A: No. OpenAI says a payment authorization can proceed without confirmation that the merchant accepted the order. Users should check the merchant’s confirmation before trying again to avoid a duplicate payment [19].

Q: Who handles returns or disputes for a Wallet purchase?

A: The customer still buys directly from the business, which handles fulfillment, returns and refunds. The card issuer handles eligible payment disputes [22][21].
