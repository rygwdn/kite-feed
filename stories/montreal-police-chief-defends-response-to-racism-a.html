Montreal police chief defends response to racism allegations

Category: Canada

Montreal Police Chief Fady Dagher took questions from residents at Montreal city hall Tuesday after allegations of racism and misconduct led the force to dismantle a 16-member patrol unit at Station 39 in Montréal-Nord in June [1][7][5]. The allegations include racist and hateful acts allegedly committed against Black and Arab people during police stops, and claims that officers cut the hair of racialized citizens and kept it as trophies [7][1]. Dagher said internal reports first alerted police in March to alleged racist behaviour involving officers at the station, and that the force opened an investigation immediately [2][8]. He said police disarmed all 16 officers on June 12, initially suspended two, assigned three to administrative duties and transferred the remaining members elsewhere; a third officer was suspended on June 18 [2]. Residents pressed Dagher and other police officials on whether Montreal police should remain in charge of the investigation, with several calling for an independent public investigation [1][7]. Montreal police defended keeping the probe in-house, saying that sending it to another organization could duplicate work and delay the process, while Quebec Public Security Minister Ian Lafrenière appointed an independent observer to monitor the investigations and assess the quality of the work [1].

Key Points

  • Public questioning: The meeting marked the first time citizens could directly question Montreal police during a public consultation by the city’s public security commission [1].
  • Political absence: Montreal Mayor Soraya Martinez Ferrada did not attend. City executive committee member Jim Beis represented the administration and told residents their anger and concerns were legitimate [1].
  • Criminal file: Prosecutor’s spokesperson Anick Cantin said police had not submitted a request to launch criminal proceedings against officers involved in the events described at the June news conference [2].
  • Community account: Resident Nadia Cenacle told La Presse her children had faced repeated random police stops and said her 12-year-old daughter no longer wants anything to do with police [3].
