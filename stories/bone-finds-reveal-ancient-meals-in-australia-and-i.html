Bone finds reveal ancient meals in Australia and Italy

Category: Science

Two newly reported archaeological and paleontological findings used bones to reconstruct ancient behavior in different settings. Near Richmond in Outback Queensland, researchers identified what Phys.org described as a rare “three-tier prehistoric meal”: evidence that a flying reptile was eaten by a sea monster before that predator itself became prey [1][4]. Separate reports on a pre-Roman Italian necropolis said buried bones offered evidence about diet and society before the rise of Rome, including clues about mobility, gender inequality and social status [2][3]. Nautilus summarized one reported dietary pattern from the Iron Age Mediterranean site as men eating more meat [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Researchers described the Queensland fossil, nicknamed 'Bag of Bones,' as the world's first evidence of a three-tier feeding sequence [1][4].

Historical Background

Richmond, Queensland, is in an area known for marine fossils from Australia’s inland Cretaceous seas, when parts of the continent were covered by shallow water [common]. The Italian findings concern pre-Roman Iron Age societies, before Rome’s political expansion across the Italian peninsula [common].
