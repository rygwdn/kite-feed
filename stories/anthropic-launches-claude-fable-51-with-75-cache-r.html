Anthropic launches Claude Fable 5.1 with 75% cache-read price cut

Category: AI

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 on Sept. 1, saying they use the same underlying model with different safeguard levels for coding, knowledge work, long-running problem-solving and research tasks [55][20][24]. Fable 5.1 is generally available through Anthropic’s products, the Claude API, AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure, while Mythos 5.1 is limited to vetted cybersecurity and life-sciences users through trusted-access programs [3][24][27]. The main pricing change cuts cache-read costs by 75% to $0.25 per million tokens. Base pricing remains $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens [3][28][58]. Anthropic estimates Fable 5.1 will cost about 25% less than Fable 5 for typical token-billed workloads and up to about 45% less for highly agentic work [55][3][37][53]. Anthropic also announced Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, a system designed to keep customer data in cloud infrastructure controlled by the customer rather than Anthropic while retaining misuse monitoring. The company said the feature will roll out to enterprise customers in phases beginning later this fall [55][20][28]. Until EFS becomes available, eligible enterprise customers can use Fable 5.1 with zero data retention [55][3][28].

Key Points

  • Benchmark gains: Anthropic reported Fable 5.1 scored 52.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 versus 24.7% for Fable 5, and 55.8% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 versus 42.0% for Fable 5 [25][42][33][55].
  • Safeguard precision: Anthropic said Fable 5.1 produced 60% fewer false-positive cybersecurity interventions per Claude Code session and 85% fewer biology interventions on benign elementary questions than earlier safeguards [3][28].
  • Benchmark caveats: Frandroid said the scope and methods for Terminal-Bench 4.0 and Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 were not publicly documented, while Harian Jogja noted the benchmark claims came from Anthropic testing rather than independent validation [33][35].
  • Guardrail changes: Anthropic said Claude Code users should see about 60% fewer cybersecurity false positives. It said Fable 5.1 may identify software vulnerabilities but not develop exploits [27][31][28].
  • Data handling: Eligible enterprise customers can use Fable 5.1 with zero data retention until Enterprise Frontier Safeguards becomes available [55][20][3].
  • Research cases: Anthropic said the models produced pre-release scientific outputs, including a custom GPU optimization and a higher-resolution Venus map assembled from existing imagery [20][50][51].
  • Benchmark gains: Anthropic reported that Fable 5.1 scored 55.8% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 versus 42.0% for Fable 5, and 52.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 versus 24.7% for Fable 5 [42][25][33][35].
  • Allowed security work: Fable 5.1 can now help discover software vulnerabilities but still may not be used to develop exploits for them [27][31][28].

Technical Details

  • Context limits: Fable 5.1 retains a context window of up to 1 million tokens and supports up to 128,000 tokens of output in one response, according to Habr and Cnyes [45][54].
  • Watermark detection: Anthropic’s detection API is in private preview for regulators, media, researchers and EU civil society groups, not publicly available [25].
  • Anti-distillation change: Huxiu and TMTPost reported that new API accounts can no longer modify prior conversation context while retaining saved thinking transcripts, a change they described as blocking a documented model-distillation technique [50][51].
  • Context limits: Several reports said Fable 5.1 keeps a context window of up to 1 million tokens and supports outputs of up to 128,000 tokens, allowing large codebases and technical documents to fit in fewer requests [45][54][50].
  • Cache reuse: Cache reads allow the model to reuse already processed context, such as system prompts, tool definitions or codebases, across later requests. They matter most when agents repeatedly inspect the same long context [50][51][33].
  • Cache reads: Anthropic charges cache reads for reusing previously processed context. Chinese technology coverage said the company cut the price from $1 to $0.25 per million tokens, while five-minute and one-hour cache-write prices remained $12.50 and $20 per million tokens [50][51].

Industry Impact

  • EU transparency: Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 add invisible watermarking to text and file outputs, and Anthropic’s detection API is in private preview for eligible organizations under EU AI Act-related requirements [25][52][27].
  • Competitive pressure: Hospodářské noviny reported that OpenAI is preparing its Astra-class models [59].
  • Semiconductor design: Wccftech reported that Samsung’s LSI division endorsed Claude Code for chip-design work, linking Anthropic’s coding tools to semiconductor engineering workflows [48].

Scientific Significance

  • GPU kernel optimization: Mythos 5.1 wrote custom GPU kernels for seven open-source biology models, speeding up inference by up to 2.5× and potentially cutting GPU costs 30–60% for genome analysis, according to Huxiu and TMTPost [50][51].
  • Scientific workflow: Anthropic presented Mythos 5.1 as capable of linking model reasoning with external scientific tools, including protein-design and folding tools that generated binders later sent to outside organizations for experimental validation [50][51].
  • Planetary mapping: Fable 5.1 used NASA Magellan radar data to generate a higher-resolution elevation map for about one-third of Venus, reducing mapped terrain detail from roughly 10–20 kilometers to 2–3 kilometers and improving height-data accuracy by up to about 25%, according to Huxiu and TMTPost [50][51].
  • Experimental biology: Huxiu and TMTPost reported that Mythos 5.1 designed protein binders across 12 targets with a hit rate near 50%, compared with Anthropic’s stated 10% to 15% typical rate in protein design, and that three targets produced binding affinity about 10 times higher than top Adaptyv Bio competition entries [50][51].
  • Benchmark limits: Frandroid cautioned that Anthropic had not publicly documented the scope and method of its cited Terminal-Bench tests, while Harian Jogja noted that the benchmark claims came from Anthropic rather than independent validation [33][35].

Business Angle

  • Anthropic's Fable 5.1 remains priced at roughly 2.5 times GPT-5.6 Sol per token, which may limit adoption among cost-sensitive developers even with the cache-read discount [50][51].
  • Enterprise Frontier Safeguards is aimed at data-control objections from regulated customers, but staged rollout means near-term adoption depends on eligibility for zero retention [55][28].
  • Reported large compute commitments make inference efficiency central to margins [54][50][51].
  • Lower cache-read pricing could reduce costs for enterprises that run agents for hours against the same codebase or document set [55][33].
  • Enterprise Frontier Safeguards targets regulated buyers that want privacy controls, but its phased rollout means near-term adoption still depends on eligibility for zero data retention [55][28].
  • Keeping base token prices unchanged means Fable 5.1’s lower-cost claim depends on cache reuse, while cheaper rival frontier models remain a competitive pressure [43][50][51].
  • Cnyes reported IPO preparations; formal filings would show revenue growth, compute obligations and inference costs [54][common].

Action Items

  • Request enterprise privacy options: Enterprise buyers with sensitive data should ask Anthropic or their cloud provider whether they qualify for zero data retention now and when Enterprise Frontier Safeguards will become available for their deployment [55][28].
