MeerKAT team traces first exoplanet radio emission to Beta Pictoris b

Category: Science

Astronomers using South Africa’s MeerKAT radio telescope array report that they have traced recurring radio bursts directly to Beta Pictoris b, a gas giant about 63 light-years away and roughly 12 times Jupiter’s mass. The team says this is the first radio emission identified directly from an exoplanet, not evidence of intelligent life. Researchers attribute the signal to auroras driven by the planet’s magnetic field, which they estimate is at least 200 times stronger than Jupiter’s [6][5][9]. Researchers could not clearly distinguish earlier possible exoplanet radio signals from emissions from their host stars. The team says MeerKAT’s observations pinpointed this signal to the planet, recording it repeatedly at multiple frequencies [6][5]. The researchers posted their paper on arXiv on September 15, and it has not yet completed peer review. The team has requested further telescope observations; the review is expected to take several months [6][9].

Did you know: The Beta Pictoris system hosts about 30 orbiting comets in addition to its planets and debris disk [6].

Key Points

  • Field estimate: Researchers used the observed frequencies to estimate a magnetic-field strength of at least about 1,250 gauss in the emission region [10].
  • Young system: Beta Pictoris is about 23 million years old; Hubble photographed its debris disk in detail in 2015 [6].
  • Open question: The researchers say the observed frequencies were unexpected because they had expected exoplanet magnetic fields to resemble Jupiter’s and produce lower-frequency radio emissions [6].

Timeline

  • 2008: Astronomers discovered Beta Pictoris b, the planet now linked to radio bursts [6]
  • 2019: Researchers announced the discovery of another planet in the system, Beta Pictoris c [6]
  • 2026: Beta Pictoris d was discovered, expanding the known planetary system [6]
  • 15 September 2026: Researchers posted the radio-emission paper on arXiv [6][9]

Q&A

Q: How long does Beta Pictoris b take to orbit its star?

A: The planet takes about 24 years to complete one orbit around Beta Pictoris [10].
