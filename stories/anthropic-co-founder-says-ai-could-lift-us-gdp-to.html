Anthropic co-founder says AI could lift US GDP to $44 trillion

Category: AI

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark said Oct. 17 at the International Monetary Fund and World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok that the company’s internal model projects artificial intelligence could raise U.S. gross domestic product to $44 trillion by 2030 from about $32 trillion if the technology keeps improving [6][1][9]. Clark acknowledged the estimate sounds implausible and said growth on that scale would require new industries and inventions. He also pointed to AI’s acceleration of scientific discovery [6][5]. Clark called for international cooperation on strict AI rules and warned that policymakers have only a few years to establish safeguards as capabilities advance [10][11]. Separately, an IMF report said a decline in AI investment could cut global GDP by 0.4% by 2028, while IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva warned that a loss of confidence could threaten financial stability [13]. Anthropic is preparing for a potential initial public offering in November that could value the company at up to $2 trillion [9][12].

Primary Source: View original article

If there are no safeguards, then the risks may exceed the opportunities.

— IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva (ENCA (AFP))

Key Points

  • Trade contribution: IMF chief economist Silvana Tenreyro said global trade grew 8.3% from 2023 to 2025, compared with an estimated 4.6% without AI-related products [13][14].
  • Workforce shifts: Clark acknowledged job losses in AI-exposed call centers and customer service but said employment is growing in scientific research. He identified the emergence of new industries as a key uncertainty [10][12].
  • Energy exposure: AI investment depends heavily on data-center and chip spending. Moody’s Analytics economists warned that a prolonged Middle East conflict could raise energy and financing costs and make AI investment less economical [13].
  • Infrastructure access: Clark said countries could participate in AI development by helping build the data-center computing capacity needed to run it [12].

Perspectives

Jack Clark, Anthropic co-founder and public-benefit head: Clark said AI-assisted science could bring risks including biological and cyber weapons, and called for the strictest human oversight and transparency in biological research [10].

Tiff Macklem, Bank of Canada governor: Macklem said AI is already visible across GDP and financial markets, adding that its broader impact is only beginning [13].

Sources: ENCA (AFP)

Kevin Warsh, Federal Reserve chair: Warsh described AI as a highly consequential economic change but said the path forward would be difficult because its development and future market structure remain uncertain [13].
