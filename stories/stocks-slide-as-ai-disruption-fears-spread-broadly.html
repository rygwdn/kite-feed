Stocks slide as AI disruption fears spread broadly

Category: Business

A fresh wave of “AI disruption” anxiety hit US stocks Thursday, sending shares sharply lower as investors sold companies seen as vulnerable to automation or shifting digital habits—even when earnings topped expectations [7][2][10][3]. The Dow fell 669 points, the S&P 500 dropped 1.6% (its second-worst day since Thanksgiving), and the Nasdaq slid 2% as tech and software led the retreat [7]. The selloff quickly spread beyond Big Tech into sectors tied to office work and services. Commercial property-services and brokerage firms such as CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield, and Jones Lang LaSalle sank for a second day in double digits as investors wrestled with what AI could mean for those businesses [6][1]. Similar fears reached transport and logistics names, with reports describing investors treating freight and shipping as the “latest victim” of the AI trade [4][12]. As the mood turned defensive, gold rebounded in choppy trading as risk appetite faded [10]. Still, several sources flagged a counterpoint: some analysts and investors say markets may be overstating AI’s near-term threat to complex, relationship-driven work. In India, a few contrarian buyers pointed to strong order flows and low valuations in IT services despite the slump [14][11][8].

There’s a real disconnect between market sentiment and the reality of our business

— Adam Foroughi (Chicago Tribune)

Key Points

  • Earnings ignored: AppLovin shares fell 19.7% despite posting stronger profit than analysts expected—an example of how the AI narrative can overwhelm near-term results [7].
  • Hardware pressure: Cisco slid 12.3% after forecasting lower profit per dollar of revenue this quarter. Analysts tied some of the margin squeeze to higher memory costs driven by AI-related demand [7].
  • India spillover: India’s Nifty IT index dropped more than 4%, with major outsourcers such as TCS, Infosys and Wipro among the biggest decliners as investors reassessed how AI could reshape enterprise software and outsourcing models [9][8].
  • Broader white-collar spillover: Financial Times reporting said the selling spread into other white-collar-facing industries, including insurance and wealth management, as investors searched for the next potential “AI casualty” after new tech launches [5].
  • Asia follows: Hong Kong shares fell in early Friday trading, with the Hang Seng Index down 1.1% as the US AI-led rout weighed on sentiment, and Baidu dropped 3.9% [15].

Perspectives

AppLovin management: CEO Adam Foroughi told analysts the company’s indicators still looked strong, saying the stock’s drop didn’t line up with the business’s operating reality [7].

Sources: Chicago Tribune

Property-sector analysts: Some analysts said the selloff in property-services firms may overstate AI’s near-term risk to complex deal-making and advisory work [14].

Sources: The Guardian

Contrarian investors in India tech: A Bloomberg report highlighted contrarians who say order flows remain strong and valuations have fallen to decadal lows in Indian IT services, even as AI disruption fears weigh on the group [11].

Sources: Bloomberg

Industry Impact

  • Commercial property services: Shares in major brokers and property-services firms (including CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield and JLL) remained under heavy pressure as investors debated whether AI tools could reduce office-related demand and transaction activity. Some analysts, however, argued the market may be overstating the near-term automation risk in complex advisory work [6][14].
  • Freight and logistics: Transport, shipping and logistics names were pulled into the selloff, with several outlets describing the sector as the latest target of “AI fear” trading [4][12].
  • Indian IT services: Indian outsourcing and IT-services stocks fell sharply. Some reports noted the Nifty IT index had dropped more than 5% for the second time in under 10 days as AI disruption worries added to existing valuation and macro pressures [8][9][13].
