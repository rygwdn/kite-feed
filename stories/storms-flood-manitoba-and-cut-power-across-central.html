Storms flood Manitoba and cut power across Central Canada

Category: Canada

Extreme weather disrupted parts of Canada from the Prairies to eastern provinces this week, with overland flooding in western Manitoba, severe thunderstorms in Ontario and Quebec, and continued heat warnings in southern Ontario and Quebec [3][6][8][17]. In Manitoba, emergency teams transferred patients after flooding closed Dauphin’s only medical centre, while Swan River faced rising water for the second time in less than a month and the province asked Ottawa for military support [3]. In Ontario and Quebec, roughly 35,000 hydro customers remained without power Friday afternoon after severe storms, down from peak outages of more than 214,000 customers in Ontario and nearly 140,000 in Quebec [5][8]. Environment Canada said strong winds uprooted trees and brought down power lines, with a 119 km/h gust recorded near Lake Saint-Pierre and a 96 km/h gust at Montréal-Trudeau International Airport [8]. Hydro One said crews were working to restore service to more than 17,000 Ontario customers, while Hydro-Québec deployed about 200 teams for more than 18,000 clients in Quebec [8]. Further storm risks continued Friday in southern Ontario, where Environment Canada ended a tornado warning for Chatham-Kent–Rondeau Park after earlier tracking a severe thunderstorm that may have been producing a tornado [7]. Hamilton, Burlington and Brantford were placed under a severe thunderstorm warning Friday morning, with the federal weather agency warning of strong wind gusts, quarter-size hail, likely local utility outages and possible damage to roofs, fences, soft shelters and trees [13].

  • Ontario heat: Southern Ontario’s Friday storms formed under heat and humidity, with forecast humidex values near 40 in the Greater Toronto Area and up to 44 near Windsor [1].

  • Electric utilities: Severe storms caused widespread outages for Hydro One and Hydro-Québec, and hundreds of crews were deployed to restore service after power-line damage [8][5].

Business Angle

  • Insurers could see higher claims from roof, fence, tree and property damage after severe storms with hail and damaging winds [13][common].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Overland flooding washed out roads across the Prairies, and downstream communities were preparing for rising river levels through the weekend [3][14]. Avoid flood-prone roads in western Manitoba.
  • Safety: Environment Canada warned southern Ontario storms could bring damaging winds, large hail and intense rainfall, and urged residents to take cover when severe storms approached [7][13]. Shelter indoors when thunder approaches.

Action Items

  • Prepare for short-notice power outages: Charge devices, keep flashlights available and avoid opening refrigerators frequently if service is interrupted; utilities were still restoring thousands of storm-related outages Friday [8][common].
  • Report dangerous electrical damage: If you see a downed line, stay away and contact the local utility or emergency services; Hydro-Québec warned downed lines may remain live [17].
