Astronomers find first stellar-mass black hole in Omega Centauri

Category: Science

Astronomers using NASA’s Hubble Space Telescope archive and recent James Webb Space Telescope observations have identified the first known stellar-mass black hole in Omega Centauri, a massive globular cluster about 18,000 light-years from Earth [3][4][5][6]. The object, named oMEGACat BH-2, was detected through the motion of a visible star orbiting something too massive and dark to be anything other than a black hole, according to accounts of the University of Utah-led research [3][4]. Omega Centauri contains about 10 million gravitationally bound stars, and models had suggested it should host roughly 10,000 smaller stellar-mass black holes. None had been confirmed in the cluster before this detection [3][4][6][7]. The research was published Monday in The Astrophysical Journal Letters, according to KSL [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Omega Centauri can be seen with the naked eye from dark southern skies and was cataloged as a star by Ptolemy before later observations showed it was a dense star cluster [common].

Key Points

  • Low mass: The black hole’s estimated mass is lower than scientists expected for a black hole binary in such a dense environment [4][5].
  • Wide orbit: The system has a reported 94-year orbit, a semi-major axis of 31 AU and an eccentricity of 0.72 [4].

Perspectives

University of Utah astronomers: The team said the black hole’s unexpectedly low mass and long orbital period challenge existing models of black hole formation in dense star clusters such as Omega Centauri.

Sources: KSL

Historical Background

In 2024, astronomers built a separate case for an intermediate-mass black hole at Omega Centauri’s center using more than 500 Hubble images and seven fast-moving stars, estimating its mass at about 8,200 solar masses [4]. Before the new finding, searches using radial velocity, radio and X-ray emission had not found the cluster’s expected population of stellar-mass black holes [4].
