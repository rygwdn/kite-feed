Astronomers detect atmosphere on habitable-zone rocky exoplanet

Category: Science

Astronomers reported evidence of an atmosphere around LHS 1140 b, a rocky exoplanet that orbits a cool red dwarf star in the star’s habitable zone [1]. The study identified a faint stream of helium escaping from the planet, which researchers said provides the strongest evidence so far that a rocky world with Earth-like temperature conditions beyond the solar system has retained an atmosphere [1][2]. The team was led by Collin Cherubim, a recent Ph.D. graduate in Earth and Planetary Sciences at Harvard University [1]. SciTechDaily described the finding as the first clear detection of an atmosphere on a rocky planet in another star’s habitable zone; SoylentNews also summarized the finding as the first atmosphere found on a planet in another star’s habitable zone [1][3].

Key Points

  • Host star: LHS 1140 b orbits a cool red dwarf. Nearby small stars can make transiting planets easier to study, making them frequent targets for atmosphere searches [1][common].

Scientific Significance

  • Scientific context: The detection concerns LHS 1140 b, a nearby rocky planet in a habitable zone about 48 light-years away [1].
  • Uncertainty and confidence: The source describes the evidence as the strongest so far, not as a complete atmospheric inventory; the reported signal identifies helium escape but does not establish surface habitability or biological activity [1].
  • Limitations and future work: The available descriptions do not give the atmosphere’s full composition, surface pressure, or water status, leaving those questions for follow-up observations [1].
