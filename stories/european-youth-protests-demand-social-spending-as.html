European youth protests demand social spending as Italian strike disrupts services

Category: World

Students and workers demonstrated across more than 25 European countries on Friday, Nov. 20, over living costs, education, housing and military spending, while Italy’s largest trade union, the Italian General Confederation of Labour (CGIL), staged a 24-hour nationwide strike [1][2][32]. Organizers said coordinated youth protests were planned in about 400 cities; in Italy, demonstrations took place in more than 100 cities [17][5]. Workers sought higher wages and stronger public services, while students in Germany and elsewhere opposed rearmament and a possible return to compulsory military service. Protesters also raised concerns about education funding, climate justice, housing costs and the war in Gaza. In Italy, the strike disrupted local transport, schools and some health services. The CGIL strike excluded rail and air travel, though a separate rail stoppage was scheduled to run until Saturday evening [2][14][21]. CGIL leader Maurizio Landini said more than 1 million people joined demonstrations in Italy, while the Interior Ministry said it was too early to provide its own estimate [39]. Police used pepper spray and made arrests at a youth protest in Vienna, and Leipzig police used water cannons after a demonstration there [2][13]. Further airport-worker walkouts are scheduled for Sunday at Milan and Venice airports [2].
