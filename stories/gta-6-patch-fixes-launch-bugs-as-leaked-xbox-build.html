GTA 6 patch fixes launch bugs as leaked Xbox build reaches menus

Category: Technology

Rockstar Games released the first post-launch update for Grand Theft Auto VI on Nov. 23, fixing seven reported problems, including crashes, failed autosaves, stuck weather, missing story-related phone calls and wanted levels that would not clear [25][33]. The patch also fixes two gameplay issues involving gym equipment and partners handing over bags for sale [33]. Separately, the hacking group CyberLeek released a 57-megabyte executable it claims belongs to a leaked Xbox build, following an earlier 209-gigabyte file dump [11][14]. Reports of unofficial tests claimed the build reached menus on Windows, but did not establish that it was a playable PC version. Rockstar has not confirmed the executable’s authenticity, and the reported footage and claims about the unofficial build remain unverified [14][64][22]. The Leonida community porting project later shut down, with its administrator citing school, sports and online abuse as factors [11]. Rockstar has not announced a PC release date. GamesBeat, citing four sources familiar with the developer’s plans, reported a possible target of the first quarter of 2027, which Rockstar has not confirmed [2][19].

Key Points

  • Patch sizes: The PlayStation 5 update is version 01.002.002 and requires a roughly 604-megabyte download; the Xbox Series X|S update is 1.6 gigabytes [43][33].
  • Scam warnings: Kaspersky recorded 27,416 detections of malware or potentially unwanted applications linked to GTA 6 from June through November 2026 and warned players against unofficial installers [83].
  • Launch figures: Sony said more than 39 million unique PlayStation 5 players had experienced the game, and players logged 683 million hours through Nov. 22 [75].
  • Leak verification: A SHA-256 checksum can show whether a downloaded file matches a posted copy, but it does not establish that the file came from Rockstar or is safe [22][64].
  • Platform access: Xbox says players can stream the game to Xbox One through Cloud Gaming using Series S graphics settings; eligible Game Pass plans include up to 15 hours of cloud play per month [23].

Perspectives

CyberLeek: The group described its executable release as free and framed its leak campaign as opposition to digital-only releases, extra charges for content and games sold as licenses; those claims and its motives have not been independently established [14][55].

Historical Background

Rockstar’s earlier PC ports also followed their console releases: Grand Theft Auto V took 574 days to reach PC, while Red Dead Redemption 2 took 375 days [2]. Former Rockstar developer Mike York has said differences in hardware and components help explain why PC ports take longer [2].
