Thieves steal four Antonello works from Sicilian museum

Category: World

Thieves stole four works attributed to 15th-century Renaissance painter Antonello da Messina from the Regional Interdisciplinary Museum of Messina in Sicily late on Aug. 15, while the city marked Ferragosto and Assumption celebrations [2][48][7]. The stolen works include three of the five surviving panels of the 1473 San Gregorio Polyptych and a double-sided devotional panel depicting the Virgin Mary and Christ in Pietà, officials and Italian media said [1][2][48]. LaPresse reported that the thieves bypassed security systems; MuMe director Marisa Mercurio said the theft happened just before 10 p.m., with guards on site and alarms working [2][8][51]. The thieves removed all five surviving San Gregorio panels from their frame but abandoned two while fleeing, and art expert Alberto Fiz estimated the stolen works at €70 million to €80 million [2][4][8]. Police and prosecutors opened an investigation into how the thieves entered the museum and who organized the robbery [48][11][27]. The museum remained closed on Aug. 16 as investigators searched for evidence [1][58].

  • Holiday timing: Italian news outlet Il Giornale dell’Arte said the perpetrators exploited the citywide distraction of the Vara procession, which drew police and crowds to central Messina [7].

MuMe director Marisa Mercurio: Mercurio told Sky TG24 the works were not merely Antonello paintings but central to local identity, artistic value and regional heritage.

Technical Details

  • Wood-panel transport: All stolen works were painted on wooden panels, which investigators noted cannot be rolled up like canvas paintings and are harder to move discreetly [48][44].
  • Altarpiece dimensions: The altarpiece from which the panels were taken measures 170 cm by 203 cm [14][34].

Industry Impact

  • Museum security: The Messina heist followed a March break-in near Parma where burglars used a tractor to breach a fence and completed the theft in under three minutes, according to NPR [15].
  • Illicit art trade: ANSA reported investigators were examining whether the paintings were stolen to order for the black market [48][27][59].
  • Cultural heritage policing: Italy's Carabinieri, including a specialist cultural heritage protection team, led the Parma recovery [15].

Historical Background

The San Gregorio Polyptych originally comprised six boards and was made for a local monastery later destroyed in Messina's 1908 earthquake [14][34][56]. Before the theft, the surviving altarpiece was described as the only Antonello work that had never left the painter's hometown [14][34]. Antonello da Messina lived in the 15th century and is widely regarded as Sicily's leading early Renaissance painter [48][36].
