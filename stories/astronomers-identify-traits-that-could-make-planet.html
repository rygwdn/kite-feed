Astronomers identify traits that could make planets more habitable than Earth

Category: Science

Scientists are examining whether some planets could offer better conditions for life than Earth, considering so-called superhabitable worlds rather than assuming Earth-like planets are the best targets [1][3][2]. The idea is a way to prioritize candidate planets, not evidence that scientists have found life [2]. One proposed profile combines a rocky planet somewhat more massive than Earth with an orange K-dwarf star. The planet's greater mass could prolong geological activity and help sustain a magnetic field, while K-dwarfs can survive for tens of billions of years [2]. The blog says next-generation telescopes may allow astronomers to examine the atmospheres of promising K-dwarf planets more closely around 2030 [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Nearby candidates: The blog estimates that roughly 4,500 K-dwarf stars lie within about 150 light-years; around 30 known planets orbiting those stars are Earth-like or super-Earths in habitable zones [2].
  • Observation trade-off: Astronomers prioritize some planets orbiting red-dwarf M stars because their atmospheres are easier to examine, although the blog describes the stars' early instability and close-in habitable zones as potential disadvantages for life [2].
  • Distant examples: Potential candidates include KOI 5715.01, about 3,000 light-years away, and Kepler-1126b, more than 2,700 light-years away—too distant for effective atmospheric study with current technology, the blog says [2].

Scientific Significance

  • Planet-size trade-off: Greater gravity could flatten a planet’s surface, creating more shallow seas that may favor life; a world that is too massive could instead become a sub-Neptune with a thick hydrogen-and-helium atmosphere [2].
  • Evidence limits: A planet's position in a star's habitable zone does not establish that its atmosphere can support life. The blog points to Venus as an example and says astronomers need atmospheric observations to assess conditions on candidate worlds [2].
