Canadian MPs reject bill to close US arms loophole

Category: Canada

Canadian MPs voted down a private member’s bill from NDP MP Jenny Kwan that would have tightened Ottawa’s arms-export controls by closing what supporters call a U.S. re-export loophole [2][1][3]. Supporters say that once Canadian-made weapons go to the United States, they can be shipped on to destinations Canada would normally restrict—letting exporters sidestep Canadian policy [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Timing/procedure: MPs voted on whether to send Kwan’s proposal forward for further consideration and study in Parliament [1].
  • Why it drew renewed attention: Reporting tied the renewed push to a U.S. effort to buy Canadian weapons for Israel despite Canadian restrictions; some Liberal MPs backed the measure, but it still failed [2].

Perspectives

Jenny Kwan (NDP MP): Kwan introduced the private member’s bill, saying it would stop Canadian export restrictions from being bypassed through U.S. re-exports [1].

Sources: National Observer

Liberal MPs (some): Some Liberal MPs voted for the proposal, but it still didn’t pass [2].
