LG sets Gallery TV to rival Samsung Frame

Category: Technology

LG says it will introduce its “Gallery TV” at CES 2026, positioning it as a canvas-style television meant to compete directly with Samsung’s Frame lineup [1][2][3][5][6][7]. The set focuses on blending into home décor with a flush-to-wall design and interchangeable magnetic frames, aiming to make a large screen look more like wall art when not in active use [1][2][5][6]. LG also highlights an art-focused viewing experience via a dedicated “Gallery Mode,” developed with museum curators to better reproduce textures and reduce glare in bright rooms [1][2].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: TechSpot reports LG’s Gallery+ service includes more than 4,500 artworks for display on the Gallery TV [3].

Key Points

  • Screen choices: LG plans to use 4K MiniLED for the Gallery TV, while Samsung’s Frame uses QLED, setting up a head-to-head on brightness, contrast, and “art realism” rather than OLED-style black levels [1][7].
  • Two sizes: The initial lineup is expected in 55-inch and 65-inch models, matching common “statement TV” sizes for living rooms and apartments [1][6].
  • Art library: LG says the Gallery TV will tap its Gallery+ content service, which TechSpot reports includes more than 4,500 pieces of artwork [3].
  • AI creativity: Owners will be able to generate new art with generative AI and also display personal photos from their own gallery, turning the TV into a rotating digital frame for custom content [3].
  • Audio approach: LG describes AI Sound Pro support that aims to simulate a 9.1.2-channel audio setup without external speakers, keeping the “clean wall” aesthetic intact [1].

Perspectives

LG: LG frames the Gallery TV as a purpose-built “art TV,” emphasizing museum-curated display tuning, anti-glare measures, and swappable frames as the core differentiators for décor-first buyers.

Sources: ZDNET

Product reviewers: Some coverage highlights this as a long-awaited alternative in a category dominated by Samsung’s Frame, suggesting more choice for shoppers who want a TV that visually disappears into a room.

Sources: Digital Trends

Technical Details

  • MiniLED backlighting: MiniLED TVs use many small LEDs behind the LCD panel to create finer local dimming control than traditional LED LCDs, which LG is using here to pursue bright, gallery-like images [1][common].
  • Glare-reduction screen: LG says the 55-inch and 65-inch models will use a specialized glare-reduction screen to help artwork remain legible and textured in well-lit rooms [1].
  • Gallery Mode calibration: LG says it developed Gallery Mode with museum curators to better reproduce subtle textures found in physical artworks, signaling a focus on color/texture presentation rather than typical “movie mode” priorities [1][2].

Industry Impact

  • Home décor retail: More competition in art-style TVs could expand partnerships among TV brands, museums/curators, and art-licensing catalogs as “ambient display” becomes a mainstream living-room feature [1][2][3].
  • TV installation services: Flush-mount designs and the expectation of minimal visible cabling can increase demand for professional wall-mounting and in-wall cable management, especially as buyers treat TVs like permanent wall art [1][common].
  • Generative AI at home: By baking generative AI art creation into a consumer TV experience, LG is pushing AI features beyond phones and PCs and into “ambient” household devices where people may interact daily [3][common].

Historical Background

Samsung’s Frame helped popularize “lifestyle TVs” that show artwork when idle, making design and ambiance a key part of the TV pitch rather than just picture quality [1]. LG previously offered slim “Gallery Design” models, but the new Gallery TV marks its first dedicated push into the art-TV category with features like interchangeable frames and a gallery-focused mode [1][6].
