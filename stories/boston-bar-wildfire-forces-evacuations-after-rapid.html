Boston Bar wildfire forces evacuations after rapid growth

Category: Canada

An out-of-control wildfire near Boston Bar, B.C., expanded to more than 12 square kilometres by Sunday afternoon, up from an earlier estimate of one square kilometre, according to BC Wildfire Service updates described by The Canadian Press and La Presse [1][3]. The Brunswick Creek fire was detected on July 2 and is believed to have been started by human activity [1][3][5][6]. The growth prompted evacuation orders from the Boothroyd Indian Band and the Fraser Valley Regional District, which ordered residents in the North Bend area to leave and later issued an order for the Fishblue Lake area [1][3][6]. The regional district’s North Bend order covered 120 properties, while officials had not specified how many properties were affected by the First Nation’s order [1][3]. BC Wildfire Service assigned three initial attack crews, two unit crews, two response officers, six daytime helicopters and two groups of helicopters equipped with night vision to the fire [1][3]. The service also sought more resources and aviation support [1][3].

Did you know: Boston Bar takes its name from a gold-bearing sandbar on the Fraser River that drew prospectors during British Columbia’s 1858 Fraser Canyon Gold Rush [common].

Key Points

  • Evacuation zones: The two main evacuation orders covered areas mostly along the west side of the Fraser River near Boston Bar, with some eastern-shore areas north of the community also included [6].
  • Alert extended: Officials extended an evacuation alert for the Boston Bar area as the fire continued to grow [2][4].
