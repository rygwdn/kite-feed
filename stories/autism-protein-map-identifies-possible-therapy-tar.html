Autism protein map identifies possible therapy targets

Category: Science

Researchers published a Science study mapping how autism-linked mutations alter protein-protein interaction networks, using molecular data to connect autism risk genes with changes in brain development [2][4]. Medical Xpress described the work as the largest molecular map of autism and said it addresses a longstanding question in the field: how mutations in hundreds of genes associated with autism spectrum disorder can alter neurodevelopment [5]. The study used systematic protein-interaction mapping to examine how causal mutations can rewire cellular networks. Researchers presented the framework as a way to identify disease mechanisms and possible therapeutic targets [2]. Coverage said the map was built using artificial intelligence and lab-grown brain tissue, and the New York Times reported that the findings could inform future therapies for people with severe autism [6][3].
