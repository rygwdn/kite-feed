Physicists observe quantum strings and simulate particle creation

Category: Science

Researchers at the University of Innsbruck and collaborators created and observed Bethe strings in an ultracold gas, a phenomenon Hans Bethe predicted in 1931. The one-dimensional quantum groups form through particle interactions rather than conventional chemical bonds, and the gas allows researchers to adjust conditions for studying their formation and interactions [1]. Separately, a Duke Quantum Center-led team used a 13-ion quantum simulator to observe string-breaking dynamics associated with particle-antiparticle formation. The experiment, published in Nature Physics on September 23, models a process relevant to the early universe [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • The Innsbruck experimental team worked with theory teams from the University of Amsterdam and the Technical University of Munich [1].
  • The string-breaking experiment used a trapped-ion system containing 13 ions [2].

Scientific Significance

  • The trapped-ion experiment models a process relevant to early-universe physics; it does not directly recreate the Big Bang [2].
