Studies assess solar-storm risks to grid and GPS

Category: Science

Two new science reports examined solar-storm vulnerabilities in U.S. infrastructure. Phys.org reported that researchers developed a model to measure the economic risks solar storms pose to the U.S. power grid, while ZME Science reported that a separate study analyzed a severe geomagnetic storm on Nov. 11, 2025, that shifted GPS positions across parts of the continental United States by more than 10 meters, or 33 feet, for hours [1][2]. The GPS study traced the disruption to the auroral oval pushing unusually far south and forming a wide band of unevenly charged air across mid-latitudes, where such large-scale interference had not previously been observed this way [2]. Phys.org cited a historical case from 1967, when a solar storm jammed U.S. early-warning radar systems and led U.S. leaders to suspect Soviet action before solar forecasters provided timely information [1].

Key Points

  • Navigation risk: ZME Science said GPS did not fail outright during the 2025 storm, but its accuracy degraded enough to threaten systems that depend on high-precision positioning [2].
  • Forecasting question: The GPS findings leave researchers asking whether they can predict when and where solar storms will push positioning systems off course [2].

Scientific Significance

  • Uncertainty and confidence: The ZME Science article reported position errors greater than 10 meters over parts of the continental United States, but it did not provide confidence intervals, sample sizes or receiver-network coverage details in the supplied text [2].

Business Angle

  • Utilities with stronger grid-hardening plans may face higher near-term capital spending but lower exposure to outage-related losses from severe geomagnetic events [1][common].
  • The 2025 storm degraded GPS accuracy for hours, a risk for businesses that rely on precise positioning and lack backup navigation or timing systems [2].
  • Insurers and infrastructure investors may need better loss models because the Phys.org report focuses on economic risks rather than only technical grid impacts [1].
