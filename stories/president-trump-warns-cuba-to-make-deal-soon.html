President Trump warns Cuba to make deal soon

Category: World

President Donald Trump has warned Cuba to “make a deal” with Washington, saying Venezuelan oil and money that have supported the island in recent years will stop after Nicolás Maduro was removed from power in Venezuela [2][5][9][10][11]. The message follows major upheaval in Venezuela, where US forces seized Maduro in an operation that reportedly killed dozens of Venezuelan and Cuban security personnel [4][5]. Cuban leaders rejected the warning and defended Cuba’s right to obtain fuel from willing exporters, arguing the US approach escalates tensions and undermines sovereignty [4][5][3].

Primary Source: View original article

THERE WILL BE NO MORE OIL OR MONEY GOING TO CUBA — ZERO!

— President Donald Trump (Dawn)

Did you know: Several reports note Cuba began relying more heavily on Venezuelan oil after 2000 under deals tied to Hugo Chávez’s era [5].

Key Points

  • Stated US goals: Asked later about the warning, President Trump said he wanted people forced out of Cuba or who “left under duress” to be “taken care of,” without detailing what a “deal” would include [4].
  • Rubio remark: Trump reposted a message suggesting Secretary of State Marco Rubio could become Cuba’s president, adding “Sounds good to me!” [5][8].
  • Energy lifeline: Cuba’s dependence on imported fuel has long been tied to arrangements with Venezuela dating back to the early 2000s, with Mexico also cited as a fuel source in recent years [5].
  • Prisoner releases: CBS reported Trump said Venezuela has “started the process” of releasing political prisoners in the wake of recent events there [6].
  • Cuba’s strains: Firstpost described Cuba as facing blackouts, fuel shortages, and deep economic strain as Washington increases pressure [1].

Perspectives

US President Donald Trump: Trump argues Cuba benefited from Venezuelan oil and money while providing “security services,” and says that support will end unless Havana reaches an unspecified deal with Washington [5].

Sources: PBS NewsHour

Cuban President Miguel Díaz-Canel: Díaz-Canel rejected the ultimatum, saying Cuba is sovereign and “nobody dictates what we do,” while blaming the island’s hardships on decades of US pressure [8].

Sources: RT

Cuba’s Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez: Rodríguez said Cuba has not been paid for “security services” to other countries and insisted it has the right to import fuel from any country willing to export it, while accusing the US of acting in a “criminal” manner that threatens global peace [5].

Sources: Dawn

Timeline

  • This month: US special forces seized Nicolás Maduro in a raid [4][5].
  • Sunday: Trump warned Cuba to “make a deal” and cutoffs [2][5].

Technical Details

  • Venezuelan oil-for-services arrangements: Cuba has historically received Venezuelan oil, with officials describing ties that included Cuban “security services” claims by Trump and denials by Cuba’s foreign minister [5].
  • Naval blockade claim: Trump said the US effectively controls Venezuela via a naval blockade of its oil sector, a key point in how Washington could restrict shipments outward [4].

Industry Impact

  • Caribbean shipping and trade: Reports of no crude cargoes leaving Venezuelan ports for Cuba after Maduro’s removal highlight how quickly political changes can interrupt maritime energy routes and related shipping services [7].
  • Cuba’s power supply: With the island already facing fuel shortages and blackouts, any sustained reduction in imported fuel can worsen electricity reliability for households and businesses [1].

Historical Background

The United States has maintained a comprehensive trade embargo on Cuba since the 1960s, shaping decades of tense relations and limiting Havana’s access to trade and finance [common]. Several reports note that since 2000, Cuba increasingly relied on Venezuelan oil under agreements associated with Hugo Chávez and later Nicolás Maduro, making Venezuela’s turmoil especially consequential for Cuba’s energy supply [5].

Q&A

Q: What specific “deal” is President Trump asking Cuba to accept?

A: The articles report Trump did not provide details on what the proposed deal would include or achieve [4][2].

Q: Which non-Venezuelan countries are mentioned as Cuba fuel sources?

A: Cuba’s foreign minister said Cuba can import fuel from any willing exporter, and the reporting notes Mexico as another historical supplier alongside Venezuela [5].

Q: What signs of near-term instability do sources flag for Cuba?

A: PBS said Cuba is bracing for potential widespread unrest, while Firstpost described ongoing blackouts and fuel shortages that strain daily life [2][1].
