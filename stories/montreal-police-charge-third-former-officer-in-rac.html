Montreal police charge third former officer in racism investigation

Category: Canada

Montreal police arrested and charged former officer Youcef Boumsied, 33, with assault over an alleged incident on or around Aug. 31, 2024. He is the third recently fired officer charged in the investigation into alleged racism and misconduct at Station 39 in Montréal-Nord [3][9][7]. Boumsied was released on a promise to appear; no court date has been set. Two other former officers, Zachary Belzil and Vincent Routhier, face assault and breach-of-trust charges. Belzil also faces a criminal-harassment charge. Both are due in court Dec. 8 [1][2]. The charges follow the firing of seven officers from two Montreal stations as criminal investigations continue. Allegations tied to Station 39 include officers cutting racialized civilians’ hair and keeping it as trophies. None of the allegations against the charged officers has been tested in court [3][7]. Quebec’s prosecution service said it would not comment further on the latest case, while Montreal police said related investigations remain underway [3].

In the end, we judge a police officer by the way they treat those who cannot defend themselves.

— Fady Dagher, Montreal police chief (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Internal reporting: The police chief said he was encouraged that Station 39 officers reported colleagues, saying it showed they felt able to break the “code of silence” [6].
  • Other allegations: Separate allegations involving Station 21 include an officer humiliating people experiencing homelessness, including by allegedly urinating on them [3][7].
  • Independent oversight: An independent observer appointed by Quebec’s Ministry of Internal Security is overseeing the criminal investigations [3][10].
  • Public disclosure: Quebec’s prosecution service said it announced the third charge publicly to preserve public confidence in the justice system [6].

Perspectives

Montreal police: The force said discriminatory or racist behaviour is unacceptable and will not be tolerated [3].

Sources: CBC News

Montreal police brotherhood: The union is contesting the seven dismissals, arguing the process was arbitrary and did not respect principles of natural justice [9].
