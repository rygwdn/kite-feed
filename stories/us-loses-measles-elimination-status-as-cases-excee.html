US loses measles elimination status as cases exceed 5,400

Category: Science

The United States lost its measles-elimination status on Nov. 17 after international health officials concluded the virus had spread continuously for at least 12 months, ending a designation the country had held since 2000 [12][2][7]. Pennsylvania reported another measles death the following day, bringing the state’s 2026 toll to eight [5]. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention had counted 5,412 U.S. cases through Nov. 12, the highest annual total since 1991; Pennsylvania accounted for 1,589 cases [5][14][7]. Measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccination coverage among U.S. kindergarteners was 92.4% in the 2025-26 school year, below the roughly 95% level experts say is needed to prevent outbreaks [11][2]. The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), the World Health Organization’s regional agency for the Americas, also revoked elimination status for Mexico and Bolivia [12][6]. The three countries are expected to submit plans in the coming weeks to stop transmission. To regain elimination status, they must demonstrate that sustained spread has been interrupted for at least a year [12][6].

Measles elimination is not a trophy that is won once and placed on a shelf. It is an achievement that must be safeguarded every day by closing immunity gaps and ensuring we reach every community.

— Dr. Jarbas Barbosa, director of the Pan American Health Organization (Associated Press)

Key Points

  • Review period: PAHO’s review relied on evidence from January 2025 through January 2026; the large Pennsylvania outbreak that began in April 2026 did not factor into the status decision [7].
  • Transmission evidence: Brewer cited measles cases reported every week since Jan. 20, 2025, and cases with no confirmed travel or contact source as evidence of sustained spread [7][3].
  • Pregnancy risks: A federal public-health alert recorded 112 measles infections among pregnant women and 13 congenital cases in newborns from Jan. 1, 2025, through Sept. 28, 2026 [14].
  • Reported outcomes: Among the pregnant women whose outcomes were reported, two lost pregnancies. Eight of 19 live births were preterm, and eight newborns required neonatal intensive care [14].
  • State response: Pennsylvania said roughly 300 health-department staff were working with communities and providers and that the state had administered more than 11,500 MMR doses in 2026 [5].

Perspectives

Pan American Health Organization: Director Jarbas Barbosa said the response should address barriers to care, missed vaccinations, misinformation and vaccine hesitancy, with broad vaccination campaigns, expanded laboratory testing and rapid case response [3].

U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention: Director Erica Schwartz said losing the designation does not change the agency’s approach and that vaccination, rather than the status itself, protects people [14].

Sources: Wisconsin Examiner

U.S. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.: Kennedy attributed vaccination gaps partly to COVID-era disruptions and said he continues to recommend MMR vaccination, while critics have faulted his previous public questioning of vaccine safety [14][11].

Sources: Wisconsin Examiner

WIRED reported that regaining elimination status could take years. William Moss said future efforts would require a stronger centralized federal response paired with very local work to identify under-vaccinated communities [3].

Josh Michaud, KFF global and public-health policy expert: Michaud said the current administration has not communicated the need for vaccination with the urgency he saw from earlier administrations responding to measles outbreaks [11].

Timeline

  • 20 January 2025: Texas outbreak began, starting the transmission period later reviewed by PAHO [7].
  • 18 August 2025: Texas declared its outbreak over after confirming 762 cases [7].
  • October 2025: South Carolina outbreak began and later reached 997 cases [7].
  • April 2026: South Carolina declared its outbreak over after nearly six months [7].
  • 17 November 2026: PAHO formally withdrew U.S. measles-elimination status [5][12].

Historical Background

Before vaccination, nearly all U.S. children contracted measles by age 15; the disease caused an estimated 3 million to 4 million infections, 48,000 hospitalizations and 400 to 500 deaths annually [1]. The Americas became the first world region to achieve measles elimination in 2016 [6]. After endemic transmission returned in Venezuela and Brazil in 2018 and 2019, respectively, the region regained its elimination status in 2024 [9].

Humanitarian Impact

Six newborns with congenital measles developed symptoms, including one infant with symptoms of a life-threatening condition involving brain swelling. Health officials urged clinicians to prepare to care for pregnant patients and infants in communities with many unvaccinated residents [14].

Q&A

Q: What public-health measures could help the United States regain elimination status?

A: Experts say the effort would require broad vaccination campaigns, expanded laboratory testing, rapid case response and contact tracing, alongside work to address barriers to care and vaccine hesitancy [3].

Action Items

  • Check vaccination records for eligible children: Confirm with a health-care provider whether a child has received the recommended MMR doses; officials identify vaccination as the principal protection against measles [6][14].
