Update: B.C. district says Tumbler Ridge students won’t return

Category: Canada

After the mass shootings in Tumbler Ridge, B.C., the Peace River South school district says students shouldn’t expect to return to the secondary school site where the attack took place. Officials are working on alternate plans to resume classes and support students and staff [1][3][4]. Provincial and federal leaders travelled to Tumbler Ridge to grieve with residents and underline national solidarity. Prime Minister Mark Carney and Opposition Leader Pierre Poilievre appeared together at a large vigil and other memorial events [6][7][8][9]. Premier David Eby said the province will make sure students have a safe place to return to learning when classes resume [5]. The shootings have also renewed attention on how B.C.’s Mental Health Act is used in crisis situations, after police said the teenage shooter had previously been apprehended under the Act [10].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Victims identified: Reports said five students and an educator were among those killed at the school, and two additional people (the shooter’s mother and brother) were killed at a home, according to RCMP accounts cited in coverage [5].
  • Community vigil remarks: At Friday night’s vigil, Carney told mourners, “Nothing I can say will bring your children home,” as he and Poilievre joined residents in paying tribute to the victims [6].
  • Memorial actions: Carney laid flowers at a memorial in Tumbler Ridge during public commemorations for the victims [9].
  • Mental Health Act: Coverage highlighted what the Act allows in a crisis, including when police can apprehend someone and take them to hospital for psychiatric assessment if legal criteria are met, after police said the shooter had previously been apprehended under it [10].
  • Town’s reality: The New York Times described Tumbler Ridge as a remote mining town now facing long-term change after the attack [13].

Perspectives

Peace River South School District: Superintendent Christy Fennell told families students should not expect to return to the current secondary school site, with more details to come [4].
