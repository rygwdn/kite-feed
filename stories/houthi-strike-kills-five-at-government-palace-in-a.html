Houthi strike kills five at government palace in Aden, Yemen

Category: World

Yemen’s Houthi movement fired five missiles at the Al-Maashiq palace compound in Aden on Thursday, killing five people and wounding 16, according to Health Minister Qasim Buhaibeh. The palace houses Yemen’s internationally recognized government and command facilities used by government forces and the Saudi-led coalition. Prime Minister Shaya al-Zindani and cabinet members were safe. The Houthis said they targeted a Saudi military operations headquarters; Yemen’s government said the attack involved missiles and drones [3][16][8][10]. The four-year informal ceasefire began to unravel in July, and fighting intensified after the Houthis launched an offensive in September. The Houthis have made gains along Yemen’s Red Sea coast, while government forces, backed by Saudi air support, have tried to retake territory near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a key shipping route. Houthi attacks have also hit Saudi infrastructure and disrupted aviation. The regional conflict has affected oil markets [16][10][3][20]. Saudi Arabia accused Iran of encouraging the Houthi escalation. Tehran has previously denied supplying weapons to the Houthis. The U.N. Security Council condemned the attacks on Saudi Arabia, including strikes and threats against civilian airports and commercial shipping. Al-Zindani condemned the Aden attack and said his government would continue operations against the Houthis [16][3].

Key Points

  • Riyadh airport: The Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis attacked King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh 20 times in October, killing 17 people and wounding 333; the figures are coalition claims [10][19].
  • Marib barrage: Authorities in Marib governorate said Houthi forces fired nine ballistic missiles and seven other projectiles at the city on Thursday [48][30].
  • Rejected meeting: Saudi official Rayed Krimly said Riyadh asked the Houthis to meet in Muscat as fighting escalated, but they refused. He said Saudi Arabia remained in contact with Iran [20][23].
  • Government offensive: Yemeni government spokesperson Majed al-Nuzaili claimed government forces carried out 1,250 attacks on Houthi positions in the preceding 24 hours and destroyed 150 vehicles [30][48].
  • Flight disruption: Flightradar24 reported dozens of cancellations or delays at Jeddah airport as attacks and air defenses affected Saudi aviation [3].

Perspectives
