Former broadcaster Pamela Wallin resigns Senate seat two years early

Category: Canada

Pamela Wallin, a former broadcaster who represented Saskatchewan in the Senate, resigned effective Oct. 18 at age 73, two years before mandatory retirement, the Senate confirmed Monday [1][5]. After 17 years in the Senate, Wallin said leaving “just felt like the right time.” She linked her decision to the federal government’s announcement last week that it would amend the Criminal Code to allow advance requests for medical assistance in dying, an issue she has championed [3]. Wallin said her advocacy grew from watching her mother’s long struggle with Alzheimer’s disease. She also argued that provinces should have more say in Senate appointments and called the chamber an important check on the House of Commons [3].

Primary Source: View original article

I just think there are so many people in that situation that want to have some control and some sense of choice in the way that they leave, add some dignity to their departure.

— Pamela Wallin, senator (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Expense scandal: A Senate audit found that about $140,000 in Wallin’s travel expenses were improper; she repaid roughly $150,000, including interest, and police did not charge her [1][5][3].
  • Broadcast milestones: Wallin became CTV’s Ottawa bureau chief in 1985, making her the first woman to lead a national television news bureau in Canada. In 1992, she became the country’s first woman to co-anchor a nightly news program [3].
