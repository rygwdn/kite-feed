Paraguay judge orders ex-president detained as Ueno investigation expands

Category: World

A Paraguayan judge ordered former President Santiago Peña held in pretrial detention on Saturday after prosecutors charged him with breach of trust and administration for personal gain in the collapse of Ueno Bank, a digital lender where the Central Bank’s intervention locked nearly 3 million customers out of their accounts [7][9][32]. The order followed Peña’s removal by Congress on Friday and his appearance for questioning in Asunción [1][11]. Prosecutors allege that public and pension funds flowed into Ueno while the bank made irregular loans to companies linked to its owners. They also seek to freeze Peña’s assets and those of former central bank chief Carlos Carvallo, who faces separate charges [5][23]. Peña denies wrongdoing. Judge Humberto Otazú said detention was needed to ensure Peña remained in the country during the investigation; Peña’s lawyer disputed that he posed a flight risk or could obstruct the investigation [9][17]. The Central Bank said 1.2 million customers had received partial or full reimbursements by Saturday [1][7]. The next phase of payments is to extend coverage, and banks were set to open Sunday so customers could complete claims. Paraguay’s largest teachers’ union planned an indefinite strike from Monday over protection of public pension funds [5][11].

Key Points

  • Guarantee coverage: The deposit guarantee covers balances up to $38,000, a limit that applies to more than 90% of individual account holders [32].
  • Payments underway: The Central Bank reported transfers totaling about $160 million to nearly 1.2 million savers under the deposit-guarantee scheme [9][26].
  • Wider probe: The investigation has named 27 people, including Ueno founder Miguel Vásquez, and six bank executives were transferred to pretrial prisons [19][24].
  • Rapid expansion: Customers described Ueno’s app-based account opening as easy and quick, while its investment returns were more generous than competitors’ [32].
  • Potential penalty: Prosecutors said a conviction on both charges could carry up to 20 years in prison [7].
  • Economic disruption: Businesses reported sales declines of as much as 80% during the week Ueno customers were locked out of their funds [32].
  • Compensation phases: The first payment stage covered balances up to about $1,500, with later payments initially planned up to 40 minimum wages, roughly $19,300 [5].
  • Initial eligibility: The first advance-payment phase covers customers owed up to $1,565 who have no outstanding loans and can receive funds through another institution [32][39].

Perspectives

Peña’s defense lawyer Ricardo Preda said Peña pleaded not guilty and argued that, because he was no longer president, he could not access information held by the state. Preda called detention the most severe available measure [9][17].

Technical Details

  • Solvency threshold: The Central Bank intervened after Ueno’s capital adequacy ratio fell below half the required minimum [17][38].
  • Bank resolution: The resolution procedure allows authorities to transfer customer funds to more stable banks before selling remaining assets to cover the failed institution’s liabilities [38].

Industry Impact

  • Technology employment: About 1,000 employees of ITTI, a technology firm owned by Grupo Vázquez, were suspended without pay [36].
  • Sports: Ueno sponsored several first-division teams and Paraguay’s national team, which said it was also a victim of the crisis [39].

Historical Background

Ueno began operating as a digital bank in 2021 and drew many people who had not previously used banking services [15]. Peña held shares in Ueno’s parent company until 2025, while former Ueno chief executive Carlos Carvallo later led the Central Bank [12][32]. The Guardian reported that Carvallo authorized Ueno’s conversion from a finance company into a bank less than three months after taking the central bank post [32].

Humanitarian Impact

About 32,000 pensioners who received public benefits through Ueno were left without payments, and the bank’s closure also disrupted salaries for 300 workers at affiliated media companies [32].

Business Angle

  • Allegations surrounding Ueno include related-party lending and weak oversight [32].
  • The Social Security Institute had about $355 million invested in Ueno [26].
  • Government deposits at the lender rose from zero when Peña took office in 2023 to more than $800 million earlier this year [34].
  • Peña also had money deposited at Ueno and held a bond issued by another company in its business group [17].
