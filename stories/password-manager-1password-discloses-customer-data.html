Password manager 1Password discloses customer-data breach; says vaults remain secure

Category: Technology

Password manager 1Password disclosed on Nov. 25 that an attacker accessed internal systems and copied account information for some customers. The company says the attacker did not access stored password vaults or the keys protecting them [18][6]. The company has not said how many users were affected [14][6]. 1Password said it began investigating Nov. 19 after detecting unauthorized access to an employee’s corporate GitHub account. The attacker used that access to reach private source code, insert malicious code into backend services and download customer records. The intruder also accessed the company’s software build-and-deployment system, which contains infrastructure credentials [18][12]. The copied records included names, sign-in email addresses, account identifiers and sign-in timestamps, along with certain invitation and recovery-link tokens. 1Password says it disabled the compromised account, removed the attacker’s access, rotated potentially exposed credentials and brought in independent cybersecurity experts. It reports no evidence of customer account takeovers and says users do not need to change their account passwords, but warns them to watch for targeted phishing messages [18][34].

Key Points

  • Recovery tokens: Some stolen tokens came from emailed invitations, provisioning links or account-recovery links. A token alone cannot open an account, and 1Password individually notified the small number of users with an active invitation or recovery process [18][12].
  • Verification codes: The company changed verification codes used in invitation and recovery processes from six numeric digits to 10 alphanumeric characters to make them harder to guess [18][9].
  • Customer scale: 1Password serves millions of individual users and more than 200,000 businesses, though it has not said how many accounts were affected [6][14].

Perspectives

Binance founder Changpeng Zhao advised 1Password users to change all passwords stored in the service, while the company said customers did not need to change credentials [48].

  • October 2023: 1Password reported an Okta-linked security incident without customer data exposure [5][13]
  • June 2026: LastPass reported another breach exposing customer information, not saved passwords [5]
  • 19 November 2026: 1Password began investigating suspicious access to an employee’s corporate GitHub account [18]

Technical Details

  • SRP parameters: The stolen records included Secure Remote Password authentication parameters, which 1Password says cannot be used to access an account. They also included public keys, which are public by design [18][8].

Industry Impact

  • Enterprise password management: 1Password says it serves millions of individual users and more than 200,000 businesses. Stolen names and email addresses could help attackers target affected users with phishing [6][12].

Action Items

  • Handle unexpected 1Password messages cautiously: Do not follow unsolicited invitation or recovery links; open the official app or type the website address yourself, and never provide your password or Secret Key in response to a message or call [18][7].
