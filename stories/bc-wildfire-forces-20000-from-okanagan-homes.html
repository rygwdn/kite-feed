B.C. wildfire forces 20,000 from Okanagan homes

Category: Canada

The Bald Range wildfire near Summerland, B.C., forced more than 20,000 people from their homes after it erupted Friday in the Okanagan region. Summerland has about 12,000 residents [12][15][16]. The fire destroyed homes and killed an 80-year-old woman [12][16]. British Columbia Premier David Eby said the fire moved faster than wildfire models predicted and left some residents with little time to evacuate; he said crews had to shift from firefighting to helping residents escape [10][15][16]. Officials had begun damage assessments by Wednesday, but the area remained unsafe and there was no timeline for residents to return [16][11]. The Regional District of Okanagan-Similkameen reopened Highway 97 through the evacuation zone on Thursday from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., with no stopping or exiting except for people with temporary access passes [7]. From the highway, parts of Summerland appeared unscathed, while the La Punta Norte inn about seven kilometres away showed severe damage where the fire crossed the road and reached the Okanagan Lake shoreline [7].

Key Points

  • Fire status: The Bald Range fire remained listed as out of control, although recent activity was concentrated on its western flanks away from Summerland [7][9].
  • Health access: Merritt Mayor Mike Goetz asked federal Health Minister Marjorie Michel to investigate rolling Interior emergency-room closures as Merritt sheltered about 300 evacuees and 150 firefighters; the local ER faced its 10th scheduled temporary shutdown of the year during the wildfire response [1].
  • Farm losses: Summerland-area farmers said wildfire evacuations cut access to orchards and fields, with Braden Hill Orchard & Farm warning it expected to lose many annual crops [2][13].
  • Animal uncertainty: Officials and residents had not confirmed the status of a small group of mountain goats living near the evacuated Summerland area [3][14].
  • Community aid: The Penticton Sikh Temple fed thousands of evacuees from the Bald Range wildfire, while volunteers in the Okanagan organized support for displaced residents [8].

Perspectives

B.C. Premier David Eby said the fire’s speed provided lessons for emergency planning and that such rapid movement may represent a 'new normal' for wildfire response.

Historical Background

Merritt and other small communities in B.C.’s southern Interior have faced intermittent emergency-room closures linked mainly to shortages of available doctors and nurses [1]. Data previously cited by Mayor Mike Goetz counted 137 emergency-room closures across B.C.’s Interior in 2025, averaging one closure every 2.6 days [1].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Do not stop or exit your vehicle on Highway 97 through the evacuation zone unless you hold a temporary access pass [7].
  • Roads: The district cut the Highway 97 speed limit through the affected area to 70 kilometres per hour [7]. Follow the posted emergency speed limit in the reopened section.
  • Evacuation: Do not return to evacuated properties until officials lift orders [16][11].

Action Items

  • Check Highway 97 access rules before travelling: Confirm current Regional District of Okanagan-Similkameen restrictions because access was limited to daytime travel with no stopping or exiting for most drivers [7].
  • Avoid evacuated areas without authorization: Wait for official evacuation-order changes because authorities said the affected area remained unsafe and residents had no return timeline [16][11].
