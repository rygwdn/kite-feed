Meta pauses employee tracking program after internal data exposure

Category: AI

Meta paused its Model Capability Initiative, an internal program that tracked employees’ computer activity to train AI systems, after sensitive data collected by the tool was left potentially accessible across the company [10][14][16][13]. The program, rolled out in April for U.S.-based employees, captured mouse movements, clicks and keystrokes; several reports also said it periodically collected screen content or screenshots [14][16][3][11]. Internal documentation cited by Reuters said the exposed data included “full prompts and transcriptions, private conversations, people & performance data, DSS sensitivity ratings (1-4),” and accounts citing Wired described employee data spread across about 45,000 Hive tables [14][16][13][6][24]. Meta spokesperson Tracy Clayton said the company had “no indication at this time” that employees improperly accessed the data, while confirming the pause would take time to roll out and that the company was investigating [14][16][3]. The pause followed weeks of internal objections over privacy and security, including a petition that more than 1,600 workers signed [6][13][17][10].

Primary Source: View original article

We have carefully designed this program with privacy safeguards, and while we have no indication at this time that any data was improperly accessed by Meta employees, we're pausing it while we investigate

— Tracy Clayton (Reuters via MyJoyOnline)

Key Points

  • Security escalation: Meta paused MCI after an employee filed a high-priority SEV security incident report over exposure of collected worker data [14][16][21].
  • CTO assurance: CTO Andrew Bosworth had previously told employees that data collected by MCI would be 'tightly controlled' [1].
  • Management rationale: CEO Mark Zuckerberg told staff in May that Meta’s AI models needed to learn by watching “really smart people” perform computer tasks [3][6].
  • Employee objection: A Meta employee wrote in a SEV discussion that staff had used work computers for personal tax and medical information after being told the data would be protected and tightly filtered [16].

Perspectives

Anonymous Meta employee: One current employee called the 30-minute pause option “just an attempt at damage control” after workers objected to the tracking program.

Sources: BBC

Fritz Jean-Louis, Info-Tech Research Group: Jean-Louis described the incident as a failure of AI-era data strategy in which high-risk telemetry was collected without access controls mature enough for the scale of the data.

Sources: Computerworld

Timeline

  • April 2026: Meta rolled out MCI on U.S. employee computers [14][16]
  • May 2026: Reuters reported broader collection and unencrypted storage concerns [14][16]
  • June 2026: Meta allowed 30-minute pauses and gave some remote workers and teams on sensitive projects limited opt-out options after worker objections [10][3][21]
  • 22 June 2026: Meta said it would pause MCI while investigating [14][10]

Technical Details

  • Access controls: Canaltech attributed the exposure to an access-control-list configuration error that let any Meta employee view data from the affected Hive tables [24].

Business Angle

  • AI training data can be an asset only if governance controls prevent sensitive information from becoming a liability [14][5][common].
  • Employee resistance matters because programs that depend on internal behavior data can lose value if staff distrust the collection process [10][13][common].
  • The incident may raise the cost of future AI data programs through security reviews, access-control redesign and privacy oversight [5][24][common].
