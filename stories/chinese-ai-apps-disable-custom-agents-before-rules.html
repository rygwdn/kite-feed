Chinese AI apps disable custom agents before rules

Category: Technology

ByteDance’s Doubao and Alibaba’s Qwen are disabling customized and user-created AI agents in China before new rules on anthropomorphic AI interaction services take effect on July 15, 2026 [1][2][3][4][5][7]. Doubao told users its agent function would go offline on July 15, while Qwen said humanlike interactive agents and user-created agents would be disabled on July 10 and broader agent functions would go offline by July 15 [1][4]. The affected tools let users create named assistants, tutors, role-playing characters or companions with fixed personas and speaking styles [1]. China’s interim measures, issued in April, require stricter oversight of personified AI services, including anti-addiction systems, minor identity checks and content review obligations [6][7]. TechNode reported that users will still be able to view agent configurations and chat histories during a transition period and should back up important content before related data becomes unrecoverable after October 15, 2026 [3].

Did you know: Shanghai’s internet regulator removed more than 14,000 non-compliant AI agents in June, Herald Corp and Global Times reported [6][7].

Key Points

  • Tencent precedent: Tencent removed a similar Yuanbao assistant feature in June, before Doubao and Qwen announced their shutdowns [1].
  • User reaction: The planned removals prompted discussion on Sina Weibo, where some users objected because they had relied on the agents for long-term companionship [7][6].

Perspectives

Users on Sina Weibo: Some users objected to the removals because they had used the agents for long-term companionship, while others described the move as a business-efficiency and compliance shift.
