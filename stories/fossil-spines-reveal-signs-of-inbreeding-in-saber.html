Fossil spines reveal signs of inbreeding in saber-toothed cats

Category: Science

Researchers studying fossilized saber-toothed cat spines from California’s La Brea Tar Pits found deformed vertebrae and rare spinal nerve tumors in the extinct animals [1][3][4]. The fossil study suggests population declines were linked to increased spinal abnormalities before extinction, with inbreeding proposed as a possible explanation [2][4]. Science reported that the cats may have become inbred and struggled to move before they disappeared, while Science News described the vertebral deformities and tumors as possible signs of inbreeding in the species [2][1]. Phys.org said the tumors can cause significant pain and reduced limb function, a finding relevant to how the animals may have hunted and moved near the end of their existence [4].
