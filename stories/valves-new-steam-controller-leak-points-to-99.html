Valve's new Steam Controller leak points to $99

Category: Technology

Reports from multiple gaming and tech outlets say Valve's newly announced Steam Controller is expected to launch at a reported $99, a price that suggests the company is not heavily subsidizing the device [2][3]. The leak also points to a dual-trackpad design, bringing back the distinctive input approach Valve used before [2]. That price has cooled hopes that Valve's new hardware might make Steam Machines especially affordable. Some coverage described the update as a setback for buyers hoping for a lower-cost way in [1].

Did you know: Valve's original Steam Controller became known for trying to replace traditional thumbsticks with trackpads, one of the boldest mainstream controller experiments in PC gaming [common].

Key Points

  • No subsidy: TechSpot said the reported $99 price fits Valve's decision not to subsidize its new hardware lineup, a choice that may keep margins cleaner but raises the upfront cost for buyers [2].
  • Design callback: The leak suggests Valve is sticking with a dual-trackpad layout, echoing an earlier controller concept meant to make mouse-style PC inputs easier to use from the couch [2].
  • Buyer reaction: Coverage aggregated by Google News said the pricing update cooled expectations that the new hardware would revive Steam Machines as a budget-friendly option [1].

Historical Background

Valve previously experimented with living-room PC gaming hardware through its earlier Steam Machine and Steam Controller efforts in the mid-2010s [common]. The original Steam Controller stood out by replacing standard dual analog sticks with trackpads, an unusual design meant to better translate keyboard-and-mouse style PC play to a handheld device [common]. This new leak matters because it suggests Valve is revisiting that same design philosophy [2].
