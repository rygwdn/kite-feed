UN climate summit ends without pact as fossil fuel dispute stalls talks

Category: World

The COP31 United Nations climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, ended on Nov. 22 without an overarching agreement after nearly 200 countries failed to resolve disputes over fossil fuels and climate finance. The talks ran about two days past their scheduled close, making COP31 the longest COP conference on record. The final draft omitted an explicit reference to fossil fuels, prompting the European Union, the United Kingdom, Panama and vulnerable island states to reject it [24][9][18]. The draft referred indirectly to the 2023 COP28 commitment to transition away from fossil fuels but did not spell out a pathway or reaffirm the wording. Oil-producing countries, including Saudi Arabia and Russia, resisted explicit references to fossil fuels, while poorer countries objected that the proposal did not set concrete funding levels for adaptation. COP31 negotiations chair Chris Bowen said fossil fuels and finance were the main obstacles; summit president Murat Kurum said he would not put forward a replacement draft [3][54][16]. Countries agreed on separate measures, including a program to support workers and communities through the energy transition and a plan to assess adaptation progress. More than 80 countries backed Türkiye’s proposed goal of raising electricity’s share of global energy use to 35% by 2035, but the goal was not part of an adopted overarching pact. UN climate chief Simon Stiell said he would seek advice on possible reforms to the consensus-based negotiating process. The next summit is scheduled for Addis Ababa [61][3][24][20].

Did you know: The first draft of the proposed pact mentioned fossil fuels six times, while later drafts removed direct references [3][13].

Key Points

  • Emissions record: A 440 Megatonnes analysis found Canada had cut emissions 8.5% from 2005 levels by 2023, compared with an average 32% reduction across the other G7 countries [30][83].
  • Electricity target: The proposed 35% electricity share by 2035 compares with about 23% today. Coal supplies roughly one-third of global electricity and fossil gas about one-fifth, so the emissions effect would depend on how the power is generated [95][47].
  • Australian fallout: Australian opposition leader Angus Taylor called for Climate Minister Chris Bowen to resign, while Prime Minister Anthony Albanese did not say whether he would dismiss him [36].
  • Finance gap: The draft dropped a proposed annual adaptation-finance figure of $120 billion, while estimated adaptation needs in the Global South alone totaled about $500 billion a year [58][20].
  • Emissions gap: The UN Environment Programme’s modeled pathway requires greenhouse-gas emissions to fall 18% by 2030, 45% by 2035 and 69% by 2040 from 2025 levels. Full implementation of current national pledges would cut emissions far less, putting the world on an estimated 2.6°C warming trajectory [48].
  • Legal challenge: Climate advocates have asked the Federal Court to require Ottawa to bring its 2030 emissions plan into compliance with the Net-Zero Accountability Act, which sets no fines or penalties for missed targets [30][84].
  • Carbon removal: The draft's explicit reference to carbon dioxide removal would have been a first in a COP final agreement. Natural approaches such as tree planting face limits of scale and permanence, while technological options remain controversial and not well advanced [27].
  • Science dispute: The final days also saw disagreement over the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change’s work, with UK climate negotiator Kate Hughes saying the draft moved backward on science [100].
  • US absence: The United States sent no delegation for the second consecutive year after President Donald Trump’s administration withdrew from the Paris climate agreement [10][25].
  • COP initiatives: At the Turkey talks, Canada announced up to $10 million for climate-risk and early-warning systems; nearly 100 countries separately backed a proposal to raise electricity’s share of final energy use to 35% by 2035 [82].

Technical Details

  • Carbon capture scale: The draft called for scaling up carbon capture, utilisation and storage to gigatonne levels by 2035, but did not specify how much this would reduce net emissions [50].

Scientific Significance

  • Emissions pathway: In the UNEP scenario summarized in the reporting, warming peaks at about 1.7°C to 1.8°C near mid-century, with roughly a 50% chance of returning to or below 1.5°C by 2100. This is a modeled pathway, not a prediction that those outcomes will occur [48].

Historical Background

The 2015 Paris Agreement established a goal of limiting warming to 1.5°C above preindustrial levels [8][62]. COP31 was the first summit since the 2009 Copenhagen conference to end without an adopted overarching political agreement [24][54].

International Reactions

  • Russia and Saudi Arabia: Both countries sought to keep fossil fuels out of the COP31 agreement [82].

  • 🇦🇺 Australia: Prime Minister Anthony Albanese attributed the breakdown to difficult geopolitical circumstances [3].
  • 🇺🇳 United Nations: Secretary-General António Guterres said he was deeply disappointed that COP31 ended without consensus on an overarching outcome [82].
  • 🇪🇺 European Union: The 27-nation bloc said it could not accept the final proposal, which did not mention fossil fuels [82].

Q&A

Q: Did the collapse cancel commitments made at earlier climate summits?

A: No. The failure to adopt an overarching agreement did not erase existing commitments under prior COP decisions or the Paris Agreement [44][64].

Q: Where will countries resume climate negotiations?

A: Countries are expected to discuss unresolved issues at interim meetings in Bonn before COP32, scheduled for Addis Ababa [20].

Q: Did the failed summit cancel climate commitments countries had already made?

A: No. The failure to adopt a new political declaration did not erase obligations or technical and legal frameworks agreed at previous UN climate conferences [38].
