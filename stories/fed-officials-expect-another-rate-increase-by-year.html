Fed officials expect another rate increase by year-end as inflation persists

Category: Business

Most Federal Reserve officials said another interest-rate increase would likely be appropriate before the end of 2026, according to minutes of their Sept. 15-16 meeting released Wednesday [3][5][15]. The Fed raised its benchmark rate by a quarter-point at that meeting to a range of 3.75% to 4%, its first increase since 2023. All 19 meeting participants supported the move [18][5]. Officials said inflation remained elevated and had made little recent progress toward the Fed’s 2% target [3][6]. Officials agreed on the September increase but differed on why rates needed to rise. Some saw higher rates as protection against energy and other supply shocks spreading into broader prices; others pointed to stronger demand. A few said their estimate of the neutral interest rate had increased [16][24]. Officials also cited higher energy costs and investment tied to artificial intelligence as inflation risks [18][24]. The minutes did not set a date for another move. Investors expect the Fed to hold rates at its next meeting in late October, with weaker jobs data released Oct. 2 adding to expectations of a pause. Markets have looked to December for a possible increase [18][3][16]. Officials said future decisions would depend on incoming economic data [24].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Fed staff economists projected that inflation would not return to the 2% target until 2029 [18].

Key Points

  • Household costs: Fed participants noted that higher energy prices weigh most heavily on low- and moderate-income households [18].
  • Inflation expectations: A New York Fed survey put median one-year consumer inflation expectations at 3.9% in September, up 0.3 percentage point from the prior month [20].
  • Bond-market planning: Some officials urged preparations for possible Treasury-market disruptions while arguing that any Fed response should preserve market price discovery and limit the central bank’s footprint [25][20].
  • Jobs data: The September employment report, released Oct. 2, showed employers added 29,000 jobs and unemployment rose to 4.2% [18].
  • Long-term yields: Officials linked rising longer-term Treasury yields to stronger economic data, expected borrowing for AI expansion and geopolitical developments [24].

Perspectives

President Donald Trump: Trump criticized the September rate increase and accused members of the rate-setting committee of acting politically, while continuing to express support for Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, whom he appointed earlier this year [3].
