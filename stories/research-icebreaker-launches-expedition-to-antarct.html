Research icebreaker launches expedition to Antarctica’s Thwaites glacier

Category: Science

Two New York Times journalists have joined scientists and engineers aboard the South Korean research icebreaker Araon, departing from Lyttelton Harbour, New Zealand, for an Antarctic field campaign focused on the fast-melting Thwaites Glacier [2][3]. The reporting will follow efforts to measure how quickly the glacier is changing and what that could mean for future global sea-level rise [2][4]. By documenting the work in real time, the Times aims to make the science and logistics of polar research more visible to the public while researchers collect new observations in one of the planet’s hardest-to-reach places [2][3].

Did you know: Antarctica is Earth’s southernmost continent and contains enough land ice to raise sea levels by many tens of meters if it all melted [common].
