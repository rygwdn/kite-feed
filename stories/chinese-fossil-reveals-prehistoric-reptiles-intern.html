Chinese fossil reveals prehistoric reptile's internal organs

Category: Science

Researchers analyzed a southern China fossil of Austronaga minuta, a prehistoric marine reptile, that preserves the stomach, liver and intestinal soft tissue, providing rare evidence of internal anatomy before the dinosaur era [2][3]. The specimen was found in Yunnan province and described in a Science Advances paper, according to a Reuters report republished by Folha [3]. The fossil also preserved evidence of the animal’s meals. Its complete skeleton showed specialized aquatic features that researchers said point to earlier and more extensive marine colonization by archosaurs than previously believed [2][3]. SCMP described the specimen as 240 million years old, while the Reuters report republished by Folha described it as 245 million years old [2][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Perspectives

Chinese-led research team: Researchers said the fossil offers a unique opportunity to study how Triassic reptiles made the transition from land to water.

Sources: South China Morning Post

Scientific Significance

  • Scientific meaning: The find adds direct anatomical evidence to debates over how Triassic reptiles moved into marine habitats after the end-Permian extinction [2][common].
  • Data quality: A complete skeleton with preserved internal organs gives researchers a stronger basis for linking body form, diet and swimming behavior than isolated bones provide [2][3].

Historical Background

The Early Triassic followed the end-Permian mass extinction, the largest known extinction event in Earth’s history [common]. Archosaurs later gave rise to major reptile lineages, including dinosaurs, pterosaurs and crocodilians [common].
