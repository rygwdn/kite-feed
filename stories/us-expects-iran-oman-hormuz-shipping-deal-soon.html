US expects Iran-Oman Hormuz shipping deal soon

Category: World

A U.S. official told Reuters that Washington expects Iran and Oman to reach an agreement soon on the Strait of Hormuz, a step the official said could restore normal oil traffic through the waterway after disruptions during the five-month U.S. war with Iran [8][12][13][17]. The official said the United States would lift its blockade of Iranian ports once an agreement is announced to restore commercial shipping “without impediments,” while tying U.S. actions to Iran’s implementation of its commitments [16][15][25][34]. Iranian officials said they had agreed to a broad framework with Oman but still needed approval from higher authorities before announcing the final text and details [11][23][36][41]. Accounts of the proposed arrangement say inbound commercial ships would use a northern route managed by Iran and outbound ships would use a southern route overseen by Oman, with several accounts saying the temporary framework would not include transit tolls [3][10][52][69]. The Strait of Hormuz carried roughly a fifth of globally traded or consumed oil before the war [9][11][13][35]. Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei said the coordinates of a navigation route had been agreed with Oman, but added that an understanding would not automatically make the strait safe for all vessels [7][35].

Key Points

  • Approval pending: A diplomat involved in mediation cited by Axios said the temporary arrangement was awaiting approval by Iran’s Supreme National Security Council [30][1][61].
  • Limited duration: Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the proposed route would be temporary and could remain open for between two and four months [7].
  • Traffic decline: Shipping traffic through Hormuz fell to 33 vessels from Monday to Thursday this week, down from 50 in the previous comparable period, according to data cited by Reuters [11].
  • U.S. condition: The Trump administration would accept only an arrangement that preserves unrestricted passage and rejects Iranian transit fees, according to AP and Japanese reporting on U.S. officials’ position [9][70].
  • Regional pact: Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement, under which an armed attack on one signatory would count as an attack on all three [47][28][70].

Washington and Tehran signed a framework agreement on June 18, but subsequent negotiations stalled over security guarantees and freedom of navigation through the strait [23].
