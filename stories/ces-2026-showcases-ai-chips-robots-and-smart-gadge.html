CES 2026 showcases AI chips, robots and smart gadgets

Category: Technology

CES 2026 is underway in Las Vegas, where companies are using the show to debut new chips, AI platforms, TVs, laptops, robots, and experimental gadgets that hint at what may reach consumers later this year and beyond [4][7][6][10]. Major chip announcements highlighted AI-focused computing: NVIDIA leaned into “physical AI” and new infrastructure platforms, AMD introduced new Ryzen AI processors (and a gaming-focused X3D chip), and Intel promoted new laptop-focused silicon updates [14][8][13]. Alongside the serious silicon, the show’s charm is also in the playful and strange—coverage spotlights everything from smart LEGO concepts to attention-grabbing novelty devices—making CES a mix of practical near-term products and imagination-fueling prototypes [7][2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: CNET’s CES coverage includes an annual “Best of CES” awards package scheduled for Wednesday during the show week [15].

Key Points

  • Robotaxi push: Transportation tech got a consumer-facing moment with Uber’s newly shown robotaxi concept highlighted among CES standouts [12].
  • Health at home: A connected toilet that can send health alerts was featured as part of the show’s wave of sensor-driven home devices [12].
  • New TV tech: TV makers pushed display upgrades including Samsung’s Micro RGB approach and large-format sets, as reporters flagged Micro RGB as a term likely to show up more in TV shopping soon [7].
  • Lego goes smart: Lego unveiled “Smart Brick” technology, signaling new directions for interactive building and play at CES [7][5].
  • Weird wins attention: Multiple outlets emphasized that CES’s most memorable moments often come from oddball concepts and offbeat gadgets, not just mainstream product launches [2][11][5].

Perspectives

NVIDIA: The company framed its CES message around “physical AI,” emphasizing tools and platforms meant to bridge simulated training and real-world machines, plus an autonomous-vehicle model and the Vera Rubin platform [14].

Sources: Fast Company

Intel: Intel positioned its announcements as a bid to refocus the PC conversation on its latest Core Ultra Series 3 (Panther Lake) laptop processors [8].

Sources: Engadget

DigiTimes: DigiTimes characterized the NVIDIA and AMD keynotes as underwhelming, underscoring that not all observers saw the speeches as strong show openers [9].

Sources: DigiTimes

Hands-on consumer press: Live blogs and roundups from outlets on the ground emphasized practical demos and the breadth of launches—from laptops and TVs to AI-enabled gadgets—rather than just keynote messaging [6][10][3].

Sources: ZDNET

Timeline

  • Sunday night: CES Unveiled showcased early product previews ahead of the show [4].
  • Monday: Press conferences delivered big chip and AI announcements before opening [8].
  • Tuesday, January 6: Show proper kicked off, with live coverage from the floor [4].

Technical Details

  • Physical AI: NVIDIA used “physical AI” to describe models trained in simulated environments (often using synthetic data) before deployment in real machines such as robots or autonomous vehicles [14].
  • Micro RGB TVs: Engadget described Micro RGB as a TV backlighting approach using red, green, and blue LEDs (rather than a white backlight), aiming for higher brightness and color accuracy with more customizable dimming zones [7].
  • AI PC processors: CES coverage emphasized laptop chips optimized for AI workloads, including AMD’s Ryzen AI line and Intel’s Core Ultra Series 3/Panther Lake push for AI-capable PCs [14][8].

Industry Impact

  • PC upgrade cycle: New laptop-focused processors and a wave of CES notebooks can encourage OEM refreshes, with Intel and AMD both framing their silicon around AI-era PCs [4][14][8].
  • Robotics ecosystem: Robots appeared as both product and platform showcases—LG’s home-robot vision and NVIDIA’s robotics tools point toward more developer activity and partnerships beyond consumer gadgets [8][14].
  • Smart home sensing: Showcase devices like a health-alert toilet and AI-linked appliances reflect a broader industry trend toward health and ambient sensing in the home, raising stakes for privacy, reliability, and long-term support [12][6][common].

Historical Background

CES is an annual January trade show where consumer electronics companies often preview upcoming product cycles and new computing platforms [common]. In recent years, AI has become a central theme across consumer devices and the chips that power them, and CES 2026 coverage continues that pattern with AI positioned as a core feature in products and platforms across categories [8][13].

Q&A

Q: What was NVIDIA’s autonomous-driving model called, and what was it positioned to do?

A: Fast Company reported NVIDIA announced Alpamayo, described as a model specifically designed for autonomous driving [14].

Q: Which CES demos were described as “more theatrical than practical,” and why?

A: Engadget said LG’s humanoid home robot CLOiD capped LG’s keynote with demos that were “more theatrical than practical,” reflecting an ambitious vision that still felt a few years away [8].

Q: If you only follow one feed, where can you track rolling CES announcements?

A: Several outlets are running continuously updated hubs or live blogs for CES 2026, including Engadget’s running list of announcements and Wired’s live updates [7][6].

Action Items

  • Use CES as a shopping shortlist: Browse multi-outlet roundups to compare categories you care about (TVs, laptops, smart home, robots) and note which products are announced as shipping soon versus shown as concepts [7][10][5].
  • Learn the display buzzwords before buying a TV: Read an explainer on Micro RGB and how it compares with OLED/Mini LED so you can map marketing terms to real tradeoffs [7].
