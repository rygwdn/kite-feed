U.S. home prices hit record as June sales slow

Category: Business

U.S. existing-home sales fell 2.4% in June from May to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 4.09 million units, the National Association of Realtors said Thursday [2][6][7][8][9]. The result missed the roughly 4.21 million pace economists expected in a FactSet survey, but sales were up 2.8% from June 2025 [2][6][7][8]. The national median sales price for existing homes rose 1.8% from a year earlier to $440,600, the highest level in NAR data going back to 1999 [2][6][7][8][9]. Prices have risen year over year for 36 consecutive months. CBS News cited NAR figures showing a $446,400 median price for existing single-family homes and $380,000 for condominiums and co-ops [2][6][4][7]. Sales remained sluggish as mortgage rates rose after the start of the U.S.-Iran war raised inflation expectations tied to crude oil prices and pushed up long-term bond yields, which lenders use to price home loans [2][7][8]. Mortgage rates remained below their year-earlier level, according to the Associated Press report carried by PBS and ABC News [2][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Inventory: Available existing homes for sale fell 0.6% in June to 1.56 million, still below the pre-pandemic range of 1.8 million to 1.9 million [9].
  • Regional prices: Median single-family prices ranged from $346,600 in the Midwest to $633,600 in the West, with the Northeast at $564,800 and the South at $377,700 [4].

Perspectives

Lawrence Yun, National Association of Realtors: Yun said affordability remains the main obstacle for would-be homeowners and said the market needs more supply.

Sources: PBS NewsHour

Ershang Liang, PNC Economics Research: Liang said low affordability reflects slower wage growth combined with stronger home-price growth.

Sources: CBS News

National Association of Realtors: NAR buyer data showed first-time buyers accounted for 33% of June purchases, below their historical 40% share.

Sources: Bend Bulletin

Historical Background

Existing-home sales have hovered near an annual pace of 4 million since 2023, well below a historic norm closer to 5.2 million [2][6][7]. Low pandemic-era interest rates helped push home costs sharply higher after the Federal Reserve cut rates to support the economy, according to CBS News’ account of the market backdrop [4].

Business Angle

  • A persistent national housing shortage can support prices, but weak transaction volume limits fee revenue for brokers, mortgage originators and title insurers [9][common].
  • Homebuilders with access to land, labor and financing may benefit from unmet demand if they can sell homes below prevailing resale prices [9][common].
  • High mortgage rates make homes less affordable and can delay turnover-dependent spending on furniture, appliances and renovations [2][6][common].
