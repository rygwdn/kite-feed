Bank of England raises rate to 4%, warns more hikes may follow

Category: Business

The Bank of England raised its key interest rate from 3.75% to 4% on Thursday, its first increase since August 2023, as the Iran war drives energy costs higher and threatens to prolong Britain’s inflation shock [8][3][6]. The increase followed a period of rate cuts and holds, with inflation reaching 3.5% in September, above the Bank’s 2% target [10][28]. Eight of the nine members of the Monetary Policy Committee backed the quarter-point increase; Catherine Mann wanted a larger rise, to 4.25% [9][23]. The Bank forecasts that inflation will peak at 4.9% in the second quarter of 2027, then fall to its 2% target in the third quarter of 2028. It also raised its growth forecasts to 1.6% this year and 1.3% next year [4][10][11]. It expects real post-tax household income to fall this year and next [23]. Governor Andrew Bailey said future decisions would depend on inflation risks and developments in the Middle East. He said the fall in energy prices over the 48 hours before the decision would need to last to alter the outlook [8][4]. The Bank’s next rate decision is scheduled for Dec. 17 [4].

Today’s increase shouldn’t be seen as the first in an inevitable long sequence of hikes. But, unless the conflict in the Middle East is resolved, neither does it seem likely to be the last increase.

— Andrew Bailey, Bank of England governor (Associated Press)

Key Points

  • Mortgage costs: The increase immediately raises payments for about 510,000 tracker-mortgage borrowers by an estimated £30.72 a month on a typical £147,000 balance; roughly 470,000 standard-variable-rate borrowers could pay about £14.28 more if lenders pass on the full rise [25].
  • Fixed-rate deals: About 87% of Britain’s nearly 8.5 million residential mortgages are fixed-rate, but around 2.3 million fixed deals expire in 2027, leaving many borrowers facing new rates [25].
  • Oil-price risk: In a severe scenario with oil reaching about $130 a barrel, the Bank projects inflation could hit 6.2% in the third quarter of 2027, compared with its central forecast of about 4.9% [23][1].
  • Budget measures: The government’s package includes a £100 energy payment for 20 million households, an extended electricity-bill tax cut and a fuel-duty freeze; the Bank estimates the measures will lower inflation by 0.25 percentage points at its 2027 peak [4][15].
  • Policy debate: Alan Taylor described the increase as a risk-management move and said further tightening would require stronger evidence of spillover into wages and prices [23][4].

Perspectives

Catherine Mann, Bank of England Monetary Policy Committee member: Mann warned that confidence in the Bank’s control of inflation could weaken [23].

Business Angle

  • Companies with variable-rate borrowing face higher financing costs as the Bank tightens policy [26][8].
  • The Bank cited AI-related investment as one contributor to stronger near-term output [1][23].

Q&A

Q: What are markets expecting after the December decision?

A: The Bank’s Market Participants Survey put the median expectation at 4% in November and 4.25% in February, though the Bank said its decisions are not on a preset path [6][5].

Action Items

  • Check how your mortgage responds to changes in the Bank’s key rate: Confirm whether you have a tracker, standard-variable-rate or fixed-rate mortgage. Tracker payments rise with the Bank’s rate, while fixed payments generally stay unchanged until the deal ends [25][26].
  • Compare savings accounts: Check whether your provider has passed on the rate increase and compare available easy-access or fixed-term rates. Providers may pass on only part or none of the increase [26][25].
