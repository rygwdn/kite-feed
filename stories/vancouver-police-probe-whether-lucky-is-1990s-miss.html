Vancouver police probe whether ‘Lucky’ is 1990s missing Canadian

Category: Canada

Vancouver police are working with Dutch authorities and Interpol to determine whether an unidentified man in the Netherlands known as “Lucky” is a Canadian who disappeared in British Columbia in the 1990s [1][3][4]. Amsterdam police said the man collapsed on an Amsterdam street on May 10, 2023, after a suspected brain hemorrhage or stroke; he had no identification and has aphasia, leaving him unable to tell investigators his name or address [1][5][8]. The case drew attention after Amsterdam police issued a public appeal and online commenters noted a possible resemblance between Lucky and John Russell Kenny, an Ontario-born man reported missing after he disappeared from Vancouver in the 1990s. Kenny would now be about 67 [1][4][6][7]. Global News reported that Amsterdam police received more than 500 tips after the appeal; Vancouver police said the investigation is ongoing and they will provide an update if they make a positive identification [5][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Many aliases: People in Amsterdam knew the man by several names, including Lucky, Louis, Lee, Ponytail, Sonny, Chris the Canadian and John [4][8].
  • Local clues: Staff at Soupenzo on Waterlooplein recognized him as “Chris the Canadian” and said he often came in with a suitcase, ordered special soup variations and had money [8].
  • Cold-case work: Amsterdam police said two retired officers volunteering with the department’s cold-case team took over the effort to reconstruct the man’s life [1].
  • Kenny file: Vancouver police records described John Russell Kenny as 34 when his family reported him missing and said he had mental-health issues and a transient lifestyle [4].

Perspectives

Amsterdam police: Spokesperson Marijke Stor said investigators have tried several ways to communicate with Lucky but still cannot determine who he is or how much he understands.

The current inquiry seeks to determine whether an unidentified patient in the Netherlands is Kenny, rather than investigate a criminal allegation [8].
