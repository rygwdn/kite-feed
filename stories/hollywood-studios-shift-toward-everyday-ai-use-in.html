Hollywood studios shift toward everyday AI use in filmmaking

Category: AI

The Economist reported that AI is entering filmmaking, and a syndicated version named Netflix, Amazon and actors among those tied to the shift [3][4]. WIRED’s interview with Puck’s Matthew Belloni said AI has quietly become part of everyday filmmaking, rather than a future question for the industry [2]. Belloni described the dispute as less about whether Hollywood will use AI than who will control its next stage [2]. The West Australian’s syndicated Economist article said the technology is already playing a role across filmmaking, while Google News listed the Economist story under the headline “Hollywood is entering its AI era” [4][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Historical Background

Hollywood’s AI labor disputes moved into public view during the 2023 writers’ and actors’ strikes, when unions sought limits on AI use in scripts, likenesses and creative work [common]. The current reporting focuses on AI use moving from contract bargaining into production workflows [2][4].

Business Angle

  • Streaming platforms such as Netflix and Amazon have scale that could make AI-driven production efficiencies more valuable if deployed across large content pipelines [4][common].
  • Labor negotiations remain a risk because actors and other creative workers are directly affected by how AI gets used in production [4][common].
