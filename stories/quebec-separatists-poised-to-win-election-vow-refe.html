Quebec separatists poised to win election, vow referendum in first term

Category: Canada

Quebec’s Parti Québécois is poised to win Monday’s provincial election, with leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon pledging an independence referendum in his first term if he becomes premier [1][3]. Quebec voters rejected independence in a 1995 referendum [1]. The party’s expected gains do not necessarily reflect broad support for independence: voters want change, but independence remains a harder sell, The New York Times reported [1]. St-Pierre Plamondon once criticized the PQ’s focus on another referendum and proposed waiting until a second term unless 20% of Quebecers demanded one. He later changed course [3]. Quebec votes on Oct. 5 [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Earlier politics: St-Pierre Plamondon donated $2,300 to the federal Liberal Party from 2004 to 2008 and described himself as a disappointed federalist during his first PQ leadership bid [3].
  • Party setbacks: The PQ won only three seats in the 2022 election, its worst result ever, but St-Pierre Plamondon remained leader [3].
  • Campaign rationale: A trusted aide said St-Pierre Plamondon’s shift toward an earlier referendum partly reflected perceived setbacks for Quebec’s language and culture during the Trudeau years [3].

Perspectives

Paul St-Pierre Plamondon: He argues that independence is necessary to protect Quebec’s linguistic and cultural future and says his earlier federalist views were not unusual among PQ leaders [3].
