OpenAI chief argues scarce RAM belongs in AI data centers

Category: Technology

OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman argued that scarce computer memory, or DRAM, should be concentrated in data centers rather than personal computers, saying home PCs use their memory only about 5% of the time. In an interview with Stripe co-founder John Collison on the Cheeky Pint podcast, published Oct. 29, he said cloud systems could use shared memory more intensively [2][1][8]. Altman did not tell consumers to stop buying RAM, but his comments drew criticism as AI companies compete with device makers for limited memory supplies [12][10]. The shortage has pushed up consumer electronics costs as memory makers direct more capacity toward data-center contracts. Apple has raised prices on some Macs and iPads, The Mac Observer reported [2]. Altman provided no data to support his 5% estimate. Local processing can offer offline access, lower latency and more control over sensitive information [2][5]. SK Hynix has warned that memory shortages could last beyond 2030 [7].

Primary Source: View original article

But from a global allocation perspective, actually, it’s quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world’s limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer.

— Sam Altman, OpenAI chief executive (The Mac Observer)

Key Points

  • Supply outlook: Samsung has reportedly secured long-term deals covering 75% to 80% of its projected 2027 memory-chip output, while analysts cited by VnExpress expect shortages to last at least through 2028 [8].
  • Consumer costs: Prices for some high-capacity DDR5 memory kits in Japan rose as much as 761% over the past year, according to Wccftech [5].

Perspectives

Consumers wary of cloud dependence: Some fear that an AI future centered on cloud services could leave users dependent on subscriptions and platforms controlled by a small number of large technology companies [7].

Sources: ITavisen

Acer’s chief executive accused memory manufacturers of seeking to prolong the shortage to maximize profits and said prices could begin falling as soon as next year [4].

Sources: TechSpot

Timeline

  • 28 October 2026: ITavisen reported SK Hynix’s warning of shortages beyond 2030 [7]
  • 29 October 2026: Cheeky Pint published Altman’s interview with John Collison [2]
  • 31 October 2026: Technology coverage detailed consumer criticism of Altman’s remarks [1][2]

Technical Details

  • Local inference: A trained AI model runs on a device’s own CPU, GPU or neural processing unit instead of sending prompts to a cloud server [8].
  • Unified memory: The Surface Laptop Ultra is designed with up to 128GB of unified memory, which can support running large models on the device [7].

Industry Impact

  • Gaming hardware: Sony raised the PS5 Pro’s official price in Germany by €100 in March because of the RAM shortage. Used units were reportedly listed for up to $1,399 [6].
  • Memory supply: Data-center companies are signing long-term supply deals. Micron has 26 strategic agreements covering more than 35% of its projected revenue through 2030, with $32 billion in customer deposits, VnExpress reported [8].
