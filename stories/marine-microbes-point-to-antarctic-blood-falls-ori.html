Marine microbes point to Antarctic Blood Falls origin

Category: Science

A study published Monday in Nature Geoscience reports that the salty water feeding Antarctica’s Blood Falls contains a distinct community of marine microorganisms, providing molecular and genetic evidence that the brine beneath Taylor Glacier originated as seawater [2][3][5][6]. Blood Falls flows from the eastern edge of Taylor Glacier in the McMurdo Dry Valleys into Lake Bonney, where iron-rich brine turns red-orange after dissolved iron reacts with oxygen at the surface [2][5][7]. Researchers sampled water, sediment and air around the site and found that organisms in the red ice were more closely linked to marine environments, while surrounding areas carried microbes more typical of freshwater settings [3]. The results support the hypothesis that seawater flooded Taylor Valley during past warm periods, then became trapped beneath the advancing glacier after sea levels fell [3][5]. The researchers said the timing of that trapping remains unclear [2].

Did you know: The brine beneath Taylor Glacier may have been trapped for at least 1.5 million years, according to some earlier estimates [7].

Key Points

  • Broad sampling: The team analyzed bacteria, viruses and fungi from water, sediment and air around Blood Falls, rather than only the brine [3].
  • Microbial focus: Scientists have studied bacteria and archaea at Blood Falls for years, but eukaryotic microbes there have received less attention [7].

Perspectives

Study authors: The authors wrote that the Blood Falls area had a dominance of marine eukaryotic lineages compared with the broader McMurdo Dry Valleys region.

Sources: Live Science

Andrew E. Allen: The UC San Diego microbial ecologist and study co-author said the diverse microbial community retains a clear marine signature, indicating the system was once connected to the ocean.

Sources: Popular Science

Historical Background

Geologist Thomas Griffith Taylor discovered Blood Falls during an Antarctic expedition in 1911 [7]. Scientists initially suspected algae caused the red color [7].
