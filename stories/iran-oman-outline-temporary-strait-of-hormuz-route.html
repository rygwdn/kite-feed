Iran, Oman outline temporary Strait of Hormuz route

Category: World

Iran and Oman said after talks in Tehran on Aug. 25 that they discussed a phased framework for ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, including a “joint temporary navigational corridor” and a joint mine-clearing project in the waterway [1][7][13][20][24][34]. The meeting brought together Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and Omani Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi as their governments seek arrangements for a waterway both countries border [13][20][29]. Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the arrangement with Oman did not mean the strait had reopened. Tehran tied full transit to U.S. steps including a return to commitments under a June memorandum, lifting what Iran calls an economic blockade, and ending the war on all fronts [7][2][8][9][15]. President Trump said all mines in the strait’s international waters had been cleared or detonated and warned that ships laying new mines would be destroyed [15][51][30]. The Strait of Hormuz handled about one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas shipments before the war began in February, and most shipping has been shut down since then [12][14][16][32][56]. Passage remains hazardous: the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said an oil tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile and disabled about 9 nautical miles, or 17 km, northeast of Oman’s Ash Shishah at the entrance to the strait [10][32].

Technical Details

  • Temporary route geometry: Gharibabadi said inbound traffic from the Gulf of Oman to the Persian Gulf would run entirely through Iranian waters, while outbound traffic would use both Iranian and Omani waters [13][20][59].
  • Route notification: Iran said it would close the southern route and report the change to the International Maritime Organization, the UN agency that receives ship-routing and maritime safety notifications [7][common].

Historical Background

The June U.S.-Iran memorandum included provisions for regulating Hormuz transit, and accounts linked to Iran point to Article 5 as the clause covering safe commercial passage and consultation with Oman on future management and maritime services [57][54]. The Strait of Hormuz connects the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea, making it the main sea outlet for several Gulf energy exporters [common]. Oman has often acted as an intermediary in U.S.-Iran diplomacy, including talks surrounding nuclear and regional security disputes [common].
