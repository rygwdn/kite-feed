Trump administration ends Minnesota immigration-enforcement surge, Homan says

Category: World

The Trump administration is winding down “Operation Metro Surge,” a major federal immigration-enforcement deployment centered on the Minneapolis–St. Paul area, after border czar Tom Homan said he recommended ending it and President Trump agreed [1][2][15][18]. Homan said agents will scale back through next week, while federal authorities say immigration enforcement will continue nationwide [4][19][16]. The operation led to thousands of arrests and became a flashpoint after mass protests and the killing of two US citizens during the crackdown [5][18][1][19]. Officials have said the effort focused on “dangerous criminal illegal aliens,” but other reporting says many people without criminal records—including children and US citizens—were detained, fueling criticism of the tactics [3][18][19].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Arrest totals: Federal authorities said the Minneapolis–St. Paul sweeps led to more than 4,000 arrests during the surge [5][13].
  • Deaths named: Reporting identified the two US citizens killed during the period as Renee Good and Alex Pretti, and their deaths intensified criticism of the operation [19][18].
  • Broader pullbacks: Semafor reported the Minnesota drawdown came amid a wider federal pullback that included a quiet withdrawal of National Guard troops from Chicago, Los Angeles, and Portland, citing The Washington Post [16].
  • Political pressure: The Wall Street Journal, cited by Semafor, said the crackdowns became a political liability for Republicans ahead of the midterm elections, as polling showed Americans souring on maximalist enforcement tactics [16].
  • Next target unclear: Homan said officers could be deployed to another location but gave no details, as speculation swirled about which city might be targeted next [19].

Perspectives

Tom Homan (White House border czar): He said he recommended ending the surge and Trump agreed, adding that a significant drawdown was already under way and would continue into next week. He also said officers could be deployed elsewhere, but did not say where.

Timeline

  • early December: White House launched Minnesota operation later dubbed “Operation Metro Surge” [6][18].
  • last week: Administration ordered withdrawal of 700 federal agents from the city [6].
  • Thursday: Homan said President Trump agreed to end the surge [1][15][18].
  • next week: Officials said agents will return home or be reassigned [4][19][15].
