SpaceX set to launch ISS mission carrying health research and solar arrays

Category: Science

SpaceX is scheduled to launch its CRS-35 cargo mission to the International Space Station from Cape Canaveral at 6:33 a.m. EDT on Oct. 13, carrying about 6,300 pounds of supplies, the final pair of station roll-out solar arrays and scientific equipment [1][4][6]. If the launch proceeds as planned, the Dragon capsule is due to dock with the station on Oct. 15 and remain for about a month [1][4]. NASA’s research payload includes a five-week study of lab-grown brain models, research on disease-infected plants and antibiotic-resistant bacteria, and the first station test of a compact device that measures health-related biomarkers [6]. Researchers will compare brain and plant samples grown in space with Earth-based controls. Astronauts will also collect samples from station surfaces during the bacteria investigation’s fourth and final planned stay [6]. The experiments study astronaut health, space agriculture and questions with potential applications on Earth, NASA said [6].

We also have a wealth of science experiments on board, including studies to manufacture artificial retinas, understand how microgravity affects the growth of 3D heart tissue models, study how glass forms when made with non-traditional elements, and better understand how inflammation is linked to neurodegenerative diseases.

— Bill Spetch (Spaceflight Now)

Key Points

  • Space crops can supply food, absorb carbon dioxide, produce oxygen and recycle water—functions NASA says will become more important as missions extend farther from Earth [6].
  • The compact reader could help crews detect infections and other health issues promptly in locations where resupply is difficult [6].
  • Station maintenance: The cargo also includes equipment housing a failed Canadarm2 joint removed during a June 30 spacewalk, along with replacement and maintenance hardware [4].

Timeline

  • 8 October 2026: Crew-12 returned to Earth, vacating the station’s Harmony docking port [4]
  • 13 October 2026: Falcon 9 CRS-35 launch scheduled from Cape Canaveral [4][6]
  • 15 October 2026: Dragon scheduled to dock at the station’s Harmony module [1][4]

Technical Details

  • Dragon C211 is making its fourth cargo flight; its previous runs were CRS-26, CRS-29 and CRS-33 [4].
  • The Falcon 9 first-stage booster B1096 is making its eighth launch, after previously flying NASA’s IMAP, CRS-34, NROL-77, NROL-95, GPS-III 9, Kuiper Falcon 01 and Starlink 6-87 missions [4].

Scientific Significance

  • Human health: The brain-model work could help researchers develop methods to counter brain-health risks astronauts may face on future missions to the Moon and beyond [6].
  • Space agriculture: Findings could guide breeding of disease-resistant crops and help reduce yield losses from fungal disease on Earth [6].
  • Bacterial resistance: The research could help develop ways to protect crews from infection on long missions and clarify how antibiotic overuse contributes to superbugs on Earth [6].
