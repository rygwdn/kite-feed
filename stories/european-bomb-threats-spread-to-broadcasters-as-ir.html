European bomb threats spread to broadcasters as Ireland cites likely campaign

Category: World

Bomb threats spread from flights to broadcasters on Friday, prompting evacuations in Ireland and Bulgaria as aircraft across Europe again received threats. Ireland’s government said the threats were likely part of a coordinated campaign of disruption, but no explosives or injuries were reported in the incidents [19][12][20][4]. The alerts extended a week of hoaxes that have triggered diversions, inspections and fighter-jet escorts. Emails bearing the name “Mikhail Andropov” went to Irish public broadcaster RTÉ and Bulgaria’s Nova TV; British broadcaster Sky also reported a security threat. RTÉ evacuated staff and suspended programming before police cleared its premises. On Friday, a Wizz Air flight from Prague to Rome diverted to Venice, while Hungarian fighter jets escorted three passenger planes through the country’s airspace [12][19][17][4]. Investigators have not identified the sender or established whether the threats to newsrooms and airlines were connected [20][24]. The European Commission activated an information-sharing mechanism among EU countries, and European security experts are due to meet in Brussels next week to discuss responses [21][4]. Ireland is also preparing a significant security operation for next week’s European Political Community and EU leaders’ meetings in Dublin [28].

Key Points

  • Traffic share: Eurocontrol said it was aware of threats against 14 flights on Thursday—0.05% of nearly 26,300 flights in its network—and seven on Tuesday [4][28].
  • Prague routes: Prague Airport said nine flights were targeted Friday: four arriving from Denmark, Spain and Britain, and five departing for the Netherlands, Cyprus, Romania, Italy and Britain [60].
  • Broadcast disruption: RTÉ replaced scheduled programming with music and recorded messages for up to three hours, while Nova TV evacuated its newsroom but continued broadcasting [16][65].
  • Attribution debate: European security agencies are investigating whether the threats originated in Russia; some intelligence officials consider that plausible, but say attribution remains uncertain and could take time [24].
  • Threat warning: An email to broadcasters warned of further attacks on transport, but investigators have not verified the sender’s claim of responsibility [25][12].
