OpenAI launches GPT-5.5 for coding and research tasks

Category: Technology

OpenAI on April 23 introduced GPT-5.5, a new flagship model aimed less at simple chatbot replies and more at handling multi-step work across coding, research, data analysis, and everyday computer use [1][3][7]. Across the coverage, the company said the model can plan, use tools, verify results, and keep going through loosely defined assignments with less user guidance, reflecting a broader push toward more autonomous, agent-style AI systems [1][3][6][4]. The rollout spans ChatGPT and Codex users on selected paid tiers. GPT-5.5 Thinking is broadly available to Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users, while GPT-5.5 Pro is reserved for Pro, Business, and Enterprise users in ChatGPT. Codex access extends to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Edu, and Go plans [5][7]. The launch also arrives as competition among frontier AI developers speeds up. CNET grouped GPT-5.5 alongside new releases from DeepSeek and Anthropic, while Nvidia-backed deployment details underscored the cost and efficiency gains needed to make heavier enterprise use more practical [2][9][10].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Nvidia said Blackwell-based systems increased token output per second per megawatt by 50 times versus prior-generation hardware, a reminder that AI progress now depends as much on power efficiency as on model design [9][10].

Key Points

  • Long context: In Codex, GPT-5.5 comes with a 400K context window for selected paid users, giving developers more room to work across larger codebases and longer task histories [5].
  • Enterprise trial: Nvidia said it gave early GPT-5.5 Codex access to 10,000 employees on GB200 NVL72 systems, which it said delivered 35x lower cost per million tokens and 50x higher token output per second per megawatt than prior-generation hardware [9][10].

Perspectives

OpenAI: The company framed GPT-5.5 as a model for 'real work,' saying it can stick with ambiguous, messy assignments, decide what to do next, and keep operating across tools with less hand-holding from users.

Timeline

  • 24 April 2026: Paid ChatGPT and Codex tier availability details were reported [5][7]
  • 24 April 2026: CNET linked GPT-5.5 to new releases from rivals [2]

Technical Details

  • 400K context window: Developer Tech reported a 400K-token context window in Codex, meant to help the model track larger repositories, longer instructions, and multi-step state across extended sessions [5].
  • GB200 NVL72 inference: TechSpot and TechRadar said Codex on Nvidia's GB200 NVL72 systems achieved 35x lower cost per million tokens and 50x higher token output per second per megawatt, metrics that matter because frontier models can become uneconomic when inference costs stay high [9][10].

Industry Impact

  • Developer tools: GPT-5.5 is rolling into Codex across Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Edu, and Go plans, signaling that coding assistants are becoming a standard feature across paid developer tiers rather than niche add-ons [5][7].
  • Enterprise AI infrastructure: Nvidia's report that 10,000 employees used early-access GPT-5.5 Codex on GB200 NVL72 hardware shows how model launches are increasingly tied to specialized data-center deployments and energy-efficiency metrics [9][10].
  • Model competition: CNET's side-by-side coverage of OpenAI, DeepSeek, and Anthropic highlights a market where major labs are releasing new frontier models in quick succession, increasing pressure on vendors to differentiate on capability, safety, and cost [2].

Scientific Significance

  • Research assistance: Several reports said OpenAI is positioning GPT-5.5 as useful for early-stage scientific research, including work involving large datasets and hypothesis generation, suggesting the company sees practical value in AI systems that can sequence subtasks rather than only return text answers [7][4].
  • Evidence limits: The available coverage includes product claims, but not peer-reviewed validation, controlled comparisons for scientific tasks, or error-rate data for research use, so confidence in those science-oriented claims remains limited based on these sources alone [3][4].

Historical Background

Generative AI products first reached broad mainstream adoption after ChatGPT launched in late 2022 [common]. Since then, major model makers have moved from conversational assistants toward systems that can use external tools and complete longer workflows, a shift reflected in OpenAI's Codex efforts and in competing releases from Anthropic and DeepSeek highlighted in this coverage [2][5]. Nvidia's growing role also fits a longer trend: advances in AI capability depend heavily on more efficient inference hardware and data-center scaling [9][10].

Action Items

  • Check your plan access: If you use ChatGPT or Codex for work, verify whether your subscription tier includes GPT-5.5 Thinking, GPT-5.5 Pro, or Codex access before redesigning workflows around the new model [5][7].
  • Pilot a bounded workflow: Test GPT-5.5 on one contained job, such as bug tracing, spreadsheet preparation, or research synthesis, so you can measure whether reduced hand-holding actually saves time in your setup [1][3].
