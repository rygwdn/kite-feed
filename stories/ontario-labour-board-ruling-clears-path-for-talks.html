Ontario labour board ruling clears path for talks as elementary teachers vote 97% for strike mandate

Category: Canada

Ontario’s labour board has ruled that several issues can remain in local bargaining, clearing the way for stalled contract talks to resume as the province’s public elementary teachers and education workers approved strike mandates with 97% support in each vote. The Elementary Teachers’ Federation of Ontario (ETFO), which represents about 84,000 members, said the votes do not mean a strike is imminent and that it remains focused on negotiated agreements [7][9][6]. ETFO said it wants smaller classes, stronger health and safety protections, job security and fair compensation. Bargaining at the union’s two central tables is expected to resume at the end of November [9][4]. The labour board rejected the government’s effort to move several matters from local to central bargaining, including short-term unpaid leave and transfer, surplus and redundancy timelines, according to ETFO. Education Minister Paul Calandra said the vote results could help bring the parties to the table, while ETFO President David Mastin said they reflected frustration over underfunding and deteriorating school conditions [7].

These strong strike mandates reflect the growing challenges educators face every day in schools and worksites across Ontario and underscore our members’ solidarity and support for ETFO’s bargaining team.

Key Points

  • Local issues: ETFO said local union leave and preparation-time payback will remain in local bargaining [7].
  • French-language vote: The Association des enseignantes et des enseignants franco-ontariens announced that 96% of its members voted in favour of a strike mandate [1].
  • Strike status: ETFO said its members are not yet in a legal strike position; a strike mandate alone does not authorize a walkout [7][6].
  • Other unions: English Catholic teachers and public high-school teachers had also voted for strike mandates earlier in the week, with support reported at 98% and 96%, respectively [1][8].
