US, South Korea cut Ulchi drills to five days

Category: World

South Korea and the United States will end the annual Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise on August 21, shortening the drill from 11 days to five, Seoul's Joint Chiefs of Staff and the U.S. Defense Department said [6][3][51][15]. The exercise began on August 17 and had been scheduled to run through August 27. Seoul said Washington requested the change, and the allies also agreed to reduce some joint field training while they continue discussing specific changes [51][21][11]. President Trump ordered the Pentagon to sharply reduce U.S. participation shortly before the exercise began, citing the cost of the drills, his relationship with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un and South Korea's refusal to join the U.S. war against Iran [10][3][14][15]. A Pentagon official said the United States had reduced associated live-training events, canceling some or turning others into simulations, but said the changes would preserve essential readiness and would not degrade U.S. training objectives [15][51]. North Korea's state-run KCNA criticized the ongoing drills on August 19 as a "most immediate and open threat" to North Korea and regional security, without mentioning Trump's order [14][21][102]. The exercises are designed to prepare the allies for a possible North Korean attack and include a largely computer-simulated command-post component [4][11][10].

Historical Background

The U.S.-South Korea alliance dates to the 1950-53 Korean War, and the allies' large joint exercises have long been a key way to prepare combined operations against potential North Korean aggression [56][78]. During Trump's first term, his direct diplomacy with Kim Jong Un led to the suspension or redesign of several major exercises, including the 2018 Ulchi Freedom Guardian [67]. After denuclearization talks collapsed in Hanoi, North Korea expanded its missile and nuclear capabilities [67].
