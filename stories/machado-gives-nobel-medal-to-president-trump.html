Machado gives Nobel medal to President Trump

Category: World

Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado handed her 2025 Nobel Peace Prize medal to President Donald Trump during a White House meeting, saying she did so on behalf of Venezuelans and because she believes he “deserves it” [6][3]. The Nobel institution responded that while a medal can change hands, the Nobel Peace Prize title and historical record remain “inseparably” linked to the original laureate [6][9][10][8]. The gesture landed in the middle of fast-moving Venezuela politics after a U.S. raid captured President Nicolás Maduro and brought him to the United States to face narco-trafficking charges, raising questions about how (and by whom) Venezuela will be governed next [6][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Venezuela is going to be free

— María Corina Machado (Time)

Did you know: The Nobel Committee noted that even if a Nobel medal or diploma later comes into someone else’s possession, it does not alter who was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize [6].

Key Points

  • Norway pushback: Politicians in Norway criticized the handover, prompting a pointed Nobel Committee clarification that the laureate and prize are inseparable even if the medal, diploma, or prize money later ends up elsewhere [6][10].
  • Trump’s rationale: Asked about accepting the medal, President Trump argued he had “ended 8 wars,” framing the moment as recognition of his peace efforts [3].
  • Machado’s pitch: After the meeting, Machado said an “orderly transition” would make Venezuela “the best ally the United States has ever had,” and she framed the choice for Washington as “between the cartel and justice,” not between specific politicians [4].
  • Interim governance: Despite Machado’s democracy message, Trump has backed interim President Delcy Rodríguez and other former Maduro loyalists as a short-term governing option that officials describe as the most stable path to maintain order and cooperate on energy priorities [7].
  • Not first medal: Nobel medals have been donated, sold, or offered for sale by past winners or their families, even though the Nobel Prize itself is not transferable [6].

Perspectives

María Corina Machado: She says gifting the medal recognizes Trump’s commitment to Venezuela’s freedom and signals the start of a difficult but “orderly transition” toward democracy and elections, with Venezuela aiming to become a close U.S. ally.

Sources: Time

President Donald Trump: He portrayed accepting the medal as justified by his record of ending wars and has spoken frequently about securing Venezuelan oil in the wake of Maduro’s capture.

Sources: NDTV

Norwegian Nobel Committee: It stressed that the Nobel Peace Prize remains linked to the named laureate in perpetuity, regardless of who possesses the physical medal, diploma, or prize money.

Sources: Deutsche Welle

U.S. administration officials (as described): They argue interim President Delcy Rodríguez offers the most stable near-term governance to keep order and ensure cooperation, including on Venezuela’s energy resources.

Sources: Time

Commentary writers: Some columnists and analysts describe the exchange as symbolically lopsided: Trump gets a medal without laureate status, while Machado holds the Nobel title without governing power or a U.S. endorsement to lead Venezuela.

Timeline

  • Jan. 3: U.S. raid removed Nicolás Maduro and sent him to U.S. custody [7].
  • Thursday: Machado gifted her 2025 Nobel Peace Prize medal to Trump [6].
  • Friday: Nobel Committee reiterated prize remains tied to the laureate [6][10].

Technical Details

  • Non-transferability of Nobel Prize: The Nobel institution says the laureate and the prize remain inseparable; even if the medal, diploma, or money changes hands, the official Nobel record does not [6][9][10].
  • Medal vs. title: Sources draw a practical distinction: the medal is a physical object that can be gifted, while “laureate” status is a fixed designation maintained by the prize institution [6][8].

Industry Impact

  • Democracy advocacy: The Nobel Committee’s statement reinforces that awards are meant to preserve historical attribution, limiting attempts to reframe or “transfer” prestige through physical possession of medals [6][9].
  • Energy stakeholders: U.S. backing for an interim Venezuelan leadership described as stability-focused, alongside repeated attention to oil, signals that energy security considerations will likely influence diplomatic choices around Venezuela’s transition [7].

Historical Background

The Nobel Peace Prize is awarded by the Norwegian Nobel Committee, and Nobel rules treat the prize as inseparable from the laureate’s identity even if the physical items (medal, diploma, or money) change hands [6][9]. The committee’s statement echoed past instances in which Nobel medals were donated, sold, or otherwise transferred, without changing who history records as the recipient [6].

Q&A

Q: What practical role can Machado play if interim leaders run Venezuela short-term?

A: She said she sees herself as one person within a broader movement and expects her movement to help shape Venezuela’s future after an “orderly transition” [4].

Q: Does the Nobel Committee have power to reclaim a gifted medal?

A: The committee’s public position focuses on attribution—stating the prize remains tied to the laureate—rather than describing any process to recover a medal once it is transferred [6][9].
