Google opens AI media watermark checker to public worldwide

Category: AI

Google has opened its SynthID Detector website to the public worldwide, allowing users to check images, video and audio for invisible watermarks indicating AI generation or editing [3][4]. The tool was previously available to selected journalists, media professionals and researchers for testing [4][1]. The checker detects SynthID watermarks embedded by Google and its partners OpenAI, Nvidia and Kakao; Apple support is planned [4][12]. It is not a universal AI detector: it cannot identify media created with systems that do not use SynthID, and a negative result does not establish that content is human-made [5][12]. The site is available in English and requires users to sign in with a Google, Apple or OpenAI account [1][12]. Google also offers SynthID checks through Search, Gemini and Chrome, and says those integrated features receive more than 1 million verification requests a day [11][16].

Did you know: Google says SynthID-marked audio already amounts to about 240,000 years of sound [3].

Key Points

  • Separate standards: Microsoft and Meta use their own watermarking and verification systems, so their AI-generated media may not register with SynthID [4][12].
  • File support: The checker accepts common image types including JPG, PNG, HEIC and TIFF, video formats including MP4 and MOV, and audio formats including WAV and MP3 [4].
  • Supported formats: The portal accepts common image formats, including JPG, PNG, HEIC and GIF; video formats MP4, MOV and WEBM; and audio formats including WAV, MP3, AAC and M4A [4][35].
  • Signal design: Google says the watermark is designed to withstand common changes such as cropping, filters, frame-rate changes and lossy compression [1][15].
  • Watermark scale: Google says SynthID has marked more than 180 billion images and videos and 240,000 hours of audio [1].
  • Usage-limit rationale: Android Headlines reports that the daily scan cap is intended to make it harder to develop watermark-removal scripts through repeated checks [14].
  • Data handling: The service stores a digital signature of uploaded media for 24 hours to enforce daily quotas and defend against automated attacks, then deletes it [1].
  • Adoption: Google says it has applied SynthID to more than 180 billion images and videos [3][11].
  • Other provenance checks: Engadget says Google's verification tools also check C2PA content credentials, a provenance standard separate from SynthID [12].

Technical Details

  • Embedded watermark: SynthID embeds a watermark in media as it is generated, rather than relying only on file metadata; Google says the watermark is designed to remain detectable after cropping, filtering and compression [1][7][10].
