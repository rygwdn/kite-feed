Fossils trace oxygen’s role and early human movement

Category: Science

A study comparing fossil limb bones identified differences in movement between Australopithecus and early Homo. Australopithecus combined upright walking with substantial time in trees, while early Homo had relatively stronger thigh bones than arm bones, consistent with more ground-based movement [1]. Separately, researchers analyzed more than 12,000 microscopic fossils from ancient rocks in northern Australia and found early eukaryotes in oxygenated settings ranging from coastal mudflats to the open sea, but not in oxygen-free samples. The fossils date from about 1.75 billion to 1.4 billion years ago; the study appeared in Nature [3][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • The Australian microfossils came from mudstone drill cores collected decades ago by mineral exploration companies and held by the Northern Territory Geological Survey [3][4].

Scientific Significance

  • Early eukaryotic fossils occurred across oxygenated marine environments and were absent from oxygen-free samples [3][4].
