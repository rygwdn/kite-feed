OpenAI's proposal for shared AI limits faces measurement hurdles

Category: AI

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki’s proposal for voluntary limits on advanced AI development depends on whether perplexity can serve as a common yardstick across labs. He wants leading developers to agree on capability thresholds they would not cross until they meet specific safety milestones for monitoring and understanding model behavior [10][21]. OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman endorsed the call, but the proposal has not become an industry agreement [10][12]. Pachocki published OpenAI’s internal log-perplexity figures alongside the proposal: GPT-3.5 scored about 0.99, GPT-4 about 0.91, and newer models fell below 0.8. He linked lower scores to gains in mathematics and cybersecurity [thedecoder.com#1][14]. He warns that AI could help accelerate research into training methods as early as 2027, potentially outpacing improvements in safety monitoring [26][21]. The measure is not directly comparable across companies that use different training data, evaluation sets and tokenizers, and it does not capture every capability gain from post-training reasoning [14][38]. Pachocki named no initial cutoff or start date, and no other developer has publicly signed on [12][18].

Key Points

  • No commitment: The proposal calls for an initial shared limit and defined conditions for lifting it, but names no enforcement body or outside verifier [12][38].
  • Metric floor: Pachocki says the metric’s lower bound is unknown; as scores approach it, smaller changes could accompany larger capability gains [12][26].
  • Progress drivers: OpenAI attributes the sharper decline in its measurements during 2026 mainly to long-running research and investment in computing, with automation contributing less [3].
  • Safety standards: Pachocki also called for shared evaluation norms, safety red-team reviews before first use and common transparency standards [10][44].

Perspectives

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki: Pachocki says releasing OpenAI’s internal pretraining figures gives competitors a clearer picture of the company’s progress, but considers the urgency of shared safety standards more important than that competitive cost.

Technical Details

  • Held-out evaluation: OpenAI scored models against a fixed snapshot of its codebase that it excluded from training. A consistent test helps track changes within OpenAI’s model series, but does not establish a shared benchmark across labs [12].

Q&A

Q: What safety milestone could let labs move beyond an agreed limit?

A: Pachocki gave a successor to current chain-of-thought monitoring systems as one possible milestone, but did not define a specific test or standard [12][26].
