Israel says it destroyed Hezbollah tunnels as 4.1 tremor hits Lebanon

Category: World

Israel said Thursday it destroyed Hezbollah underground infrastructure beneath the Ali al-Taher ridge in southern Lebanon, after taking operational control of the strategic area above and below ground [2][5][12][14]. The U.S. Geological Survey recorded a magnitude 4.1 tremor in the area, and Lebanon’s state-run National News Agency said Israel carried out a “massive explosion” in the Nabatieh region that caused tremors and a powerful rumble [2][10][11][14]. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defence Minister Israel Katz called the site “strategic terrorist infrastructure” built over two decades with Iranian funding and planning, and said the demolition completed Israel’s operational control of the ridge [10][16][22][69]. The Israeli military said the complex extended for more than two kilometres and contained Iranian-made rockets, missiles, UAVs, machine guns, anti-tank missiles, mines and explosive devices, as well as a command centre belonging to Hezbollah’s Badr unit [10][63][25][39]. Israel said its forces would stay in the area to prevent Hezbollah from re-establishing its presence and rebuilding capabilities [12][13][14]. AFP journalists in southern Lebanon reported hearing large explosions, and Katz posted video showing a large fireball rising into the night sky [4][6][10].

Key Points

  • Demolition scale: Israel used more than 1,100 tonnes of explosives to demolish two Hezbollah tunnels, according to Israeli military accounts cited by regional and Israeli media [8][33][14][45].
  • Tunnel network: The Times of Israel and Czech Television reported that Thursday’s demolition completed the destruction of a broader eight-tunnel network with a combined length of about 5.4 kilometres [8][37].
  • Local shockwaves: Nabatieh mayor Abbas al-Din said the tremors were felt across southern Lebanon and as far away as Beirut, about 70 kilometres away [21][43][44].
  • Israeli military account: An Israeli military official told AFP that some Hezbollah militants operating near the ridge had abandoned the area, while others were killed by aerial strikes and artillery fire [10][16][41].

Perspectives

Israeli military: The IDF said the underground site had command rooms, weapons storage areas and living quarters that let Hezbollah operatives manage combat and remain underground for extended periods.

  • 10 September 2026: Israel demolished tunnels; USGS recorded a magnitude 4.1 tremor [2][14].

Historical Background

Ali al-Taher lies about 15 kilometres from the Israeli border [12][40][46]. During negotiations, the United States proposed that the Lebanese army take control of the ridge, but Hezbollah did not agree, according to Latvian and Estonian reports citing AFP/LETA material [41][47].

Humanitarian Impact

Lebanese authorities say Israeli strikes since March have killed more than 4,300 people, while Israeli forces occupy what they call a security zone in southern Lebanon [40][41][46][47]. Sources did not report confirmed casualties from Thursday’s tunnel demolition.
