GTA 6 launches at 30 fps as leak fuels unofficial PC port efforts

Category: Technology

Rockstar Games released Grand Theft Auto VI on Nov. 19 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, targeting 30 frames per second on every console to preserve the density of its environments and crowds. Rockstar technology production director Marc Chambers said the PlayStation 5 Pro’s stronger graphics processor improves effects such as ray-traced shadows, but its CPU cannot handle the game’s complex simulation at 60 fps [16]. Rockstar has not announced a PC release date [23]. An Xbox Quick Resume glitch can remove the frame-rate cap on Series X. Players report 45 to 100 fps, but the glitch also speeds up gameplay and causes inconsistent performance in crowded areas [9][7]. Reports on Series S performance conflict: Digital Foundry testing cited by IOL found frame rates in the low 20s during heavy explosions, while early hands-on reviews described smooth play [14][6]. Some PS5 players also report crashes that block story progress; Rockstar has not confirmed a fix [26]. An anonymous leaker known as Cyberleek says a 209 GB Xbox build is available but has withheld the executable, promising to release it Nov. 23. The files have not been independently confirmed as genuine [21]. A separate volunteer project, Leonida, says its recompilation work cannot yet boot the game and its developers need the executable to begin that step [19].

Key Points

  • Player engagement: On launch day, 61% of active U.S. PlayStation 5 users and 56% of global Xbox users played GTA VI. Players averaged 513 minutes on PlayStation and 494 on Xbox, Circana adviser Mat Piscatella said [1].
  • Console network: PlayStation Network experienced scattered outages before launch but recovered quickly, and preload downloads had no widespread failures. The eventual launch of GTA Online will pose a larger network test, Push Square said [11].
  • Crash reports: Some Xbox Series X players also reported crashes, and Rockstar support directed players with console error codes to Sony or Microsoft for help [26].
  • Crypto condition: Decrypt reported that Cyberleek offered to release the executable early if its CYBERLEEK token reached a market value of $15 million. The token’s market value was around $1.3 million at the time [21].
  • PC groundwork: Leonida says it has translated 26,503 Xbox graphics shaders into SPIR-V, but its graphics layer still lacks pipelines, draw calls and a swapchain [19].

Perspectives

Xbox Series S players: Mashable’s reviewer urged players who cannot upgrade not to let console comparisons spoil the experience, arguing the game remains immersive on Series S despite unknown graphical trade-offs [6].

Technical Details

  • Frame-generation processing: Veyra and NeuralScreen can apply upscaling and frame generation to captured console video, but the console still renders gameplay at 30 fps; added processing can increase input latency [17].
  • Wave64 shader support: Leonida says 21,545 shaders use wave64; translation is exact on 64-wide subgroups, while some vertex, pixel and geometry operations remain approximate on 32-wide subgroups [19].
  • Supported graphics operations: Leonida’s Vulkan-backed layer can handle queues, command lists, fences, resource creation, barriers, clears, uploads and copies; the repository says these operations have been checked on one GPU [19].
