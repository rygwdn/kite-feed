Israel says Gaza commercial imports halted as merchants and Hamas dispute fees

Category: World

Israel said commercial shipments into Gaza had stopped because merchants suspended coordination over alleged Hamas fees and demands that they conduct 30% of sales in cash. Hamas denied imposing taxes, saying the 10,000-shekel payment was a voluntary contribution to a relief fund and that it encouraged cash transactions because of a severe cash shortage [1][2][4][9]. The halt cuts off a key source of food and other goods for roughly 2.1 million people in Gaza, where aid agencies typically bring staples such as flour while private traders supply perishables including fruit and meat. Aid workers warn that relief organizations alone cannot meet people’s needs and that reduced commercial supplies could make goods less affordable [2][9][10]. Nahed Sheheiber, head of Gaza’s truckers’ union, disputed Israel’s account, saying Israel imposed the restrictions. He said humanitarian aid and some commercial goods from Egypt were still entering. Israel says aid deliveries continue, while Nickolay Mladenov, the Gaza representative of the US-backed Board of Peace, called for the rapid deployment of a Palestinian technocratic administration and an international stabilization force [8][14][10].

The humanitarian sector on its own cannot meet the needs of all the people.

— Shaina Low, spokesperson for the Norwegian Refugee Council (ABC News)

Key Points

  • Truck volumes: Sheheiber estimated that about 300 trucks entered Gaza daily before the disruption, with roughly two-thirds carrying commercial goods [2][8].
  • Reported threats: COGAT, the Israeli agency overseeing civilian affairs in Gaza, told international representatives that Hamas security personnel had arrested a merchant’s son after the merchant refused the alleged demands; Hamas rejected the broader tax allegations [4][11].
  • Governance plan: The National Committee for the Administration of Gaza, a committee of Palestinian technocrats, was formed in January but has not been allowed to enter Gaza [10].

Perspectives

COGAT, the Israeli agency overseeing civilian affairs in Gaza: COGAT says humanitarian deliveries are sufficient to meet Gaza’s needs even without private-sector shipments [14].

Sources: Jewish News Syndicate

Nahed Sheheiber, head of Gaza’s private truckers union: Sheheiber said the Ministry of Economy had asked merchants for 10,000 shekels per truck; at a union meeting, a ministry undersecretary described the payment as a donation to help pay civil servants [9][7].

Sources: ABC News

Nickolay Mladenov, Gaza representative of the US-backed Board of Peace: Mladenov condemned reported threats and coercion against merchants, saying interference in supply chains deepened the suffering of Gaza’s civilians [4][10].

Sources: The National

Timeline

  • 7 October 2023: Hamas-led militants attacked Israel, killing about 1,200 people and taking 251 hostages [9]
  • August 2025: Famine was declared in Gaza [9]
  • October 2025: A US-mediated ceasefire took effect with a 600-truck daily aid minimum [8][10]
  • July 2026: A global hunger monitor warned over two-thirds could face acute hunger by year-end [2][10]
  • 13 October 2026: Merchants reportedly told COGAT they were suspending coordination over the alleged demands [9][13]

Humanitarian Impact

A food-security report said most families in Gaza were not getting nearly enough to eat and warned that even small disruptions to humanitarian access could rapidly reverse recent gains [9].

Q&A

Q: What would need to happen for commercial shipments to resume?

A: COGAT said it was prepared to coordinate truck entries as usual and could reverse the suspension at the merchants’ request [9].

Q: Could aid agencies make up for the lost commercial shipments?

A: It was unclear whether aid agencies would be allowed to increase shipments to compensate for the loss of commercial goods [9].
