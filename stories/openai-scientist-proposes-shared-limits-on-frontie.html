OpenAI scientist proposes shared limits on frontier AI development

Category: AI

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki has proposed that frontier AI developers agree on shared capability limits based on perplexity and other measures, and refrain from crossing them until their ability to monitor and understand model behavior meets defined safety milestones. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman endorsed the proposal, but Pachocki specified no initial threshold, start date or enforcement mechanism. It remains a call for an industry agreement, not an adopted restriction [21][33][2][25]. Perplexity measures how well a model predicts the next token, with lower scores indicating better predictions. Pachocki says the metric can track pretraining progress as task-based tests become outdated. He argues that AI could accelerate research into pretraining methods as early as 2027, potentially outpacing improvements in safety monitoring [33][21]. He also acknowledged that perplexity alone cannot provide a common yardstick: labs use different training mixtures and evaluation data, and the measure misses some capability gains from reasoning after pretraining. Pachocki’s proposal calls for shared evaluation, red-team and transparency standards alongside staged limits, with further development dependent on concrete advances in monitoring [3][18][19].

Key Points

  • Published scores: OpenAI reported log-perplexity of about 0.99 for GPT-3.5 and 0.91 for GPT-4; Pachocki linked scores below 0.8 to emerging mathematics and cybersecurity capabilities, and cited results on open math problems at about 0.76 [33][3][19].
  • What drove the curve: OpenAI attributed most of the steep decline in its 2026 loss curve to long-term research and compute investment, with AI automation contributing a smaller share [19][18].
  • Uncertain next threshold: Pachocki forecast further transformative capabilities as log-perplexity reaches 0.68–0.70, but said their nature is unknown and current evaluations may not test for them [3][19].
  • Policy debate: The Straits Times reported that Democrats’ midterm gains could renew congressional scrutiny and efforts to introduce AI safety rules, while President Donald Trump opposes broad restrictions on AI development [7].

Perspectives

Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI chief scientist: Pachocki said publishing OpenAI’s internal measurements gives competitors a clearer view of the company’s position, but he judged shared safety measures more urgent than that competitive cost [33][3].

Technical Details

  • OpenAI reported log-perplexity, or cross-entropy loss, measured on a held-out evaluation set drawn from its internal codebase [33][18].
  • OpenAI’s Nov. 10 study found that AI models can solve problems or control the contents of their inference records without writing out their reasoning, making it harder to monitor them through those records [1][18].
