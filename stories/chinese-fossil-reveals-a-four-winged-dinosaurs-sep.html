Chinese fossil reveals a four-winged dinosaur’s separate flight evolution

Category: Science

Researchers described Norellraptor barsboldi, a feathered microraptorine dinosaur preserved in the Jiufotang Formation of northeastern China, in Nature Communications. The 57-centimeter fossil retains feather traces and four wing-like limbs [4][6][8]. The researchers compared flight-associated features across paravian dinosaurs and found that the traits appeared in a different sequence in microraptorines than in the bird lineage. They said the pattern supports the possibility that flight-related adaptations evolved independently in different groups rather than being inherited from a shared flying ancestor [4][6][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Age estimate: Researchers estimated that the specimen was at least three years old [4].
  • Predatory features: The dinosaur had sharply curved teeth and claws [5].
  • Geological setting: The Jiufotang Formation dates to roughly 145 million to 100 million years ago, placing the fossil in the Early Cretaceous [5][6].

Scientific Significance

  • Limits of inference: The comparison addresses the evolution of anatomical features associated with flight; the articles do not establish the animal’s precise flight performance or show that it flew in the same manner as early birds [4][5].
