Researchers identify Maya astronomer Sak Tahn Waax

Category: Science

Researchers writing in Antiquity identified Sak Tahn Waax, translated as “White-chested Fox,” as a Maya astronomer-mathematician whose name appears beside a mathematical formula at the Xultun archaeological site in Guatemala [3][4][1][7][8][9]. The research team said the identification marks the first time a specific work in mathematics or astronomy from the Classic Maya period has been attributed to a named individual [3][1][6][7][8]. Researchers found the inscription in a small masonry building where they documented more than 50 short mathematical and astronomical “microtexts” containing dates, numbers and calculations [3][4][8]. One deciphered mural, called Text 19 by the researchers, contains calculations involving Earth, Mars and Venus and places Sak Tahn Waax’s name at the bottom of the text. Gizmodo reported that the wording reads “so says Saktahnwaax” [4][7]. The team used drawing, photography and digitally enhanced images to reconstruct and transcribe the text [3][8]. Researchers said the formula is unusual in known Maya texts and connects several time cycles, including the 260-day ritual calendar, the solar year and the cycles of Venus and Mars [3][1][8].

Historical Background

The Classic Maya period, roughly 250–900 CE, produced major developments in architecture, city-building, art, writing, mathematics and astronomy [3][1][8]. New Scientist reported that the Maya civilization flourished in Central America between roughly 2000 BC and AD 1697 and that much Maya mathematical and astronomical knowledge was lost after Spanish missionaries burned Maya books [4].
