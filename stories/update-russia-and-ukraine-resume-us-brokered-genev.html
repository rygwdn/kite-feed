Update: Russia and Ukraine resume U.S.-brokered Geneva talks

Category: World

Russian and Ukrainian negotiators resumed U.S.-brokered talks in Geneva this week as Washington pushes for momentum toward ending the nearly four-year war that began with Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 [2][3][4]. After the first day, U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff said the sides had made “meaningful progress” and agreed to brief their leaders and keep working, though multiple outlets cautioned that expectations for a breakthrough remain low [4][8][9][2][3]. The meetings come as fighting and long-range strikes continue. Ukrainian officials said Russia launched a large missile-and-drone barrage shortly before the Geneva session, reporting deaths, injuries, and power outages in parts of southern Ukraine; Kyiv said the attacks undercut diplomacy, while President Volodymyr Zelensky said he was ready to move quickly toward a “worthy agreement” but questioned Moscow’s seriousness [5][4][7]. The two sides also arrived still far apart on core issues such as territory and the security guarantees Ukraine wants to deter another invasion, so this round appears aimed more at testing options than sealing a deal [2][3][4].

Key Points

  • Agenda focus: Ukraine’s lead negotiator, Rustem Umerov, said the Geneva talks focus on “security and humanitarian issues,” and that Kyiv came in “without excessive expectations” [5].
  • Pressure debate: Zelensky has complained that Kyiv faces the greatest pressure to make concessions, as President Trump urges both sides to move quickly toward a deal, according to coverage of the U.S. posture [6].
  • Indirect Iran track: PBS reported that, in the same Geneva diplomatic push, the U.S. also pointed to progress in indirect talks on Iran’s nuclear program—highlighting the week’s broader focus on high-stakes negotiations [1].

Historical Background

The latest Geneva sessions are the newest U.S.-mediated push to stop the fighting, following two earlier rounds in Abu Dhabi that did not produce a breakthrough, according to reporting on the current format’s track record [2][4][5][common].
