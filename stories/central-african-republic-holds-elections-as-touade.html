Central African Republic holds elections as Touadera seeks term

Category: World

The Central African Republic is voting on December 28 in a multi-level election that includes the presidency and seats in the National Assembly, with additional local contests also reported by several outlets [5][8][6][7]. Incumbent President Faustin-Archange Touadera, in office since 2016, is running again after constitutional changes, and several reports say he is widely expected to win, possibly in the first round [1][3][5][8]. Voting comes as the government highlights improved security and credits support from Russian and Rwandan forces, while opponents seek to mobilize voters frustrated by persistent conflict and day-to-day hardship [2][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: UN-cited election data says women make up over 1.14 million of the Central African Republic’s registered voters for this election [4].

Key Points

  • Who can vote: The UN figure cited in local reporting puts the electorate at more than 2.39 million registered voters, including over 1.14 million women [4].
  • Vote package: Observers described the ballot as a “quadruple election,” combining the presidential vote with parliamentary and municipal/local races, increasing the logistical load for election administrators [7][6].
  • Security narrative: Touadera’s campaign message emphasizes security gains, with reporting noting assistance from Russian mercenaries and Rwandan soldiers as a key part of the government’s pitch to voters [2].
  • Opposition strategy: Opposition figures aim to channel public anger in a country where conflict remains common, betting that everyday insecurity and hardship can translate into votes [7].
  • Russia ties: RFI-focused coverage says Touadera’s bid follows a period of closer ties with Russia alongside the constitutional change that opened the way for another run [3].

Perspectives

President Faustin-Archange Touadera and supporters: The president’s camp points to improved security and presents continued leadership as a path to stability, citing support from Russian and Rwandan forces as part of those gains.

Sources: The Straits Times

Opposition parties and critics: Opponents say voters should use the ballot box to respond to daily insecurity and hardship, arguing the country’s ongoing conflict undercuts claims of progress.

Sources: The Guardian

Election coverage focused on process: Several outlets frame the day primarily as a national civic exercise, with citizens choosing a president and 140 National Assembly members alongside other representatives.

Sources: Reuters

Technical Details

  • First-round win: In presidential systems that use a two-round format, a candidate can win outright by meeting the legal threshold in the first round; multiple reports say Touadera is projected/expected by some observers to clear that hurdle [1][5].
  • National Assembly seats: Voters are also choosing 140 members of the National Assembly, an outcome that can shape how easily the next president governs through legislation [8].
  • Multi-level elections: Several sources describe the vote as combining national and local contests (presidential, legislative, and municipal/regional races), which typically requires more ballots, staffing, and tallying capacity than a single-office election [6][7].

Historical Background

Faustin-Archange Touadera has led the Central African Republic since 2016, a period in which armed conflict has remained a central challenge in national politics [1][7]. Reporting says constitutional changes enabled him to seek another term, and coverage also notes the government’s deepening ties with Russia in recent years [3]. Several articles describe the country as chronically unstable, which helps explain why security features so prominently in the campaign [2][8].
