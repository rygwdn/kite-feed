Quebec separatists win 59 seats, pledge to delay independence referendum

Category: Canada

Quebec’s Parti Québécois won Monday’s provincial election with 59 of the legislature’s 127 seats, five short of a majority, returning the separatist party to power and ending eight years of Coalition Avenir Québec government [4][20]. Leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon said he would not hold an independence referendum while U.S. President Donald Trump remains in office and that a vote would not be an early priority for his minority government [5][8]. St-Pierre Plamondon said he would work with Ottawa when it serves Quebec’s interests, while seeking consultation and respect from the federal government [5]. His party has also opposed increases in federal immigration levels and pledged to withdraw Quebec from the proposed Alto high-speed rail project linking Toronto and Quebec City. The Parliamentary Budget Officer estimated the project could cost more than $110 billion [20][5]. Prime Minister Mark Carney said he and the premier-designate discussed infrastructure funding, support for Quebec workers affected by U.S. tariffs and cooperation on housing, electricity capacity and trade [4][2]. Carney urged a focus on current economic issues rather than a referendum years away. Alberta is due to hold a non-binding independence vote this month [4][20].

Key Points

  • Vote share: The PQ won 27.9% of the vote, the lowest share recorded for a winning party in a Quebec election; vote-splitting among five major parties contributed to its victory [20].
  • CAQ collapse: The CAQ, which won majority governments in 2018 and 2022, lost every seat; the PQ increased its seat count from seven to 59 [4][20].
  • Rail dispute: The PQ leader has described the Alto project as a potential “enormous financial fiasco,” while federal ministers have defended its importance to the economy and national unity [5].
  • Ottawa response: Industry Minister Mélanie Joly said the federal government would use a “co-operative federalism approach” to protect jobs and build projects, and noted that the PQ must work with opposition parties [4].
  • Referendum support: Polls put support for Quebec independence at 30%, near record lows [20].

Perspectives

Mark Carney, prime minister: Carney called for a “strong Quebec within a strong Canada” and described Canadian sovereignty as expanding trade and strengthening the country’s resilience [5].

Industry Impact

  • High-speed rail: The proposed Alto corridor would connect Toronto and Quebec City and cut travel times by as much as half; the Parliamentary Budget Officer put its potential cost above $110 billion [5].

Historical Background

Parti Québécois governments held Quebec’s two previous independence referendums, in 1980 and 1995; both failed [4][20]. The PQ was founded in the late 1960s as a pro-independence party [4].
