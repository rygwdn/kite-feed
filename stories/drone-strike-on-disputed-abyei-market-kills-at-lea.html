Drone strike on disputed Abyei market kills at least 153, displaces thousands

Category: World

A drone strike hit Amiet Market in Abyei on Oct. 19, killing at least 153 people and setting a nearby fuel depot ablaze, Abyei caretaker information minister Yohanna Akol Ngor said. The market is north of Abyei town in a territory disputed by Sudan and South Sudan. The fire was still burning the following day as emergency workers tried to recover bodies. Ngor said about 8,000 people fled the area [1][2][13]. South Sudan’s government and Abyei officials blamed Sudan’s military. A senior Sudanese military officer said the army targeted fuel tankers he alleged were carrying supplies for the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), the paramilitary group fighting Sudan’s army. The officer said those killed were RSF members or fuel smugglers and that the army does not target civilians. The Associated Press could not independently verify the claimed targets or the victims’ identities [7][1]. South Sudan summoned Sudan’s ambassador to protest. The United Nations condemned the attacks without assigning responsibility, and its interim peacekeeping force in Abyei sent medical, firefighting and rapid-response teams. Doctors Without Borders (MSF) said it was treating more than 20 injured people, including patients with burns, at Ameth Bek Hospital [6][7][20].

Key Points

  • Wider escalation: The RSF launched drones at Khartoum airport, killing four people, including three children, as the Sudanese army and RSF traded strikes on strategic sites [9].
  • Competing claims: Sudanese military officials have said the RSF operates in parts of Abyei; Ngor denies its presence there, and Reuters said it could not verify the rival claims [3][12].
  • Market role: Amiet Market serves the Ngok Dinka and Misseriya communities and sells food staples. Goods are also transported from the market into Sudan [6][3].
  • Civilian casualties: Abyei district authorities said the victims included women, children, older people, merchants, South Sudanese returnees and Sudanese refugees [8][9].
  • Drone toll: The United Nations said more than 1,100 people were killed in drone attacks across Sudan in the first half of 2026 [9].

Perspectives

Historical Background

Abyei’s status remained unresolved after the 2005 peace agreement that ended decades of civil war and when South Sudan became independent in 2011 [6]. A proposed referendum stalled over disagreement about who would qualify to vote. The Ngok Dinka majority generally favors joining South Sudan, while Misseriya nomads who use the region’s pastures favor Sudan [2][1].

A: MSF said it was providing surgical services, wound dressing and other emergency care at Ameth Bek Hospital [3].
