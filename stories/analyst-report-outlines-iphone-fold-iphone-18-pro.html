Analyst report outlines iPhone Fold, iPhone 18 Pro specs

Category: Technology

A new investor note from analyst Jeff Pu claims Apple’s 2026 high-end iPhone lineup could center on three premium models—iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, and a new “iPhone Fold”—expected around September 2026 [3][1][7][6]. Across reports, the biggest theme is a shared next-generation A20 Pro chip, with multiple outlets tying it to TSMC’s 2nm “N2” process and pointing to performance and efficiency gains versus the prior generation [7][6][8][9]. The foldable-specific claims include a book-style design with a smaller outside display and a tablet-like inner display, plus Touch ID instead of Face ID—suggesting Apple may prioritize practical reliability in a first-gen foldable [3][6]. Several stories frame all of this as rumor-based and unconfirmed, while noting Pu has had accurate calls before but can be aggressive on timing [1][6].

Primary Source: View original article

But one thing is certain: for the first time in years, it seems very likely that the folding iPhone is actually going to happen this year.

— Roman Loyola (Macworld)

Did you know: MacRumors notes that the rumored WMCM approach would place RAM on the same wafer as major compute blocks, a packaging shift aimed at improving performance while reducing chip footprint [7].

Key Points

  • Split launches: Pu’s note, as summarized by MacRumors, says Apple may delay the regular iPhone 18 and a lower-cost “iPhone 18e” until spring 2027, implying a staggered release strategy rather than one single fall event [7].
  • Biometrics choice: The iPhone Fold is expected to use Touch ID rather than Face ID, according to Pu’s spec list shared by 9to5Mac—an approach that could also help when using the device in different folded/unfolded orientations [3].
  • Display sizes: Macworld reports Pu expects a 5.3-inch outer display and a 7.8-inch inner display for the foldable, outlining how Apple could position it as both phone and mini-tablet [6].
  • Camera layout: Pu’s specs described by Macworld include two 48MP rear cameras and two selfie cameras (one for closed use, one for open use), hinting at a design aimed at making both modes feel “complete” [6].
  • New modem: Multiple write-ups say Pu expects Apple’s upcoming C2 modem across the iPhone 18 Pro models and the foldable, continuing Apple’s push toward more in-house cellular components [6][7].

Perspectives

AppleInsider: Treats Pu’s sheet as rumor-grade: it says his data can be accurate, but his assumptions and timelines tend to be too aggressive, and it flags the information as only “possible.”.

Sources: AppleInsider

Macworld: Says Pu’s claimed specs largely match other recent leaks, notes limited provenance for the info, but argues it increasingly looks plausible that a foldable iPhone is nearing release.

Sources: Macworld

TechRepublic: Frames the rumored iPhone 18 generation as a potential “biggest shift in years,” emphasizing cleaner displays, a smarter AI-powered Siri, and the possibility of a foldable design.

Sources: TechRepublic

Technical Details

  • TSMC N2 (2nm) process: Pu’s note is described as tying the A20 Pro to TSMC’s N2 process; MacRumors summarizes expected gains as up to 15% faster performance and up to 30% better efficiency than A19-class chips, which would typically translate into snappier on-device tasks and improved battery life [7].
  • WMCM packaging: MacRumors reports the A20 Pro may use TSMC’s Wafer-Level Multi-Chip Module (WMCM) approach, integrating RAM onto the same wafer as compute blocks (CPU/GPU/Neural Engine), which it says could boost Apple Intelligence performance and free internal space for other components [7].
  • SHPMIM capacitors: MacRumors says N2 introduces “super-high-performance metal-insulator-metal (SHPMIM)” capacitors, described as improving power stability and energy efficiency by increasing capacitance density and reducing electrical resistance [7].

Industry Impact

  • Semiconductor supply chain: Multiple reports highlighting a 2nm A20 Pro point to rising stakes for leading-edge foundry capacity, with TSMC’s N2 and advanced packaging (WMCM) positioned as enabling features for next-gen mobile computing workloads [7].
  • Foldables competition: A credible iPhone Fold (even as a rumor) signals additional competitive pressure in the foldables segment, where Apple’s design choices—like Touch ID and dual selfie cameras—could influence expectations for usability across folded and unfolded modes [3][6].
  • Mobile AI features: TechRepublic’s emphasis on a “smarter Siri powered by AI,” combined with MacRumors’ focus on Neural Engine-related packaging benefits, suggests vendors will keep treating on-device AI responsiveness and efficiency as a headline differentiator in premium phones [2][7].

Historical Background

Foldable smartphones have been commercially available for years, with Samsung’s Galaxy Fold line among the best-known examples, creating a familiar product category Apple would be entering later in the cycle [common]. TechRepublic and others portray Apple’s rumored foldable efforts as part of a broader redesign moment for the iPhone lineup, alongside display and AI/Siri changes [2].

Q&A

Q: If the iPhone Fold uses Touch ID, where might Apple place the sensor?

A: Pu’s list reported by 9to5Mac only says Touch ID (not its location), but in modern phones it commonly appears in a power button or under-display sensor depending on design constraints [3][common].

Q: What does Apple’s rumored C2 modem change for everyday users?

A: The articles only state that Pu expects a C2 modem across the premium lineup; they do not quantify speed or battery improvements, so any real-world impact would depend on Apple’s final implementation and carrier conditions [6][7].

Q: How would a staggered iPhone release affect trade-in timing?

A: If Apple truly holds mainstream models until spring (as MacRumors summarizes), buyers who typically upgrade annually might see different resale/trade-in sweet spots depending on whether they target fall “Pro” devices or spring releases [7][common].

Action Items

  • Pressure-test foldable rumors before budgeting an upgrade: If you’re considering waiting for a foldable, compare what outlets attribute directly to Pu (e.g., Touch ID, display sizes) versus broader rumor framing, since even supportive coverage notes the information is not independently verified [3][6][1].
  • Make your current iPhone feel faster now: If your interest is mainly “smarter Siri/AI” rather than new hardware, review which on-device features your current model supports and adjust settings for responsiveness (e.g., limiting background activity), since some rumored gains hinge on new silicon and may not arrive equally on older devices [2][common].
