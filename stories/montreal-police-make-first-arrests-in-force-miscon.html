Montreal police make first arrests in force misconduct probe

Category: Canada

The first criminal arrests in a Montreal police misconduct investigation have prompted civil-rights advocates to call for accountability and wider scrutiny. One said the case could set a precedent for holding officers responsible [1][9]. Allegations involving a second station, downtown Station 21, include mistreatment of unhoused people and a claim that an officer urinated on someone while on duty [4][9]. Quebec prosecutors charged former Montreal police officers Vincent Routhier and Zachary Belzil with assault and breach of trust; Belzil also faces a criminal-harassment charge. Both were released on promises to appear, and no court date had been set [1][9][10]. The force’s union is challenging the administrative dismissals of seven officers, and Chief Fady Dagher has said they could be reinstated if those challenges succeed [9]. Related criminal investigations continue under the supervision of an independent observer appointed by Quebec’s public security ministry [2][9].

It validates everything we had hoped would take place

— Fo Niemi, executive director of the Center for Research-Action on Race Relations (CBC News)

Key Points

  • Other allegations: A Station 39 officer was accused of deleting evidence from a domestic-violence victim’s phone after recognizing the alleged abuser [2][9].
  • Street checks: The Red Coalition says street checks enable racist and discriminatory policing and is calling on Montreal’s police service to end the practice [1].
  • Dismissal process: Montreal police revoked the seven officers’ security clearances, allowing the force to dismiss them before criminal or disciplinary proceedings concluded [9].

Perspectives

Red Coalition: Founder and executive director Joel DeBellefeuille questioned why allegations were believed when raised within the police force but not when raised by the public. He said advocacy groups and residents had spoken out for decades without seeing action [2].

Sources: The Canadian Press via Toronto Star

Montreal Mayor Soraya Martinez Ferrada: She said people who come forward must be heard and taken seriously. She thanked officers who broke what she called the code of silence [2].

Sources: The Canadian Press via Toronto Star

Timeline

  • March 2026: Montreal police began investigating Station 39 after internal complaints [4]
  • June 2026: The force dismantled a 16-member Montréal-Nord patrol unit [9][10]
  • 28 September 2026: Montreal police submitted an investigation report to Quebec prosecutors [9][10]
  • 2 November 2026: The police chief announced seven officers’ administrative dismissals [2][9]
  • 21 November 2026: Prosecutors laid charges and police announced two arrests [1][9]

Historical Background

Chief Fady Dagher was brought in with a mandate to confront years of mistrust between officers and the communities they police. In late October, he warned that more disturbing photographs and videos involving officers could emerge, saying, “It’s not going to be pretty” [9].
