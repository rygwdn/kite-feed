Ubisoft cancels six games, closes studios in reset

Category: Technology

Ubisoft has cancelled six games—including the long-delayed Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time remake—as it reorganizes its business and tightens how it greenlights projects [1][3][2][10]. The company is also closing studios in Halifax, Canada, and Stockholm, Sweden, while restructuring other locations and delaying seven additional titles [3][4][6]. Ubisoft says the overhaul aims to make the company more agile and improve quality thresholds, even as investors reacted sharply, sending its share price down more than 30% in a day in several reports [6][4][5][7]. Several outlets also note Ubisoft’s intention to lean more heavily on player-facing generative AI as part of its future development approach [2][7][5].

Primary Source: View original article

It is a radical move, relying on a more decentralized creative organization with faster decision-making and best-in-class cross-functional core services supporting and serving each Creative House,

— Yves Guillemot (Fast Company)

Did you know: Ubisoft employs about 17,000 people worldwide, according to the company’s restructuring statement cited in reports [6].

Key Points

  • Creative houses: Ubisoft said it will consolidate teams into five genre-specific “creative houses” that combine production and publishing to speed decisions and share core services across the company [6].
  • Guidance cut: Ubisoft lowered its net bookings outlook to about €1.5 billion, down roughly €330 million from prior guidance, as it reshapes its slate [6].
  • Quality bar: Ubisoft said the Prince of Persia remake fell short of its “enhanced” quality criteria after years of delays and a development move from Ubisoft Mumbai to Ubisoft Montreal [6][4][1].
  • Market cap drop: Following the restructuring news, multiple reports said Ubisoft’s valuation fell below €1 billion in market capitalization as the stock hit historic lows [5].
  • Labor concerns: A Canadian union argued the Halifax studio shutdown amounted to union-busting, a claim noted amid broader layoffs and closures [7].

Perspectives

Ubisoft leadership: Ubisoft framed the reorganization as a “major reset” meant to make the company more agile, cut costs, and set a path toward sustainable growth with higher quality standards [6][10].

Sources: Fast Company

Financial markets: Investors sold off Ubisoft shares heavily after the cancellations and restructuring, with reports putting the one-day decline at more than 30% and the multi-day slide as steep as the mid-30% range [6][4][7][5].

Sources: Android Headlines

Game press analysts: Commentators described the Prince of Persia remake’s multi-year slippage as an example of how large-budget game projects can suffer from long timelines and shifting plans [7][1].

Sources: Mashable

Labor advocates: A Canadian union criticized the Halifax closure as union-busting, underscoring worker anxiety around layoffs tied to the reset [7].

Sources: Mashable

Technical Details

  • Generative AI: Generative AI refers to models that produce text, images, audio, or other content from prompts; Ubisoft says it plans to put more emphasis on player-facing generative AI features in its games [common][2][7].
  • Live-service model: Live-service games deliver ongoing updates and monetization over time; Ubisoft’s reset includes a stronger focus on open-world and live-service titles, according to reports [common][2].
  • Greenlight process: A project “greenlight” is a formal go/no-go decision for funding and development; Ubisoft said it will tighten greenlights as it concentrates spending [common][1].

Industry Impact

  • Remake economics: The cancellation highlights how even well-known legacy remakes can become high-risk when delays stretch for years, potentially pushing publishers to reassess remake budgets and outsourcing strategies [1][7].
  • AI adoption: Ubisoft’s stated emphasis on player-facing generative AI adds momentum to an industry trend where major publishers test AI-driven content pipelines and in-game features, likely increasing demand for AI tooling and governance practices [2][7][5].
  • Regional studios: Studio closures in Halifax and Stockholm show how publishers may retrench to fewer hubs during downturns, affecting local game-dev ecosystems and hiring pipelines [3][4][6].
  • Financing climate: A steep share-price reaction and a reported drop below €1 billion market cap may make external financing and talent retention harder for mid-to-large publishers, reinforcing the industry’s current push toward fewer, bigger bets [5][6].

Historical Background

Ubisoft announced the Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time remake in 2020 and repeatedly delayed it over several years, including shifting development from Ubisoft Mumbai to Ubisoft Montreal [1][4]. Several outlets describe the latest actions as part of a longer struggle with costly, long development cycles and uneven commercial performance in recent years [7][6].

Q&A

Q: Which specific seven games did Ubisoft delay besides the one mentioned?

A: Several reports say Ubisoft delayed seven titles but do not list the full set; at least one delayed game had been slated for release in April, without naming it [4][3].

Q: What happened to other long-running Ubisoft projects like Beyond Good and Evil 2?

A: A Reuters-linked news roundup noted that Beyond Good and Evil 2 survived the cancellations announced in the restructure [9].

Q: What does “net bookings” mean in game publishing financials?

A: Net bookings generally refer to the net value of products and services sold digitally or physically during a period, often used by publishers to indicate sales performance including digital add-ons [common].
