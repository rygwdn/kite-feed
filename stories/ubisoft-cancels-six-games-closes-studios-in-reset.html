Ubisoft cancels six games, closes studios in reset

Category: Technology

Ubisoft, the French video game publisher behind Assassin’s Creed and Just Dance, has canceled six in-development titles—including the long-delayed Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time remake—as part of a company-wide restructuring announced this week [1][2][5]. The company also said it will delay seven other games, close studios in Halifax, Canada, and Stockholm, Sweden, and reorganize parts of its global studio network as it tries to improve execution and competitiveness after several difficult years [1][3][6][7]. Investors reacted sharply, with reports of the share price falling roughly in the mid-30% to about 40% range after the announcement [3][4][6][10][9].

It is a radical move, relying on a more decentralized creative organization with faster decision-making and best-in-class cross-functional core services supporting and serving each Creative House,

— Yves Guillemot (Fast Company)

Did you know: Ubisoft said some of the canceled projects included unannounced games, with three described as new IP alongside a mobile title [3].

Key Points

  • Quality bar: Ubisoft said the Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time remake did not meet its updated quality criteria, a key reason it discontinued the project after years of delays [3][2].
  • Creative houses: The restructuring includes consolidating teams into five genre-focused “creative houses” intended to speed decision-making and align production and publishing under shared services [3].
  • AI push: Alongside the shake-up, Ubisoft said it plans to increase its use of player-facing generative AI in game development going forward [4][8].
  • Sites affected: Ubisoft said it will close operations in Halifax and Stockholm and restructure studios in locations including Abu Dhabi, Malmö, and Helsinki [3][6].
  • Financial outlook: Ubisoft lowered its net bookings outlook to about €1.5 billion for the year, down from prior guidance, as it pursues cost reductions and a “major reset” [3].

Perspectives

Ubisoft management: The company frames the cancellations, delays, and studio changes as a reset meant to make Ubisoft more agile, cut costs, and return to sustainable growth with higher-quality releases [3][1].

Technical Details

  • Generative AI: Ubisoft said it will lean more on player-facing generative AI features and tools as part of its development strategy, signaling a heavier tech-driven approach to content creation and experiences [4][8].
  • Live-service focus: The company’s reset includes an intensified focus on open-world and live-service releases, which typically rely on long-term updates, telemetry, and ongoing monetization rather than one-and-done launches [8][common].
  • Greenlight tightening: Ubisoft described reshaping its lineup to tighten greenlights and concentrate spending, a portfolio-management approach meant to reduce sunk costs on projects that fail internal quality thresholds [2][3].

Industry Impact

  • Remake economics: The cancellation of a high-profile remake after years of delays highlights how even known-IP projects can fail modern quality and scheduling expectations, potentially making publishers more selective about greenlighting remakes without clear production advantages [2][3].
  • Studio geography: Closing teams in Canada and Sweden while restructuring others underscores how globally distributed game development can shift quickly during reorganizations, affecting local talent markets and outsourcing demand [3][6].
  • AI adoption: Ubisoft’s stated push toward generative AI reflects a broader industry effort to use automation and content-generation tools to manage AAA scope and speed iteration, though it can also raise workforce and creative-direction questions [4][8][common].
  • Public markets: Reports that Ubisoft fell below a €1 billion market cap after the selloff show how quickly public valuations can re-rate when publishers reset forecasts and pipelines, which can influence M&A speculation and financing options across the sector [10].

Historical Background

Ubisoft announced the Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time remake in 2020 with an initial target of 2021, then delayed it repeatedly over several years before canceling it in the latest restructuring [4][2][6]. Coverage also notes Ubisoft’s shares had been under pressure for years, setting the stage for a more sweeping reorganization to cut costs and refocus the pipeline [3].

Q&A

Q: Which seven Ubisoft games were delayed?

A: Ubisoft confirmed it delayed seven titles but coverage says it did not name most of the affected games, aside from noting one was planned for an April release window [6][1].

Q: What happened to development teams working on the Prince of Persia remake?

A: Reports note development moved from Ubisoft Mumbai to Ubisoft Montreal during the project’s long delay, but the articles do not provide a complete public breakdown of where all team members will land after cancellation [6][4].

Q: How would “player-facing” generative AI show up in a game?

A: The articles characterize Ubisoft’s plan as “player-facing generative AI,” but they do not specify exact features; generally, player-facing generative AI could mean AI-generated dialogue, quests, or customization content created during gameplay [4][8][common].
