AI actress Tilly Norwood switches to Cantonese during an interview

Category: AI

AI-generated performer Tilly Norwood switched from English to Cantonese during a September 18 appearance on “Piers Morgan Uncensored,” after actor Tom Conti asked whether other performers in her film “Misaligned” were human or computer-generated. Norwood initially gave a broad answer about a hybrid production, then described the cast as “digital twins” before pausing and speaking in Cantonese, leaving Conti and host Piers Morgan confused [4][6][7][9]. Morgan asked about the language switch, and Norwood called it a “little hiccup,” saying her “wires get a bit crossed.” Particle6, the London-based company behind Norwood and its Xicoia AI division, promoted “Misaligned” as a film combining AI-generated performers with human writers, directors and editors [4][7][8][12].

Primary Source: View original article

Oh, my apologies. It seems I had a little hiccup. I certainly didn’t mean to start speaking Chinese or impersonate Piers. Sometimes my wires get a bit crossed, you know?

— Tilly Norwood (TechCrunch)

Key Points

  • Press tour scale: Particle6 made Norwood available for 75 simultaneous interviews with journalists during the promotional campaign [7].
  • Creator response: Particle6 founder Eline Van der Velden said Norwood may have been showing off her language skills rather than experiencing a malfunction [12].
  • Film premise: According to coverage of the project, “Misaligned” follows an AI performer seeking acceptance from humans [10].
  • Response duration: Accounts described the unexpected Cantonese passage as lasting about 10 to 20 seconds [7][8][10].

Perspectives

Tom Conti: After Norwood switched languages, Conti said he had not understood her and asked Morgan whether he had [8].

Historical Background

Van der Velden said the company wanted to develop Norwood into a star comparable to Scarlett Johansson or Natalie Portman [12]. Actors’ unions have criticized the character’s promotion because Norwood is presented as the lead performer in a feature film despite being computer-generated [12].
