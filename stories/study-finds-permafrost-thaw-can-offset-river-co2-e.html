Study finds permafrost thaw can offset river CO2 emissions

Category: Science

A Nature study summarized by ScienceDaily found that thawing permafrost on the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau can speed rock weathering, a geological process that removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere [1]. The researchers said that, in some studied regions, weathering-driven carbon uptake fully offset or exceeded greenhouse gas emissions from rivers linked to permafrost thaw [1][3]. Scientists from Umeå University in Sweden and East China Normal University in China conducted the research [1]. The study says that as frozen ground degrades, buried minerals are exposed and water interacts more extensively with rock surfaces, increasing chemical weathering that consumes atmospheric CO2 [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Known emissions: Permafrost thaw exposes long-frozen organic matter that microbes can break down, releasing greenhouse gases from ancient carbon stores [1][3].
  • River pathway: ScienceDaily’s summary links the weathering effect to river carbon dioxide emissions as permafrost degrades, not to all permafrost emissions globally [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Uncertainty and scope: The evidence cited comes from rivers on the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau, so the source material supports a regional finding rather than a quantified global offset for thawing permafrost [1].
  • Model relevance: The finding identifies a process that climate assessments would need to distinguish by landscape and river system, because the reports describe offsets in some regions rather than a uniform effect [1].
