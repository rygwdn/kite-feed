Meta signs three nuclear deals totaling 6 GW

Category: Technology

Meta says it has signed three nuclear-energy agreements to support electricity demand from its AI data centers in the US, including large new projects in Ohio and Louisiana [2][7][4]. Across Vistra, TerraPower, and Oklo, Meta expects these arrangements to deliver roughly 6.6 gigawatts of generation by 2035, combining power from existing plants with planned new reactor builds [2][8]. The company framed the move as part of securing long-term, dependable power for hyperscale computing, as data-center growth has strained parts of the US grid [8][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Oklo’s investor base includes OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, who Engadget reports owns just over 4% of the company [2].

Key Points

  • Ohio focus: The Vistra agreement centers on output from the Perry and Davis-Besse nuclear plants in Ohio, plus upgrades there and at Beaver Valley in Pennsylvania that Vistra says should add 433 MW in the early 2030s [1][2][8].
  • Natrium design: TerraPower’s deal involves its Natrium reactor concept, which uses sodium as a coolant instead of water, and includes development of two reactors projected to supply up to 690 MW as early as 2032, plus rights to power from additional units later [2].
  • Fuel financing: Wired reports Meta will finance Oklo’s purchase of uranium for its planned reactors, adding a funding component beyond standard power-offtake agreements [3].
  • Grid bottlenecks: TechCrunch notes much of the planned power would connect through PJM, a Mid-Atlantic/Midwest grid operator that has become saturated with data centers, underscoring why large new generation is being pursued [8][1].
  • Approval hurdles: Oklo has struggled to get its reactor design approved by the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, a factor that could affect how quickly its 1.2 GW target comes online [1].

Perspectives

Meta: Meta presents the agreements as a way to secure long-term electricity for AI infrastructure and says the Oklo partnership could create thousands of construction and operations jobs in Ohio.

Sources: Engadget

Vistra: Vistra’s role emphasizes extending and uprating existing nuclear plants via a 20-year supply arrangement and planned capacity additions expected in the early 2030s.

Sources: TechCrunch

Oklo: Oklo aims to begin supplying power to the grid as early as 2030, but faces regulatory and execution challenges highlighted in reporting.

Sources: Slashdot

The Register: The Register stresses that much of the new nuclear capacity tied to these deals likely will not arrive until around 2030, even as Meta’s data-center power needs grow now.

Sources: The Register

Timeline

  • December 2024: Meta issued an RFP seeking 1–4 GW by early 2030s [8][1].
  • Early 2030s: Vistra plans 433 MW uprates across Ohio and Pennsylvania plants [8][2].

Technical Details

  • Small modular reactors (SMRs): Oklo and TerraPower are developing SMRs—smaller nuclear units intended to be built in multiples—an approach Meta is backing through agreements for several reactors [1][2].
  • Sodium-cooled reactor: TerraPower’s Natrium design uses sodium rather than water as a coolant, a key engineering choice highlighted in the TerraPower-Meta agreement [2].
  • Nuclear plant uprates: Meta’s Vistra deal includes adding capacity at existing nuclear stations, an example of uprating/expanding output rather than building entirely new plants [8][1].

Industry Impact

  • AI infrastructure planning: The agreements reflect a broader shift where AI compute expansion pushes companies to source large blocks of firm power, blending near-term contracts from existing plants with longer-term bets on new nuclear builds [2][6].
  • Regional power markets: By targeting generation that will flow through PJM, the deals highlight how grid interconnection backlogs and data-center clustering can influence where new capacity gets built and who can secure it [8][1].
  • Nuclear startups: Meta’s participation—especially financing related to reactor fuel procurement for Oklo—signals a potential new model where hyperscalers provide more direct support to early-stage nuclear developers [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Reactor innovation: The TerraPower agreement spotlights alternative reactor engineering, specifically sodium-cooled designs, which differ materially from the water-cooled systems used in most commercial nuclear plants [2][common].
  • Deployment uncertainty: The reporting emphasizes practical uncertainties—especially regulatory approval for Oklo’s design and long construction lead times—which can be as important as the underlying science in determining real-world impact [1][6].

Historical Background

US data-center electricity demand has risen rapidly alongside cloud computing and, more recently, large-scale AI training and inference workloads [common]. Several big tech firms have pursued long-term nuclear contracts to obtain steady, low-carbon power, including Microsoft’s plan tied to the Three Mile Island site and Meta’s prior nuclear agreement with Constellation Energy, as cited by Engadget [2].

Q&A

Q: Which of these agreements is likely to affect Meta’s power supply first?

A: TechCrunch says the 20-year deal with Vistra should have the most immediate impact because it involves electricity from existing nuclear plants, while new capacity additions arrive later [8].

Q: Where might Oklo build reactors linked to this arrangement?

A: Slashdot reports that if Oklo meets its timeline, new reactors would be built in Pike County, Ohio [1].

Q: How large is Meta’s planned “Prometheus” AI data center site?

A: Engadget describes Prometheus as a 1-gigawatt data center being built in Ohio [2].
