Trump media ban prompts lawsuit, TV pool boycott

Category: Business

CNN, MS NOW and Politico sued President Trump’s administration in federal court in Washington, D.C., on Sept. 21 after the White House barred their reporters from the grounds and revoked their credentials. The organizations are seeking a temporary restraining order, arguing that the ban violates the First Amendment and due process protections because the administration imposed it in response to coverage it disliked [4][13][36]. Trump said the outlets were banned because of what he called accumulated negative or “fake news” coverage. He later said the restrictions targeted “FAKE NEWS,” not the free press [2][5][22]. The dispute disrupted presidential television coverage when ABC, CBS, NBC, Fox News and CNN suspended their rotating pool duties for Trump-related events, including his New York trip for the United Nations General Assembly. The networks said the public has a vital interest in accurate, independent information about the government [1][14].

Key Points

  • Court hearing: A federal hearing on the media ban was scheduled for Wednesday, with the outlets seeking emergency relief to restore access [2][36].
  • Court assignment: U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly was assigned the case; Trump nominated him during his first term [32].
  • Coverage gap: The pool did not appoint a replacement crew for Trump’s New York events, leaving major presidential appearances without the usual shared television feed [14][10].
  • Alternative channel: The White House launched a streaming channel called Trump TV after the networks suspended pooled coverage [33].

Perspectives

CNN, MS NOW and Politico: The three organizations argued that Trump could not reserve presidential coverage for outlets offering favorable reporting simply because he dislikes their coverage [24].

Industry Impact

  • Broadcast operations: The boycott interrupted the shared production system that lets five networks split the cost, staffing and physical logistics of live presidential coverage [3][8].
  • Still-image coverage: The Associated Press, Agence France-Presse, The Washington Post and The New York Times also said they would not publish photographs from presidential events [25].

Historical Background

The White House press pool has traditionally been administered by the White House Correspondents’ Association, but the Trump administration announced in February 2026 that it would take control of access to the rotating pool [23]. In February 2025, the administration also restricted Associated Press journalists from certain presidential settings over the organization’s use of “Gulf of Mexico” rather than the administration’s preferred name; that case remained in litigation [23].
