UK air traffic control issue cancels more than 1,000 flights

Category: World

Britain's air traffic control provider NATS said a technical issue in its flight data processing system disrupted flights across UK airports on Tuesday, forcing more than 1,000 cancellations and delaying hundreds more at major hubs including Heathrow, Gatwick, Manchester, Stansted and Birmingham, as well as airports in Scotland [2][4][8][12][13]. NATS said it had resolved the issue and that safety was not compromised, but recovery continued after aircraft and crews were left out of position [12][9][6]. Disruption continued on Wednesday, when Flightradar24 said 177 scheduled flights to and from UK airports had already been cancelled, nearly all at London Heathrow [1][2][3][5]. Heathrow said departures had resumed after the issue forced it to suspend arrivals, while airports and airlines warned passengers to check flight status as carriers worked through backlogs [1][6][4][9]. The UK transport secretary, Heidi Alexander, said she was seeking assurances that lessons would be learned and that aviation support systems were fit for purpose, and was expected to meet NATS chief executive Martin Rolfe on Wednesday [1][2]. Rolfe told Sky News he accepted "full responsibility" and said NATS had not yet completed its investigation into the cause [1][12].

Key Points

  • Passenger scale: The disruption affected hundreds of thousands of passengers, with some travellers stranded overnight as airlines tried to clear a major backlog [2][14][6].
  • Airline pressure: Ryanair and Wizz Air called for an overhaul of NATS after the disruption, while Ryanair's chief operating officer Neal McMahon urged the UK government to replace Rolfe [11][1].
  • Airport spread: The failure initially hit departures at several large airports before disruption spread to incoming flights across much of southern England [2].

Timeline

  • 8 September 2026: Breakdown began at about 1 p.m. Tuesday [2]
  • 8 September 2026: NATS fixed the flight processing system almost four hours later [2]
  • 8 September 2026: NATS said the issue was resolved at 1834 GMT [12]
  • 9 September 2026: Flights resumed early Wednesday, with recovery delays expected [9]

Historical Background

The latest failure was Britain's third major air traffic control stoppage in just over three years [2]. NATS also faced scrutiny after a flight-plan processing failure three years earlier caused even more widespread disruption [12].

Business Angle

  • Airlines with dense UK schedules face disproportionate disruption when aircraft and crews need repositioning after a system outage [9].
  • Repeated air traffic control failures can disrupt carrier schedules during peak travel periods, especially for airlines with heavy UK exposure [2][common].
  • Ryanair and Wizz Air called for a NATS overhaul, while Ryanair's chief operating officer urged the UK government to replace Rolfe [11][1].

Travel Advisory

  • Flight status: UK airports warned passengers to check flight status with their airline as disruption continued after the air traffic control issue [4][6]. Confirm your flight directly with your airline before travelling to the airport.
  • Connections: Airlines expected delays to continue while they cleared backlogs and repositioned aircraft and crews [6][9]. Allow extra time for connections and contact the airline before rebooking separate onward travel.

Action Items

  • Check your airline's disruption policy: Use the airline's app or website for rebooking, hotel and meal-voucher options if your UK flight was cancelled or delayed [6][4].
  • Document delay expenses: Keep receipts for food, accommodation and transport while waiting for airline instructions, because passenger-care claims usually require proof of costs [common].
