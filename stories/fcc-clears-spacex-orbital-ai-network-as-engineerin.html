FCC clears SpaceX orbital AI network as engineering questions persist

Category: Technology

A key engineering question for SpaceX’s proposed Starmind network is whether orbiting AI servers can shed the heat produced by powerful processors: in the vacuum of space, heat must radiate away rather than move through air [4]. The Federal Communications Commission (FCC), the U.S. communications regulator, authorized SpaceX on Nov. 25 to deploy as many as one million computing satellites, the first approval for a full orbital data-center constellation [4][3]. SpaceX’s planned AI1 satellite would pair Nvidia computing hardware with large solar arrays and deployable liquid radiators. Satellites would exchange data through optical links and connect to Earth through SpaceX’s Starlink network [40][27]. SpaceX has described clusters of about 10 satellites linked at roughly 10 terabits per second. The FCC approved radio-frequency links as backup if optical links fail [27][8]. Researchers and satellite operators have warned that heat management, radiation damage, orbital congestion and debris remain unresolved risks [4][21]. The FCC deferred SpaceX’s request to place satellites above 600 kilometers into disposal orbits, saying it lacked enough information to assess the plan. SpaceX would need further approval for those disposal methods [10][17].

Key Points

  • Astronomy concerns: An expert said the FCC was not convinced the constellation would interfere with the Square Kilometre Array Observatory, while concerns about ground-based optical astronomy remain [54].
  • Sky impact: The American Astronomical Society said preliminary simulations indicate tens of thousands of satellites could be visible to the naked eye if SpaceX deploys the full network, while reflected sunlight could streak telescope images [6].
  • Orbit range: The FCC ruling places the proposed satellites at altitudes of 550 to 1,002 kilometers, according to an expert’s account of the authorization [54].
  • Signal links: The satellites would rely primarily on optical links for network connections, with approved backup radio links in the 18.8–19.3 GHz downlink and 28.6–29.1 GHz uplink bands [39][26].
  • Earlier precedent: SpaceX previously sought approval for 42,000 Starlink satellites but has just over 11,000 in orbit [25][24].
  • License conditions: SpaceX must have 100,000 satellites operating by November 2035, 500,000 by November 2038 and the full million by November 2040; missing those deadlines would void the authorization [6].
  • Public comments: The FCC received about 1,500 public comments and said it would not review some launch-emissions and astronomy concerns because they fall outside its authority [10][4].

Technical Details

  • Backup spectrum: The FCC permitted Ka-band communications at 18.8–19.3 GHz downlink and 28.6–29.1 GHz uplink only if optical links fail, on a non-interference basis [8][23].
  • Orbit and power: The FCC authorization covers six orbital shells between 500 and 1,002 kilometers; in sun-synchronous orbit, solar arrays could receive near-continuous sunlight, but processors must still withstand radiation and temperature extremes [3][21].
  • Compute and cooling payload: A reported AI1 design uses 72 Rubin GPUs and 36 Vera CPUs, with computing capacity rated at up to 250 kilowatts; deployable liquid radiators are intended to reject heat in vacuum [40][27].
  • End-of-life disposal: SpaceX sought permission to move satellites above 600 kilometers into graveyard or heliocentric orbits rather than have them re-enter the atmosphere. The FCC deferred that request, citing insufficient detail on the disposal plan [1][54].

Industry Impact

  • Satellite operations: The FCC requires SpaceX to file twice-yearly reports on close-approach events involving its satellites and other spacecraft [14].

Scientific Significance

  • Orbital collision risk: Researchers warn that adding a very large constellation to low Earth orbit could increase collisions and trigger a cascade of debris, a risk described as the Kessler effect. The expert comments do not quantify the scale or likelihood of such an outcome [54].
  • Atmospheric effects: According to the expert response, the FCC characterized the atmospheric effects of satellites burning up on re-entry as speculative [54].

Historical Background

Orbital AI data centers were central to Elon Musk’s pitch to investors ahead of SpaceX’s June initial public offering. SpaceX has cautioned that the concept relies on unproven technologies and may never be commercially viable [37].
