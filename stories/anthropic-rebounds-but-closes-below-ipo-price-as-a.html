Anthropic rebounds but closes below IPO price as AI funding doubts persist

Category: AI

Anthropic, the developer of the Claude AI assistant, rebounded during Wednesday trading but closed at $82.85, just below its $83 offering price, as technology shares fell again [49][47]. The rebound followed a volatile first week on Nasdaq, with concerns persisting about whether demand for advanced AI can support the cost of computing infrastructure [22][9]. CNBC said the stock’s weak reception could complicate OpenAI’s fundraising and affect expectations for companies supplying chips, cloud computing and data-center equipment [2]. Investors are weighing the cost of building and running AI systems against revenue forecasts, while a Reuters analysis found many technology stocks had fallen despite rising earnings estimates [9]. Anthropic is expected to report its first results as a public company early next year [23].

Investors pay up for earnings they believe will last and discount those they think will fade.

Key Points

  • OpenAI timing: On Kalshi, traders put the chance that OpenAI would formally announce an IPO by June 2027 at about 28%, down from more than 50% earlier in November [48].
  • Compute expense: Anthropic reported $9.9 billion in third-quarter computing and infrastructure costs, up 77% from the prior quarter, while revenue rose 47% sequentially [50].
  • Enterprise demand: Wedbush said Anthropic had more than 11,900 customers spending over $100,000, including about 1,900 spending more than $1 million [21].
  • Model contracts: Anthropic disclosed commitments of about $121 billion for Google Cloud tensor-processing-unit capacity through 2033 and $110 billion for Amazon Web Services Trainium chips through 2036 [12].

Perspectives

Andrew Freedman, communications and software analyst at Hedgeye Risk Management: Freedman said Anthropic could become an operating system for white-collar work and put his fair-value estimate at $92 a share, while warning short-term investors that upcoming share-lockup expirations could add selling pressure [14].

Sources: Bloomberg

Felix Wang, global technology analyst at Hedgeye Risk Management: Wang recommended shorting the shares, setting a $59 target and arguing that tighter financing for AI computing capacity and uncertain demand could constrain Anthropic’s growth [14].

Technical Details

  • Aggregate compute commitments: Anthropic estimates $894.5 billion in computing obligations over 19 years, with about $70 billion due by the end of September 2027 and $451.6 billion by the end of 2030 [12].

Q&A

Q: Does Anthropic’s reported $104 billion net loss represent that much cash spent on operations?

A: No. TheWill reported that about $94.3 billion of the nine-month net loss came from fair-value adjustments to financing instruments; it put the operating loss at about $1.3 billion [50].
