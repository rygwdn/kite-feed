AI cyberattacks heighten personal data exposure risks

Category: AI

Security writers and researchers described widening personal-data risks tied to generative AI, phishing and large-scale hacking. PCMag senior writer Kim Key said she closed her first Gmail account after AI-powered scams filled her inbox with phishing links and malware-laden attachments, then investigated how her email address may have reached the dark web [1]. Futurism reported that advances in AI coding tools are helping attackers find and exploit unknown software vulnerabilities as companies defend stores of consumer data [2]. The article cited recent warnings about the sensitivity of data accumulated over two decades of internet use, including social media, email, messaging, health, banking, pornography and purchases, and said institutions ranging from corporate giants to banks, utilities and government agencies face AI-powered cyberattack risks [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Data resale: PCMag said companies can sell customer data during acquisitions or bankruptcy settlements, creating another path for personal information to circulate beyond its original context [1].
  • Breach baseline: BlackFog ransomware and cybersecurity expert Darren Williams told PCMag that leaked personal data has become nearly universal, saying, "Everybody on planet Earth has had their data leaked at this point" [1].
  • Daily breaches: Futurism noted that "companies are getting hacked every single day" [2].

Perspectives

Kim Key, PCMag: Key detailed her investigation into which company first lost her email address, mapping the multiple pathways personal data can take from user devices to dark-web marketplaces [1].

Sources: PCMag

Historical Background

OpenAI's 2022 release of ChatGPT made generative AI systems widely available to consumers, accelerating public use of tools that can generate convincing text at scale [common]. Large data breaches and dark-web credential markets existed before generative AI, but PCMag’s account ties today’s scam volume to older pathways such as data breaches, acquisitions and bankruptcy-related data sales [1].

Action Items

  • Retire heavily targeted email accounts: If an inbox has become dominated by phishing and malware attempts, move sensitive services to a new address and close or quarantine the old account, as PCMag’s writer did after AI-powered scams overwhelmed her Gmail inbox [1].
  • Review where companies share your data: Check privacy notices and account settings for data-sale or sharing permissions, especially after mergers, acquisitions or bankruptcy notices, because PCMag identified those events as routes for customer data transfers [1].
