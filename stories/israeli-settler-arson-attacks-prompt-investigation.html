Israeli settler arson attacks prompt investigation and political clash

Category: World

Suspected Israeli settlers torched at least a dozen Palestinian vehicles and several homes and spray-painted racist slogans in at least four villages in the occupied West Bank overnight Saturday into Sunday, residents and Israeli authorities said. No injuries were reported. Police opened an investigation, and Israeli forces deployed to Deir as-Sudan, Deir Abu Masha’al, Kafr Malik and At-Tuwani [1][8][6]. Gadi Eisenkot, the opposition leader tasked with forming Israel’s next government, called the attacks “Jewish terrorism” and urged arrests and severe sanctions. He blamed policies pursued by National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich. Foreign Minister Gideon Saar condemned the attacks, while Ben-Gvir accused Eisenkot of prejudging the investigation and courting Arab-party support [1][18]. An October 27 election left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu without a parliamentary majority. President Isaac Herzog assigned Eisenkot to form a government, but coalition talks remain unresolved. Eisenkot has until December 6, with a possible 14-day extension, to secure a majority. If no one succeeds in forming a government, another election is due next year [4][26].

Key Points

  • Village damage: Residents and local reporting counted seven burned vehicles in Kafr Malik, three in Deir Abu Mashal and two in Deir as-Sudan [18].
  • Violence frequency: The U.N. Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs recorded an average of six settler attacks a day in the West Bank in 2026; a U.N. tally cited by CNN counted 410 attacks linked to the olive harvest since September [6][34].
  • Security resources: The Israel Defense Forces said the incidents diverted military resources from other security work, including counterterrorism operations [15][18].
  • Enforcement dispute: B’Tselem spokesperson Yair Dvir said condemnation alone was insufficient, arguing Israeli authorities had made no real effort to stop the attacks [6].
  • Outpost reference: Graffiti reading “Greetings from Susya” appeared to refer to the recent evacuation of an Israeli settler outpost in the southern West Bank [6][32].

Perspectives

Itamar Ben-Gvir, Israel’s national security minister: Ben-Gvir rejected Eisenkot’s characterization, saying Eisenkot did not know the facts of the case or the investigation. He accused Eisenkot of offering political support to Arab lawmakers in exchange for their backing for a government [18].

Historical Background

Israel has occupied the West Bank since 1967. More than 500,000 Israelis live in settlements there alongside about three million Palestinians; the United Nations and most countries regard the settlements as illegal, while Israel disputes that position [5][10]. Violence in the territory has risen since the Hamas-led October 7, 2023, attack on Israel and the ensuing Gaza war; at least 1,119 Palestinians and 51 Israelis have been killed in the West Bank since then, according to tallies and official figures cited by AFP [10].
