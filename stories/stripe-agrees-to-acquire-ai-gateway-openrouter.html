Stripe agrees to acquire AI gateway OpenRouter

Category: AI

Stripe said on Aug. 19 that it agreed to acquire OpenRouter, a developer platform that routes requests across competing artificial intelligence models through a single interface; the companies did not disclose terms [5][6][4]. TechCrunch, citing The New York Times, reported a $7.5 billion price, while Silicon Republic cited recent reports putting the deal between $7 billion and $10 billion, well above OpenRouter’s $1.3 billion valuation after a $113 million funding round a few months earlier [1][6]. The proposed acquisition would move Stripe beyond payments into AI usage infrastructure, where developers compare model cost, speed and reliability instead of integrating separately with each model provider [5][4][6]. Stripe said the deal will support its work on token-based billing and token-cost optimization, and Silicon Republic reported that OpenRouter has more than 10 million users globally [4][6]. TechCrunch, citing The New York Times, said Stripe outbid other interested buyers, including Databricks, and reported that OpenRouter’s founders would receive $1.5 billion from the sale, with investors receiving the remaining $6 billion [1]. Digitimes reported separately that Bloomberg and Axios had described Stripe as being in acquisition talks for more than $7 billion, more than five times OpenRouter’s May 2026 valuation [10].

Key Points

  • Routing logic: OpenRouter evaluates task complexity, price, speed and reliability, then routes a request to a suitable model and provider endpoint [4].
  • Model access: Stripe said OpenRouter supports more than 400 models from over 80 providers, while Silicon Republic reported access to more than 500 large language models [4][6].
  • Cost spread: In a June 2026 example, OpenRouter listed Llama 3.3 70B input pricing at $0.10 per million tokens through DeepInfra and $1.04 through Together [4].

Technical Details

  • Dual routing: OpenRouter separates model selection from endpoint selection, so a request can first choose an appropriate model and then choose among providers serving that model [4].
  • Rolling telemetry: The platform measures latency and throughput for model-provider combinations with rolling performance data, so customers can route requests by cost and performance requirements rather than a fixed provider [4].
  • Endpoint constraints: Customers can set maximum prices or minimum performance levels and prioritize endpoints by price, throughput or latency [4].

Business Angle

  • At the reported price, Stripe would be paying several times OpenRouter’s May 2026 valuation, a multiple that could be harder to justify if AI model routing becomes commoditized or model providers improve their own switching tools [1][common].
  • Cost and latency comparisons across providers could be important for AI businesses whose gross margins depend on inference costs [4][common].
