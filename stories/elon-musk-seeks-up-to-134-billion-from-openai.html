Elon Musk seeks up to $134 billion from OpenAI

Category: Technology

Elon Musk is asking a US court to award him damages that his filing values as high as $134 billion from OpenAI and Microsoft, tied to what he alleges were “wrongful gains” after OpenAI moved away from its original nonprofit mission [2][4][5]. The latest court filing argues Musk deserves a share of the value created after he provided early backing and other startup help, including about $38 million in seed funding and recruiting and networking assistance [2]. The dispute is part of a lawsuit Musk first brought in March 2024 and later expanded to include Microsoft, with the case now spotlighting how AI labs balance mission, governance, and commercialization as they scale [2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

This is the only chance we have to get out from Elon

— Greg Brockman (Tom's Hardware)

Did you know: Engadget reports Musk said he contributed roughly 60% of OpenAI’s nonprofit seed funding and provided about $38 million during the startup years [2].

Key Points

  • Expert calculations: Musk’s filing relies on analysis by financial economist C. Paul Wazzan, who estimates OpenAI’s alleged “wrongful gains” at $65.5–$109.43 billion and Microsoft’s at $13.3–$25.06 billion [2].
  • Valuation anchor: The damages theory references OpenAI’s reported recent valuation of about $500 billion as part of the basis for calculating what Musk claims he is owed [2].
  • Governance backstory: Tom’s Hardware reports that notes attributed to OpenAI cofounder Greg Brockman describe discussions with Sam Altman about restructuring and separating from Musk’s influence, including the line: “This is the only chance we have to get out from Elon” [3].
  • Parallel complaint: Separately from the nonprofit-status lawsuit, Musk has also accused OpenAI and Apple of monopolistic practices that he says disadvantage xAI in the App Store [2].
  • Wealth context: TechCrunch notes Musk is seeking up to $134B in the case even as it cites his fortune at roughly $700B [5].

Perspectives

Elon Musk: Musk argues OpenAI abandoned its nonprofit purpose and that he should be compensated for value he says was built from early contributions and alleged “wrongful gains” shared with Microsoft [2][4][5].

Timeline

  • March 2024: Musk filed lawsuit alleging OpenAI violated nonprofit status [2].
  • Later: Musk added Microsoft and sought an injunction over reorganization efforts [2].

Technical Details

  • Nonprofit status: A nonprofit structure generally means an organization is governed for a stated mission rather than to distribute profits to owners, a distinction at the heart of Musk’s claim that OpenAI changed course [2][common].
  • Damages model: The filing’s damages claim uses an expert’s estimate of alleged “wrongful gains” attributed to OpenAI and Microsoft as the basis for monetary relief [2].
  • App Store gatekeeping: Musk’s separate complaint characterizes the App Store as a distribution bottleneck where platform rules can affect AI app reach and competitive access [2][common].

Industry Impact

  • AI governance: The filing and reported internal notes keep attention on how AI organizations design governance when moving from a nonprofit origin toward more commercial structures [2][3].
  • Big Tech partnerships: Because Microsoft is named as a defendant and included in the claimed “wrongful gains,” the case may influence how future AI lab–platform partnerships allocate rights, oversight, and economics [2].
  • Startup funding norms: The dispute spotlights how early “seed” contributions—cash, recruiting help, and introductions—can become central evidence later when a company’s valuation grows dramatically [2][5].

Historical Background

Musk’s lawsuit dates back to March 2024, when he sued OpenAI alleging it violated its nonprofit status, and he later added Microsoft as a defendant as OpenAI pursued a corporate reorganization [2]. The new damages filing frames his early support—financial and operational—as the starting point for claims to a share of later value creation [2][4].
