AI researcher Andrew Tulloch leaves Meta for Anthropic

Category: AI

Andrew Tulloch, a prominent AI researcher who rejoined Meta less than a year ago, is leaving for Anthropic. An Anthropic spokesperson told Business Insider he will work on the startup’s inference and performance team, optimizing model training and how models respond to users [3][4][5]. Semafor first reported his departure from Meta, and Business Insider reported that he will start at Anthropic next week [3]. Tulloch spent 11 years at Meta before leaving in 2023 for OpenAI, where he helped train GPT-4o, GPT-4.5 and o3. He co-founded Thinking Machines Lab in 2025 before returning to Meta in October [3][4][5]. The Wall Street Journal previously reported that Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg offered Tulloch a package that could have totaled $1.5 billion; Meta called that characterization inaccurate, and Axios later reported that the actual package was lower [3][4][5]. Tulloch’s departure followed Meta’s launch of Muse, an AI agent intended to complete tasks for users. Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, Google and other companies continue to compete for experienced AI researchers [3][2][4][5]. Meta and Tulloch did not immediately respond to Business Insider’s requests for comment [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: In 2016, OpenAI tried to recruit Tulloch, but Logos Press said he declined because the offered salary was about three times lower than his then income of $800,000 [6].

Key Points

  • Muse timing: Semafor reported that Tulloch waited until Meta shipped Muse before leaving, and TNW said Muse launched on Tuesday before the departure report on Wednesday [4].
  • Product scope: Meta described Muse as a personal AI agent that can handle email, bookings, shopping and forms after users authorize links to apps such as email, calendars, payments and smart-home services [5].
  • Lab role: Tulloch worked in Meta’s TBD Lab, described by Semafor as the center of Meta’s AI operation and led by Scale AI founder Alexandr Wang [2][4].
  • Reliability concerns: Cnyes cited Reuters as saying Meta internal testing found login problems, failed task execution, monitoring interruptions and cases where an AI agent could bypass some safety restrictions and access private data [5].
  • Talent churn: Cnyes said Tulloch is the third Thinking Machines Lab co-founder recently to change roles, with two others moving to Google and Meta [5].

Perspectives

The Next Web: TNW said it had not independently verified Tulloch’s move and noted that his personal website still listed him at Meta’s TBD Lab on Thursday.

Technical Details

  • ResNet-50 training: Tulloch helped reduce ResNet-50 training on ImageNet to about one hour using 256 GPUs with about 90% scaling efficiency [5].
  • Muse Spark: Cnyes said Meta’s Muse uses the Muse Spark model family, with Muse Spark 1.2 released in August for coding, multimodal understanding and tool use [5].
  • FBGEMM: Tulloch previously worked on FBGEMM, a high-performance compute operator library used for recommendation systems, quantized computation and generative AI workloads [5].

Industry Impact

  • AI safety incidents: Reuters reported that Meta's internal AI-related technical and safety incidents increased about 40% year-over-year, with staff time spent handling them up about 70% [5].
  • Consumer agents: Muse is available first in the United States through a standalone app and WhatsApp, with a free basic tier and paid plans priced at $20 and $100 per month [5].
  • Infrastructure spending: Cnyes cited Reuters as saying Meta expects AI chip and related infrastructure spending to exceed $130 billion this year [5].

Historical Background

During his first stint at Meta, Tulloch rose from machine-learning engineer to distinguished engineer [5]. Meta later tried to buy Thinking Machines Lab, then approached individual employees after Murati rejected the acquisition effort, according to reports cited by TNW and Cnyes [4][5].
