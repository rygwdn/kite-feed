Country music icon Dolly Parton dies at 80

Category: Canada

Dolly Parton died Tuesday, Aug. 25, in Nashville at age 80, her publicist Marcel Pariseau said [5][8][27][37]. Her nephew Bryan Seaver announced her death in a video posted to social media [2][19][36]. Parton was a singer-songwriter, actor, entrepreneur and philanthropist whose career spanned more than six decades and included songs such as “Jolene,” “Coat of Many Colors,” “9 to 5” and “I Will Always Love You” [5][8][14][32]. Canadian coverage noted tributes in Ontario and around the world after her family announced her death, while La Presse described her as an American songwriter, actor, businesswoman and philanthropist who became an absolute country music legend [2][7].

Key Points

  • Health concerns: Parton canceled or postponed Las Vegas performances in 2026 while discussing treatment for immune and digestive system problems and other health issues [10][19][21][27].
  • Recorded legacy: Her songs and recordings sold more than 100 million worldwide, and AP cited more than 1 billion online streams for classics including “Jolene” and “I Will Always Love You” [5][8][14].
  • Literacy program: Parton founded the Imagination Library to send free books to children, an effort tied to her father’s struggles with reading after leaving school to work on a farm [5][8][14].
  • Career honours: Reuters reported that Parton won 11 Grammy Awards, including a Lifetime Achievement Award, in a career that also included television, film and the Dollywood theme park [32][58].

Historical Background

Parton was born Jan. 19, 1946, in rural Tennessee and grew up as one of 12 children in a family she described as “dirt poor” [5][8][57]. She appeared on the Grand Ole Opry at 13 and moved to Nashville after graduating high school in 1964 [5][8][14]. Her work with Porter Wagoner’s television show in the late 1960s helped bring her to a national country music audience [31][57].
