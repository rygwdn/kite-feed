CES 2026 showcases practical AI gadgets, fresh form factors

Category: Technology

CES 2026 in Las Vegas wrapped up with a mix of practical upgrades and playful experimentation across consumer tech, from repair-friendlier laptops and more capable robot vacuums to a renewed wave of smart glasses and unusual concept gadgets [3][6][2][4]. Multiple outlets highlighted a swing toward devices that try to save time (cleaning, cooking, home management) and improve everyday usability, even as AI messaging dominated many booths and keynotes [2][12][31]. The show also surfaced a small but noticeable “phone comeback,” including foldables and a hardware-keyboard throwback from Clicks aimed at people who miss tactile typing [1][52][10]. At the same time, watchdog groups and some commentators raised concerns that parts of CES leaned into overcomplicated, privacy-invasive, or hard-to-repair designs, underscoring the tradeoffs that come with smarter devices [43][12].

Did you know: Engadget’s Best of CES 2026 “Best of the Best” winner was Lego Smart Play, which it said avoids smartphone pairing and screens while using a “Smart Brick” with a tiny 4.1mm ASIC chip to react to how you build and play [13].

Key Points

  • Robot vacuum focus: Robot vacuum brands emphasized stronger cleaning and better navigation, with Roborock introducing Saros 20 variants (including a “Sonic” model that uses a VibraRise flat pad with sonic edge scrubbing) and launching its first roller-mop robot vacuum, Qrevo Curv Flow 2 [2].
  • AR glasses leap: Xreal previewed two new AR glasses and pitched a gaming-focused model it says is the first micro-LED smart glasses with a 240 Hz refresh rate, alongside a free “Real 3D” over-the-air feature that converts 2D content into 3D on supported models [4].
  • Laptop repairability: CES laptop coverage pointed to mainstream makers paying more attention to easier repairs and component replacement in enterprise machines, with Dell, HP, and Lenovo cited as pushing more modular approaches even if they still trail Framework’s repair-first reputation [6].
  • Smart home practicality: Engadget spotlighted “considered” smart-home hardware such as GE’s Profile Smart Fridge, which uses a small eight-inch display, a built-in barcode reader, and an AI assistant aimed at practical tasks like tracking filters and produce [16].
  • Back-to-keys phones: Clicks discussed a $79 magnetic “Power Keyboard” accessory (Qi2 attachment, Bluetooth, integrated battery) and a small Communicator phone concept with a physical keyboard, explicitly targeting people who prefer tactile typing over all-screen slabs [52].

Technical Details

  • Qi2 magnetic attachment: Clicks’ Power Keyboard is designed to magnetically attach to phones that support Qi2 and connect via Bluetooth, letting it act like a slide-out keyboard and a small battery pack [52].
  • 240 Hz refresh rate: Xreal’s upcoming ROG-branded AR gaming glasses are described as micro-LED smart glasses with a 240 Hz refresh rate, a spec aimed at smoother motion for fast-moving content like games [4].
  • Sonic edge scrubbing: Roborock’s Saros 20 Sonic shifts from spinning pads to a flat VibraRise-style mopping pad with sonic scrubbing along edges, aiming to improve hard-to-reach cleaning performance [2].

Industry Impact

  • Wearable display competition: Crowded smart-glasses aisles at CES pushed brands like Xreal to compete on measurable display specs (refresh rate) and software features (OTA 3D conversion), signaling faster iteration in AR wearables [4].
  • Home robotics realism: Coverage suggested robot vacuums are staying the most practical home robot category, with brands prioritizing navigation and cleaning performance over flashy add-ons—an indicator that the market rewards reliability [2].
  • Repairability pressure: The prominence of “Worst in Show” criticism from repair and rights groups adds pressure on manufacturers to improve repair access and reduce e-waste, potentially influencing design choices and consumer expectations over time [43].
  • Smart home interoperability: IKEA’s CES announcements centered on Matter-aligned smart-home products (including a low-cost Matter bulb), reinforcing a broader industry shift toward cross-brand compatibility as a selling point [38].

Historical Background

CES, held annually in Las Vegas, is a long-running consumer electronics trade show where major brands and startups preview upcoming products and prototypes [common]. Recent CES cycles have increasingly emphasized AI across product categories, and CES 2026 coverage again noted heavy AI messaging alongside more concrete hardware updates like new PCs, wearables, and home robots [12][31]. The show has also become a recurring forum for debates over repairability, privacy, and sustainability as more devices add sensors, subscriptions, and cloud features [43][35].

Q&A

Q: What’s the main difference between Roborock’s Saros 20 and Saros 20 Sonic?

A: Mashable says the “Sonic” model switches to a VibraRise-style flat mopping pad system with sonic scrubbing along edges, rather than relying on spinning mopping pads [2].

Q: When are Xreal’s ROG AR gaming glasses expected to ship?

A: Mashable reports the ROG Xreal R1 glasses are slated to arrive later in 2026, while the Xreal 1S is available now [4].

Q: Why did some critics single out CES gadgets for sustainability concerns?

A: Engadget noted sustainability tech felt relatively sparse at the show, while the “Worst in Show” awards highlighted products they say add unnecessary e-waste and complexity, including a single-use music-playing lollipop [35][43].
