B.C. wildfire evacuees return as 6,000 properties remain under evacuation orders

Category: Canada

Some British Columbia wildfire evacuees began returning home after officials downgraded evacuation orders in the Okanagan and Thompson-Nicola regions, including 400 properties in Summerland’s Trout Creek neighbourhood on Friday afternoon [1][11]. Other evacuees returned to assess damage and begin rebuilding after fires forced people from their homes across affected communities [6][9][13]. The Bald Range wildfire continued to burn out of control. Properties in the Regional District of Okanagan-Similkameen, the District of Summerland and the Snpink’tn Indian Band remained under evacuation orders [1]. Global News reported that the Bald Range wildfire forced more than 18,000 people from their homes [12][2]. Returning residents found smoke, ash and burned debris on properties in Summerland, and officials said some areas west of Clinton remained under evacuation orders because of the continued threat from the Pear Lake wildfire [1][6]. Le Devoir reported that many evacuees had lost everything and had nowhere to stay [13].

Key Points

  • Partial returns: The Clinton-area downgrade covered specific corridors and communities, including sections of Highway 97, Clinton-Pavillion Road, Big Bar Lake, Beaverdam Lake, Chasm and Meadow Lake [11].
  • Older evacuees: The Star reported that some older evacuees faced communication and mobility challenges, including one Summerland couple who said they did not have a phone when neighbours woke them during the evacuation [10].
  • Migrant workers: Canada’s National Observer reported that wildfire evacuations left some migrant farm and ranch workers in British Columbia facing uncertainty, with advocates calling for stronger provincial and federal support [8].

Perspectives

Christine Coletta, Summerland winery owner: Coletta said Andrew Peller winery housed nine temporary foreign workers and handled paperwork to transfer work permits so they could keep working during the wildfire crisis.

Sources: Vancouver Sun

Timeline

  • 7 August 2026: Winery owners received a smoke warning and activated fire plans [2].
  • 14 August 2026: The Trout Creek order for 400 properties was downgraded to an alert [1].
  • 15 August 2026: The Clinton-area downgrade took effect for about 373 properties [11].
  • 15 August 2026: Trainer returned to ash, debris and smoke odour [1].

Humanitarian Impact

Evacuees arriving in Okanagan communities needed shelter, food and basic necessities after leaving suddenly, while hundreds of volunteers and groups including Mamas for Mamas organized donated goods [12].

Action Items

  • Confirm your property status before returning: Check relevant local evacuation maps because the Clinton-area downgrade applied only to specific roads and communities, while other areas remained under orders [11].
  • Prepare for smoke and debris at home: Expect ash, burned vegetation, surface residue and lingering smoke odour if returning to properties near the Bald Range fire zone [1].
