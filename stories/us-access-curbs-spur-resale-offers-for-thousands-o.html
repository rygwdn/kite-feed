U.S. access curbs spur resale offers for thousands of Nvidia AI chips

Category: AI

Sellers are seeking buyers for an unusually large volume of Nvidia graphics processors after new U.S. rules restricted Chinese companies’ remote access to advanced AI computing in overseas data centers. Offers include thousands of B300 chips, but no completed transactions were reported [17]. The rule took effect Nov. 19 after the Commerce Department announced it on Nov. 16 [17][16]. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang returned to the company after an 11-day absence as scrutiny of Chinese access to its chips continued [18][51]. Lenders are also reassessing or delaying data-center financing in Southeast Asia, where facilities serve Chinese technology customers [35]. The reported resale offers involve hardware originally intended for data centers renting computing capacity to Chinese clients. Nvidia declined to comment on the offers, and the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security did not immediately respond to CNBC [17]. Existing data-center services covered by the rule have a 90-day window to seek required licenses [16].

Key Points

  • Delivery delays: A report citing supply-chain sources put lead times for new B300 orders at 26 to 40 weeks and waits for GB300 orders at 14 to 26 weeks, with some orders potentially arriving in the second or third quarter of 2027 [63].
  • Export enforcement: The U.S. added Singapore-based data-center company Megaspeed to its trade blacklist; the company has been accused of transferring Nvidia chips to China and denies the allegation [51][87].
  • Loan exposure: Lenders are reviewing a proposed $6.6 billion loan for Malaysia’s Bridge Data Centres, while a bank passed on a $372 million Digital Halo loan and lenders reassessed a $480 million Aolani loan [35].
  • Industry supply: Morningstar analysts said Huang’s return may reduce the risk of disruption to Nvidia’s product roadmap, but warned that normal operations are not assured [10].
  • Talent competition: Carnegie Endowment research cited by CNBC found China’s share of global AI talent surpassed the United States’ in three years; experts said Singapore and South Korea could benefit if engineers move between countries [3].
  • Chip alternatives: OpenAI has pursued AMD GPUs and custom chips, while Anthropic uses accelerators from Google and Amazon; Bernstein projects custom accelerators’ market share will rise from 10% this year to 20% in 2027 [7][8].
  • Supplier compliance: Analysts expect Taiwanese manufacturers to face higher compliance costs, longer approval procedures and greater legal risks as they are asked to identify end customers and track product use [70].
  • New oversight: Nvidia’s board created an independent National Security and Export Control Committee, and the company is seeking a chief compliance officer to oversee compliance throughout the company [70][10].

Technical Details

  • Aggregate processing threshold: The rule applies to covered chips with a combined total processing performance (TPP) of more than 12 million at one site or across linked facilities when computing is supplied to specified restricted entities [16].

Industry Impact

  • Taiwan market rebound: After Huang returned, Nvidia shares rose 31.5% and Taiwan’s TAIEX gained 9%, following a severe sell-off [70].
  • Policy outlook: Analysts said Huang’s return was unlikely to ease U.S. export restrictions on China, with congressional momentum for tighter controls continuing [57].

Historical Background

The United States introduced sweeping restrictions on advanced chip exports to China in October 2022 and tightened them a year later to cover more chips and prevent suppliers from designing products just below performance limits [21]. In January 2025, the Biden administration extended licensing requirements globally, but the Trump administration said in May it would not enforce that rule. Reuters reported that the gap allowed overseas data-center capacity to grow in places such as Singapore [21].
