US protests and strike target Minneapolis ICE operation

Category: World

Protests and walkouts spread across the United States on Friday as activists called for a “no work, no school, no shopping” blackout to oppose the Trump administration’s stepped-up immigration enforcement, which has drawn intense scrutiny after fatal shootings involving federal agents in Minneapolis [2][3][4][7]. Much of the organizing has centered on Minnesota, where demonstrators have demanded that federal immigration agents withdraw after a nearly month-long operation that sent thousands of agents into the state [8][6][7]. Alongside the street protests, the US Justice Department opened a federal civil rights investigation into the killing of Alex Pretti, a Minneapolis resident who was shot by Border Patrol officers during the enforcement push, with officials saying the FBI would lead the investigation [9][11][10][12]. The White House also signaled possible operational changes, with border czar Tom Homan telling reporters authorities were working on a “drawdown” of federal personnel in Minnesota, even as President Trump publicly criticized Pretti in social media posts after a video circulated online [1][17][15].

Key Points

  • Other fatalities: Organizers and coverage of the protests also pointed to additional deaths linked to encounters with federal agents elsewhere, including Keith Porter in Los Angeles and Silverio Villegas González in Illinois, as part of the broader backlash [8].
  • Student organizers: Student leaders, including organizers tied to the University of Minnesota, promoted coordinated walkouts and a consumer boycott as a way to apply economic pressure for policy change and accountability [2][8][7].
  • Funding standoff: The Minneapolis shootings spilled into federal budget politics, with Democrats pressing to remove or renegotiate Department of Homeland Security funding to add constraints on enforcement as the Senate moved toward a vote to avert a government shutdown [17].
  • Video dispute: A video shared online purporting to show Pretti scuffling with agents days before his death became a flashpoint, though at least one outlet reported it could not immediately verify the footage [17][16].
  • Sustained resistance: Minnesota has seen repeated mass mobilization tied to “Operation Metro Surge,” with reporting describing multiple general strikes and some of the most sustained street resistance of President Trump’s second term [5].

Perspectives

Protest organizers: Organizers urged a Friday “blackout” and nationwide walkouts, calling for ICE to leave Minnesota and arguing that nonviolent economic disruption can push accountability and reform.

Technical Details

  • Federal civil rights probe: A DOJ civil rights investigation examines whether law enforcement actions unlawfully deprived someone of constitutional rights; officials said the FBI would lead the probe into Pretti’s death [12][9].
  • Border enforcement surge: Reporting described a large-scale federal deployment in Minnesota tied to an immigration crackdown, with 3,000 agents deployed across the state and discussion of a potential drawdown [1].
  • General strike tactics: Organizers framed the “no work, no school, no shopping” blackout as a coordinated work stoppage and consumer boycott designed to apply economic pressure without violence [2][8].

Industry Impact

  • Local commerce: Organizers explicitly urged people not to shop during the Friday blackout, aiming to translate protest energy into measurable pressure on businesses and local economic activity [2][8].
  • Higher education: University-linked student organizing and calls for walkouts could disrupt campus operations and highlight the role of universities as hubs for civic mobilization [2][7].
  • Federal security agencies: Mixed signals about continuing or scaling back the Minneapolis operation and the opening of an FBI-led probe could affect training, oversight, and public-facing protocols for future enforcement actions [1][12].

Historical Background

US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) was created in 2003 as part of the Department of Homeland Security after the September 11 attacks reshaped US internal security structures [common]. In recent years, US immigration enforcement has repeatedly triggered large protests and litigation, with public debate often focusing on the balance between border enforcement, due process, and community safety [common].
