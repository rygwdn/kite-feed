Environment Canada issues rain warning for Metro Vancouver

Category: Canada

Environment Canada has issued rain and snow-related warnings for parts of British Columbia, with Metro Vancouver expecting a soaking beginning Monday night and continuing into Tuesday [1][3]. Forecasts call for roughly 30–40 mm in central Vancouver, with higher totals nearer the North Shore mountains and the potential for precipitation to switch to snow at higher elevations as freezing levels fluctuate [1]. For many residents, it’s a familiar West Coast mix—wet in the city, wintry up high—while ski areas anticipate fresh snowfall that could be a bright spot for mountain recreation once conditions settle [1].

Key Points

  • Warning levels: Environment Canada described the rainfall as “significant” and issued a yellow rainfall warning—its lowest of three colour-coded warning levels [1].
  • Where it hits: The rain is forecast to start near midnight, then intensify into heavier downpours Tuesday morning before easing later in the day, affecting Vancouver, Burnaby, New Westminster, the North Shore, and northeast Metro areas like Coquitlam and Maple Ridge [1].
  • Mountain snow: Local forecasts cited by the story suggest Grouse Mountain could see 5–10 cm overnight Monday and another 15–20 cm Tuesday morning, while Mt. Seymour Resort projected 13 cm Monday night and 19 cm Tuesday morning before tapering to snow showers [1].
  • Sea to Sky: A yellow snowfall warning also covers the Sea to Sky Highway from Squamish through Whistler, with travelers advised to plan for winter driving conditions [1].
  • Road hazards: Drivers are urged to watch for water pooling on roads during the heavier rain periods [1].
