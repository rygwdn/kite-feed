NASA and Asian agencies advance lunar exploration plans

Category: Science

NASA plans to award three more Commercial Lunar Payload Services task orders by the end of 2026: two lander missions and an orbiter imaging service that company executives described as a replacement for the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter [5]. Firefly Aerospace chief executive Jason Kim referred to the planned orbiter as an “orbiter imaging service,” while Intuitive Machines chief executive Steve Altemus said NASA expected a task order later this summer or in early fall for an “orbiter surveyor” to map the moon [5]. NASA also plans to send three small rovers to the moon with a single broad instruction to decide among themselves how to explore a patch of terrain [3]. The Hindu reported that NASA has invited ISRO to participate in a lunar program aimed at building a human outpost near the moon’s south pole, where longer sunlight periods and permanently shadowed craters with water ice deposits are central to site planning [4]. South Korea’s space agency is pursuing a cislunar space science probe for launch in 2031, aiming to operate beyond low Earth orbit, gain precise orbit-insertion capability and build independent space-environment observation capacity [6]. The agency has framed cislunar operations as the first step in a broader roadmap that later extends to Sun-Earth Lagrange point 4 exploration around 2035 [6].

Key Points

  • Commercial pathway: NASA has used CLPS for lunar missions since issuing the first task orders in 2019, and landers have accounted for nearly all missions under the program so far [5].
  • Korean capability: South Korea has experience with low Earth orbit satellites and a lunar orbiter, but not with a probe that travels long distances and then autonomously enters a precise target orbit [6].
