Bangladesh opposition BNP wins landslide, Tarique Rahman set

Category: World

Bangladesh’s Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP) and its allies appear to have won a decisive parliamentary majority in the country’s first national election since the 2024 youth-led uprising that removed longtime leader Sheikh Hasina [35][24][31][32]. Multiple outlets said the BNP-led bloc cleared the 151-seat threshold in the 300-seat Jatiya Sangsad. Reuters and others cited projections of about 209 seats—enough for a commanding mandate—while official results were still being compiled [35][32][11]. Tarique Rahman, the BNP chairman who returned to Bangladesh in December after roughly 17 years in exile, is now positioned to become prime minister, bringing the party back to power after about two decades [23][12][31]. Several sources cast the vote as an early measure of Bangladesh’s democratic reset—and its ability to steady itself after months of unrest that disrupted daily life and key industries [31][32]. Still, the contest drew criticism and friction: Hasina called the election not credible, and some accounts reported isolated violence during the campaign and on polling day [27][18]. Early signals from abroad suggest key partners want to work quickly with the incoming leadership if the handover stays peaceful—an early hint that regional diplomacy may shift from crisis management toward practical cooperation [5][17].

Key Points

  • Seat picture: Broadcasters and wire reports projected the BNP-led coalition winning roughly two-thirds of the 300 seats (about 209). Other tallies put BNP in the low 180s and Jamaat-e-Islami in the low 60s as counting continued [35][32][30].
  • Turnout signals: Projections cited by Reuters from local media suggest turnout could top the 42% recorded in the previous election cycle, with some reports expecting more than 60% of registered voters to have cast ballots [33].
  • Competing blocs: The race largely pitted coalitions led by the BNP and Jamaat-e-Islami—former allies—with some reporting showing Jamaat losing ground as the BNP pulled ahead [33][8][10].
  • Process details: Accounts described a tightly managed election day during a national holiday period. Officials began counting after polls closed, with clearer trends expected overnight into Friday morning [29][33].
  • Reform track: Alongside the parliamentary vote, Bangladesh also held a referendum on constitutional changes, with some reporting describing reforms meant to reshape institutions and strengthen checks and balances [28][15].

Perspectives

Sheikh Hasina: Hasina denounced the election as a “well-planned farce,” alleging negligible turnout in many polling centres and calling for “free, fair and inclusive” polls.

Timeline

  • 2024: Youth-led uprising topples Sheikh Hasina, leaving many dead [12][32].
  • Christmas Day 2025: Tarique Rahman returns to Bangladesh after a roughly 17-year exile [12].
  • February 12, 2026: Bangladesh holds parliamentary election and constitutional referendum [28][22].
  • Friday morning (Feb. 13, 2026): Broadcasters project BNP victory as counting continues [31][32].

Historical Background

Several accounts described the Feb. 12 vote as a step out of the interim period and back toward an elected parliament, with the Awami League absent from the ballot in some reporting and the contest framed largely around BNP- and Jamaat-led alliances [14][33]. The vote took place under heightened security and during a multi-day national holiday, with polling hours set from morning to late afternoon [29]. Officials began counting as polls closed, and election officials and local reports said clearer trends would likely emerge around midnight and into Friday morning as results were compiled [33].
