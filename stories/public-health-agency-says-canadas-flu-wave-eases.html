Public Health Agency says Canada’s flu wave eases

Category: Canada

Canada’s influenza wave appears to have peaked over the winter holidays, with national surveillance showing test positivity at a three-season high during late December before beginning to trend downward in early January [1][2][4][5][3]. Health officials and physicians say activity remains elevated even as indicators stabilize or improve, and they continue to encourage vaccination and everyday precautions to reduce spread and severe illness [2][4][5]. Some regions are still feeling the strain in hospitals, with Eastern Ontario facilities reporting intense pressure during the surge [6].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: In Canadian hospital emergency colour codes, “Code Orange” is used to signal a disaster situation [6].

Key Points

  • Hospital strain: Eastern Ontario hospitals reported severe operational pressure during the surge, and Perth and Smiths Falls District Hospital declared a Code Orange (a hospital disaster code) just before New Year’s Day [6].
  • Reported deaths: Health Canada said 74 deaths had been reported this year by participating provinces and territories, while Public Health Ontario reported six deaths during the first week of January [6].
  • Reporting window: PHAC’s latest national snapshot covered Dec. 14 to Jan. 3, and it described new detected cases as remaining high but “stable or decreased” [4][5].
  • Season comparison: PHAC data cited by The Globe and Mail show the previous high positivity rate over the last three viral seasons was 27.6% in 2024-25, underscoring how intense this winter’s peak has been [2].

  • Code Orange: A Code Orange is a hospital emergency colour code that indicates “disaster,” used when an organization faces extreme strain or disruption [6].
  • National surveillance window: PHAC’s weekly influenza reporting aggregates lab and other surveillance inputs over defined periods (e.g., Dec. 14 to Jan. 3 in the latest update) to track trends across Canada [4][5].
