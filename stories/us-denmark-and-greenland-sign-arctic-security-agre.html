U.S., Denmark and Greenland sign Arctic security agreement

Category: World

President Donald Trump, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenlandic Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen signed a security agreement Sept. 22 at the United Nations in New York. The agreement permits a larger U.S. military presence in Greenland while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenland’s right to self-determination [6][7][15]. It allows Washington to expand Pituffik Space Base and establish defense areas at Narsarsuaq in southern Greenland and Mestersvig on the east coast. Trump said the United States would develop a significant military presence and build two major bases [15][18][4]. The pact falls short of Trump’s demand that Greenland become U.S. territory, but it bars non-NATO countries from establishing military bases or making sensitive investments without the parties’ approval. It remains in effect if Greenland later becomes independent [7][12]. The agreement amends the 1951 U.S.-Denmark defense arrangement following months of tension over Trump’s threats to acquire the autonomous Danish territory [15][13][dw.com#2].

Did you know: During the Cold War, the United States maintained 17 military facilities in Greenland. Before this agreement, it had one active installation and roughly 200 troops [23][35].

Key Points

  • NATO continuity: If Greenland becomes independent, the agreement says Denmark and Greenland will seek to keep the future state inside NATO [40][66].
  • Base history: Narsarsuaq was formerly the U.S. Bluie West One airbase, while Mestersvig currently hosts Denmark’s Sirius Dog Sled Patrol [49].

Technical Details

  • Defense-area structure: Any of the three parties may propose additional defense areas, subject to the other parties’ approval. The agreement does not expire [7].
  • Missile defense: The pact authorizes U.S. military access needed to establish a Golden Dome defense system intended to counter ballistic-missile threats [12][29].
