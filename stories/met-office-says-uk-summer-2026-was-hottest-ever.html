Met Office says UK summer 2026 was hottest ever

Category: Science

The UK recorded its hottest summer since records began, with provisional Met Office data showing a June-August mean temperature of 16.5C in records dating to 1884 [2][5][7][9][13]. The figure topped the previous record of 16.1C set in 2025, making 2026 the second consecutive record-breaking UK summer [9][13][14]. Met Office scientists said human-induced climate change made a summer as hot as 2026 about 130 times more likely. Mark McCarthy, the agency's head of climate attribution, said such a summer would occur roughly once every 1,000 years without human-generated greenhouse gases, compared with about once every nine years in the current climate [7][12][13]. The season brought repeated heatwaves, dry weather and drought conditions. England, Wales and Northern Ireland each set summer mean-temperature records, while Scotland had its joint-sixth warmest summer [5][2][9].

Did you know: Only nine UK temperature readings above 37C had been recorded before 2026; three occurred this year [25].

Key Points

  • Persistent heat: The UK recorded 40 days above 30C, beating the previous record of 34 days, after five widespread heatwaves [5][25][18].
  • Peak temperature: Kew Gardens in London recorded 38.1C on August 13, provisionally the UK's fifth-hottest day by maximum temperature [5][2][9][25].
  • Dry conditions: England recorded its driest July, while drought was declared across large parts of the country [12][13].
  • Recent concentration: The five warmest UK summers have all occurred since 2003; 1976 now ranks seventh by mean summer temperature [7][2][12][10].

Perspectives

Public services: The Guardian reported that persistent heat affected education, health services and transport.

  • Wildfire response: ARY News carried a Reuters report saying the season tested wildfire-fighting capacity; Energy Live News reported that 370 acres burned at Dunwich Heath in Suffolk [7][15].

Historical Background

The all-time UK maximum temperature record remains 40.3C, measured at Coningsby, Lincolnshire, in July 2022 [5].
