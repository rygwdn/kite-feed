Eastern Canada ice storm strains 911, knocks out power

Category: Canada

A winter storm brought freezing rain, blowing snow and strong winds across parts of Eastern Canada on Monday, creating hazardous sidewalks and roads, especially around Montreal [1][5][6]. Montreal’s Urgences-santé reported a sharp spike in emergency demand, at times fielding about 100 calls an hour, many tied to slips and falls on ice [5]. The same system also left tens of thousands of Ontario electricity customers without power, highlighting how quickly winter weather can ripple from transportation to essential services [1][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Some Montrealers used hiking poles for extra stability on ice-coated sidewalks during the storm [5].

Key Points

  • Hospital pressure: At least seven people with fractures or concussions arrived at an overcrowded downtown Montreal hospital ER after icy conditions, adding strain during a flu season that the paper says is already above last year’s peak in Quebec and linked to a new strain [7].
  • Stay-home advice: Urgences-santé urged residents to stay home unless travel is absolutely essential as treacherous streets and sidewalks drove a peak in 911 calls [8].
  • Transit disruption: The Réseau express métropolitain (REM) had two weather-related interruptions Monday morning between Deux-Montagnes and Bois-Franc before service returned to normal later in the day [9].
  • Storm footprint: Forecast coverage extended from Ontario through Quebec into Atlantic Canada, including Newfoundland and Labrador, as the system pushed a mix of freezing rain, wind and blowing snow eastward [6].

Technical Details

  • Freezing rain: Liquid precipitation that freezes on contact, creating an ice glaze that makes walking and driving hazardous [common].
  • Ice-storm outages: Ice accumulation and strong winds can weigh down or snap branches and power lines, which can trigger widespread electricity outages [common].

Industry Impact

  • Emergency services: The spike in 911 demand in Montreal shows how quickly icy conditions can overwhelm dispatch and ambulance capacity, especially when many calls involve falls on untreated or ice-coated surfaces [5][8].
  • Electric utilities: Tens of thousands of Ontario customers lost electricity during the storm, underlining the operational challenge of restoring power safely in high wind and ice conditions [1][5].
  • Public transit reliability: Weather-related REM interruptions illustrate how ice and wind events can disrupt automated rapid transit operations and push more travellers toward road traffic or remote work for a day [9].

Historical Background

Freezing rain forms when snow melts into rain in a warm layer aloft and then re-freezes on contact with surfaces that are below 0°C, quickly coating sidewalks, trees and power lines in ice [common]. In Eastern Canada, these storms routinely create a double challenge: sudden slip-and-fall injury risk in cities and potential outages when ice loads and wind stress overhead wires [common].

Q&A

Q: What kinds of incidents drove many of the Montreal emergency calls during the storm?

A: Urgences-santé and other reporting linked a large share of calls to people falling and injuring themselves on ice-coated sidewalks [5][8].

Q: Which REM segment was specifically affected by freezing-rain disruptions?

A: Service issues were reported between the Deux-Montagnes and Bois-Franc stations [9].

Q: How did the storm intersect with Quebec’s broader health situation this week?

A: The Montreal Gazette reported that Quebec’s flu wave was already above last year’s peak and associated with a new strain, adding context to concerns about ER crowding [7].

Action Items

  • Reduce slip risk on short trips: If you must go out, use traction aids (or stability supports like poles) and choose well-salted routes where possible [5].
  • Help keep 911 available: Follow Urgences-santé’s guidance to stay home unless travel is essential so emergency lines remain available for urgent cases [8].
  • Plan around transit interruptions: Before commuting, check REM service alerts and be ready to switch to an alternate route if disruptions occur [9].
