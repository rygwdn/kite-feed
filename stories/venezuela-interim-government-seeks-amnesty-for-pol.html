Venezuela interim government seeks amnesty for political detainees

Category: World

Venezuela’s acting president, Delcy Rodríguez, announced plans to send a “general amnesty” bill to the National Assembly that could free hundreds of people detained for political reasons, including opposition figures, journalists, and human rights activists [3][6][8][1]. Rodríguez framed the proposal as an effort to “heal” divisions after years of political confrontation, and several outlets reported that the legislature is expected to debate the text soon [5][1][8]. Alongside the amnesty push, Rodríguez said the government intends to close or repurpose the capital’s notorious El Helicoide detention facility—long criticized by rights groups—and convert it into a center focused on sport and social services, while also promising changes to a major prison complex [5][9][2]. The measures are presented by the interim authorities as part of a broader attempt to restore justice and lower political tensions, though the bill’s scope includes exclusions for people accused of serious crimes such as killings, human rights abuses, and drug trafficking [2].

May it be a law that serves to heal the wounds left by political confrontation, violence and extremism.

— Delcy Rodríguez (South China Morning Post)

Key Points

  • Scope window: Le Monde reported the draft is intended to cover the period of political violence from 1999—when Hugo Chávez took power—to the present [7].
  • Legislative pace: Rodríguez told a gathering of justices and magistrates that the ruling party-controlled National Assembly would take up the bill “with urgency” [8].
  • Exclusions listed: The Straits Times said the planned amnesty would not apply to people accused of killings, human rights abuses, or drug trafficking [2].
  • Diplomatic signal: Le Monde reported that the United States said it plans to re-establish its diplomatic presence in Venezuela following the announcement [7].
  • Conditional releases: Firstpost said the proposed law would also address the situation of former prisoners who have already received conditional release [4].

Historical Background

Several reports frame the amnesty initiative against Venezuela’s long-running political polarization and detentions tied to political unrest [3][6]. Le Monde said the proposal is designed to cover a broad span of political violence dating back to 1999, the year Hugo Chávez assumed power [7]. The government’s stated plan to close or repurpose El Helicoide also reflects years of allegations about abuses at the facility raised by rights groups [9].

Q&A

Q: What concrete changes are planned for Venezuela’s broader prison system beyond El Helicoide?

A: The Straits Times reported the government also vowed to convert a major prison center alongside the amnesty push, though details were not fully specified in the article [2].

Q: Where did Rodríguez make the announcement?

A: MercoPress reported Rodríguez delivered the announcement at an event held at Venezuela’s Supreme Tribunal of Justice [1].

Q: Who could be among those released if the bill passes?

A: DW reported the measure could include opposition leaders, journalists, and human rights activists among those currently detained for political reasons [3].
