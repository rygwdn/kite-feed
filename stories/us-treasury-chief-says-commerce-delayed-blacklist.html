US Treasury chief says Commerce delayed blacklist for Nvidia chip buyer

Category: World

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Friday accused the Commerce Department, led by Howard Lutnick, of failing for months to blacklist Singapore-based Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer suspected of routing advanced chips to China. Authorities are investigating the alleged chip diversion and the relationship between former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang and Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence suspects of working for China. Beijing denies the espionage allegation [35][1][4]. Bessent said the Treasury, Defense and State departments, along with the U.S. Trade Representative, had supported adding Megaspeed to the Commerce Department’s Entity List, which restricts access to U.S. technology. He said existing export-control authority was sufficient and questioned whether Nvidia adequately vetted its customer. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing and said it takes U.S. export rules seriously [33][35][1]. Lutnick has agreed to testify voluntarily before the Senate Banking Committee, and lawmakers have requested testimony from other Commerce officials and Nvidia representatives. The Justice Department is investigating whether Megaspeed violated export laws [23][35][3].

Timeline

  • 2024: Commerce’s export-control bureau opened an investigation into Megaspeed [3].
  • May 2025: Commerce halted enforcement of an advanced-chip rule before its planned start [10].
  • 12 November 2026: Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia chief executive on Thursday [35][1].
  • 13 November 2026: Bessent publicly blamed Commerce for delaying Megaspeed restrictions [33][35].

Technical Details

  • Entity List licensing: U.S. exporters must obtain licenses for covered transfers to companies on the Commerce Department’s Entity List. Bessent said the department had sole authority to add Megaspeed [6][33].
  • H200 review: Commerce said it would review applications to export Nvidia H200 processors to China case by case under security requirements. Bessent said China should not receive Nvidia chips more advanced than the older H100 generation [1][35].

Industry Impact

  • AI chip supply controls: The case has prompted a bipartisan push for tighter restrictions on China’s access to advanced AI processors, with lawmakers seeking to attach new export limits to annual defense legislation [26][1].

Historical Background

U.S. efforts to restrict China’s access to advanced chips began during President Donald Trump’s first administration, when Washington blocked Chinese telecommunications company Huawei from buying semiconductors made with U.S. technology, according to Council on Foreign Relations senior fellow Chris McGuire [10]. The Biden administration continued those restrictions [10].

International Reactions

  • 🇨🇳 China: China rejected the espionage allegations against Alice Huang and warned Washington against imposing additional semiconductor export restrictions, saying such controls could disrupt global industrial supply chains [26].
