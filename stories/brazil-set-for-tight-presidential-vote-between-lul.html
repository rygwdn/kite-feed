Brazil set for tight presidential vote between Lula and Bolsonaro

Category: World

Brazilians were set to vote Sunday in a closely contested presidential election between incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, 45, the son of former President Jair Bolsonaro. Nearly 159 million people are eligible to vote. If no candidate wins more than half the valid votes, the top two advance to a runoff on Oct. 25. Polls put Lula narrowly ahead in the first round but suggested a close second-round contest [7][14][5][37]. The result will determine whether Lula’s left-leaning government or the Bolsonaro family’s right-wing movement leads Latin America’s largest country. Lula has made national sovereignty a campaign theme, criticizing President Donald Trump’s pressure on Brazil. Flávio Bolsonaro has emphasized tougher crime policies and fiscal austerity, and pledged to seek the release of his father, who was sentenced to 27 years for attempting to overturn the 2022 election [4][36]. Voters are also choosing governors and legislators, including all 513 members of the Chamber of Deputies and 54 senators. The campaigns ended Saturday with rallies in São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro. Brazil’s electronic voting system is expected to produce preliminary results within hours of polls closing [7][2][14].

Key Points

  • Court appointments: The next president is expected to nominate four of Brazil’s 11 Supreme Court justices [36].
  • Security proposals: Flávio Bolsonaro has proposed building El Salvador-style megaprisons, designating criminal groups as terrorist organizations and lowering the age for adult criminal prosecution to 16 [44].
  • Rally turnout: Organizers estimated that about 150,000 supporters attended Lula’s closing rally in São Paulo, while Flávio Bolsonaro led a motorcycle caravan in the state before a rally in Rio de Janeiro [5][2].
  • Ballot scale: Twelve candidates are registered. Voting is mandatory for literate citizens aged 18 to 69 and optional for illiterate citizens, 16- and 17-year-olds, and people over 70 [14].

Historical Background

Brazil adopted electronic voting machines in the 1990s after local elections were marred by ballot-box stuffing, stolen boxes and falsified paper ballots. The electoral authority has reported no significant fraud in the system, despite Jair Bolsonaro’s repeated unfounded attacks on its reliability before the 2022 election [44].
