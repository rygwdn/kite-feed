Canada extends U.S. tariff relief through June 2028, broadens coverage

Category: Canada

Canada is extending tariff relief for businesses importing U.S. goods until June 30, 2028, and expanding eligibility to cover non-steel goods used in mining, quarrying and oil and gas extraction, as well as goods used by public education bodies, including those mandated by Indigenous organizations [5][3][1]. Ottawa says the changes aim to support firms affected by U.S. tariffs while encouraging them to use Canadian-made products where available [5]. The government will end relief for certain trailers on Dec. 1, 2026, and for specified aluminum wire and extrusions and paperboard products on Feb. 1, 2027, because it says those goods can be sourced in Canada [5][1]. It will retain case-by-case relief for firms facing exceptional circumstances, including shortages or existing contracts. Canada introduced the remission program in April 2025 and imposed counter-tariffs of 15% to 50% on some U.S. goods on Sept. 8, 2026 [5].

Key Points

  • Domestic sourcing: The government says it will give importers advance notice before removing products from tariff relief so they can adjust supply chains or apply for individual remission [5].
  • Broader exemptions: Certain goods tied to national security, public health and packaging in agriculture, food and beverage sectors are also exempt from tariffs [1][2].
  • Published schedules: The Finance Department plans to publish detailed product schedules online and incorporate them by reference into the tariff order [5].

Perspectives

Canadian Steel Producers Association: The industry group has criticized the remission program, saying it removes pressure on importers to switch to Canadian suppliers [1].
