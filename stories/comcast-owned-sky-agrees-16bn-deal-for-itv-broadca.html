Comcast-owned Sky agrees £1.6bn deal for ITV broadcasting arm

Category: Business

Comcast-owned Sky agreed to buy ITV’s media and entertainment business for up to £1.6 billion, or about $2.1 billion, in a deal covering ITV’s free-to-air channels and ITVX streaming service [3][5][11][13][14]. ITV Studios, the production business behind programmes including Love Island and I’m a Celebrity, is excluded and will remain a separate listed company [5][3][11][31]. The price includes £1.2 billion in cash, the transfer of Comcast’s Love Productions business to ITV for £200 million, and a possible further £200 million in the second half of 2028 depending on 2027 advertising revenues [3][11][22]. Sky and ITV said the combination would help them compete with Netflix, YouTube, Amazon Prime Video and Disney by joining free-to-air TV, advertising-funded streaming, subscription TV and Sky’s broadband, mobile and business services [3][11][13][14]. The transaction requires regulatory clearance and shareholder approval, with UK regulators and lawmakers expected to scrutinise it [14][20][8]. The Guardian reported that approval could take 12 to 18 months, while La Voz de Galicia said completion is expected in the second half of 2027 [3][27].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Audience scale: The combined Sky-ITV business would account for about 20% of UK in-home viewing, behind the BBC and ahead of YouTube [11][18][35].
  • Content supply: Sky committed to buying £2.1 billion of content from ITV Studios over five years, with some sources specifying the 2028-2032 period [18][8][32].
  • Cost savings: The transaction is expected to generate about £200 million in annual cost synergies by the end of the third year after closing, mainly from operational savings [18].
  • Viewer access: ITV programmes such as Coronation Street, Emmerdale, Love Island and I’m a Celebrity are expected to remain free-to-air at least until ITV’s public service obligations expire in 2034 [5][6][33].
  • Advertising scrutiny: The merged group could hold as much as 70% of the UK television advertising market, a level expected to draw antitrust scrutiny [22][38].

Perspectives

Sky CEO Dana Strong: Strong told the BBC ITV shows would not change immediately and said Sky had a five-year deal for major ITV Studios content before future negotiations [5].

Timeline

  • 2006: BSkyB bought a 17.9% stake in ITV, which it later divested [4]
  • November 2025: Sky first showed interest in ITV’s media arm [35]
  • June 2026: Comcast announced a Sky and NBCUniversal spinoff plan [7][35]
  • 6 July 2026: Sky and ITV announced the £1.6bn deal [3][5]
  • 2027-H2: Completion expected in the second half of 2027 [27]
  • 2028-H2: Contingent £200m payment due if ad targets are met [3][22]

Industry Impact

  • UK creative production: ITV Studios will receive Love Productions, the maker of The Great British Bake Off and The Piano, from Comcast as part of the transaction [3][8][43].
  • News provision: Company statements said ITV News and Sky News will continue as distinct editorial operations after the merger [18][35][20].
  • Independent producers: Programming acquired under Sky’s ITV Studios supply deal will not count toward ITV’s independent production quotas, preserving quota capacity for independent producers [18].
  • Workforce: Sky warned some roles would be cut after the takeover of ITV’s broadcasting arm, while also pointing to planned annual cost savings [36][18].
  • Channel 4 programming: The Great British Bake Off could move from Channel 4 to ITV after ITV Studios receives Love Productions, though Channel 4’s current rights term is unclear [43].

Business Angle

  • Sky gains a larger distribution platform, but the deal’s value depends on advertising and subscription revenue from that reach [11][common].
  • ITV shareholders get near-term cash, with around £950 million expected to be returned, while retaining exposure to ITV Studios and its guaranteed five-year Sky supply agreement [22][18].
  • Regulatory delays or conditions could reduce the expected £200 million in annual cost synergies if regulators require asset sales, ad restrictions or governance remedies [18][common].
