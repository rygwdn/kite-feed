NASA Curiosity detects honeycomb fracture field on Mars

Category: Science

NASA's Curiosity rover entered a Martian valley nicknamed Valle Grande and returned images of a broad field of honeycomb-like polygonal fractures, each about 1.5 to 3 inches, or 4 to 8 centimeters, across [1][3][4][5]. The rover had seen smaller patches of similar geometric patterns before, but NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory said the Valle Grande field appears to be on a scale Curiosity had not previously encountered [1][3][4][5]. A 360-degree panorama captured on June 19 and 20, during the mission's 4,930th and 4,931st Martian sols, shows the polygonal shapes throughout the rover's view and continuing around a nearby butte called Miraflores [1][4][5]. NASA said the features may help researchers distinguish among possible formation processes, including mud cracking, temperature cycling and compression that forced water from buried sediment [3][4][5].

Key Points

  • Miraflores butte: Miraflores rises about 20 feet, or 6 meters, above the surrounding terrain and has a thick sand cap [1][4].
  • Water clues: Researchers plan to compare polygon dimensions, shapes and chemistry across the continuous field to test whether ridges and centers contain different minerals [1][3][4].

Perspectives

Scientific Significance

  • Habitability context: Curiosity's work at Mount Sharp has documented ancient water-related environments, and these fractures may add data on how long water persisted in the region [3][5].
  • Limits of current evidence: The sources do not report a concluded formation mechanism for the Valle Grande polygons; NASA said the rover measured their shapes and chemistry and that researchers will examine the data [1][4].
