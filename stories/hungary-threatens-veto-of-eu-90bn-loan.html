Hungary threatens veto of EU €90bn loan

Category: World

Hungary has signaled it may block the European Union’s planned loan package for Ukraine, tying its approval to the restart of Russian oil deliveries to Hungary via the Druzhba pipeline, which runs through Ukraine [1][3][6][7][5][2][4]. The Hungarian government says oil transit stopped in late January and argues Ukraine is using energy supplies as leverage — a claim Kyiv rejects, pointing instead to damage from Russian strikes [6][7][2]. The standoff complicates the EU’s push to secure new financing for Ukraine, as Brussels looks for unity ahead of the anniversary of Russia’s full-scale invasion [6][5].

Primary Source: View original article

We will not give in to this blackmail.

— Péter Szijjártó (POLITICO Europe)

Key Points

  • Unanimity needed: The loan requires unanimous approval from all 27 EU member states, giving Hungary a clear veto over the entire package [7][6].
  • Cash pressure: POLITICO reports Ukraine’s funds could start running low from April, raising the pressure on Brussels to bridge the financing and energy-transit dispute [6].
  • Election backdrop: Several outlets say Prime Minister Viktor Orbán has sharpened his anti-Ukraine rhetoric ahead of an election in which he risks losing power after more than 15 years [5][6].

Perspectives

Hungarian government: Foreign Minister Péter Szijjártó said Hungary is blocking the loan until Druzhba oil transit resumes, and accused Ukraine of “blackmail.”

Timeline

Historical Background

The Druzhba pipeline is a Soviet-era route that has long carried Russian crude to Central Europe, including Hungary and Slovakia, via Ukraine [7]. Since Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, energy transit and infrastructure have repeatedly become pressure points in EU-Ukraine relations and intra-EU politics [common].
