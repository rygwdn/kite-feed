BC Hydro locates floating island in Williston Reservoir

Category: Canada

BC Hydro said a newly formed, tree-covered floating island in northern British Columbia’s Williston Reservoir has been located after drifting about 30 kilometres northwest from its last known position [1][7][20][14][22]. The utility said satellite imagery dated Aug. 15 showed the mass at the north entrance of the Ospika Arm, where the Ospika River flows into the reservoir, and farther from the W.A.C. Bennett Dam [1][20]. The island drew attention after boaters first spotted and filmed it in August; BC Hydro spokesperson Bob Gammer said the utility initially considered whether the footage was AI-generated before satellite images confirmed it was real [8][6][11][14]. BC Hydro has estimated the mass at about 140 metres long and 70 metres wide, or roughly 9,800 square metres, and Gammer said the leading theory is that rising reservoir levels lifted a shoreline mass of trees, logs and decaying vegetation into the water [7][16][20][24]. Officials warned boaters not to climb onto the island or land boats on it because the structure could be unstable [7][4][3][24]. Simon Fraser University earth sciences professor John Clague told The Canadian Press he had never heard of such a case, while BC Hydro said the mass was not currently causing problems [7][20].

Key Points

  • Early verification: A Sentinel-2 satellite image from July 21 confirmed the floating mass was in the reservoir before a later Aug. 5 image no longer showed it at that location [8][11][16][22].
  • Wind movement: BC Hydro said wind likely pushed the island across the reservoir because water in the reservoir moves slowly toward the W.A.C. Bennett Dam [20].
  • Possible erosion: John Clague said decades of shoreline erosion and wave action on shallow-rooted trees may have helped detach a large vegetated section from the reservoir edge [20].

Perspectives

BC Hydro said it will continue to monitor the island's location using satellite imagery and on-the-ground reports.

  • 31 August 2026: Avery announced the island had been found again [11][16].

Historical Background

Williston Reservoir was created in 1968 behind the W.A.C. Bennett Dam and has been described as the seventh-largest artificial lake in the world [20][14]. The reservoir's size and eroding shoreline allow wind and waves to move large detached debris masses [20].
