Amazon investigates AWS outages tied to AI tools

Category: AI

Amazon Web Services had at least two service disruptions in December that involved internal AI coding tools, according to Reuters reporting that cited the Financial Times [2]. One incident lasted about 13 hours and was tied to engineers using an in-house “agentic” coding tool called Kiro [4]. Amazon has pushed back on the idea that “AI agents” independently caused the outages. It told PCMag that one disruption began while engineers were using Kiro, but said the root cause was a misconfigured role — not an autonomous system acting on its own [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Permissions question: Sources said the AI tools were treated like operators, with operator-level permissions. Changes could go through without a second person’s approval, even though that’s typically required [4].
  • How many outages: Reuters said AWS had at least two outages in December that involved AI tools, citing the Financial Times [2].

Perspectives

Amazon Web Services: AWS disputed that the recent disruptions were “linked” to AI agents. It said one issue stemmed from a misconfigured role while engineers were using its Kiro tool.

Sources: PCMag

Amazon (statement cited by FT reporting): Amazon called the outage an “extremely limited event” that affected one service in parts of China, and said it was a coincidence that AI tools were involved.

Sources: Futurism

AWS employees (as reported): People described multiple production outages as foreseeable, and blamed them on letting AI agents make changes without intervention — and without a second-person approval.
