NASA releases patriotic space images for U.S. 250th anniversary

Category: Science

NASA marked the United States' 250th anniversary with red, white and blue astronomy images from Hubble, Chandra, James Webb and ground-based observatories, showing targets from Milky Way star clusters to galaxies millions of light-years away [1][14]. The images included Hubble views of the globular clusters NGC 6426 and Messier 3, a stellar nursery called LH 95 in the Large Magellanic Cloud, and a Chandra collection featuring a supernova remnant, a nebula, a galaxy cluster and a spiral galaxy [9][10][11][1]. The imagery covers several stages of stellar and galactic evolution: NGC 6426 is estimated to be about 13 billion years old, Messier 3 contains more than 500,000 stars, and LH 95 contains about 2,500 young stars still developing in a gas-rich star-forming region [9][12][13][11][6][10]. The Chandra set combined X-ray observations with optical, infrared and ultraviolet data to show environments including Cassiopeia A, the remains of a massive star that exploded about 340 years ago and is roughly 11,000 light-years from Earth [1][14].

Did you know: Blue stragglers in old globular clusters can appear younger than neighboring stars because they may form through stellar mergers or mass transfer between stars [6].

Key Points

  • Ancient cluster: NGC 6426 lies in the Milky Way's outer halo and is almost as old as the universe, making it a record of the early cosmos [9][8][12].
  • Distance markers: Messier 3 contains more than 240 RR Lyrae variable stars, the largest known population of its kind in any Milky Way globular cluster, and astronomers use those stars for distance measurements [11][6][15].
  • Star formation: The largest blue stars in LH 95 have at least three times the Sun's mass and shape nearby hydrogen gas with ultraviolet radiation and stellar winds [10][7].
  • False color: Hubble's LH 95 image maps shorter visible wavelengths as blue and longer visible and near-infrared wavelengths as red, while its crimson glow traces hydrogen-alpha emission [7][10].

Scientific Significance

  • Early-universe record: NGC 6426 is a surviving stellar population from the Milky Way's formative era, with stars dating to conditions shortly after the Big Bang [9][12][13].
  • Stellar evolution: LH 95 is a nearby case study in how multiple generations of stars coexist in one association, with young stars still accreting gas and dust for millions of years [10].
  • Milky Way archaeology: Messier 3 may preserve evidence of a past globular-cluster merger and contains rare stellar populations, including blue straggler candidates and RR Lyrae variables [6][11].

Historical Background

NASA has operated Hubble since space shuttle Discovery deployed it in 1990; it is one of the longest-running space observatories [common]. Chandra has been in orbit since 1999 and observes X-rays from high-energy regions that visible-light telescopes cannot fully capture [1][14]. Globular clusters have long served as records of early Milky Way history because many of their stars formed from the same gas cloud at roughly the same time [9][11].
