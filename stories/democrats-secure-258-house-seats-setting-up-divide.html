Democrats secure 258 House seats, setting up divided government

Category: World

Democrats have secured at least 258 of 435 seats in the U.S. House of Representatives, their largest majority since 1992, after North Carolina Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Virginia Foxx in a recount. Republicans hold at least 173 seats, with four contests unresolved. Democrats also won a 56-seat Senate majority, giving them control of both chambers under Republican President Donald Trump beginning in January [15][5][3]. Democrats say their first 100 days will focus on the cost of living and health care, including votes to reverse Republican Medicaid cuts and restore expired Affordable Care Act tax credits. They also plan investigations into the administration and the finances of Trump and his family. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, expected to become speaker, has not ruled out another impeachment effort [1][14]. Trump can veto legislation passed by the Democratic majorities. The four outstanding House races include contests in Minnesota and Tennessee and two Louisiana runoffs scheduled for December; the results could increase the Democrats’ majority [5][11].

Key Points

  • Narrow recount: Hubbard beat Foxx by 330 votes; North Carolina law allows a recount when the margin is below 1% [5][29].
  • Caucus differences: Rep. Troy Carter says Democrats are “not a monolith” and members bring different ideas [1].
  • Competitive districts: A Democratic campaign committee memo said the party won 70 of the 92 House districts it had identified as battlegrounds [31].
  • Senate leadership: Sen. Chris Van Hollen is challenging Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer [26].

Historical Background

Foxx had represented North Carolina’s 5th Congressional District since 2005 and defeated Hubbard by nine percentage points in the 2024 midterms [5][29]. Hubbard’s recount victory flipped a district long held by Republicans [29].
