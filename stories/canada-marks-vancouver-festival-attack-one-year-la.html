Canada marks Vancouver festival attack one year later

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney and British Columbia Premier David Eby marked the first anniversary of the Lapu Lapu Day festival attack in Vancouver by calling the violence senseless and urging people to remember the victims and all those still living with its effects [2][4][6]. The anniversary prompted reflection across British Columbia and within the Filipino community on the vehicle attack that struck a community celebration in Vancouver [3][2]. Coverage of the anniversary focused not just on remembrance, but on recovery. Witnesses and Vancouver police recalled a devastating scene, while community members described a year of grief that has given way to more complicated questions about healing, accountability and how to move forward together [3][5].

Key Points

  • Official tributes: Eby issued a formal statement for the anniversary, while national and provincial leaders framed April 26 as a day to honour those killed, injured and traumatized by the attack [6][2][4].
  • Witness memories: Global News reported that police officers and witnesses still describe the aftermath in stark terms, showing how deeply the event remains etched in Vancouver's collective memory one year later [3].
  • Community strain: The Tyee reported that grief within the Filipino community has not faded but grown more complex, with divisions emerging over mourning, leadership and what justice should look like after the attack [5].

Historical Background

Lapu Lapu Day honours Datu Lapu-Lapu, an Indigenous leader in the Philippines associated with resistance to Spanish colonization in 1521 [common]. In British Columbia, the festival had become a public celebration of Filipino heritage before the 2025 attack turned it, a year later, into a site of mourning and remembrance [3][2]. The anniversary coverage shows how violence can give a community cultural event wider civic significance, drawing statements from both provincial and national leaders [6][2].
