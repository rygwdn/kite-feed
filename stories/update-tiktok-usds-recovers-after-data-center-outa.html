Update: TikTok USDS recovers after data center outage

Category: Technology

In the latest development, TikTok says it’s making “significant progress” restoring service in the United States after a power outage at a U.S. data center disrupted access, posting, and feed behavior for many users [7][1][2]. The newly formed majority U.S.-owned TikTok USDS Joint Venture said the outage triggered a “cascading systems failure” that produced multiple bugs, including timeouts and inaccurate view/like displays [5][9][4]. The technical stumble landed just days after TikTok finalized its U.S. ownership deal, amplifying user concerns about reliability, moderation, and privacy during the transition [4][8]. Still, TikTok says engagement data is safe and some issues are only display errors, a reassuring note for creators and viewers as fixes roll out [5].

Primary Source: View original article

Since yesterday we’ve been working to restore our services following a power outage at a U.S. data center impacting TikTok and other apps we operate

— TikTok USDS Joint Venture (Engadget)

Did you know: Before last Thursday, UpScrolled averaged fewer than 500 downloads a day, according to AppFigures data cited by Engadget [6].

Key Points

  • Creator earnings: TikTok USDS said some creators temporarily saw earnings appear to disappear, describing it as a display error caused by server timeouts rather than lost data [5].
  • Algorithm oddities: Users reported repeated or older videos and other “for you” feed strangeness during the disruption, alongside problems posting new content [2][5].
  • Censorship fears: Some users said videos about ICE activity in Minnesota or the Minneapolis shooting were routed into review, flagged as “Ineligible for Recommendation,” or led to temporary suspensions, prompting censorship concerns [4][8].
  • Alternative apps: As frustration grew, UpScrolled surged up U.S. App Store rankings and drew tens of thousands of downloads in a few days, with other alternatives like Skylight also seeing growth [6][13].
  • Ownership details: The TikTok USDS Joint Venture is described as 80.1% owned by U.S. and global investors including Oracle, Silver Lake, and Abu Dhabi-based MGX as managing investors, plus other U.S. stakeholders like the Dell Family Office and affiliates of Susquehanna International Group [4].

Perspectives

TikTok USDS Joint Venture: The company attributes the disruption to a data center power outage that cascaded into multiple bugs, and says it is stabilizing service with its data center partner while making progress on recovery [5][7].

Sources: TechCrunch

Users alleging censorship: Some users believe the timing and content-related posting problems suggest intentional suppression of videos criticizing ICE raids in Minnesota rather than purely technical failures [8].

Sources: Wired

Frustrated users switching apps: App analytics and rankings show a spike in TikTok uninstalls and a surge for indie competitor UpScrolled as some users look for alternatives amid glitches and trust concerns [6][13].

Sources: Engadget

Privacy-focused critics: Some coverage highlights renewed attention to TikTok’s privacy policy language and points to “privacy-first” rival apps attracting interest during the turmoil [12].

Timeline

  • Thursday: TikTok announces TikTok USDS joint venture to operate in U.S. [1][6]
  • Over the weekend: U.S. users report trouble logging in, posting, and refreshing feeds [1][2][3]
  • Monday: TikTok says power outage caused cascading systems failure and bugs [5][9]
  • Tuesday: TikTok says it’s making significant progress restoring U.S. infrastructure [7]

Technical Details

  • Data center power outage: A loss of power at a U.S. data center disrupted TikTok US operations and also affected other apps TikTok operates, according to the company [1][5].
  • Cascading systems failure: TikTok said the initial outage propagated across dependent systems, creating “multiple bugs” such as timeouts, slow loads, and incorrect engagement displays [5][4].
  • Server timeouts: TikTok attributed ‘0’ views/likes and missing-earnings displays to server timeouts, saying underlying engagement data remained safe [5].

Industry Impact

  • Creator economy: Glitches that show ‘0’ views/likes and hide in-app earnings—even if only display errors—can interrupt creator workflows and revenue planning, making platform transparency a competitive differentiator [5].
  • App store competition: With UpScrolled and other alternatives climbing rankings, the episode illustrates how outages and trust concerns can quickly redirect user attention toward smaller platforms [6][13].
  • Cloud resilience: A single data center power event triggering widespread app issues highlights the importance of redundancy, failover design, and incident communications for large consumer platforms [5][3][common].
  • Privacy positioning: Renewed scrutiny of TikTok’s privacy policy language has given “privacy-first” apps a marketing opening, regardless of whether TikTok’s policy wording materially changed [4][12].

Historical Background

U.S. lawmakers and regulators have pressured TikTok for years over national-security concerns tied to its Chinese ownership, creating recurring uncertainty about whether it could keep operating in the U.S. [4][13]. Last week, TikTok said it reached a deal to continue operating in the U.S. via a majority U.S.-owned structure (TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC), and U.S. users were prompted to review new Terms of Service soon after [1][4].

Q&A

Q: What other apps did TikTok say were affected by the same outage?

A: TikTok said the power outage affected TikTok “and other apps we operate,” but it did not name those apps in its statement [1][5].

Q: Who built UpScrolled, and what is its pitch?

A: Fast Company says UpScrolled was developed by Palestinian-Australian Issam Hijazi and markets itself around equal reach and avoiding “shadowbans” or pay-to-play favoritism [13].

Q: How big was the reported change in TikTok uninstalls?

A: Engadget reports Sensor Tower data showed a 150% rise in TikTok uninstalls in the U.S. versus the prior three months [6].

Action Items

  • Check whether your TikTok analytics are real-time: If you see ‘0’ views/likes or missing earnings, wait and recheck later; TikTok says these can be display errors from server timeouts while underlying data remains safe [5].
  • Reduce creator risk with cross-posting: Consider posting key videos to at least one additional platform (for example, UpScrolled) so an outage on one app doesn’t pause your reach [6][13].
  • Review updated terms and privacy settings: If TikTok prompts you to accept updated Terms of Service, read the changes and revisit privacy controls, especially around precise location data [1][4].
