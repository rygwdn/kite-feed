EU chief signals €500bn technology plan after first AI summit

Category: AI

European Union leaders used their first summit devoted to artificial intelligence, held Friday in Dublin, to discuss building the bloc’s AI industry, reducing reliance on foreign technology and controlling risks. Leaders discussed Europe’s efforts to compete with the United States and China but made no major decisions or concrete commitments [2][5][14]. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said she would present a proposal at the next EU summit in late November to strengthen the bloc’s technological sovereignty, estimating that the effort would require at least €500 billion [14][20]. Arthur Mensch, chief executive of French AI company Mistral, told leaders that Europe needs faster decisions, more financing and closer coordination. He said choices in the next two years will shape the industry for the following decade [3][23]. A plan for seven AI gigafactories—large facilities combining computing, software and data-center infrastructure—has stalled over disputes about competitiveness spending in the EU’s 2028-34 budget [2][16]. Nobel Prize-winning AI researcher Geoffrey Hinton addressed AI’s effects on rights, jobs and security. Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin said Hinton urged leaders to build safeguards into AI at the design stage; Estonia and Finland separately called for an EU-level mechanism to respond to serious AI incidents [19][4]. French President Emmanuel Macron said he and the Dutch prime minister would convene technology leaders in Paris on Dec. 2 [14].

Key Points

  • Capacity gap: Europe had 11.92 gigawatts of installed data-center capacity, compared with 53.73 gigawatts in the United States and 31.9 gigawatts in China, figures cited by Foreign Policy [12].
  • Existing funding: The European Commission’s InvestAI program, launched in February 2025, includes a €20 billion fund for four planned AI gigafactories, each designed to house about 100,000 advanced AI chips [9].
  • Business adoption: One in five EU enterprises used at least one AI technology in 2025, up from less than one in 10 a few years earlier, while the bloc’s data-center capacity remained far below that of the US and China [12].
  • Safety proposals: Von der Leyen cited pharmaceutical testing and product transparency as a possible model for controlling powerful AI before release, while acknowledging that the EU AI Act is not perfect [19].
  • Capital dependency: A European Council steering note said non-EU capital often leads large, high-risk AI investments and identified foreign reliance in computing power, advanced models, chips, cloud infrastructure and software [3].

Perspectives

European Parliament President Roberta Metsola rejected the claim that Europe had already lost the AI race, pointing to its talent and technology ecosystem. She said the priority was to secure financing to develop ideas in Europe [13].

Timeline

  • 2022: ChatGPT’s release helped trigger the latest AI investment surge [18]
  • February 2025: The Commission announced InvestAI and its gigafactory funding plan [9]
  • 13 November 2026: EU leaders held their first summit dedicated to artificial intelligence [2][14]

Historical Background

The EU’s 2023 Chips Act aimed to attract advanced semiconductor manufacturing and double the bloc’s share of the global chip market to 20% by 2030; Reuters reported that the target appeared likely to be missed [2][12].

Business Angle

  • Mistral said its September 8 Series D funding round raised €3 billion at a post-money valuation above €21 billion. The company described it as the largest equity fundraising by a European technology company [3].

Q&A

Q: What safety risks did officials single out?

A: EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas pointed to AI-equipped drones in Ukraine as an immediate battlefield risk, saying they were being trained to find targets [4].
