Spain reports 72 deaths after migrant surge into Ceuta

Category: World

Spanish officials said at least 72 people died during mass crossings from Morocco into Ceuta, Spain’s North African enclave, after five more bodies were found along the coast on Sunday [19][20][9][59]. More than 50,000 people crossed into Ceuta by land and sea in a surge that began Thursday at one of the European Union’s two land borders with Africa; other estimates put the total near 60,000 [19][9][7][20]. Spanish authorities said some people drowned and others died in a stampede or were crushed near a breakwater and border fence [9][46][59]. Most migrants had returned to Morocco by Sunday, including more than 48,000 who returned within 48 hours, Spanish authorities said [19][10][59]. Ceuta’s shops and cafes reopened as police, troops and Civil Guard units continued patrols and searches near the beaches and surrounding hills [9][22][12]. Morocco’s Interior Ministry reported 11 deaths on its side of the border and blamed social media misinformation, human trafficking networks and misinterpretation of a Spanish court ruling that limits immediate returns of migrants intercepted at sea [9][66][59][48]. EU interior ministers are set to hold an emergency video conference on Tuesday, August 4, to discuss the mass crossings [10][15][20]. Spanish authorities reinforced the Tarajal area with a 500-meter floating barrier, and Madrid rejected suggestions that its migration policy encouraged the crossing [51][56][70].

Key Points

  • Medical toll: The Spanish government representative in Ceuta said more than 1,000 people had been treated by health services since Thursday [19][46][59].
  • Remaining migrants: Ceuta’s president estimated Monday that 3,000 to 5,000 migrants remained in the city, while Tagesschau cited a Spanish Interior Ministry estimate of about 2,500 Moroccans still there [21][39].
  • Border route: Many migrants reached Ceuta by swimming around the end of the border fence that extends into the Mediterranean near Tarajal [23][51][20].
  • Political pressure: Far-right leaders including Vox chief Santiago Abascal and activist Alvise Pérez traveled to Ceuta and called for expulsions or blamed Spain’s government for the situation [4][69][12].

Historical Background

Ceuta and Melilla are Spanish territories on the North African coast that have long been flashpoints for migration [23][44]. Spain and Morocco signed a Treaty of Friendship, Good Neighborliness and Cooperation in the 1990s, but their cooperation has repeatedly been strained by migration and Western Sahara disputes [16]. In May 2021, about 10,000 migrants entered Ceuta in 48 hours after Spain allowed Polisario Front leader Brahim Ghali to receive medical treatment [27].

Humanitarian Impact

Families in Fnideq searched for missing relatives after the crossings, including people looking for children who had not returned from attempts to reach Ceuta [14][8]. Some migrants who stayed in Ceuta described hunger, exhaustion and reliance on residents for food [7][5].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Avoid the Tarajal border area and beaches used in the crossings, where authorities are conducting recovery operations after the deaths [9][51].
