Canada commits $8 million food aid for Cuba

Category: Canada

Canada is sending $8 million in food assistance to Cuba through United Nations partners and Canadian NGOs, as the island faces deepening shortages and long blackouts tied to a severe fuel crunch [2][4][5]. Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand and international development secretary of state Randeep Sarai said the package is meant to help Cubans meet basic needs as conditions worsen [6][2]. The announcement comes as U.S.-Cuba tensions rise, including around a tightening U.S. oil blockade and broader pressure on Havana—dynamics Canadian officials said are unlikely to destabilize Cuba’s government in the near term [1]. Opinion coverage argued Canada should lean into its “middle power” role to encourage de-escalation and offer practical support [3].

Key Points

  • Fuel supply disruption: The Globe and Mail reported Cuba lost its main fuel source in January after the U.S. took control of Venezuela’s oil reserves [5].
  • Compounding factors: Global Affairs Canada said last year’s Hurricane Melissa worsened the energy crisis and left millions more vulnerable [2].
  • Dueling narratives on U.S. pressure: Cuba’s envoy to Ottawa said the U.S. is trying to overthrow the government, while Canada’s foreign service described the regime’s hold on Havana as “quite stable” [1].
