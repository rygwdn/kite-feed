Eisenkot opens coalition talks after receiving mandate to replace Netanyahu

Category: World

Israeli President Isaac Herzog on Sunday, Nov. 8, formally gave former military chief and Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form a government. Eisenkot began negotiations to replace Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu after an Oct. 27 election left no bloc with a parliamentary majority [6][17]. Israel’s 120-seat parliament, the Knesset, is divided among Netanyahu’s bloc, with 53 seats; Eisenkot’s Jewish opposition bloc, with 48; and Arab-led parties, with 19 [3][17]. At the Jerusalem ceremony, Eisenkot called for a national emergency government and invited Netanyahu’s Likud party and the far-right Religious Zionism party to join, provided they accept him as prime minister and agree to his stated principles [7][40]. He said Israel faced a “devastating diplomatic crisis,” citing warnings from close allies that they may reassess ties [7]. Eisenkot must turn the endorsements that secured his mandate into support for a governing coalition. Arab-led parties have offered cooperation, but the Joint List says it will not enter the Cabinet, while members of Eisenkot’s Jewish bloc oppose relying on Arab parties [27][22]. He has 28 days to reach an agreement, with a possible 14-day extension. If he fails, Herzog can assign the task to another lawmaker. Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister in the meantime [11][18][46].

Key Points

  • Coalition conditions: Eisenkot’s stated requirements include recognition of Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, national service for all, and recognition of Hamas as a terrorist organization with a commitment to dismantle its governing and military capacity [17][25].
  • Arab parties: Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen called for improved social services, greater funding for municipalities and schools, youth programs, and action against crime [27].
  • Liberman’s position: Yisrael Beiteinu leader Avigdor Liberman rejected a government dependent on Arab lawmakers, warning it could harm security and help Netanyahu return, while signaling openness to an agreement with ultra-Orthodox parties [10].
  • Military-service dispute: An agreement with ultra-Orthodox parties would have to reconcile Eisenkot’s pledge of universal service with their demand to preserve exemptions for their community [10][30].
  • Next attempt: If another lawmaker also fails to form a coalition, Israel could face a new election [18][36].

Perspectives

Joint List: Leader Yousef Jabareen argued Arab lawmakers’ backing would be needed to keep Netanyahu from returning as prime minister, and said Eisenkot would struggle to advance his program in parliament without coordination with the list [27].
