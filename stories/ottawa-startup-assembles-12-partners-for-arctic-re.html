Ottawa startup assembles 12 partners for Arctic-ready combat drone

Category: Canada

Ottawa-based Dominion Dynamics announced 12 partnerships to develop Scout, an artificial-intelligence-enabled uncrewed aircraft designed to fly alongside fighter jets. The startup plans to invest $50 million in the project and aims to compete for a federal contract to supply autonomous collaborative platforms to the Royal Canadian Air Force [1][2][3]. Dominion says Scout is being designed to fly long distances in rugged Arctic conditions and take off from gravel or short runways. Founder Eliot Pence expects Ottawa to launch a procurement competition in 2028 or 2029. BetaKit reported that the company plans to finalize the design by March 2027, conduct the first flight in 2029 and begin production near the end of 2030 [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

The intention here is for it to be the most ambitious air platform since the Avro in Canada

— Eliot Pence, founder of Dominion Dynamics (The Canadian Press)

Key Points

  • Navy contract: Dominion won a $17-million, 24-month federal contract to build a remotely controlled marine drone that will survey the ocean floor for underwater mines [2][3]
  • Company funding: Since launching in June 2025, Dominion has raised C$169 million, including a C$139-million Series A financing round [3]
  • Satellite link: A separate agreement with Telesat covers development of antennas for Arctic use to connect uncrewed military systems to Telesat’s planned Lightspeed low-Earth-orbit satellite network [1][2]

Perspectives

Dominion Dynamics: Senior vice-president of aerospace engineering Benoit Breault says Scout is being developed with homegrown talent and that Canadian capability is central to its supply chain [3].
