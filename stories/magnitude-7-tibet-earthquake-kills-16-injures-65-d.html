Magnitude-7 Tibet earthquake kills 16, injures 65, displaces thousands

Category: World

A magnitude-7 earthquake struck Baqen County in northern Tibet at 10:33 p.m. Thursday, killing at least 16 people and injuring 65, including 11 seriously, Chinese state media reported. At least 339 homes and 20 livestock sheds collapsed. The U.S. Geological Survey placed the epicentre about 191 kilometres east-northeast of Nagqu, in a sparsely populated, high-altitude area [41][11][2]. More than 1,600 people were deployed for rescue work. Rockfalls blocked part of National Highway 317, communications failed in some areas and railway services were halted. Crews faced forecasts of intermittent snow [3][2][46]. Emergency supplies included prefabricated shelters, tents, folding beds and winter clothing [24][17]. President Xi Jinping ordered search-and-rescue operations and medical care for the injured. By Friday morning, electricity had been restored to 1,937 households, and passenger services on the affected section of the Qinghai-Xizang Railway had resumed at reduced speeds. Damage assessments and relief work continued [24][52].

Key Points

  • High-altitude terrain: The average elevation within five kilometres of the epicentre is about 4,864 metres, complicating access to isolated communities [41].
  • Aftershock sequence: The U.S. Geological Survey recorded at least seven aftershocks measuring 4.3 to 5.2, each at a depth of about 10 kilometres [2][3].
  • Rescue deployment: China’s regional fire and rescue corps sent 347 personnel, while more than 300 police officers were dispatched for relief work [57][37].
  • Relief funding: The Chinese government announced an allocation of 50 million yuan for disaster relief [11].
  • Aerial survey: An unmanned aerial vehicle from the People’s Liberation Army Western Theater Command Air Force surveyed the disaster zone and sent live footage to its base [57].

Perspectives

President Xi Jinping: Xi directed officials to monitor seismic activity and weather changes to prevent secondary disasters, repair damaged infrastructure and arrange accommodation for residents [24].

Technical Details

  • Depth estimates: The U.S. Geological Survey estimated the main quake’s depth at 18 kilometres, while China’s Earthquake Networks Center put it at 10 kilometres [2][6].
  • Aftershocks: At least seven aftershocks ranged in magnitude from 4.3 to 5.2 and were recorded at a depth of 10 kilometres, according to the U.S. Geological Survey [2].

Historical Background

A magnitude-6.8 earthquake in Tibet’s Tingri County killed at least 126 people in January 2025 [2][9]. A 2010 magnitude-7.1 quake in neighbouring Qinghai province killed at least 2,698 people and left nearly 300 missing [41][45].

Humanitarian Impact

Authorities said 12,489 people had been evacuated by Friday morning. Immediate needs include temporary shelter, warm clothing and medical care, with temperatures in the high-altitude area forecast to fall to minus 9 degrees Celsius and intermittent snow expected [52][24].
