ICE detains 5-year-old during Minnesota immigration operation

Category: World

US immigration enforcement in the Minneapolis area drew fresh scrutiny after school officials and a family lawyer said federal agents detained a five-year-old boy, Liam Conejo Ramos, during an operation that also took his father into custody, with the child later ending up in federal custody in Texas [8][3][7]. The Columbia Heights school superintendent said agents effectively used the child “as bait,” while the Department of Homeland Security said officers did not target the child and stayed with him for safety after the father ran [1][6][8]. Vice President J.D. Vance visited Minneapolis amid rising tensions over the broader enforcement push, urging residents to stop opposing immigration enforcement even as local outrage has grown [2][11][10].

Did you know: Texas, where the child was reported taken to a detention facility, is the second-largest US state by area [common].

Key Points

  • Disputed accounts: School officials said agents took the boy from a running car in the family’s driveway after preschool pickup, while DHS said the father fled, leaving the child behind and prompting an officer to remain with him for safety [3][6][8][1].
  • Wider detentions: Columbia Heights Public Schools said this is the fourth student from the district detained by immigration officers in recent weeks, and it reported a separate incident where a 17-year-old student was taken by armed, masked agents while heading to school [3][6][5].
  • Vance visit: Vance met Minneapolis-area leaders, said he heard the “terrible story,” and later emphasized he was told the child was detained rather than arrested, while also backing ICE’s work publicly [2][11].
  • Federal posture: Reporting described a large-scale enforcement operation in Minneapolis, with 3,000 officers deployed as part of President Donald Trump’s immigration crackdown, which the president has framed as targeting violent criminals [6].
  • Local unrest: Minneapolis has seen weeks of outcry over ICE tactics, and tensions rose further after an ICE officer shot and killed Renee Good, a 37-year-old US citizen and mother of three, earlier in January, according to coverage of Vance’s trip [10][13].

Perspectives

Columbia Heights Public Schools: District leaders say agents effectively used the child to draw out and apprehend his father and that multiple students from the district have been detained in recent weeks.

Family lawyer: The family’s lawyer says the boy and his father were taken into custody and transferred to Texas, disputing the government’s characterization of events.

Timeline

  • Early January: ICE officer fatally shot Renee Good during Minneapolis operation [10].
  • Tuesday: Agents detained Liam Conejo Ramos and his father, accounts differ [8][6][3].
  • The same day: School district says masked agents detained a 17-year-old en route [6].
  • Thursday: Vice President Vance visited Minneapolis and defended ICE [11][2].

Technical Details

  • Detained vs arrested: Officials and reporting distinguish “detention” (temporary custody during an operation) from an “arrest,” a point highlighted by Vice President Vance when discussing the child’s case [2].
  • ICE operations protocol: DHS says when a parent is targeted, officers ask whether the parent wants the child removed with them or placed with a person the parent designates [6].

Historical Background

US immigration enforcement has long involved cooperation and friction between federal agencies and local communities, with periodic surges tied to presidential priorities [common]. President Trump’s current crackdown, as described in the reporting, has increased enforcement activity in Minneapolis, intensifying debates over tactics and community trust [6][10].

Q&A

Q: What does DHS say happens to children when a parent is apprehended?

A: DHS says targeted parents are asked if they want to be removed with their children or have the children placed with a person designated by the parents [6].

Q: What sparked the recent wave of protests and tension in Minneapolis tied to ICE activity?

A: Coverage linked the unrest to complaints about aggressive tactics and to the early-January fatal shooting of Renee Good by an ICE officer [10][13].

Q: How are local leaders framing the child’s detention beyond the legal questions?

A: The school superintendent described the child as having been used as “bait,” emphasizing community concerns about enforcement tactics involving minors [1][2].
