Dozens protest President Trump immigration crackdown at Montreal consulate

Category: Canada

Demonstrators gathered in downtown Montreal on Sunday outside the U.S. Consulate on Sainte-Catherine Street to protest President Trump’s immigration crackdown in the United States [1][2][3][5]. Organizers and participants focused their criticism on U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), and some speakers also accused Canada of being “complicit” in aspects of the enforcement effort [1][2]. The Montreal rally capped a weekend of demonstrations in multiple places across North America, reflecting wider concern about U.S. immigration enforcement tactics and recent incidents that protesters cited to underscore the stakes [5][3]. While the event was a protest, it also served as a civic outlet for Montrealers to publicly express solidarity with people affected by immigration enforcement and to call for accountability from governments on both sides of the border [1][2].

Historical Background

Immigration enforcement in the U.S. is primarily carried out by federal agencies including U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), which was created in 2003 as part of the Department of Homeland Security reorganization after the Sept. 11 attacks [common]. Canada and the United States cooperate on border management through multiple long-running agreements and information-sharing arrangements, which periodically become a focus of political debate during periods of heightened enforcement [common].
