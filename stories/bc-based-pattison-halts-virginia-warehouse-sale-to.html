BC-based Pattison halts Virginia warehouse sale to ICE

Category: Canada

Vancouver-based Jim Pattison Developments says it will not proceed with selling a Virginia warehouse property to the U.S. Department of Homeland Security after the site was identified for use as an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) processing facility [3][4][5]. The planned deal drew protests and criticism on both sides of the border amid heightened scrutiny of ICE during President Trump’s immigration crackdown in the United States [4][5]. The decision spotlights how Canadian companies can become part of U.S. political debates through cross-border business links, and it has also energized advocacy and civic engagement in Canada around migrant rights and public accountability [2]. In parallel, activists and writers are urging Canadians to pay attention to — and learn from — how communities in the U.S. are organizing in response to immigration enforcement [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Civil society response: Canadian civil society groups said they were “horrified” by reports of violent and deadly ICE actions in Minnesota and backed U.S. allies calling for “ICE Out of Everywhere!” [2].
  • Minnesota flashpoint: Canada-focused coverage of the Pattison decision noted that two U.S. citizens were shot dead by federal agents in Minneapolis this month, helping fuel protests and wider scrutiny of ICE [4].
  • Cross-border lesson: Author and activist Harsha Walia argued that Canadians can draw practical lessons from U.S.-based organizing against anti-migrant state violence and build cross-border solidarity [1].

Historical Background

ICE is a U.S. federal agency within the Department of Homeland Security that conducts immigration enforcement operations [common]. Canada and the U.S. have long-running, tightly linked economic ties, so corporate decisions by Canadian firms can quickly become part of U.S. domestic political disputes when they involve U.S. government contracts or facilities [common].
