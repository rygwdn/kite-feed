Update: Pakistan and Afghanistan trade strikes, urge talks

Category: World

Pakistan and Afghanistan’s Taliban-led government traded cross-border attacks this week. Pakistan’s defense minister called it “open war” after Afghan forces hit Pakistani border positions and Pakistan answered with airstrikes that struck sites in Kabul, Kandahar, and other areas [3][16][14][12]. Pakistan says it targeted militant threats it links to Afghan territory. Taliban officials say their attacks were retaliation for earlier Pakistani strikes and say they are willing to negotiate [1][17][12][5]. The escalation has sparked urgent calls to step back. The United Nations said it is deeply concerned and pointed to harm to civilians [24][11]. Some reporting suggested the risk of a wider war remains contained for now, as diplomatic messages moved between Kabul and Islamabad and talk of mediation circulated [13][20][11].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Scale and depth: Reports said Pakistan bombed more than 20 locations—some accounts put the number of targets at 22—in one of its deepest incursions into Afghanistan in years, including strikes in Kabul and Kandahar [10][22][27].
  • Competing casualty narratives: Pakistani and Afghan officials gave sharply different accounts of who was killed and whether civilians were hit, with Afghan officials reporting civilian deaths including women and children [10].
  • Wider geography of clashes: Fighting flared across multiple border areas and provinces, suggesting the confrontation wasn’t limited to a single crossing or sector [28][15].
  • Mediation signals: Even as fighting continued, several countries were reported to be urging talks or floating mediation, and diplomats cited allied messages as a brake on escalation [20][13].
  • What’s new about this round: Taliban officials said the Pakistani strikes stood out for hitting the Taliban government’s forces in major Afghan cities, not just border areas [5].

Perspectives

Pakistan government: Pakistan’s defense and information officials described the strikes as a response to attacks launched from Afghan territory, and accused the Taliban of sheltering or enabling militants who target Pakistan [3][1].

Sources: DW

Taliban authorities (Afghanistan): Taliban representatives said their cross-border attacks were retaliatory, while publicly offering dialogue to settle the dispute [12].

Sources: Al Jazeera

United States: Washington said it supports Pakistan’s “right to defend itself” and offered condolences for those killed; President Trump said he would not intervene [33].

Sources: Al-Monitor (Reuters)

United Nations: The UN chief said he is deeply concerned about the escalation and its toll on civilians, and urged restraint and an immediate ceasefire [24].

Sources: The Hindu

Timeline

  • 26 February 2026: Afghan forces attacked Pakistani border positions, setting off heavier exchanges [16][3].
  • 27 February 2026: Pakistan launched airstrikes that hit targets in Kabul and Kandahar [16][14][12].
  • 28 February 2026: The Taliban said it was open to talks as diplomacy intensified [5][22].

Historical Background

Pakistan backed the Taliban’s rise in the 1990s as part of its strategy for influence in Afghanistan, but the relationship has shifted since the Taliban returned to power in 2021 [6][9]. A long-running dispute over the Durand Line—the border drawn in 1893 between Afghanistan and British India—has repeatedly fueled tensions, with Afghan leaders historically rejecting it as a permanent boundary [10][1]. In recent years, Pakistan has accused Taliban-run Afghanistan of harboring militants who attack Pakistan, a central driver of the current escalation [17][1].
