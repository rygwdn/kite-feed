Six presumed drowned after B.C. charter boat sinks

Category: Canada

Six people are missing and presumed drowned after a commercial fishing charter boat carrying 10 people sank at about midday Sunday in the Strait of Georgia near Roberts Bank, off Richmond, B.C. Four people were rescued [2][5][6][7][8]. Officials said the charter did not send a mayday before it sank [7][8]. Brian Angus and Dorothy Stauffer, sailing their 33-foot yacht Malaika after leaving Vancouver’s Coal Harbour, spotted people in the water, called a mayday and helped rescue survivors [1][3][5][8]. RCMP said the six presumed drowned are four men and two women [2][6]. Richmond RCMP said the sunken vessel is believed to be in 150 to 180 metres of water, making recovery complex and likely requiring underwater drones or similar equipment instead of divers [2][6]. The Transportation Safety Board said Tuesday it had deployed a team to investigate the sinking [2].

Did you know: At just under 201 metres, Living Shangri-La, Vancouver’s tallest building, is only slightly taller than the upper estimate of the water depth where police believe the boat sank [2].

Key Points

  • No life-jackets: Officials and rescuers said none of the people found in the water were wearing life-jackets [1][3][5][7][8].
  • Rough seas: The Vancouver Sun reported seas of up to 2.5 metres, conditions too rough for rescuers to throw a life-ring [5].
  • Makeshift raft: Angus and Stauffer trailed their dinghy on a 15-metre line, allowing three people to grab it one by one [5].
  • Hazardous area: The sinking occurred near where fresh river water meets the ocean, an area described as having hazardous currents and difficult survival conditions [7][8].
