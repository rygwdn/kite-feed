US Supreme Court blocks Trump birthright citizenship limits

Category: World

The U.S. Supreme Court on Tuesday struck down President Trump’s executive order restricting birthright citizenship, ruling 6-3 that the government cannot deny automatic citizenship to children born in the United States because their parents are undocumented, temporary visitors or temporary visa holders [1][4][11][12][23][38]. Trump signed the order on January 20, 2025, his first day back in office, directing federal agencies not to recognize such children as citizens if neither parent was a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident; lower courts blocked it before it took effect [1][4][20][38]. Chief Justice John Roberts wrote that children born in the United States to parents “unlawfully or temporarily present” are “subject to the jurisdiction” of the United States and are citizens at birth under the 14th Amendment, an opinion joined on constitutional grounds by Justices Amy Coney Barrett, Sonia Sotomayor, Elena Kagan and Ketanji Brown Jackson [11][12][23][41]. Justice Brett Kavanaugh agreed the court should strike down the order but said it violated federal law rather than the Constitution, while Justices Clarence Thomas, Samuel Alito and Neil Gorsuch dissented [1][20][31][41]. Trump called the ruling “too bad” on Truth Social and urged Congress to start work on legislation to end birthright citizenship [3][23][50].

Key Points

  • Narrow exceptions: The ruling left intact the established limited exceptions to birthright citizenship, including children of foreign diplomats and invading soldiers [64][52][23].
  • Courtroom appearance: Trump personally attended April oral arguments, staying for Solicitor General John Sauer’s presentation but leaving before the ACLU argument by Cecillia Wang [11][12][63].
  • Birth tourism: Sauer argued during Supreme Court proceedings that unrestricted birthright citizenship encourages illegal immigration and “birth tourism,” while the Justice Department later directed prosecutors to prioritize birth-tourism investigations after the ruling [63][21].
  • Visa families: Firstpost said the decision gives relief to Indians in the United States on temporary visas because their U.S.-born children remain protected by automatic citizenship [2].

Perspectives

President Trump: Trump criticized the ruling in a Truth Social post by congratulating President Xi Jinping and China, even though China does not grant automatic citizenship solely by place of birth.

Technical Details

  • Constitutional versus statutory holding: Five justices found a 14th Amendment violation, while Kavanaugh provided the sixth vote to invalidate the order on statutory grounds, leaving disagreement over whether Congress could legislate new exceptions [20][41][31].

Historical Background

The 14th Amendment was ratified in 1868 after the Civil War and overturned the Supreme Court’s 1857 Dred Scott decision, which had declared that people of African descent could not be U.S. citizens [48][23]. In 1898, the Supreme Court’s Wong Kim Ark precedent addressed birthright citizenship for a child born in the United States to noncitizen parents, a case cited by legal experts in the current debate [51][65].

Travel Advisory

  • Visa: The Trump administration had warned foreign nationals against birth tourism, and Nigerian reports said the U.S. embassy in Nigeria stated last July that travel applications could be denied if consular officers believed birthright citizenship was the main motivation [55][60]. Do not use a U.S. visitor visa primarily to give birth for citizenship purposes.
