Egypt train collision kills seven, injures at least 112 near Cairo

Category: World

Two passenger trains collided in al-Ayyat, Giza province, about 52 kilometres south of Cairo, killing seven people, including a train conductor. Egypt’s Health Ministry reported 112 injuries, while later accounts citing official figures put the number at 125. A Giza-to-Fayoum train struck the rear of a Cairo-to-Aswan service [6][18][65]. An initial inspection indicated that the Aswan-bound train was stationary on the same track, but authorities have not announced a cause [4][12]. Egyptian prosecutors opened an investigation into the technical causes, and President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi ordered state agencies to provide medical care and hold anyone found negligent accountable [6][1]. Rescue teams used cranes to clear the wreckage and restore traffic on the Cairo-Upper Egypt line; the railway authority gave no timetable for resuming service [6][33]. The Health Ministry said hospitals treated people for injuries ranging from bruises to fractures and head and chest wounds [47]. Egypt has invested billions of dollars in transport infrastructure over the past decade, including highways and high-speed rail, while much of its existing rail network remains ageing [5][8].

Key Points

  • Hospital care: The Health Ministry said 71 injured people had been discharged by late Sunday, while 43 remained under observation across three hospitals [65].
  • Emergency response: Egypt deployed 72 ambulances, and hospitals in Giza and neighbouring Beni Suef were ordered to raise their readiness levels [55][52].
  • Crash location: Railway authorities identified the collision point as the line between Kafr Ammar and Al-Riqqa stations; the two services were trains 134 and 2010 [55][65].
  • Public pressure: Calls emerged in Egypt for the transport minister to resign after the crash [7].

Perspectives

Humanitarian Impact

Three hospitals received 132 casualties: Al-Wasta Central Hospital received 87, including three of the dead; Al-Ayyat Central Hospital received 34; and Atfih Central Hospital received 11, including four of the dead [65]. Eleven injured people were transferred to two hospitals in Beni Suef for further treatment [65].

Q: What compensation has been set for victims’ families?

A: Egypt’s Financial Regulatory Authority increased compensation for deaths and total permanent disability in this crash from 30,000 to 50,000 Egyptian pounds; other injuries remain covered under existing rules after medical reports are submitted [65].
