Anthropic IPO tests whether AI labs can finance compute independently

Category: AI

Anthropic’s planned Nasdaq listing would raise money directly from public markets to pay for the computing infrastructure needed to train and run its AI systems. The Claude developer’s investor roadshow has drawn orders for about six times the number of shares on offer. Investors are weighing whether Anthropic can maintain its edge as Google and other rivals advance and whether it can win customers beyond coding [32][41][21]. Chief Executive Dario Amodei has described computing capacity as the company’s central business dilemma: Anthropic must commit to capacity one to two years before it knows whether future revenue will cover the cost. Its commitments have reached nearly $901 billion, most tied to computing and chips, with about $71 billion due by September 2027, according to Bloomberg figures cited by The Decoder [9]. Nvidia supplies chips and has helped finance AI labs and data-center developers. A public listing could give Anthropic another source of capital, though investors question whether it can sustain demand and pricing [34][32]. Anthropic is expected to set its IPO price on Nov. 18 and begin trading on Nasdaq on Nov. 19, subject to regulatory clearance [23].

Key Points

  • Recent models: Models released in the six months before the IPO generated more than 80% of Anthropic’s third-quarter revenue, according to a Shinhan Investment & Securities analysis [40].
  • Strategic funding: Seven technology companies, including Nvidia, Alphabet, Microsoft and Broadcom, committed a combined $26 billion to buy Anthropic shares in a private placement alongside the IPO [37][19].
  • Compute financing: Nvidia, rather than Anthropic, holds the data-center lease in Anthropic’s $35 billion cloud-computing deal with Nvidia-backed Lambda [34].
  • Public opposition: A Spanish-language ABC report said opposition to data-center construction helped mobilize voters in the U.S. midterm elections, with some winning candidates campaigning for AI regulation and limits on data-center growth [33].

Perspectives

Dario Amodei, Anthropic chief executive: Amodei said Anthropic’s gross margins are strong and that cash reserves provide a buffer against compute uncertainty. He said the company buys relatively little capacity upfront and can use the spot market for additional compute if demand rises, comparing that flexibility to insurance [9].

Industry Impact

  • Cloud-provider exposure: Anthropic agreed to spend at least $110 billion with Amazon over 10 years, even if it uses less computing capacity [26].
  • Enterprise spending: Existing Anthropic customers reportedly spend roughly nine times as much in their second year as in their first [10].

Historical Background

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by seven people, including former OpenAI executives Dario and Daniela Amodei, after they became concerned about OpenAI’s approach to AI safety [38]. The group included researchers and technical staff who had worked on OpenAI models, and company leaders said they wanted to shape the development of advanced AI [38].

Q&A

Q: What financing options could a public listing open up beyond the IPO?

A: Anthropic could later raise capital through at-the-market share sales, private placements or convertible bonds, according to Bloomberg Opinion columnist Shuli Ren [34].

Q: What are investors using to estimate Anthropic’s future scale?

A: Banks are using projected 2028 revenue of about $270 billion to $280 billion in valuation models. One investor who declined to participate estimated 2028 revenue at about 30% below that range [32][41].
