SpaceX shares fall 13% after AI spending surge

Category: Business

SpaceX shares fell more than 13% on Wednesday after the company’s first quarterly report as a public company showed stronger-than-expected revenue but a sharp increase in spending on artificial-intelligence infrastructure [4][54][74]. The company reported second-quarter revenue of about $7.8 billion, up 92% from a year earlier, and a net loss of $541 million, compared with about $1 billion a year earlier [2][12][19][20]. Investors focused on capital spending: SpaceX disclosed total second-quarter capex of roughly $18.4 billion, including about $15.8 billion for AI infrastructure, and executives said spending could remain near current levels for several quarters [10][18][20][76]. Starlink remained the company’s largest reported revenue source, with connectivity revenue of $4.3 billion and subscribers doubling to 12 million; AI revenue rose to about $2.6 billion, helped by cloud-computing deals with Google and Anthropic [12][18][54][67]. President and Chief Executive Elon Musk told investors that people were “underestimating” SpaceX and said the company now expects to reach $1 trillion in annual revenue in 2030, one year earlier than its prior forecast [3][4][44][40]. The stock traded below its $135 IPO price after topping $200 shortly after its June debut, while investors also watched the first post-IPO lock-up expiration, which would allow some insiders to sell shares [11][58][1].

Key Points

  • Cash pressure: AI-related investments accounted for about 86% of SpaceX’s second-quarter capital expenditures, as investors scrutinized free-cash-flow outflows despite the revenue beat [10][70][54].
  • Segment split: SpaceX’s space segment reported $962 million in revenue and a $542 million net loss in the quarter, while Starlink was described as the only profitable business line [47][43][52].
  • Compute target: Musk said SpaceX aims to expand AI compute capacity from about 1.4 gigawatts currently, or around 2 gigawatts by year-end in some accounts, to roughly 10 gigawatts in 2027 [3][31][64].
  • Mobile ambitions: SpaceX outlined plans to challenge major U.S. telecom operators through Starlink mobile services, including potential competition with Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile US [67][74].

Industry Impact

  • AI cloud infrastructure: SpaceX is rapidly expanding its AI compute rental business, putting it in direct competition with established cloud and AI-infrastructure providers [3][67][58].
  • Telecommunications: Starlink’s rapid subscriber growth and SpaceX’s plans to compete more directly in mobile connectivity are increasing pressure on incumbent telecom operators [12][67][74].
  • Launch market: SpaceX completed 38 launches in the quarter, down from 46 a year earlier, while management said it wanted Starship launch frequency to rise to at least once a day within a year [24].

Business Angle

  • Starlink leads revenue and is the only segment repeatedly described as profitable [12][52][67].
  • SpaceX’s AI capex coincided with continuing losses, keeping cash returns in focus for investors [10][54].
  • The lock-up schedule could increase tradable shares and add pressure even if operating metrics continue to improve [1][76].
  • Analysts questioned a valuation near trillion-dollar levels because the company remains loss-making despite rapid revenue growth and long-range targets [69][14].
