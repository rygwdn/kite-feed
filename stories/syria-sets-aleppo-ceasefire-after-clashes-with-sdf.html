Syria sets Aleppo ceasefire after clashes with SDF

Category: World

Syria’s Defence Ministry announced an early-Friday ceasefire covering three northern Aleppo neighbourhoods after several days of clashes between government forces and Kurdish-led fighters [1][3][4]. The fighting, described as the worst violence since Islamist authorities took power last year, killed at least 21 people and forced large-scale displacement from the city [1][7][11]. The truce also included a limited window for armed groups to leave the contested areas, in a bid to prevent further fighting inside residential districts [3][7][14].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: The SDF was a key force in the territorial defeat of the Islamic State group in Syria in 2019 [7].

Key Points

  • Evacuation measures: Syrian authorities published maps of areas they said would be targeted and imposed a curfew in Sheikh Maqsoud and Ashrafieh, while also opening an evacuation corridor for civilians to leave contested districts [2][10].
  • Scale of flight: Reporting on the ground described widespread displacement from Aleppo, with one estimate putting the number of people driven from their homes at more than 140,000 during the latest fighting [2][6].
  • Mediation efforts: Reuters reporting cited by multiple outlets said the United States was spearheading efforts to reach a ceasefire between Damascus and the SDF [2][12][3].
  • Deal still stuck: Several outlets linked the clashes to stalled implementation of an agreement meant to merge SDF-linked civil and military structures into the central government by the end of 2025 [10][7][13].
  • Earlier autonomy: RT said Damascus and Kurdish leaders struck an Aleppo deal in April granting autonomy under Damascus’ rule, but said a broader reintegration agreement was never implemented, with both sides blaming the other [5].

Perspectives

Syrian Defence Ministry: It said it ordered a ceasefire around Sheikh Maqsoud, Ashrafiyeh and Bani Zeid to prevent further escalation in residential areas, and framed the step as enabling displaced civilians to return to normal life in security and stability.

Sources: The New Arab

Syrian government (Damascus): It demanded the withdrawal of what it called “militia groups” and accused the SDF of fueling instability and violating a prior accord.

Sources: RT

Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF): It said its forces pushed back government attacks, and its commander Mazloum Abdi warned that strikes and tank deployments undermined chances for understandings and increased risks for civilians in the area.

Sources: Dawn

International mediators/observers: Some coverage emphasized fears the Aleppo fighting could widen unless mediation succeeds, as displacement grows and talks remain fragile.

Sources: DW

Timeline

  • April: RT reports Damascus-Aleppo Kurdish deal granted local autonomy [5].
  • March: Damascus signed an integration deal with the SDF [10].
  • Tuesday: Clashes erupted in Aleppo after talks stalled [2][8][13].
  • Thursday: Authorities announced curfews and issued evacuation guidance and maps [2][10].
  • Friday: Defence ministry announced ceasefire and time window to leave [1][3][7].

Technical Details

  • Ceasefire window: Several reports said the truce included a time-limited window (described as six hours in one account) for armed groups to leave contested neighbourhoods, aiming to reduce fighting in residential areas [3][7].
  • Curfew and targeting maps: Authorities announced curfews in specific districts and released maps marking areas they said would be targeted, a tactic intended to push evacuations and shape movement during operations [2].
  • Drones in fighting: RT reported footage showing Syrian troops using drones during the clashes in Aleppo’s northern districts [5].

Industry Impact

  • Aid and rescue services: The latest shelling set fires that rescue workers rushed to extinguish, underscoring how quickly renewed urban fighting can overwhelm local emergency response capacity [2][3].
  • Governance and energy leverage: The SDF’s control over large parts of Syria’s oil-rich north and northeast adds leverage to political negotiations, and prolonged standoffs can complicate efforts by Damascus to centralize authority [7].

Historical Background

Syria has experienced about 14 years of conflict, and President Ahmed al-Sharaa has said he wants to reunite the country after the civil war [2]. The SDF emerged as a key U.S.-backed force and played a central role in the territorial defeat of the Islamic State group in Syria in 2019 [7]. More recently, Damascus and the SDF signed an agreement intended to integrate the SDF into the Syrian army by the end of 2025, but outlets say disagreements over implementation persisted and talks stalled ahead of the latest fighting [10][13].

Q&A

Q: Which neighbourhoods did the ceasefire specifically cover, and what restrictions accompanied it?

A: Reports said the ceasefire applied to Sheikh Maqsoud, Ashrafiyeh and Bani Zeid, and authorities also imposed a curfew in parts of the Kurdish-majority areas [5][7].

Q: What warning steps did Syrian authorities take before renewed strikes in contested areas?

A: Coverage said the army issued maps identifying areas it said would be targeted and provided a window for residents to evacuate before launching new strikes [2].

Q: What concern did SDF commander Mazloum Abdi raise about the fighting’s effects on civilians and local dynamics?

A: He said strikes and tank deployments undermined chances for understandings, could create conditions for “dangerous demographic changes,” and put trapped civilians at risk [2].
