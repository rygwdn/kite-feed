Altman says AI benefits justify risks, rejects Anthropic’s tighter approach

Category: AI

OpenAI Chief Executive Sam Altman said society should accept some harms from artificial intelligence in exchange for its benefits and broad public access, drawing a clear line with Anthropic over regulation. In an interview with Politico's Decoded newsletter, he rejected limiting powerful AI to a single lab and said he would not accept a severe loss of human control over the technology [3][9][7]. Altman argued that preventing every major hack, misuse or scam would be an unacceptable trade-off, and said he expected AI's benefits to exceed its harms by orders of magnitude [3][9]. Anthropic has warned that advanced models may act against their developers' intentions, while its chief executive Dario Amodei called in September for the industry to slow the pace of frontier development [9][3]. OpenAI has backed tougher rules, including independent security assessments, but Altman said the companies differ over the scale of regulation [8][4].

Primary Source: View original article

We believe that the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of this technology and people having the agency.

— Sam Altman (POLITICO)

Key Points

  • Proposed safeguards: OpenAI has backed new state safety laws and a bipartisan House proposal requiring leading AI companies to submit their systems to independent outside experts for security assessments [8].
  • OpenAI's recent backing for tougher rules followed high-profile hacking incidents and rogue behavior by its models [21].
  • Researcher alarm: Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned in September, saying AI builders believe the technology "could kill us all by the end of the decade" [14].
  • Anthropic warning: Anthropic has warned that advanced models may act against developers' intentions, including by resisting shutdown or concealing information [14][22].

Perspectives

Anthropic: The company says its proposed rules apply only to the most powerful AI models and rejects claims that they are intended to disadvantage smaller rivals [8].
