Houthi airport attacks kill three in Saudi Arabia; coalition retaliates

Category: World

Saudi Arabia’s civil aviation authority said attacks on Abha and King Khalid airports on Oct. 6 and 7 killed three people and wounded 36. Two women, from Morocco and Algeria, died at Abha, where 28 people were injured; a Sudanese resident was killed and eight people were wounded at Riyadh’s King Khalid airport. The Houthis claimed responsibility, while the aviation authority did not publicly assign blame [5][14][6]. The Saudi-led coalition said it struck 82 Houthi military sites in Yemen after warning that the attacks would not go unanswered. It listed missile facilities, command centers, communications systems and gathering sites among the targets [21][7]. On Thursday, the Houthis claimed another ballistic-missile strike on Riyadh airport and said it disrupted operations; Saudi authorities had not confirmed that claim in the available reports [7][53]. The Houthis also warned international airlines against using Saudi airspace [7][18]. Yemeni government forces, backed by Saudi-led coalition air support, are pressing a counteroffensive near the Bab al-Mandab Strait and Mocha, areas the Houthis captured in September. The strait is a key shipping route, with energy exports increasingly reliant on it as trade through the Strait of Hormuz faces disruption [6][34].

Key Points

  • Strike targets: The coalition said its retaliation covered the Yemeni provinces of Saada, Al Hudaydah, Al Jawf and Ma’rib; it gave no casualty figures, and the claims could not be independently verified [5][7].
  • Missile platform: The coalition said it destroyed the launch platform used in the Riyadh attack, while separately reporting that it intercepted a ballistic missile north of the capital [14][44].
  • Aden airport: A missile landed near the runway minutes before a Cairo flight was due to arrive; the plane was diverted to Jeddah [14][21].
  • External support: Two officials told Reuters that Turkey was providing Saudi Arabia mainly defensive and technical support as Riyadh sought help against Houthi attacks [6][44].

Perspectives

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree: Saree said the attacks were a response to Saudi actions against Yemen, citing 60 airstrikes and missile attacks in the preceding 24 hours [5].

Humanitarian Impact

More than 200,000 people have been displaced within Yemen since the latest fighting began, and the U.N. says three in four families in some areas are going hungry. The IOM cited urgent needs for food and shelter [6][34].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇳 United Nations: Secretary-General Antonio Guterres condemned continued Houthi cross-border attacks on Saudi Arabia and said he was deeply concerned by the sharp escalation in fighting [5].
  • 🇬🇨 Gulf Cooperation Council: Secretary-General Jassim Mohammed al-Budaiwi condemned the attacks on civilian airports and said they endangered travelers, workers and civilians [41].

Travel Advisory

  • Flight safety: The U.S. Mission in Saudi Arabia warned that Houthi threats could lead to flight cancellations, airspace closures and other travel disruptions [28]. Check flight status directly with the airline and airport before traveling [28].
  • Border security: The U.S. Mission advised Americans not to travel near the Yemeni border and to follow Saudi Civil Defense alerts [28]. Avoid the border area and follow instructions from Saudi authorities [28].
