Israel says Gaza Rafah crossing reopens Monday

Category: World

Israel says the Rafah crossing between Gaza and Egypt will resume limited movement of people from Monday, following a “pilot” reopening aimed at testing procedures after a long period of near-closure since May 2024 [1][4][14][17]. Officials described the initial phase as pedestrian-only travel, with movements expected in both directions under coordination with Egypt and with oversight linked to an EU mission mechanism referenced by Israel [2][18][15]. The step is presented as part of the ceasefire’s next phase, and many residents see Rafah as Gaza’s most direct route to the outside world, including for medical travel [11][5][23]. At the same time, several outlets report that key details—such as who qualifies to leave and how many can go—remain unclear, leaving patients and families uncertain about when help will arrive [3][19][8].

Primary Source: View original article

The Rafah crossing has reopened for movement of people only.

— COGAT (Israel’s Coordinator of Government Activities in the Territories) (Politico Europe)

Did you know: The Rafah crossing has been widely described as Gaza residents’ primary “gateway to the world” because it is Gaza’s direct link with Egypt rather than Israel [5].

Key Points

  • Daily capacity: France 24 reported Israel said about 150 people would be able to cross per day during the initial arrangement [21].
  • Returnees waiting: A Palestinian official told Al Jazeera that roughly 80,000 Palestinians who left Gaza are seeking to return via Rafah from Egypt [15].
  • Aid still blocked: Multiple reports say the reopening plan focuses on people, while humanitarian aid and goods are not part of the announced Rafah arrangement at this stage [20][8][18].
  • EU monitoring role: The New Arab cited an EU official saying the EU Border Assistance Mission at Rafah (EUBAM) would monitor agreed procedures under earlier arrangements and does not decide whether the crossing opens or closes [8].
  • MSF dispute: Politico and The Globe and Mail reported Israel said it would terminate Doctors Without Borders’ operations in Gaza over a registration-related dispute; MSF said it would not provide staff lists without assurances [15][22].

Timeline

  • May 2024: Rafah crossing largely shut, limiting Gaza’s direct exit route [4][12].
  • Sunday: Israel begins a pilot reopening to test Rafah operations [14][15].
  • Monday: Officials expect pedestrian travel in both directions to start [1][17].

Technical Details

  • Pilot reopening: Israel described the initial step as a test run to assess Rafah’s operational procedures before broader movement begins [14][15].
  • Pedestrian-only travel: Several outlets report the current plan allows people to cross on foot, without vehicle transit as part of this phase [2][18][4].
  • Security clearance mechanism: Israel said crossings would require prior security clearance of individuals, coordinated with Egypt and overseen under an EU-linked mechanism [15].

Industry Impact

  • Medical referrals: Hospitals and referral networks may see increased demand for cross-border transfers if Rafah processes stabilize, but reports emphasize uncertainty over criteria and throughput during the pilot [3][19].
  • Humanitarian operations: Aid groups’ ability to operate faces administrative and regulatory pressure, highlighted by reports of Israel’s move against Doctors Without Borders over registration requirements [15][22].

Historical Background

Before the current war, Rafah was the only crossing that connected Gaza directly to Egypt, making it the main route for most residents to reach the outside world and a key entry point for assistance [4][12][23]. Multiple outlets say it has been largely shut since May 2024, contributing to Gaza’s near-isolation and making any reopening significant for families separated across borders and for those seeking treatment abroad [4][5][12]. The current move is framed by several sources as part of the ceasefire’s subsequent phase, with operational mechanisms referencing earlier EU-supervised arrangements [11][14][15].

Q&A

Q: What documents or approvals will travelers need to pass through Rafah under the new mechanism?

A: Israel said travel would require prior security clearance of individuals and coordination with Egypt under an EU-supervised mechanism, but detailed document requirements were not fully specified in the reports [15][19].

Q: Will emergency medical evacuations be prioritized in the first weeks, and if so, how will cases be selected?

A: Coverage describes confusion about criteria and limited slots for patients during the pilot phase, with no clear, publicly defined selection process reported across the cited articles [3][8].

Q: Could Rafah later reopen for goods and humanitarian aid, and what would need to change?

A: Several reports say the current plan focuses on the movement of people and does not include goods or a surge in aid via Rafah at this stage, implying a separate decision would be needed for cargo access [18][20][8].
