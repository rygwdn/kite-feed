Netflix drops Warner Bros. Discovery bid as Paramount advances

Category: Technology

Netflix has dropped out of the bidding to acquire Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD), opting not to raise its offer after WBD’s board said Paramount Skydance’s proposal was better and gave Netflix a short window to respond [5][1][11]. Netflix co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters said matching Paramount’s latest price no longer made financial sense [2][7][11]. With Netflix stepping back, Paramount Skydance has agreed to buy WBD, and WBD has formally accepted Paramount’s offer. Under the prior deal terms, Netflix will collect a $2.8 billion termination fee [8][2]. The merger still needs regulatory approval in the US and abroad, and California’s attorney general has also signaled scrutiny [8][4]. For consumers and the streaming ecosystem, the near-term takeaway is a likely shake-up in how big content libraries and streaming services are packaged, priced, and distributed. Paramount has floated additional concessions aimed at easing industry and regulator concerns, and those promises could ripple into release and distribution norms beyond the combined company [8].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Ars Technica reported Paramount agreed to a “ticking fee” of $0.25 per share for every quarter the deal doesn’t close, starting on September 30, 2026 [11].

Key Points

  • Deal terms: Engadget reported Paramount’s offer values WBD at $31 per share and includes commitments such as 30 theatrical films a year and a 45-day minimum theatrical window before VOD [8].
  • Regulatory backstop: Paramount agreed to a $7 billion regulatory termination fee if antitrust blocks the merger, according to Ars Technica [11].
  • Investor reaction: Netflix shares rose 8.5% in after-hours trading after it walked away, a sign investors welcomed the price discipline [7][11].
  • What Netflix wanted: Engadget said Netflix mainly wanted Warner Bros. proper, while Paramount Skydance wanted the whole company, including the cable networks [8].
  • Scope of control: Wired detailed how the Ellison family’s control could expand significantly if the deal is approved, as Paramount Skydance’s media footprint would grow with WBD assets [9].

Perspectives

Netflix (Sarandos and Peters): The co-CEOs said Netflix chose not to match Paramount Skydance’s latest bid because the required price was no longer financially attractive, describing the acquisition as a deal that was only worth doing at the right price [2][7].

Sources: Android Headlines, MacRumors

Tech and media critics: Techdirt argued the deal would concentrate control of major media brands under Larry Ellison, calling him a Trump ally and highlighting the breadth of assets involved [12].

Sources: Techdirt

Newsroom staff concerns: Boing Boing wrote that CNN employees were bracing for “synergies” that could mean layoffs and editorial changes under new ownership [13].

Sources: Boing Boing

Timeline

  • 5 December 2025: Netflix and WBD announce merger intentions [11].
  • 25 February 2026: Paramount raises bid by $1 per share and adds fees [11].
  • 27 February 2026: WBD board deems Paramount offer superior; Netflix declines to match [5][1][8].
  • 27 February 2026: Paramount agrees to buy WBD; WBD pays Netflix a breakup fee under the prior deal terms [8].
  • 30 September 2026: Paramount’s $0.25-per-share quarterly ticking fee begins on September 30 if the deal has not closed, according to Ars Technica [11].
  • 2026-Q3: Companies target merger close by Q3 2026 [8].

Industry Impact

  • Release strategy signaling: Paramount’s agreement includes theatrical-focused assurances that could pressure other streamers and studios to maintain (or re-adopt) more theater-first release patterns when trying to win over regulators and industry stakeholders [8].
  • Streaming packaging and licensing: Because Paramount Skydance is pursuing the entire WBD business (not just studio assets), the combined company could renegotiate licensing and bundling from a stronger position—one that includes major streaming brands alongside legacy cable channels. That could shift what stays exclusive versus what gets licensed out [8][11].
  • Profitability and execution risk: Ars Technica noted both Paramount and WBD have struggled with profitability and have leaned heavily into streaming and cable, so the merger’s impact may hinge as much on cost-cutting and integration as on headline-grabbing IP consolidation [10].

Historical Background

Paramount has reportedly eyed Warner Bros. Discovery for years, but the current contest intensified after Netflix and WBD announced merger intentions on Dec. 5, 2025 [11]. In that earlier structure, Netflix was set to acquire a narrower slice of WBD focused on streaming and studio assets, while Paramount Skydance pushed to buy the entire company, including its cable networks—an important distinction that shaped how each bidder argued value and regulatory risk [11][8].
