Trump demands NATO loyalty after Iran war dispute

Category: World

President Trump told NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte in the Oval Office on June 24 that alliance members had “let down” the United States by not backing the U.S.-Israeli war against Iran, naming Italy, the United Kingdom, Germany, France and Spain among countries that disappointed him [4][9][5][8][10]. Trump said Washington did not need military help but wanted allies to offer support, and he cast the dispute as a demand for “loyalty” from countries the U.S. protects through its troop presence in Europe [7][8][16][62]. Rutte defended NATO members during the meeting, saying allied reluctance involved “isolated cases” [9][67][45]. The White House meeting came before the NATO leaders’ summit scheduled for July 7-8 in Ankara, Turkey. It followed Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s announcement of a six-month review of U.S. troop deployments in Europe that could lead to reductions [17][73][76][67]. The United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran on February 28, and Trump did not consult NATO beforehand, while key European allies questioned the necessity of the war [9][24][96].

Key Points

  • Spending pitch: Rutte brought printed charts to the Oval Office showing increased NATO defense spending since Trump first took office in 2017, including a “Trump Trillion” presentation that the Lithuanian business daily VZ described as highlighting $1.2 trillion in defense spending [82][83][101].
  • Italy dispute: Italy’s defense ministry rejected Rutte’s statement that 500 U.S. planes took off from U.S. bases in Italy for Operation Epic Fury, saying Rome authorized only technical and logistical, non-kinetic activities and rejected requests beyond that scope [39][61][34].
  • Hormuz friction: Trump also faulted allies for refusing to help reopen the Strait of Hormuz after the February 28 U.S.-Israeli attack disrupted a major oil shipping route [78][76][77].
  • Summit leverage: Trump said he would attend the Ankara summit because Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan urged him to be there, adding that he did not think he would have gone otherwise [2][101].

Industry Impact

  • Energy shipping: The Strait of Hormuz dispute drew NATO into an energy-security fight because the U.S.-Israeli attack disrupted a major oil shipping route and Trump wanted allies to help restore traffic [78][76][77].
  • Defense production: Rutte argued that European and Canadian defense investments benefit the U.S. job market as arms production rises to meet demand, while Euronews reported he planned to highlight $1 trillion in overall allied spending [75][98].

Historical Background

NATO was founded in 1949, and Article 5 of its treaty treats an armed attack on one member as an attack on all members [common]. The alliance has 32 members, whose leaders are scheduled to meet at the Ankara summit on July 7-8 [17][73]. Trump has repeatedly threatened to leave the 77-year-old alliance while pressing members to spend more on defense [67][70].
