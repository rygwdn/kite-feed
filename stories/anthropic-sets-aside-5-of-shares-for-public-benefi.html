Anthropic sets aside 5% of shares for public-benefit fund

Category: AI

Anthropic, the maker of the Claude AI assistant, said on Oct. 18 it would commit 5% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund, subject to shareholder approval. The company says the fund could make large grants to nonprofits or direct payments to specific groups, including to address AI-related labor-market disruption. Anthropic has already committed $200 million to study and address that issue [36][46]. The fund would be a wholly owned limited-liability company within Anthropic, rather than a traditional nonprofit. The board could allocate an additional 2% of company shares for every 50% increase in Anthropic’s value above an undisclosed baseline. Anthropic says the shares would not be used for lobbying or political contributions [36][43][46][56]. Anthropic says existing commitments by its co-founders and an employee share-donation matching program would bring the portion of shares designated for public purposes to about 20%, excluding possible future additions [36]. The company is planning a potential initial public offering later this year, possibly as soon as November, and says it will provide more details on the fund’s governance and valuation thresholds in the coming months [36][56].

Key Points

  • Estimated value: One report valued the proposed 5% stake at about $48 billion, based on Anthropic’s latest valuation [60].
  • Existing pledges: Anthropic says its six co-founders have pledged to donate 80% of their wealth, while about half its workforce participates in an employee equity-matching program. The company says roughly 20% of its shares are earmarked for public purposes across its existing commitments [71][36].
  • Risk disclosures: Anthropic devoted roughly 80 pages of its IPO prospectus to risk factors and said AI could pose “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity” [39][72].
  • IPO scale: Anthropic’s potential listing could value the company at more than $1 trillion, according to the BBC. The company told prospective investors its revenue rose 45% to $16.8 billion in the three months through September [72].
  • Work inside the company: Anthropic says much of its public-benefit work, including AI safety, interpretability and biomedical research, will continue within the company [43].
  • Worker impact: Anthropic cited AI’s labor-market effects as a possible priority. The International Monetary Fund estimates that AI could affect nearly 40% of jobs globally and up to 60% in advanced economies [38][40].
