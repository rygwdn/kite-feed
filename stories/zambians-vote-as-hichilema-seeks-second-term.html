Zambians vote as Hichilema seeks second term

Category: World

Zambians voted Thursday in a general election in which President Hakainde Hichilema is seeking a second five-year term after a first term dominated by debt restructuring, inflation control and public frustration over living costs [3][10][4][12]. Polling stations opened at 6 a.m. and were due to close 12 hours later, with 8.7 million registered voters, AFP reported [4][12][29][31]. Voters were also choosing members of parliament and local government leaders, and the electoral commission expected results on Monday [3][10][32]. Fourteen candidates are contesting the presidency, with Brian Mundubile of an opposition alliance regarded as Hichilema’s strongest challenger [3][10][11][12]. Hichilema argues that he inherited an economy in crisis in 2021 and has since overseen debt restructuring, renewed growth and investor interest, while many Zambians say unemployment, high food prices and unstable power supplies still strain household budgets [3][10][30][16]. Zambia’s copper sector is central to the country’s export earnings and to global demand for power grids, electric vehicles and renewable-energy infrastructure [6][7][18]. Zambia has long been regarded as one of Africa’s more stable democracies, but the campaign drew scrutiny over dissent, opposition restrictions and allegations of political intimidation [4][26][2][21].

Key Points

  • Economy: Inflation fell to 6.8% in April and the kwacha strengthened, but households continued to face high food and energy costs [4][29].
  • Jobs: Zambia's unemployment rate stands at nearly 10 percent, with employment and living costs among voters' main worries [20].
  • Critical minerals: The US and China are competing for access to Zambia's critical minerals [6].
  • Voting logistics: Election officials planned voting at 13,529 polling points nationwide, according to Zambian Electoral Commission data cited by BirGün [34].

Timeline

  • 2020: Zambia defaulted on sovereign debt during the pandemic [15][18].
  • 2021: Hichilema defeated Edgar Lungu and took office [11][9].
  • June 2025: Edgar Lungu died in South Africa [9].
  • 13 August 2026: Polling stations opened for presidential and legislative voting [4][12].
  • 17 August 2026: The Electoral Commission expected results by Monday [3][10].

Technical Details

  • Constituency expansion: The number of directly elected National Assembly seats rose from 156 to 226 after 70 new constituencies were created ahead of this vote [14][34].

Industry Impact

  • Mining: KoBold Metals, backed by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates, broke ground in May on a $2 billion copper project expected to become one of Zambia’s largest mines [18].
  • Public finance: Zambia’s public debt-to-GDP ratio stood above 90% in 2025, down from a 124% peak in 2021, but the government’s March debt sustainability analysis still classified the country at high risk [18].
  • Energy: Zambia announced what Die Presse described as the world’s first debt-for-energy swap in June, using debt-service savings to support expansion of an electricity grid that remains heavily dependent on hydropower [36].

Historical Background

Former president Edgar Lungu, who died in June 2025, remains a factor in the election. A long-running feud between Hichilema and Lungu's family persisted after Lungu's death, with the government going to court to block a family burial and the dispute reaching South Africa's supreme court of appeal [9].

International Reactions

  • 🌐 International Monetary Fund: Zambia’s debt restructuring after its 2020 default unlocked an IMF bailout and eased pressure on public finances [18].
