Clashes at Italian living-cost protests; 371 briefly detained

Category: World

Thousands of people demonstrated across Italy on Saturday, Oct. 24, against rising living costs and war. Police and protesters clashed in Rome, Turin and Bologna, while the Milan march was largely peaceful [5][19][16]. Police used tear gas and water cannons in several cities. Authorities said nine people were arrested and 371 were temporarily detained in eight cities, including 110 minors [20][22]. Demonstrators raised concerns about housing and fuel costs, wages, public services and military spending. Security measures followed the previous weekend’s looting and clashes in Milan, where shops in the fashion district boarded up windows as a precaution [9][5][20]. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni’s government sent 500 additional soldiers to Milan and approved tougher measures involving minors. On Saturday, police also detained 30-year-old freelance journalist Youssef Siher, suspected of organizing the 17 October Movement. They searched more than 150 locations in Milan, Monza and about 20 other cities as part of investigations into the earlier unrest [9][18][16].

Key Points

  • Far-right activists: About 30 activists linked to Inter Milan briefly appeared at the Milan march, chanting anti-immigrant and anti-Muslim slogans before police isolated them [5].
  • Online mobilization: The 17 October Movement reportedly had more than 40,000 Instagram followers and planned to share march locations shortly before gatherings, a tactic that complicated security planning [29][19].
  • Economic pressures: The demonstrations followed truck blockades in southern Italy that caused fuel and food shortages for a week; the blockades have since been lifted [9][20].
  • Investigation focus: Police searches targeted people under investigation over alleged threats and plans to attack police, damage vehicles and set buildings on fire [16][19].
