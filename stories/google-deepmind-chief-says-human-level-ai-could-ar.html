Google DeepMind chief says human-level AI could arrive by 2028

Category: AI

Demis Hassabis, co-founder of Google DeepMind, says artificial general intelligence (AGI) could arrive within two to three years, or in 2028 or 2029. In an interview published Oct. 22, the AI researcher said progress in autonomous agents had increased his confidence; his previous estimate was three to five years [4][2]. Hassabis has described a demanding test for AGI: whether a system could independently derive Albert Einstein’s 1905 physics theories from knowledge available through 1901. He said AI remains years away from meeting that benchmark [2].

Primary Source: View original article

It's soon.

— Demis Hassabis, Google DeepMind co-founder (Tom's Guide)

Key Points

  • Drug research: Hassabis created Isomorphic Labs within Google to use AI to overhaul drug research and development [2].
  • Safety proposal: He proposed an industry-funded, federally regulated body to test the most advanced AI models before release [2].
  • Remaining gaps: Hassabis has identified continuous learning, durable memory, reliable reasoning and consistency as capabilities current AI systems still lack [4].

Perspectives

Demis Hassabis: He said some of his urgent language about AGI was intended to prompt governments, economists and the public to take the approaching technology more seriously [4].
