Apple launches second-generation AirTag with Apple Watch finding

Category: Technology

Apple has released its second-generation AirTag (still branded simply as “AirTag”), keeping the familiar $29 puck design while adding practical upgrades aimed at making everyday item-finding easier and more reliable [7][2]. The biggest user-facing change is that Precision Finding now works on compatible Apple Watch models, so you can get directional guidance from your wrist as well as from an iPhone [1][7][8]. Across hands-on reviews and comparisons, outlets highlight longer effective range for Precision Finding, a louder speaker with a new chime, and updated wireless components that help the tag communicate more effectively with the Find My ecosystem [7][2][8][11]. Teardowns also point to internal redesigns—like a thinner main board and more adhesive—that appear intended to make physical tampering (especially with the speaker) more difficult [3][4][5]. For buyers, multiple guides frame AirTag 2 as an incremental but meaningful refresh: it preserves accessory compatibility and the replaceable CR2032 battery, while nudging the experience forward for people who frequently misplace keys, bags, or luggage—especially those who prefer using an Apple Watch as their primary “finder” device [4][8][12].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: MacRumors reports AirTag 2’s chime shifts from the note F to G [8].

Key Points

  • Setup steps: To use Precision Finding on Apple Watch, you need a compatible Watch model paired with a supported iPhone and then start the “Find Items” flow from the watch to get on-wrist direction and distance guidance [1].
  • Hardware changes: A teardown shows Apple slimmed the motherboard, enlarged the speaker coil, and used extensive glue while keeping the same exterior and CR2032 battery format [4].
  • Anti-tamper focus: Teardown coverage says the AirTag 2 speaker is now harder to remove, a change positioned as making tampering more difficult [3][5].
  • Upgrade trade-offs: Some reviewers note the AirTag still lacks a built-in keyring hole, so many owners will continue to rely on holders or cases [7].
  • OS compatibility: MacRumors reports the new model requires iOS 26.2.1 or later, which may matter for older-device households considering discounted first-generation stock [8].

Technical Details

  • Ultra Wideband (UWB) chip: AirTag 2 uses Apple’s second-generation Ultra Wideband chip to improve Precision Finding distance and enable compatible Apple Watch models to provide directional guidance [7][8].
  • Find My network: AirTag location can update via the Find My network by leveraging nearby Apple devices that detect it, supplementing close-range Precision Finding [7][11].
  • Rotating Bluetooth identifiers: MacRumors says the new AirTag rotates Bluetooth identifiers more frequently than the prior generation, alongside other wireless changes [8].

Industry Impact

  • Tracker accessories: Because the new AirTag still lacks a keyring hole, accessory makers for holders, cases, and keychains may continue to benefit from ongoing demand [7].
  • Repairability debates: Teardowns noting more glue and harder-to-remove speaker components may intensify discussion around serviceability versus safety/anti-tamper design in small consumer electronics [4][3][5].
  • Wearable utilities: By bringing Precision Finding to Apple Watch, Apple strengthens the watch’s role as a “daily problem-solver” device beyond fitness and notifications [1][7].

Historical Background

Apple introduced the original AirTag in 2021 as a Find My network accessory for locating personal items [8][11]. Since then, the Find My ecosystem has expanded across more Apple devices, and the new AirTag leans into that by extending Precision Finding beyond iPhone to supported Apple Watch models [7][1][8].

Q&A

Q: Does AirTag 2 still use a replaceable coin-cell battery?

A: Yes—teardown reporting says the second-generation AirTag still uses a CR2032 coin-cell battery [4].

Q: Can you reuse first-generation AirTag accessories with the new model?

A: Yes—MacRumors says the outward design remains the same and accessory compatibility is retained [8].

Q: What’s one small change that affects resetting the tag?

A: MacRumors notes AirTag 2 requires leaving the battery out for at least five seconds during each reset cycle [8].

Action Items

  • Enable Precision Finding on Apple Watch: Confirm you have a supported Apple Watch model, then follow the watch-based Find Items workflow to get direction and distance guidance to your AirTag [1].
  • Decide whether to upgrade: If you already own AirTag 1, compare the new model’s Apple Watch support, louder chime, and range changes against discounted first-gen prices before buying [8][12].
  • Choose a holder early: Plan on a keychain or holder if you want it on keys, since the AirTag still doesn’t include a keyring hole [7].
