Trump-backed de la Espriella leads Colombia runoff count

Category: World

Conservative political newcomer Abelardo de la Espriella claimed victory in Colombia’s June 21 presidential runoff after preliminary results showed him narrowly ahead of left-wing Senator Iván Cepeda. With nearly all ballots counted, de la Espriella had about 49.66% of the vote to Cepeda’s 48.70%, a margin of roughly 250,000 votes; election officials had not formally certified a winner, and a slower official count was expected in the coming days [4][1][12][14][22][28]. Cepeda said the preliminary count was “not yet official or binding” and said his campaign would challenge results from about 33,000 polling stations or ballot boxes, while President Gustavo Petro said neither candidate could be declared president until the formal process ended [1][12][14][22][2]. De la Espriella, a 47-year-old lawyer and businessman who has never held public office, campaigned on a tougher security policy against armed groups and drug trafficking and received President Trump’s endorsement during the campaign [8][13][16][17][28]. The runoff chose a successor to Petro, Colombia’s first left-wing president, who could not seek another term under the constitution [26][66]. De la Espriella said Trump congratulated him after the vote, and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the Trump administration looked forward to working with the future government on regional security cooperation, illegal migration and economic ties [4][37][36][48].

Key Points

  • Vote mechanics: Blank ballots made up about 1.6% of votes in the preliminary tally, a share larger than many past Colombian runoff margins but far below the two candidates’ totals [22][26][59].
  • Security agenda: De la Espriella proposed ending negotiations with armed groups, expanding military operations against guerrillas and drug traffickers, and building El Salvador-style megaprisons [16][67][47].
  • Economic plans: He pledged to strengthen Colombia’s oil and gas sector, cut taxes and reduce the state apparatus by up to 40%, while keeping Petro’s 23% minimum-wage increase and other popular social measures [49][53][66].
  • Governing hurdles: Reports citing Reuters said the next president would face high public debt and a divided Congress that could slow or dilute reform proposals [18][49][53].
  • Turnout record: Turnout exceeded 63.5% in the preliminary count, which Colombia Reports described as a historic record [59].

Industry Impact

  • Energy sector: De la Espriella campaigned on strengthening Colombia’s oil and gas sector, a shift from Cepeda’s pledge to continue Petro-aligned policies that included a moratorium on new oil projects [49][53].
  • Public administration: His platform included reducing the size of the state by up to 40%, a proposal that would affect public-sector employment, procurement and service delivery if implemented [49][53][66].
  • Security and prisons: His plans for megaprisons, longer sentences and wider military operations would redirect state spending toward security forces and detention capacity [16][67].

Historical Background

Colombia signed a peace accord with the FARC guerrilla movement in 2016, but armed groups remain active a decade later [38][47]. Petro became Colombia’s first left-wing president in 2022 and pursued talks with several illegal armed groups under his “Total Peace” strategy, which Cepeda promised to continue and de la Espriella promised to reverse [13][30][16].
