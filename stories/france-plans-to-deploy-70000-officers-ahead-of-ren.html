France plans to deploy 70,000 officers ahead of renewed protests after Oct. 17 riots

Category: World

France planned to deploy about 70,000 police and gendarmes nationwide on Saturday, 10,000 more than the previous weekend, ahead of demonstrations. The Oct. 17 protests were followed by looting, arson and clashes in several cities [8][10][5][15]. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said the operation would include elite units, helicopters, drones, armored vehicles and water cannon, citing online calls for violence and looting [6][12]. The demonstrations began with high school students protesting staff shortages and poor school facilities, then broadened into protests over the cost of living and public services [10][15]. Authorities restricted or banned gatherings in several cities. In Paris, police rerouted a union march away from Bastille and Nation; unions said they would follow the original route, which they considered safer than the proposed alternative [5][12]. In Orléans, a counter-protest expected to draw 20,000 to 30,000 people coincided with a National Rally party congress [2][30]. President Emmanuel Macron described a fatal shooting west of Paris on Friday as a racist terrorist attack [9]. Macron met student representatives on Friday, but they said they would continue mobilizing and were awaiting concrete proposals. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu was scheduled to meet student representatives on Monday [6][20].

  • Court sentences: Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin said courts had sent 217 people to prison over the Oct. 17 unrest; about half were first-time offenders [4][2].
  • Election polling: A Harris Interactive poll put Marine Le Pen at 33% to 35% in the first round and projected a 70% runoff result against Jean-Luc Mélenchon [3].
  • Rights concerns: Amnesty International said protest bans jeopardized the right to demonstrate peacefully, while Human Rights Watch raised concerns about reports of children seriously injured by crowd-control weapons [4][3].
  • Business losses: About 3,000 stores were looted and 2,300 people arrested on Oct. 17, according to figures reported by the Financial Times [10].
  • Student demands: Student representatives seek better school infrastructure and an overhaul of Parcoursup, France's university-admissions system [29].
