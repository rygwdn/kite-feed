Update: Iranian president’s son urges internet restoration amid crackdown

Category: World

Iran remains under a nationwide internet blackout as accounts and videos emerging despite restrictions describe a lethal crackdown on the country’s latest wave of protests [5][2][9]. Multiple reports cite exceptionally high casualty estimates, with TIME citing local health officials suggesting the death toll could exceed 30,000, including claims of tens of thousands killed over two days [3][5]. In a notable sign of internal debate, Yousef Pezeshkian—son of Iran’s president and a government adviser—publicly urged authorities to restore connectivity, arguing the shutdown deepens public anger and postpones accountability for violence shown in images and video [7][8][10].

Primary Source: View original article

Keeping the internet shut will create dissatisfaction and widen the gap between the people and the government

— Yousef Pezeshkian (The Hindu)

Did you know: Some Iranians have reported brief, unexplained periods of internet access even during the blackout, creating small openings for information to get out [9].

Key Points

  • Communications squeeze: Protests erupted amid a communications blackout, with outside reporting relying on fragments of video and witness testimony that filter out despite restrictions [2][5].
  • Street-level security: In multiple cities, heavily armed security forces patrol streets after the crackdown, while day-to-day commerce has been disrupted and businesses struggle to operate [4].
  • Medical signal: RFE/RL’s live coverage cited a hospital director reporting a sudden surge in eye injuries, a pattern often associated with crowd-control munitions during unrest [6].
  • Diaspora anxiety: Iranians living in the United States told The Guardian they feel anxious and helpless watching events unfold from afar, and they weighed the risks of possible U.S. involvement [1].
  • Connectivity flickers: Even under the two-week blackout, some Iranians have experienced brief and unexplained windows of internet access, widening what can be documented and shared [9].

Timeline

  • More than two-week blackout: Internet shutdown persists, though brief connectivity windows appear [5][9].

Technical Details

  • Internet blackout: A nationwide shutdown restricts access to online platforms and slows information flow, making verification harder and limiting coordination among protesters [5][2].
  • Communications windows: Intermittent, unexplained reconnections allow some users to upload footage or contact relatives before service drops again [9].
  • Eye injuries spike: A hospital director reported a sudden rise in eye injuries, a clinical pattern that can be consistent with certain crowd-control weapons during street clashes [6].

Industry Impact

  • Small businesses: With streets patrolled and instability continuing, businesses in Iran are reported to be struggling to stay afloat, suggesting near-term pressure on livelihoods and local supply chains [4].
  • Telecoms access: The prolonged blackout has left many relying on sporadic connectivity, reshaping how news, services, and even basic communications function inside the country [5][9].

Historical Background

Iran’s most recent unrest is described as the most serious since the 1979 revolution, underscoring how protest movements and state stability remain tightly linked in the Islamic Republic’s modern history [1].
