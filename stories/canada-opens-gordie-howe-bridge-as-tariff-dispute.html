Canada opens Gordie Howe Bridge as tariff dispute continues

Category: Canada

Canada marked the completion of the Gordie Howe International Bridge between Windsor, Ontario, and Detroit with a ceremony on July 24, while President Trump threatened new 50% tariffs on hundreds of Canadian goods slated to take effect Aug. 19 [4]. The six-lane crossing over the Detroit River opened to traffic on July 27 and creates a direct link between Interstate 75 in Michigan and Highway 401 in Ontario [6]. The tariff dispute has accelerated Canadian trade talks in Washington, David Paterson, Ontario’s representative there, told CBC. Paterson said the Trump administration’s complaint that U.S. auto exports to Canada have fallen 22% since April shows Canada’s retaliation is being noticed [4]. Canada still has retaliatory tariffs on U.S. cars, auto parts, steel and aluminum after lifting other levies last summer, and the Trump administration also cited provincial bans on U.S. alcohol in its tariff fact sheet [4]. A Leger poll found 80% of respondents had heard about President Trump’s latest tariff announcement and 76% viewed it as a serious economic threat, while 50% supported some level of tariff response [5]. Ontario Premier Doug Ford has urged Ottawa to target energy, potash, minerals and electricity, while Prime Minister Mark Carney has said “everything is on the table” [1].

  • Auto corridor: The Automotive Parts Manufacturers’ Association estimates $29 billion worth of U.S.-made auto parts enters Canada each year, much of it through Windsor [6].
  • Alcohol dispute: U.S. spirit makers called provincial bans on U.S. alcohol 'devastating' to their sales [4].
  • Retaliation debate: Trevor Tombe argued in The Hub that retaliation should be judged by whether it changes U.S. policy, not by whether it satisfies public anger or provincial pressure [1].

Historical Background

The Detroit-Windsor corridor has long been a central route for integrated North American manufacturing, especially the auto sector [common]. The Ambassador Bridge, a nearly century-old private crossing, forced many trucks onto city streets before reaching the border, while the Gordie Howe International Bridge adds a public crossing with direct freeway connections [6].
