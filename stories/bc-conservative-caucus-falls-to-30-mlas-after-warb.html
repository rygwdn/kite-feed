B.C. Conservative caucus falls to 30 MLAs after Warbus leaves

Category: Canada

Chilliwack-Cultus Lake MLA Á’a:líya Warbus said Monday she would leave the B.C. Conservative caucus and sit as an Independent, becoming the seventh MLA to exit in August while citing concerns about leader Kerry-Lynne Findlay [7][6][1]. The caucus now has 30 MLAs, down from the 44 elected under the Conservative banner in 2024, when the party came within three ridings of forming government [3][2]. Warbus said she lost confidence in Findlay after the leader cancelled a scheduled caucus meeting Monday, and cited what The Globe and Mail described as a “critical breakdown in communications” [7][5]. A small group of B.C. Conservative MLAs later met at a private home in Surrey to address caucus divisions, with participants including both Findlay supporters and MLAs unhappy with her leadership, according to Postmedia sources [1].

We're not getting those direct updates around staffing, around high level changes, around operations, the transparency and accountability that I would expect from this level of government

— Á’a:líya Warbus (CBC News)

Key Points

  • Earlier rupture: John Rustad expelled Dallas Brodie on March 7, 2025, over remarks he said mocked residential school survivors, and Tara Armstrong and Jordan Kealy left caucus later that day in sympathy with Brodie [3][2].
  • Leadership fallout: Rustad removed Elenore Sturko from caucus in September 2025 after surviving a leadership review, accusing her of plotting against him, an allegation Sturko denied [2].
  • Findlay seat: One of the 14 MLAs no longer in the Conservative caucus resigned her seat to clear the way for new leader Kerry-Lynne Findlay to seek a legislature seat [2].
  • Warbus concerns: Warbus told Postmedia that Findlay’s hires, the firing of caucus and party staff, and leaked audio about advisory roles for MLAs who stepped aside for a byelection contributed to her decision [1].
