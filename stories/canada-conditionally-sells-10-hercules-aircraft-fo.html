Canada conditionally sells 10 Hercules aircraft for wildfire tankers

Category: Canada

Canada has conditionally agreed to sell 10 retired Royal Canadian Air Force CC-130H Hercules aircraft to Coulson Aviation, a Port Alberni, B.C.-based company that plans to convert, maintain and operate them for aerial firefighting [1][3][5][7][8][9]. Secretary of State for Defence Procurement Stephen Fuhr announced the sale on Aug. 7 in Abbotsford, B.C., and Ottawa said the aircraft would strengthen Canada’s private-sector wildfire response capacity after certification for civil use [1][3][8]. The 10 planes come from a fleet of 12 CC-130H Hercules retired by the Royal Canadian Air Force in January 2026, with the remaining two aircraft to go on public display at bases and museums [3][5][8][9]. Coulson said it expects the first aircraft to be ready for the next wildfire season and, in a best-case timeline, could convert two aircraft by next year [3][4][5]. The sale still requires final contract arrangements and Transport Canada approvals to certify the former military aircraft for civil use [5][8][9]. Ottawa is handling the transaction through GCMil, the Public Services and Procurement Canada group that sells surplus military assets for National Defence, and the selling price and bid details have not been made public [1][8][5].

They are just such a powerful and capable aircraft.

— Wayne Coulson (Times Colonist)

Did you know: Coulson said the acquisition would make it the third-largest Hercules operator in the world [5].

Key Points

  • Tank capacity: Coulson plans to equip the aircraft with 4,000-gallon tanks, adding high-capacity suppression drops to its fleet [5].
  • Operational reach: Coulson said the Hercules aircraft can use short, rough runways and offer the range, versatility and suppression ability suited to wildfire missions [5].
  • Global shortage: Fuhr said Canada, Europe, Australia and the United States need more firefighting aircraft as the supply of water bombers tightens [3].
  • Private funding: Coulson will pay to convert and operate the aircraft before offering them for firefighting work [3].

Perspectives

Stephen Fuhr, Secretary of State for Defence Procurement: Fuhr called the transaction a “gold standard” divestment project and said he could not think of a better use for the aircraft because it would add aerospace jobs and firefighting capacity.

Sources: The Canadian Press via Lethbridge Herald

Wayne Coulson, Coulson Aviation CEO: Coulson described the large-capacity aircraft, which can use rough runways, as a “game changer” and a “potent firefighting aircraft,” and said the public will be pleased with their performance in Canada.

Sources: Times Colonist

Industry Impact

  • Aerospace jobs: Ottawa said Coulson’s planned conversions would support Canadian aerospace jobs and strengthen domestic private-sector wildfire response capacity [1][7].
  • Air tanker market: Coulson said the aircraft will double the size of its Hercules fleet and support firefighting contracts in markets including Australia, the United States and South America [5].
  • Provincial firefighting: Coulson said it is discussing how to integrate C-130s into British Columbia’s air tanker fleet, after having limited prior contracts to fight fires in Canada and B.C. [5].

Historical Background

The CC-130H Hercules served the Royal Canadian Air Force in transport and search-and-rescue roles before the fleet’s retirement in January 2026 [5][8].

Business Angle

  • Coulson gains aircraft that will double its Hercules fleet, but revenue depends on completing conversions and winning firefighting contracts after Transport Canada certification [5][8].
  • Canada keeps the sale price and bid details confidential, limiting outside assessment of whether Ottawa captured fair value for specialized surplus assets [8][5].
  • Provinces compete for a limited pool of aerial firefighting aircraft, making aircraft availability a constraint [8].
