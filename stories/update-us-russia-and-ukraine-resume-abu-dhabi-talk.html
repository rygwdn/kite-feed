Update: US, Russia and Ukraine resume Abu Dhabi talks

Category: World

In the latest development, Ukrainian, Russian and U.S. envoys resumed closed-door peace talks in Abu Dhabi on Saturday, continuing the first known trilateral meeting format since Russia’s full-scale invasion began in February 2022 [1][4][5][8][9]. Negotiators are working through a U.S.-backed plan pushed by President Trump, but the most difficult questions remain territorial—especially the Donbas region—and whether any security arrangement (such as European peacekeepers) can win acceptance from both sides [1][13][3][6]. The diplomatic push comes as Russia kept up air attacks that Ukrainian officials say worsened winter hardships, including damage that left parts of Kyiv without heat and fresh casualties reported in Kyiv and Kharkiv [1][17][21][22]. Even so, the fact that all three delegations are still meeting suggests at least a shared interest in keeping dialogue going, with the UAE positioning itself as a facilitator of de-escalation [1][7].

Key Points

  • Who is there: Ukraine’s delegation includes national security chief Rustem Umerov, general staff chief Andrey Gnatov, negotiator David Arakhamia, Deputy Foreign Minister Sergey Kislitsa and Zelensky aide Kirill Budanov, while Russia is led by Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov and GRU chief Admiral Igor Kostyukov, with U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner representing Washington [7].
  • UAE mediation: The UAE foreign ministry said the talks are part of ongoing efforts to promote dialogue and find a political solution, and its foreign minister argued sustainable outcomes require “dialogue and de-escalation” [1][7].
  • Pressure points: Kyiv faces mounting U.S. pressure to reach a deal, as early versions of the U.S. plan drew criticism in Ukraine and Western Europe for favoring Moscow’s line, while later iterations drew Russian objections over the idea of European peacekeepers [1][10][13].
  • Parallel diplomacy: The Abu Dhabi meetings followed President Zelensky’s appearance in Davos and separate U.S. outreach that included President Trump meeting Zelensky at the World Economic Forum and envoy Steve Witkoff meeting President Putin in the Kremlin [1].
  • Security and economy: Russia said its team would focus on security issues, and the Kremlin said Witkoff was expected to meet Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev separately to discuss economic matters [6][7].

Perspectives

Ukraine (negotiating team): Ukraine said talks focused on parameters for ending the war and the logic of further negotiations, while President Zelensky emphasized territory—especially Donbas—as central and urged Russia to show readiness to end the war [1][14][15].

Russia (Kremlin delegation): Moscow reiterated demands tied to territory, saying the territorial issue is the biggest obstacle and it wants Ukraine to accept Russia’s claimed borders, while insisting it can pursue battlefield goals if talks fail [6][3][16].

United States (Trump administration): Washington is pushing a President Trump-backed roadmap to end the war and joined the trilateral format in Abu Dhabi, with a White House official describing the first day as “productive,” according to RT’s reporting [1][5][6].

European Union: The EU accused Moscow of deliberately depriving civilians of heat and said it has sent hundreds of generators as Ukraine’s energy system suffers winter strikes [1].

Timeline

  • Friday: Trilateral talks begin in Abu Dhabi behind closed doors [7][3].
  • Thursday: President Trump meets Zelensky at Davos; Witkoff meets Putin [1].
  • Overnight into Saturday: Attacks hit Kyiv and Kharkiv as talks continue [17][22][21].
  • Saturday: Second day of Abu Dhabi negotiations scheduled to continue [1][4][6].

Technical Details

  • European peacekeepers: Later versions of the U.S. peace proposal floated European peacekeepers, which Russia criticized, making the security architecture itself a sticking point alongside territory [1][13].
  • Shahed drones: Kharkiv’s mayor reported attacks involving Iranian-made Shahed drones, highlighting the drone component of Russia’s strikes in the region [20].
  • Air raid alerts: Ukrainian authorities issued air raid alerts warning of drones and ballistic missiles during the overnight assault, underscoring the mixed-weapon nature of the attacks [18].

Historical Background

Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, starting a war that has killed tens of thousands and displaced millions, according to the reporting in these articles [1][3]. The Abu Dhabi meetings mark the first known trilateral talks including the United States, Russia and Ukraine since that invasion began [3][5][8][9].

Q&A

Q: Why are the Abu Dhabi negotiations being held behind closed doors?

A: The UAE-hosted talks are closed-door and “the press is not expected,” according to TASS reporting cited by RT, reflecting the sensitivity of territorial and security issues under discussion [7].

Q: What role could separate U.S.-Russia economic discussions play alongside peace talks?

A: The Kremlin said U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff was expected to meet Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev separately to discuss economic issues, suggesting a parallel track running alongside security and territorial negotiations [7].

Q: How have energy attacks influenced the diplomatic atmosphere this week?

A: Several reports link continuing strikes to worsened civilian conditions, including heating outages in Kyiv during sub-zero weather, which Ukrainian and EU officials cite as part of the broader pressure surrounding the talks [1].
