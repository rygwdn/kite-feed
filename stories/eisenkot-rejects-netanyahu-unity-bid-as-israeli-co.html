Eisenkot rejects Netanyahu unity bid as Israeli coalition talks continue

Category: World

Israeli caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu renewed his call Saturday for opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to form a national unity government, but Eisenkot rejected the appeal as a political maneuver as he negotiated to replace Netanyahu. Netanyahu said a broad coalition was needed to address security threats, particularly Iran’s nuclear program. Eisenkot said the parties’ negotiating teams had already arranged to meet and accused Netanyahu of trying to obstruct the formation of a government [6][7][16][3]. The October 27 election left no bloc with a majority in the 120-seat Knesset: Netanyahu’s bloc won 53 seats, Eisenkot’s bloc 48 and Arab-led parties 19. President Isaac Herzog tasked Eisenkot with forming a government, giving him 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension. Eisenkot says he could include Likud members and Religious Zionism if they accept his principles and sever ties with far-right allies, including supporters of National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir [6][7][27]. The Knesset speaker vote is scheduled for Tuesday, Nov. 17. Eisenkot has urged Likud and Religious Zionism lawmakers to back his bloc’s nominee, Matan Kahana. Netanyahu’s appeal did not clarify whether he would accept a government led by Eisenkot or exclude his far-right partners [4][7][16].

Key Points

  • Speaker vote: The opposition wants to replace Netanyahu ally Amir Ohana with Matan Kahana, a move that would give it control of the Knesset’s parliamentary agenda [7][4].
  • Draft dispute: Military conscription for ultra-Orthodox men is a central issue in coalition talks. Eisenkot supports a universal draft, while the Haredi community opposes it. Netanyahu said the matter could be negotiated with the community’s representatives [7][15].
  • Arab representation: Joint List lawmaker Ahmad Tibi said Netanyahu confidant Natan Eshel privately sought Arab lawmakers’ help against Eisenkot; Likud called Tibi’s allegation “a complete lie” [9].
  • Election claims: Israeli election authorities said there was no evidence to support claims by Netanyahu’s allies that Arab-led parties were involved in electoral fraud [9].

Timeline

  • 10 November 2026: The new Knesset held its swearing-in ceremony [7]
  • 12 November 2026: Netanyahu and Eisenkot held a security briefing that Netanyahu later said reinforced his call for unity [7]
  • 13 November 2026: Ahmad Tibi displayed messages he said showed Netanyahu associate Natan Eshel seeking Arab lawmakers’ help to block Eisenkot [9]

Q&A

Q: How could Eisenkot’s proposed minority government pass without a majority?

A: Under the plan described by Netanyahu, Eisenkot’s 48 lawmakers would vote for the government and Netanyahu’s 46 would oppose it, while the remaining 26 Arab and ultra-Orthodox lawmakers could abstain, allowing approval without an absolute majority [6].
