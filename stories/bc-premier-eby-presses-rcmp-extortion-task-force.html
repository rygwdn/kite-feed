B.C. premier Eby presses RCMP extortion task force

Category: Canada

B.C. Premier David Eby publicly warned the head of the province’s anti-extortion task force to show greater urgency tackling a wave of blackmail-related shootings in the Lower Mainland, or step aside [1][3]. RCMP Assistant Commissioner John Brewer later apologized, saying his remarks disputing the use of the word “crisis” had affected public confidence, while stressing the RCMP’s commitment to the work [1][3]. Local leaders in Surrey say residents and business owners feel unsafe amid a recent rise in extortion reports [2].

Key Points

  • Surrey complaints: Surrey Mayor Brenda Locke said the city logged 34 reported extortion cases in the last three weeks and described residents as living in “constant fear” [2].
  • Crisis wording: Brewer said he did not intend to call the RCMP’s commitment into question when he challenged describing the shootings as a “crisis,” before issuing an apology [3].
  • Leadership message: Eby’s warning framed the issue as an accountability test for the task force’s top leadership, saying urgency must be demonstrated or the leader should step aside [1][3].
  • Regional safety: The reports describe blackmail-linked shootings across the Lower Mainland that have intensified public concern and political scrutiny [3][1].
