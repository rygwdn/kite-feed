OpenAI seeks real work samples to test agents

Category: AI

OpenAI is asking some contractors to upload projects from their past jobs so the company can evaluate how well AI “agents” handle realistic office-style tasks, according to WIRED [1]. WIRED reports contractors are instructed to remove confidential and personally identifiable information themselves before uploading, raising questions about how reliably sensitive data can be screened out in practice [1]. Separately, OpenAI is also developing a career-focused product described as “ChatGPT Jobs,” aimed at helping with job search tasks like resume tips and career guidance, suggesting the company is pushing AI tools deeper into everyday work life [3].

Did you know: Personally identifiable information (PII) commonly includes items like full names, email addresses, phone numbers, and government ID numbers, and many privacy regimes treat its handling as a regulated compliance issue [common].

Key Points

  • Contractor workflow: WIRED describes the program as relying on contractors to decide what to upload and to strip out sensitive details before submission, placing much of the privacy and confidentiality burden on individuals rather than on a fully automated intake system [1].
  • Agent evaluation goal: The stated purpose of collecting past-work artifacts is to benchmark AI agents against practical workplace tasks—an approach that favors “messy” real-world examples over synthetic test prompts [1].
  • Career assistant push: TestingCatalog says OpenAI is developing “ChatGPT Jobs,” framing it as an AI agent meant to support career changes through features like job search help, resume tips, and guidance on next steps [3].
  • Broader product signal: Taken together, the contractor data request and the “ChatGPT Jobs” effort point to a strategy of building AI agents that can operate across end-to-end work workflows, from producing documents to navigating career transitions [1][3].

Industry Impact

  • Data labeling and contracting: If contractors are asked to supply realistic work artifacts and self-redact sensitive details, it could expand the scope and responsibility of contract work beyond labeling into data stewardship and compliance-adjacent tasks [1].
  • Enterprise AI procurement: Testing agents on real document types may appeal to businesses looking for proof that AI can handle their specific workflows, but the reliance on human redaction highlights why buyers often demand strong data-handling controls and auditability [1][common].
  • Career tech tools: A dedicated “ChatGPT Jobs” agent could increase competition for job-search platforms and resume tools by bundling guidance and search-oriented assistance into a general AI product ecosystem [3].

Historical Background

AI labs have increasingly emphasized “agentic” systems that can plan and execute multi-step tasks (like drafting, scheduling, and tool use) rather than only answering single prompts [common]. As these systems move toward workplace use, evaluations have tended to shift from academic benchmarks to more realistic task suites and human-in-the-loop testing [common]. The WIRED report fits that pattern by describing OpenAI’s attempt to test agents against artifacts drawn from real jobs rather than purely synthetic examples [1].
