Ugandan opposition leader Bobi Wine seized as results roll

Category: World

Ugandan opposition leader Bobi Wine was taken from his home in Kampala in what his party said involved an army helicopter, after a tense national election held amid a nationwide internet blackout [1][3][4]. As provisional results were released, Uganda’s Electoral Commission figures reported President Yoweri Museveni holding a large lead with more than half of polling stations reporting, while Wine and his party alleged irregularities and restrictions on their activities [8][5][11]. Police spokespeople were quoted as saying they were not aware of Wine being under house arrest, a claim repeatedly made by his party as security surrounded his residence [10][11][7]. The competing accounts, alongside reports of election-day violence, have raised concerns about political freedoms and the integrity of the process even as many Ugandans hoped the vote would offer a peaceful way to shape their country’s future [4][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Bobi Wine, whose real name is Robert Kyagulanyi, is a former pop star who became a leading opposition politician [10].

Key Points

  • Vote snapshot: With close to 60% counted, the Electoral Commission tally cited by SCMP put Museveni at 75.4% and Wine at 20.7% [5].
  • Observers present: Uganda accredited more than 1,600 international and regional observers, including delegations from the African Union, East African Community, EU, US, UK, China and others [9][10].
  • Observer praise: A Russian observer delegation said it saw orderly queues, identification checks, and ballot designs intended to help non-literate voters, and reported no serious violations affecting results [9].
  • Violence claims: A senior opposition figure told AFP that security forces shot dead 10 campaign team members inside his home on polling day, an allegation also echoed in other reports of election-related violence [7][4][5].
  • Rigging allegations: Wine alleged “massive ballot stuffing” and said opposition polling agents and supervisors were abducted, while warning he could call protests if the vote was “rigged” [10].

Technical Details

  • Nationwide internet blackout: Multiple outlets reported an internet shutdown around election day, limiting communication and online reporting as results and allegations circulated [1][7][10].
  • International election observation: Observer missions involve accredited delegations visiting polling stations and compiling assessments of procedures; Uganda accredited more than 1,600 such observers from regional and global bodies [9][10].
  • Ballot accessibility design: Russian observers reported ballots included photographs to help non-literate voters identify choices, an election-administration measure aimed at accessibility [9].

Historical Background

Yoweri Museveni has led Uganda since taking power in 1986, and this election could extend his rule further [7][10]. In the 2021 presidential election, Museveni won 59% to Wine’s 35%, underscoring how central this rivalry has become in Ugandan politics [10]. Over successive election cycles, rights groups and opposition figures have accused the state of repression, a backdrop referenced again during this campaign period [7].
