Vancouver elects four Greens and four COPE councillors as incumbents fall

Category: Canada

Vancouver’s new city council will include four Green Party of Vancouver councillors, four COPE councillors, one OneCity member and one ABC Vancouver member after Green councillor Pete Fry defeated Mayor Ken Sim in British Columbia’s Oct. 17 municipal election. The two left-leaning parties will hold most council seats alongside Fry, Canada’s first Green mayor [17][1]. Fry has said his priorities include affordable housing, financial transparency and community safety [1][21]. Incumbent mayors also lost in Surrey, Kamloops, Prince George, Delta and other cities across the province. Former Surrey mayor Doug McCallum won back the office, while some incumbents, including Burnaby Mayor Mike Hurley, won re-election [1][6]. The election also brought complete turnover on the Greater Victoria School Board: the only incumbent who ran again, Angela Carmichael, lost her seat. The incoming trustees take office after a dispute that led the province to dismiss the board in 2025 and later reinstate it following a court challenge [22].

Key Points

  • Kamloops result: Mike O’Reilly, a former councillor who opposed Mayor Reid Hamer-Jackson, won 44% of the vote. Hamer-Jackson finished fourth with 12%, and only three of seven incumbent councillors seeking re-election won [14].
  • Langley spending: In Langley Township, Jay Lundgren and his Langley Strong party defeated Mayor Eric Woodward after four years of infrastructure development financed by hundreds of millions of dollars in borrowing [14].
  • Richmond council: Mayor-elect Rob Howard’s Richmond Forward slate won no council seats, leaving him to seek support from councillors elected under four other party banners [6].
  • Surrey split: Doug McCallum won Surrey’s mayoralty with about 33% of the vote in a seven-candidate field, while his Safe Surrey Coalition was on track to take seven of eight council seats [14].
  • Trustee priorities: Greater Victoria trustees were elected by electoral area for the first time. Several winners had the Greater Victoria Teachers’ Association’s endorsement, and one trustee named music education as his top priority [22].

Perspectives

Pete Fry, Vancouver mayor-elect: Fry said he would set aside partisan politics and lead a council that works for all Vancouver residents [5].

Q&A

Q: What challenges will Greater Victoria’s new school board encounter immediately?

A: Several senior district staff members were on medical leave shortly before the 2026-27 school year began, and trustee Ann Whiteaker said the new board would need time to understand the situation [22].

Q: Did incumbent mayors lose everywhere in the province?

A: No. Nanaimo Mayor Leonard Krog won with just over 10,000 votes, ahead of Sarah Lovegrove’s 8,400, while Kelowna Mayor Tom Dyas edged Ron Cannan, 19,451 to 18,482 [1].
