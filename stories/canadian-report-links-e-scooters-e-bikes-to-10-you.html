Canadian report links e-scooters, e-bikes to 10 youth deaths

Category: Canada

A Canadian Paediatric Surveillance Program report found that 10 young people died in 2025 from injuries involving powered personal micromobility devices, including e-scooters and e-bikes [4][6][2]. The report also documented 26 hospitalizations lasting more than two days and 12 ICU admissions among serious pediatric cases reported by doctors [6][5]. Pediatric emergency physician Dr. Daniel Rosenfield of SickKids hospital in Toronto told CBC News that e-scooter trauma has increased sharply, with SickKids recording 26 injury cases in 2023, 46 in 2024 and 107 in 2025 [1][5]. He said the devices can be marketed like toys despite rapid acceleration and high speeds, while doctors and safety advocates say current rules have not kept pace with use by children and teens [1][7][5].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Require a helmet and protective gear for child riders.
  • Safety: E-scooter laws vary significantly by province [5][1]. Check local age, road-use and device-speed rules before allowing use.

Action Items

  • Check device speed settings: Before a child rides, confirm whether the scooter or e-bike has a speed limiter or lower-speed mode, since some devices can reach 40 km/h within seconds [5].
  • Report serious injuries consistently: Ask treating clinicians whether surveillance reporting will capture a severe e-scooter or e-bike injury, as pediatricians are calling for better tracking [6].
