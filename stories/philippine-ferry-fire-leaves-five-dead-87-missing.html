Philippine ferry fire leaves five dead, 87 missing

Category: World

A fire aboard the passenger ferry M/V June Aster off Coron in Palawan province killed at least five people and left 87 unaccounted for after the vessel caught fire Wednesday evening while sailing from Manila to Coron, the Philippine Coast Guard said [3][10][13][12][45][52]. The manifest listed 134 people on board, including 117 passengers and 17 crew members, while officials reported 42 or 43 rescues as search figures continued to shift [2][7][9][15][36][35]. The Coast Guard deployed multiple vessels and other assets, used drift-modelling tools to track where currents may have carried people, and asked nearby ships and coastal villages to watch for survivors [2][9][12][25]. Rescuers faced thick smoke, extreme heat, strong currents and explosion risks that delayed entry into the charred ferry, while the cause remained under investigation [2][7][43][44].

Key Points

  • Fire timing: The blaze erupted around 6:45 p.m. local time Wednesday and was still burning as of 11:00 a.m. Thursday, the Coast Guard told the BBC [7][27][28].
  • Vessel drift: The ferry sent a distress signal about 4 nautical miles off Turda village before strong currents carried it to about 7.68 nautical miles offshore [35][43].
  • Foreign nationals: Coast Guard information officer Joy Gumatay said authorities had no information that foreigners were on board, while Japan's embassy said it had no information as of 9:30 a.m. Thursday that Japanese nationals were involved [35][54].

Historical Background

Ferries are a common means of transport across the Philippine archipelago, where maritime accidents have been linked to storms, poor vessel maintenance, overcrowding and uneven enforcement of safety rules in remote provinces [43][44]. In 1987, the ferry Doña Paz sank after colliding with a fuel tanker in the central Philippines, killing more than 4,300 people in what the Associated Press described as the world's deadliest peacetime maritime disaster [43].

Humanitarian Impact

Seven survivors were taken to a hospital in Culion, while authorities had not yet clarified how many rescued passengers suffered injuries [7].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: A search-and-rescue operation was underway after the M/V June Aster fire off Palawan [2][12]. Travelers with ferry bookings to Coron should confirm sailing status directly with operators and local port authorities before departure.
  • Local conditions: Authorities reported strong currents, smoke and difficult weather conditions during rescue operations [7][27][44]. Avoid approaching coastal rescue areas and follow instructions from the Philippine Coast Guard or local officials.

Action Items

  • Seek information about missing passengers: Contact the Philippine Coast Guard or local authorities in Coron, which coordinated with coastal villages during the search [9].
