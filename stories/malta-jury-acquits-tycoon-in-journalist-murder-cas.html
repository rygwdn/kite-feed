Malta jury acquits tycoon in journalist murder case

Category: World

A Maltese jury acquitted businessman Yorgen Fenech on Sept. 2 of charges that he masterminded the 2017 car-bomb killing of investigative journalist Daphne Caruana Galizia, finding him not guilty of complicity in murder and criminal association [3][4][7][10]. Jurors in Valletta reached an 8-1 verdict after about eight hours of deliberations; Fenech, 44, had pleaded not guilty and faced up to life imprisonment if convicted [4][10][11][33]. Caruana Galizia, 53, was killed on Oct. 16, 2017, when a bomb detonated in her car as she drove near her home, after years of reporting on suspected corruption, money laundering and organized crime in Malta [4][7][9][11]. Prosecutors accused Fenech of ordering and paying for the killing; he denied the allegations and was the last of six people charged in the plot to face prosecution [7][9][10]. The verdict drew immediate anger from Caruana Galizia’s family and press freedom advocates, while Fenech’s relatives cheered in court and some people jeered him outside the courthouse [3][4][11][16]. Her family said “the institutional failures that enabled her murder remain unaddressed and unreformed,” and local reports said the opposition Nationalist Party organized a demonstration late Wednesday demanding justice [4][11][10].

Did you know: Caruana Galizia was facing about 40 libel suits at the time she was killed [11][24].

Key Points

  • Earlier convictions: Five other men have been convicted in the case. Vincent Muscat pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 15 years in 2021 for his role; brothers George and Alfred Degiorgio received 40-year sentences in 2022; and Robert Agius and Jamie Vella were given life sentences in 2025 for supplying the bomb [4][49][50][53].
  • Political fallout: The killing triggered mass protests and a political crisis that led then-Prime Minister Joseph Muscat to resign in January 2020 [6][16][23][50].
  • Reporting targets: Caruana Galizia’s work included the Panama Papers and allegations involving offshore companies linked to people in Muscat’s inner circle [4][7][10].
  • Trial record: Fenech’s trial ran for 55 days, and he took the relatively unusual step of testifying in his own defense for four days [64][67].

Perspectives

Caruana Galizia family: The family said Fenech’s acquittal was “denying her the justice she deserves” and argued that accountability remains overdue for people who enabled, facilitated or protected her killers. They said that had she not been murdered, she would have exposed the links between Fenech, then-Energy Minister Konrad Mizzi and the prime minister’s chief of staff over payments through Fenech’s company 17 Black. They also noted that Fenech remains charged with bribery and money-laundering offences, and that other prosecutions may follow.

Timeline

  • 16 October 2017: Caruana Galizia was killed by a car bomb near her home [4][7].
  • November 2019: Police arrested Fenech aboard his yacht as he was leaving Malta [49][50].
  • 2021: Fenech was indicted on complicity and criminal conspiracy charges [7][9].
  • July 2021: A public inquiry found the state bore responsibility for failures tied to impunity [23][24].
  • 2022: The Degiorgio brothers received 40-year prison sentences [4][50].
  • June 2025: Two men were convicted of supplying the car bomb [49][50].
  • 2 September 2026: Jurors cleared Fenech on both charges by an 8-1 vote [3][64].

International Reactions

  • 🇪🇺 European Centre for Press and Media Freedom: The press freedom group said the verdict sent “a disturbing message to those who attack and kill journalists” [16][23].
  • 🇪🇺 Pieter Omtzigt: The former Council of Europe special investigator called the acquittal “extremely painful” and said there was still no real justice nearly a decade after the murder [63].
