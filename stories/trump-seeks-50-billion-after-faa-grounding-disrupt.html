Trump seeks $50 billion after FAA grounding disrupts US flights

Category: World

President Donald Trump urged Congress on Sunday to approve $50 billion for air traffic control upgrades, two days after the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), the US air safety agency, halted nearly all departing flights nationwide for almost five hours. The ground stop on Friday, Nov. 13, triggered cancellations and delays that continued through Sunday, when more than 11,500 US flights were affected. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said full operations were expected to resume Monday [3][6][33]. Officials said the disruption began during work to replace copper communications cable with fiber-optic lines. A router problem flooded the FAA network with data and interfered with communications and radar feeds, but investigators had not identified the root cause by Sunday. Duffy said there was no evidence of a cyberattack or other malicious intent [6][18][33]. Trump blamed former President Joe Biden’s administration for the system’s condition and said upgrades should be completed all at once. Duffy raised his funding request from $30 billion to $50 billion, while Democratic lawmakers questioned why more money was needed before the $12.5 billion approved in 2025 had been spent. The FAA said it disabled the affected routers and about 60 similar units as the investigation continued [2][18][21].

Key Points

  • Funding dispute: Democratic Representatives Rick Larsen and André Carson said about $11 billion of the $12.5 billion approved in 2025 remained unspent; Duffy said that allocation did not cover new airspace-management software [18][33].
  • Airline effects: On Sunday, JetBlue canceled about 28% of its flights and American Airlines about 20%, while United, Southwest and Delta reported cancellation rates of 3% to 5% [33][32].
  • Outdated equipment: Duffy said the routers implicated in the failure were bought in 2001 and were obsolete by 2006; the FAA also faces a shortage of several thousand air traffic controllers [21][18].
  • Political shift: Democrats are set to take control of Congress in January 2027 after gains in the Nov. 3 midterm elections [33][18].
  • Infrastructure funding: Airports Council International Director General Justin Erbacci argued that modernizing air transport requires private investment as well as government funding, with passenger traffic projected to more than double by 2050 [21][33].

Timeline

  • 13 November 2026: FAA halted nearly all departing flights nationwide for almost five hours [33]
  • 14 November 2026: Airlines canceled more than 6,400 US flights and delayed over 8,000 [33]
  • 15 November 2026: More than 11,500 flights were affected as Duffy projected recovery Monday [33]
  • 16 November 2026: Officials expected normal air service to resume after the weekend disruption [6][25]

Technical Details

  • Router data flood: The FAA said its network received about 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, flooding it with data and destabilizing communications and radar feeds [6].
  • Cross-site cascade: Duffy said router work at an FAA facility in El Dorado, Arkansas, triggered problems with a recently replaced router in Amarillo, Texas, which then overloaded routers at other air traffic control centers [21][33].

Historical Background

Before the Nov. 13 grounding, the last nationwide FAA ground stops were issued after the Sept. 11, 2001, attacks and during a computer outage in January 2023 [4][11].

Humanitarian Impact

Passengers were stranded at airports across the country, with some waiting overnight and trying to rebook as airlines repositioned aircraft and crews [19][24].

Action Items

  • Verify flight status before traveling: Check directly with your airline for cancellations, delays and rebooking options while schedules recover [6].
