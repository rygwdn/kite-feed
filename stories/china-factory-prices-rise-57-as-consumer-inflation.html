China factory prices rise 5.7% as consumer inflation cools

Category: Business

China’s producer-price index rose 5.7% year over year in October, its fastest increase since June 2022, while consumer inflation slowed to 0.7%, official data released Monday showed [1][2][4][12]. The split reflects higher energy and raw-material costs, which economists linked to the conflict around Iran, alongside weak household demand that is limiting companies’ ability to pass those costs on to customers [2][12]. Producer-price inflation accelerated from 5.0% in September, while consumer inflation eased from 0.9% and fell short of the 0.8% forecast. Core consumer inflation, which excludes food and energy, slipped to 0.8% [3][4]. Nguyen Hoang Nam, China economist at Capital Economics, said the external price shock should fade next year. He forecast that producer prices would return to deflation and consumer inflation would average 0.4% in 2027 [5][12].

Primary Source: View original article

We think the price shock from the Middle East conflict will keep inflation elevated over the coming months. But its impact should fade next year.

— Nguyen Hoang Nam, China economist at Capital Economics (Reuters)

Key Points

  • Energy costs: October prices rose 35% for oil, coal and other fuel processing and nearly 30% for chemical raw materials, according to China’s National Bureau of Statistics [2].
  • Input pressures: Prices paid by producers jumped 7.9% year over year, their fastest rise since June 2022, widening the gap with output prices [5][11].
  • Uneven sectors: Only half of the 30 key industrial sectors tracked by China’s statistics bureau recorded price gains, despite the overall producer-price surge [5].
  • Food prices: Fresh vegetable prices fell 16.9% and pork prices dropped 8.6% year over year, while gasoline prices rose 22.4%, pulling consumer inflation in opposite directions [8][11].

Perspectives

Pinpoint Asset Management: Chief economist Zhang Zhiwei said low consumer inflation reflects weak domestic demand and that the strength of exports may explain why Beijing has not moved quickly to introduce new stimulus [12].

Sources: Business Recorder

China’s state planner: An official said external shocks were having a greater effect on economic activity and that the imbalance between robust supply and weak domestic demand remained pronounced [12].

Sources: Business Recorder

Industry Impact

  • Electronics manufacturing: Prices for computer, communications and other electronic equipment rose 5.9% in October. Energy-related, chemical and electronics industries led producer-price gains [2].

Historical Background

China experienced more than three years of deflationary pressure before the outbreak of war in Iran pushed producer and consumer price indexes back into positive growth, according to the Financial Times [2]. A prolonged property downturn has depressed household sentiment, making it harder to revive domestic demand [2].

Business Angle

  • A strong yuan has squeezed exporters’ margins even as shipments remained strong [12].
  • The global buildout of data centres is adding to demand for AI-related products and some raw materials [5].
