Carney and party leaders trade jokes at Ottawa press dinner

Category: Canada

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney shared the stage with comedians impersonating him and U.S. President Donald Trump at the annual Parliamentary Press Gallery dinner in Ottawa on Saturday. Journalists, politicians and lobbyists attended the traditional night of political satire. Carney joked about Trump’s Oval Office makeover and Canadian politics [2][8][1]. Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre, Bloc Québécois Leader Yves-François Blanchet, New Democratic Party Leader Avi Lewis and Green Party Leader Mike Morrice also spoke. Carney left after his remarks, then watched Poilievre’s speech from the back of the room before departing; Poilievre stayed for the full dinner [2][3].

Primary Source: View original article

I am pressing the emergency comedy button.

— Prime Minister Mark Carney (CBC News)

Key Points

  • Telecom joke: Carney used a joke about oligopolies to refer to his remarks the previous week about Quebecor’s proposal to acquire telecom company Telus [2][4].
  • Media funding: Poilievre ended his remarks by pretending to distribute money to journalists, mocking Liberal government funding for journalism [2][1].
  • Green leader: Newly elected Green Party Leader Mike Morrice joked about his own lack of a seat in Parliament and Poilievre’s loss of his Carleton riding in the 2025 federal election [2][3].
  • Governor General: Governor General Louise Arbour joked that Carney was often away from Ottawa and made pointed remarks about homelessness, the residence at 24 Sussex and environmental work in Alberta [2][4].

Perspectives

Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre: Poilievre portrayed Carney as controlling, citing reports that Carney yells at and micromanages staff and treats journalists poorly. He then joked that Carney was stealing his ideas [2].

Bloc Québécois Leader Yves-François Blanchet: Blanchet broke from the roast format to say independent journalism is essential to democracy [2][3].

New Democratic Party Leader Avi Lewis: Lewis joked about Carney’s early departure, saying he stayed for the full evening despite not having a seat in the House of Commons [2].
