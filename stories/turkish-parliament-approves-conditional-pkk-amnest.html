Turkish parliament approves conditional PKK amnesty law

Category: World

Turkey's parliament approved legislation on Monday offering a conditional pardon and legal protections to thousands of members of the Kurdistan Workers' Party, or PKK, as part of Ankara's effort to advance a peace process with the group [3][1][5][9]. Lawmakers voted 468-88, with six abstentions, after about 12 hours of debate; President Recep Tayyip Erdogan's AK Party, its nationalist MHP allies and the pro-Kurdish DEM party backed the 12-article bill [2][1][11][34]. The law would suspend some prosecutions, trials and prison sentences for five or 10 years, depending on the offense, and cases would be dropped or sentences considered served if eligible people commit no terrorism-related crime during that period [15][1][36][53]. The measures take effect only after Turkey's National Security Council confirms that the PKK and affiliated groups have disbanded and surrendered their weapons, with people convicted of intentional killings or other serious crimes excluded [1][15][44][18]. Lawmakers said around 3,500 PKK-linked detainees could be released in an initial phase, while the legislation does not settle the status of PKK founder Abdullah Ocalan, 77, who has been held on Imrali prison island near Istanbul since 1999 [2][1][11][16].

Key Points

  • Application window: Eligible people would have six months to apply after the National Security Council's decision appears in Turkey's Official Gazette [15][36].
  • Monitoring bodies: The proposal sets up ministerial and parliamentary committees to monitor the disarmament process, while FAZ reported that Turkey's MIT intelligence service would help oversee disarmament and surrender procedures [15][36][30].
  • Regional effects: The Associated Press said the measure could support closer Turkish-Iraqi ties and aid Syrian government efforts to integrate areas controlled by the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces, which are affiliated with the PKK [3][5][12][47].
  • Political stakes: AP reported that the process could help Erdogan court DEM support because he could run again if parliament calls an early election during his current term [3][5][12][50].

Perspectives

Turkish presidency: The presidency welcomed the vote as an important step toward implementing a 'terror-free Turkey' and said it would strengthen peace, unity and territorial integrity.

Industry Impact

  • Prison system: Lawmakers said about 3,500 PKK-linked detainees could be released at first, roughly one-third of the PKK-linked people held in Turkish prisons [2][1][11][45].

Historical Background

The PKK launched its armed insurgency against the Turkish state in 1984 and is designated as a terrorist organization by Turkey, the United States and the European Union [9][22][54]. Ocalan founded the PKK in 1978, was imprisoned in 1999, and issued a February 2025 call for the group to disarm and dissolve [29][2][22]. The PKK later announced in May 2025 that it would end its armed struggle and dissolve, and a group of fighters symbolically burned weapons in northern Iraq two months later [22][34].

Humanitarian Impact

The conflict has killed more than 40,000 people and placed a heavy economic burden on mainly Kurdish southeast Turkey, according to Reuters-based reporting carried by Al Jazeera and Czech Television [9][22]. Some sources cited estimates of at least 50,000 deaths, reflecting varying tallies for the decades-long conflict [16][28][39].
