FIFA scraps $4.2 billion World Cup investment plan

Category: World

FIFA President Gianni Infantino abandoned the proposed FIFA Forward Enterprise project after opposition from football confederations and officials inside the governing body, saying the plan had “created divisions” and “will not proceed” [1][3][5][8][15][38]. The proposal would have created a commercial subsidiary to run FIFA events, including the World Cup and Club World Cup, and raise up to $4.2 billion by selling about a 20% stake at a $20 billion valuation to outside investors [5][15][12][17][32]. FIFA said the project aimed to increase funding for its 211 member associations and support football development, especially in countries needing more assistance [1][7][26]. UEFA opposed the plan and threatened to boycott FIFA competitions if it went ahead, while Concacaf and the Asian Football Confederation also rejected or condemned it; Conmebol asked FIFA for more details without taking a final position [8][5][15][2]. Infantino said he intends to bring interested parties together in the coming days and weeks to discuss growing football globally [7][26][32].

Having listened carefully to all the views, it has become clear that the project has created divisions of a nature that, regardless of the level of support, are no longer in the interest of the objective set out in the first place.

Key Points

  • Member payments: Infantino had sought approval by September 19 and reportedly offered national associations $40 million if they supported the investor deal [8][12][28][32].
  • Internal dissent: FIFA chief operating officer Kevin Lamour said the administration had been “deceived,” and senior adviser Carlos Cordeiro resigned after calling the proposal “a bad deal for football” [8][42][54].
  • Investor link: FIFA had said Thrive Eternal, linked to Thrive Capital founder Joshua Kushner, was expected to lead the proposed investor group [11][12][24].

Perspectives

FIFA: Infantino said FIFA intended the project to strengthen member associations and would pursue such a plan only with member support and consultation with the FIFA Council, confederations and wider stakeholders.

Technical Details

  • Minority equity structure: The plan called for selling about 20% of the new FIFA Forward Enterprise unit while FIFA retained majority ownership, with investors receiving minority, non-controlling stakes [5][1][11].
  • Commercial rights bundle: FIFA Forward Enterprise would have grouped commercial rights, including broadcast, sponsorship, ticketing and licensing, in a separate vehicle tied to FIFA competitions [7][12].

Industry Impact

  • National associations: FIFA’s 211 member associations would have been offered large direct funding benefits, including a reported $40 million for supporting the plan and, according to Dawn, a $20 million one-off payment plus an increase in 2027-2030 allocations from $8 million to $20 million [8][15].
  • Private investors: The withdrawal ended plans for a proposed investor group expected to be led by Thrive Eternal, a fund linked to Thrive Capital founder Joshua Kushner [11][12].

Historical Background

Infantino has led FIFA since February 2016 [8]. On July 19, 2026, he joined President Trump at the World Cup final trophy presentation [4][9].

Business Angle

  • The proposal valued FIFA Forward Enterprise at $20 billion and would have sold about 20% of a commercial rights business with continuing revenue streams [5][common].
  • UEFA, Concacaf and the AFC opposed the deal, and three confederations together had enough votes to stop the plan [47][48].
  • The reported $40 million member-association incentive offered an upfront payment, but Cordeiro and other critics inside FIFA questioned whether the transaction served football’s long-term interests [8][54].
