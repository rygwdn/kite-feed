X adds first parental controls for teens after safety scrutiny

Category: Technology

X introduced its first parental controls on Nov. 26 for users ages 13 to 17, giving parents and guardians tools to manage teens’ feeds, contacts and time spent on the social network [1][2][8]. Teen accounts default to protected mode, which restricts sensitive media and blocks targeted advertising. Adults can set daily usage limits, approve new followers, filter words, limit recommendations to accounts the teen follows and control who can send direct messages [4][7]. Supervision requires consent from both the adult and the teen. Parents can see weekly usage and which accounts their teen has messaged one-on-one, but cannot read the messages, view search or viewing histories, log in to the teen’s account or post on their behalf. Teenagers can see every change made [11][8]. One adult can supervise up to five teen accounts, while each teen account can be linked to only one adult. Supervision ends automatically when the teen turns 18 [2][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Parents want practical tools, and teens deserve room to grow. With Parental Controls on X, families can set the guardrails that fit their home, while teens keep ownership of their accounts and can see every change. Striking that balance is vital and what we set out to build.

— Michael O’Herlihy, head of safety at X (TechCrunch)

Key Points

  • Account linking: The parent starts setup in X’s settings and the teen completes it by scanning a QR code while logged into their account [5][11].
  • Age verification: X may verify a user’s age through a selfie check or identity document in some cases, but the platform has not specified when it will require either method [21].
  • Safety testing: The Center for Countering Digital Hate said two UK accounts posing as 13-year-olds could find sexual acts and pornographic images and videos on X [6].
  • Legal scrutiny: Oregon and Virginia attorneys general sued X and other Musk companies, alleging Grok helped spread non-consensual sexual images and child sexual abuse material [6][11].
  • Default account limits: Teen accounts are set so only approved followers can view posts and only accounts the teen follows can send direct messages [7].

Perspectives

X: The company says its platform is not designed or promoted for children, but says it takes extra care with young people who use it [12][1].

Sources: Gizmodo

Ofcom: The UK regulator’s investigation found that some age-gated pornography remained accessible to accounts not verified as belonging to adults. X subsequently changed its restrictions [6].

Timeline

  • 2024: A study found nearly one-third of Gen Alpha children had X accounts [1]
  • September 2026: Ireland’s media watchdog opened an inquiry into X’s age checks [6]
  • 25 November 2026: X’s Safety account announced the parental-control tools [19]

Industry Impact

  • Social media safety features: X’s controls include feed recommendation limits and oversight of teen messaging contacts, features that Digital Trends says Instagram and TikTok had offered for some time [6].

Historical Background

X had operated for 20 years without parental controls for teen accounts [1].

International Reactions

  • 🇦🇺 Australia: Australia banned users under 16 from X in December 2025, according to TechJuice [7].
  • 🇬🇧 United Kingdom: The UK announced plans to include X in a proposed social-media ban for minors [7].

Q&A

Q: Can a teenager end supervision after agreeing to it?

A: Teens may request to end supervision, but the parent or guardian can approve or deny the request; supervision ends automatically at 18 [11][9].

Q: Does requiring approval for new followers change a teen account’s visibility?

A: Yes. Requiring approval for new followers also makes the teen’s account private [6].
