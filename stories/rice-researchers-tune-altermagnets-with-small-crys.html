Rice researchers tune altermagnets with small crystal strain

Category: Science

Rice University researchers reported that mechanical strain can alter key electronic and magnetic behavior in altermagnetic materials, a newly recognized class of magnets being studied for spintronics. In iron sulfide, Phys.org said gently squeezing a crystal changed both its weak magnetic signal and how electricity moved through it [2]. In manganese telluride, another Phys.org report said strain flipped the material's Hall signal, pointing to a possible way to control an altermagnet without relying on large overall magnetization [3][1]. The manganese telluride work focused on time-reversal symmetry, in which a system's internal physics differs when viewed forward or backward in time [3]. Phys.org said physicists have looked for that behavior in systems with almost no net magnetization because spintronics aims to carry information using the quantum spins of electrons rather than only electric charge [3].
