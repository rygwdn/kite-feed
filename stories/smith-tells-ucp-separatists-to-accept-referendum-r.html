Smith tells UCP separatists to accept referendum result or leave the party

Category: Canada

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith on Wednesday told separatists to stop publicly advocating for independence or leave her United Conservative Party (UCP), after voters rejected moving toward a binding separation referendum by roughly three to one. The nonbinding Oct. 19 referendum also asked about immigration and constitutional changes. Preliminary results showed majorities for eight of the other nine proposals [35][25][34]. Smith said people may hold their own views, but UCP candidates, provincial board members and constituency association representatives must support “a strong Alberta within a united Canada.” She warned that a year of internal division could cost the party the provincial election, scheduled for October 2027 [16][35]. Separatist organizers say they will seek influence inside the party rather than form a new one. They are organizing for the UCP’s annual meeting, which opens Nov. 27, where delegates elect board members and set party policy. Smith has not detailed how she will enforce the new rules [18][35].

The United Conservative Party is not an independence party.

— Danielle Smith, Alberta premier (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Meeting campaign: Separatist organizer Mitch Sylvestre says he plans to bring rural supporters to the three-day UCP meeting and seek control of its board. Nearly 7,000 people had registered, and Smith expected attendance to exceed 7,500 [18].
  • Party backing: Cypress-Medicine Hat MLA and cabinet minister Justin Wright said local boards and many UCP legislators support Smith’s position, while former local party official Alan Rose urged the party to move beyond the issue [25].
  • Rural divide: Preliminary results showed about 86% of voters in Edmonton and 83% in Calgary chose to remain in Canada, compared with about 66% elsewhere in Alberta [28].
  • Treaty concerns: Tsuut’ina Chief Ellery Starlight said the campaign raised questions about governments making decisions affecting treaty relationships without consulting First Nations [33].
  • Leadership review: Two UCP constituency associations supported triggering a review of Smith’s leadership. At least 22 associations are needed to bring a motion to the party’s annual meeting [25][18].

Perspectives

Danielle Smith, Alberta premier: Smith said disputes with Ottawa should be pursued through political and legal institutions, not separation, and that she would not hold another separation referendum while premier [3].

Tanya Clemens, UCP member: Clemens said a leadership review would be a democratic way to hold Smith accountable for her decisions during the referendum campaign [18].

Keith Wilson, co-chair of Let Alberta Decide: Wilson said Smith’s message was being understood by separatist members as a signal they are unwelcome in the UCP, and warned it could widen the party split [35].

Naheed Nenshi, Alberta NDP leader: Nenshi said Smith must find a way to regain the trust of Albertans distressed by the referendum, with a provincial election due in 2027 [4].

Q&A

Q: What does Alberta plan to do with the other referendum results?

A: Smith said the government intends to pursue immigration reforms voters endorsed, while constitutional changes would require talks with Ottawa and other provinces [34].

Q: Who can Smith bar from serving in party roles?

A: Smith has authority over candidate selections, party-board memberships and cabinet appointments, giving her levers to enforce the directive [17].
