China tariff deal offers Canadian pork exporters relief after losses

Category: Canada

China will suspend its additional 25% tariff on most frozen Canadian pork cuts from Jan. 1, 2027, under an expanded trade arrangement Prime Minister Mark Carney and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed to in Shenzhen. The deal also extends tariff relief on other Canadian exports, including seafood, while Canada extends exemptions for some Chinese steel and aluminum products [41][8][2]. The pork measures cover about C$116 million in exports and run through Dec. 31, 2028, but do not apply to every pork product. Canadian exporters have lost customers and market share since the tariffs took effect; the Canadian Meat Council says Canadian pork exports to China fell from about C$469 million in 2024 to C$354 million in 2025 [41][8]. Agriculture Minister Heath MacDonald praised the new market access, while Conservative and Bloc Québécois members criticized the partnership with China over dependence, human rights and security concerns. China’s commerce ministry said the two governments would complete domestic procedures and work to put the measures into effect [12][45].

Key Points

  • Export losses: The Canadian Meat Council estimates exporters have lost C$126 million to date [41].
  • Pork exclusions: China’s relief covers most frozen cuts, but offal and other Canadian pork products remain outside the arrangement [8][41].
  • Seafood access: The expanded list includes Pacific spot prawns, geoduck, sea cucumber and Greenland halibut; about 16% of Canada’s fish and seafood exports went to China in 2024 [8].
  • Canola tariffs: China’s canola-seed tariff remains 14.9%, while canola oil is still subject to a 100% tariff [8].
  • Business links: Canada plans a trade mission to China in 2027 and has designated it a bilateral e-commerce year to help small and midsize firms reach Chinese consumers [43].

Perspectives

Conservative Party of Canada and Bloc Québécois: Conservative MPs argued the government was trading U.S. dependence for dependence on China; Bloc Québécois Leader Yves-François Blanchet said the agreement offered too little on human rights and raised concerns about Chinese aluminum entering Canada [12].

Industry Impact

  • Pork processing: Chinese consumers value a range of pork cuts, including offal, which remains outside the tariff relief [8].
  • Steel and aluminum supply: Canada expanded tariff-remission coverage to 32 Chinese steel and aluminum items—23 steel and nine aluminum—for which domestic supply is insufficient or unavailable [41].
  • Crop producers: The agreement also opens China’s market to Canadian fava beans, chickpeas and lentils [8].
