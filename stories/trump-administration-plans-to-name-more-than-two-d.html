Trump administration plans to name more than two dozen U.S. AI national champions

Category: AI

The Trump administration plans to designate more than two dozen artificial intelligence companies, including Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic, as “national champions” to help them win overseas business against Chinese competitors, The Wall Street Journal reported Nov. 15, citing people familiar with the plan [6][2]. The administration has not confirmed the proposal, and Reuters said it could not independently verify the report [2]. An announcement was expected within a week, though the timing could change [5][19]. The proposed support includes faster export-license reviews, government help with overseas operations and access to federal financing. The plan would package U.S. chips, AI models and related technology for foreign governments and businesses; selection would not itself guarantee a license or funding [11][9]. The initiative aims to expand U.S. technology exports. Washington faces questions about advanced Nvidia chips reaching China through overseas supply chains [12][19]. U.S. officials and lawmakers are pressing for closer scrutiny of chip exports and possible new restrictions [5][15].

Key Points

  • Congressional scrutiny: Jensen Huang agreed to testify before three House committees in a closed-door session, with dates still being finalised [41].
  • Server declarations: Singapore authorities say companies under investigation bought servers that were exported through Singapore to Malaysia without entering Singapore’s market. Authorities are examining whether the servers were later routed elsewhere contrary to declarations [22][29].
  • Application process: The Commerce Department said in July that companies had submitted 78 applications; officials were to assess them against published criteria before the commerce secretary made final selections [11].
  • Audit scope: Megaspeed chief executive Tan Yong Pong said the company hired a U.S. law firm to conduct a “comprehensive census” of its graphics processing units and review its export-control practices [29].
  • Export precedent: President Trump authorized sales of Nvidia’s H200 chips to China in late 2025, while barring sales of chips more advanced than the H200, according to Yahoo Finance [5].
  • Cloud deal halted: Oracle cancelled a proposed $7 billion deal to give Tencent access to 100,000 AI chips through Southeast Asian data centres after U.S. officials raised concerns [22].
  • Congressional hearings: Senators summoned Jensen Huang to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month, and he agreed to closed-door House testimony before Thanksgiving [5].
  • Congressional scrutiny: Lawmakers are considering measures to restrict Chinese firms’ remote access to overseas chips through cloud services, with proposals potentially attached to the annual defense authorization bill [5].
  • Orders under review: A Wall Street Journal investigation cited records indicating Megaspeed arranged orders for nearly $12 billion in advanced Nvidia chips for Chinese companies between March 2024 and June 2025, but the records did not establish how many chips arrived or where they all went [22].
