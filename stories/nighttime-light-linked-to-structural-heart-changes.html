Nighttime light linked to structural heart changes in 11,071 people

Category: Science

Nighttime light exposure was associated with changes in heart structure and function in a study of 11,071 UK Biobank participants led by Tulane University researchers. Participants wore wrist light sensors for seven days, and researchers examined their hearts with cardiovascular MRI about three years later. Those exposed to higher nighttime light levels had thicker, less flexible heart muscle and early signs of reduced function [1][4][7]. The study compared people exposed to more than three lux at night with participants exposed to almost no light. The higher-exposure group had thicker left-ventricle walls, less room for blood to fill the chamber and reduced ability of the heart muscle to deform during a heartbeat. The European Heart Journal published the findings on September 9, 2026 [1][4][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Previous observational studies have linked nighttime light exposure with a higher risk of cardiovascular disease, but little is known about the related cardiac structure and functional changes.

— Professor Lu Qi (ScienceDaily)

Key Points

  • Light-source context: Coverage said even weak nighttime light, such as light from a mobile phone, could be relevant to heart health [7].

Perspectives

Lu Qi and Tulane researchers: Qi characterized the reported structural and functional changes as a pattern commonly seen in response to chronic stress and said such remodeling can ultimately lead to heart failure [7].

Timeline

Scientific Significance

  • Association versus causation: The study found an association between measured nighttime light exposure and later cardiac characteristics. Available reports describe it as observational and do not establish that light exposure directly caused the heart changes [1][5].
  • Reporting limitations: Available reports do not provide the full exposure distribution, effect sizes, confidence intervals or adjustment details [1].
  • Follow-up evidence: Further research is needed to test whether reducing nighttime light changes MRI measures or cardiovascular outcomes [4][7].

Action Items

  • Reduce bedroom light at night by limiting outdoor light entering the room and turning off unnecessary indoor and device lights, a prevention strategy suggested in study coverage [1].
