Finnish officials suspect Ukraine struck Russian cargo ship in Gulf of Finland

Category: World

The Russian cargo ship Sparta II caught fire after two drones struck it in international waters in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday, then drifted into Finnish waters. Finnish armed forces spokesperson Aki-Mauri Huhtinen said Ukraine was likely responsible, though Kyiv had not commented [2][7]. The 16 crew members left the ship for a nearby Russian vessel, while Finnish authorities led the rescue operation as the fire continued to burn [2][6]. The ship was bound for Kaliningrad and has links to Oboronlogistika, a Russian logistics company that serves the Defence Ministry. Finland’s Border Guard said the ship’s cargo was unconfirmed and could include military material. The ship is under U.S. sanctions [2][6]. Finnish authorities also suspected that a Russian helicopter briefly entered Finnish airspace hours after the strike, possibly during rescue or surveillance operations [8][10]. Finland imposed temporary air and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland, informed its allies and began coordinating the response. Russian vessels could tow the damaged ship back [2][7][23]. Finland’s defence minister said the Border Guard would investigate the attack with other authorities [7].

Key Points

  • Drone barrage: Leningrad Region Governor Aleksandr Drozdenko said Russian air defences downed 80 drones over the region overnight, though the figure has not been independently verified [21][23].
  • Ust-Luga damage: Drone strikes also damaged Novatek’s oil-product terminal and petroleum-product infrastructure at Ust-Luga, Ukraine’s Security Service said [23].
  • Diesel exports: Baltic ports’ role in fuel trade grew after an October agreement allowed Russia to partially resume diesel exports; the first tanker carrying diesel left Primorsk that month [23].
  • Ship dimensions: The Russian-flagged Sparta II is 121 metres long and left St. Petersburg for Kaliningrad on Friday, according to shipping data cited by Reuters [6][12].

Industry Impact

  • Baltic shipping: The Gulf of Finland connects St. Petersburg to open Baltic waters; the wider Baltic Sea carries about 15% of global freight, and attacks have raised risks to routes used by cargo ships and oil tankers [23].
  • Russian grain exports: Baltic ports handled 65.3% of Russia’s seaborne grain exports in October, with Ust-Luga the country’s largest grain export port that month at 684,000 tons [23].

Historical Background

Russia’s invasion prompted Finland to end decades of military non-alignment and join NATO in 2023; Sweden followed in 2024 [10][2].
