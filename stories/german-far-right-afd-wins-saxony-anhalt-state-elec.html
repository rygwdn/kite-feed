German far-right AfD wins Saxony-Anhalt state election

Category: World

Alternative for Germany won the Saxony-Anhalt state election on September 6 with 43.8% of the vote, its strongest result in a German state contest, while Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s Christian Democratic Union fell to 17.2% and the Social Democrats took 9.3% [4][10][59]. The AfD secured 39 seats in the 83-seat state parliament, short of the 42 needed to govern alone, leaving coalition talks or outside support as routes to a possible first far-right state government in postwar Germany [11][68][48]. Merz vowed to remain chancellor after saying the result had shaken the CDU “to its very foundations,” and ruled out cooperation with the AfD, whose Saxony-Anhalt branch Germany’s domestic intelligence service classifies as confirmed right-wing extremist [3][11][13]. All other parties have said cooperation with the AfD is out of the question under the mainstream “firewall” policy of excluding the party from governing arrangements [13][30]. AfD leaders sought to convert the result into power in Magdeburg, with lead candidate Ulrich Siegmund saying the party had initiated talks toward building a governing majority [46][43].

Key Points

  • High turnout: Turnout reached nearly 78%, up from 60% in 2021 and the highest level in Saxony-Anhalt since German reunification, according to reports citing official preliminary figures [22][68].
  • Intelligence access: Regional governments have explored blocking the AfD from intelligence briefings if it enters government, according to Semafor’s account of post-election scenarios [9].
  • Next elections: Two more regional votes are scheduled for September 20 in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Berlin, where Romanian broadcaster ProTV reported the AfD leads in one and is tied with rivals in the other [64].
