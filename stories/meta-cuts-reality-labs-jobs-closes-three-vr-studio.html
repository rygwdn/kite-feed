Meta cuts Reality Labs jobs, closes three VR studios

Category: Technology

Meta has started job cuts of more than 1,000 roles in its Reality Labs unit, with reporting that the reduction targets roughly a tenth of the division’s workforce [6][1][7][4]. As part of the shift, Meta is shutting down three in-house VR game studios—Twisted Pixel Games, Sanzaru Games, and Armature Studio—and the VR fitness app Supernatural will remain supported but stop receiving new content and feature updates [1][5][8][9]. Meta says it is reallocating investment away from metaverse-focused work toward wearables and other AI hardware, including its Ray-Ban smart glasses, while keeping VR plans on a leaner roadmap [5][6][7].

Did you know: Twisted Pixel was previously owned by Microsoft from 2011 to 2015 before later being acquired by Meta in 2022, according to UploadVR’s historical summary cited by Slashdot [9].

Key Points

  • Mobile pivot: CTO Andrew Bosworth told staff that Meta will shift metaverse efforts toward mobile experiences, citing faster growth and a larger potential user base there [6].
  • Wearables momentum: Reality Labs sales grew 40% in 2024, a gain eWEEK attributes to demand for Ray-Ban Meta glasses even as the unit remained unprofitable [10].
  • Production talks: DigiTimes reports Meta is in discussions with EssilorLuxottica to lift annual Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses output to 20 million units by end-2026, with a possible upside to 30 million if demand holds [11].
  • Partner devices paused: Meta previously paused planned Horizon OS headsets from Asus and Lenovo, a sign the company is tightening its VR hardware pipeline alongside organizational changes [5].
  • New AI ops unit: DigiTimes says Meta launched a "Meta Compute" division to manage global AI computing and data-center expansion as it pursues its “Superintelligence” goal [12].

Perspectives

Meta leadership: CTO Andrew Bosworth framed the restructuring as a resource shift toward wearables and mobile-driven adoption while running the VR organization in a leaner, flatter form [6].

Sources: Engadget

Meta spokesperson: Meta says it will reinvest savings from Reality Labs changes to support wearables growth this year [5].

Sources: Engadget

Oculus Studios management: In an internal memo reported by Bloomberg and relayed by Engadget, Oculus Studios director Tamara Sciamanna said the changes do not mean Meta is moving away from video games and instead shift investment toward third-party developers and partners [5].

Sources: Engadget

Affected creators and users: Employees from Twisted Pixel and Sanzaru publicly posted about the studio closures, while Supernatural told users it will stay active but stop shipping new content and feature updates [5].

Sources: Engadget

Technical Details

  • Reality Labs: Meta’s Reality Labs is the division that develops augmented- and virtual-reality products and related metaverse initiatives, and it is the unit experiencing the current job cuts [7][6].
  • AI smart glasses: Meta’s Ray-Ban smart glasses are positioned as AI-powered wearables and have become a key focus as the company shifts investment away from metaverse-heavy VR work [2][6].
  • Horizon OS: Horizon OS is Meta’s software platform for VR headsets; Engadget reports Meta paused planned third-party Horizon OS headsets from Asus and Lenovo amid the broader pullback [5].

Industry Impact

  • VR game publishing: Closing Twisted Pixel, Sanzaru, and Armature reduces first-party Quest-exclusive development capacity, potentially shifting more opportunity (and responsibility) to third-party VR studios and publishers [1][5].
  • Wearables supply chain: If Meta increases Ray-Ban Meta production targets, suppliers tied to smart glasses components and assembly could see higher demand through 2026, though DigiTimes notes no final agreement has been reached [11].
  • VR fitness apps: Supernatural’s move to “active but not updated” status highlights how subscription-style VR apps can face content risk when corporate priorities change, even if the service remains available for existing users [5].

Historical Background

Meta rebranded from Facebook to Meta in 2021 to emphasize its bet on the metaverse [8][6]. Since the start of 2021, Reality Labs has accumulated more than $70 billion in losses, a performance that multiple outlets cite as context for today’s reallocation toward wearables and AI hardware [6][2][10].

Q&A

Q: What happens to in-development VR projects at the shuttered studios?

A: The reports detail the studio closures but do not specify whether specific unannounced projects will be transferred, canceled, or released in another form [1][5].

Q: Will Meta still release a next-generation Quest headset soon?

A: Engadget reports Meta is not abandoning VR entirely, but Bosworth described a more focused VR roadmap and cautioned readers not to expect a quick follow-up to recent Quest hardware [6].

Q: Which teams inside Reality Labs are most affected by the cuts?

A: Several outlets say the layoffs disproportionately impact metaverse and VR-related groups, including work tied to Horizon Worlds and related platform efforts [2][3][10].

Action Items

  • Download and export your Supernatural workout history: If you use Supernatural, export or record key routines and progress now, since the app will remain active but stop receiving new content and feature updates [5].
  • Wishlist VR titles from impacted studios: If you want to preserve access and encourage ongoing storefront support, add existing games from Twisted Pixel, Sanzaru, and Armature to your Quest library or wishlist while store listings remain available [1][5].
