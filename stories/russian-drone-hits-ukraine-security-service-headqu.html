Russian drone hits Ukraine security service headquarters

Category: World

A Russian drone struck the headquarters of Ukraine’s Security Service, the SBU, on Volodymyrska Street in central Kyiv on Friday, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said, adding that the aircraft was aimed directly at the office of acting SBU chief Oleksandr Poklad [4][6][9][8][13]. Kyiv authorities reported at least 12 people wounded, with two in serious condition and nine requiring hospital treatment, while emergency crews responded near St. Sophia Cathedral, a UNESCO World Heritage site [4][5][13][17][28]. Zelenskyy said he spoke with Poklad after the strike and instructed him to prepare an “appropriate, tangible response,” while Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha called the attack a “major escalation” as U.S. officials prepared talks on ending the war [4][9][18][16]. A U.S. official said President Trump’s envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were expected to travel to Moscow and then Kyiv over the weekend and into early next week [4][34]. Russia did not immediately comment on the strike [13][49][82].

Key Points

  • Office empty: Poklad was not in his office at the time of the strike, Ukrainian Pravda reported, and a senior official told AFP he was not nearby [8][13][23][49].
  • Nearby damage: The nearby Zavertailo cafe or bakery was damaged or destroyed, and the St. Sophia museum administration said windows shattered in an 18th-century administrative building [9][15][43][64].
  • SBU operations: The SBU has been involved in long-range attacks on Russian oil infrastructure, the “shadow fleet,” the Kerch Bridge in 2022 and the 2025 “Spiderweb” drone operation against strategic bombers [8][7][57][75].
  • Drone campaign: Russia has intensified its aerial campaign with more frequent daytime strikes on Kyiv, keeping the capital under near-constant alert for over a week [4][70][79][72].
  • Ceasefire proposal: Zelenskyy proposed a pause in air attacks during the Witkoff-Kushner visits, saying Ukraine would not launch airstrikes if Russia also stopped while the talks took place [6][34][14].

  • 4 September 2026: A Russian drone hit the SBU headquarters in Kyiv [4][9].

Historical Background

The SBU emerged from the Ukrainian branch of the Soviet-era KGB after Ukraine gained independence and has broad domestic security powers [7][75]. Since Russia’s full-scale invasion, it has investigated war crimes, countered cyberattacks, conducted counterintelligence work and detained suspected Russian agents [75]. Ukrainian government buildings have rarely been damaged during more than four-and-a-half years of war [13][64][43].

Humanitarian Impact

Beyond the SBU strike, Kyiv and the wider region have recorded at least 53 deaths and 134 injuries since the latest wave of attacks began last month, according to figures cited by The News [21]. A WHO spokesman said one of the organization’s key warehouses holding humanitarian medical supplies in the Kyiv region was hit in an air attack, with no WHO staff injured [41].

International Reactions

  • 🇪🇸 Spain: A Spanish minister called the Kyiv strike an “unacceptable escalation,” according to the Guardian’s live coverage [5].
  • 🇺🇸 United States: The United States was preparing Witkoff and Kushner visits to Moscow and Kyiv, though Reuters reported the trip had not been finalized because of U.S. security concerns [4][34].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Air-raid alerts sounded repeatedly in Kyiv on Friday, and the city has faced sustained drone and missile attacks in recent weeks [53][4]. Follow official air-raid instructions immediately and move to shelters when alerts sound.
  • Movement: Roads around the SBU strike site were closed after the attack, and the surrounding streets were cordoned off [53][6]. Avoid central Kyiv near Volodymyrska Street and St. Sophia Cathedral until local authorities reopen the area.

Action Items

  • Check travel alerts for Kyiv: If you are in or traveling to Kyiv, review your government’s Ukraine advisory and local air-raid channels before moving around the city.
  • Locate the nearest shelter: Use Kyiv’s official shelter maps or building-management guidance and identify the closest protected space before an air-raid alert.
