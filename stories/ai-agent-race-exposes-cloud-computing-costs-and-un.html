AI agent race exposes cloud computing costs and uncertain paths to profit

Category: AI

AI agents designed to keep working in the cloud face a cost challenge: extended tasks can require repeated model calls, and heavier use may raise operating expenses even as individual calls become cheaper, developers and analysts told Huxiu [3]. Anthropic’s Claude Duet, a work-focused agent released in research preview on Oct. 21, is competing with Meta’s Muse and OpenAI’s Dots [3][2][7]. Interviewees said profitability depends on computing costs, sustained use and a viable revenue model. Workplace agents may be easier to sell because businesses can weigh completed reports or customer follow-ups against labor saved, but data protection, permissions and software integrations add costs [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Platform access: Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse from shopping, saying the agent did not clearly identify itself and citing concerns about account access and credentials [3].
  • User entry: Instinct lets users delegate tasks by text message. Howie Xu said this could lower customer acquisition costs by letting people describe the outcome they want instead of navigating a workflow [3].
  • Task design: Meta’s Muse organizes ongoing assignments on a Goals page, where the agent can keep pursuing a target and return to the user for approval or a decision [3].

Perspectives

Meta chief executive Mark Zuckerberg said an assistant should use relevant personal information to help with a task without disclosing unnecessary details to others. He cited dining choices informed by a user’s pregnancy as an example [3].

Sources: Huxiu

AgentSky chief executive Xiaoyin Qu said Muse could help Meta improve ad targeting by revealing purchase intent before a user turns to search, potentially increasing ad conversions even when the agent does not handle the transaction [3].

Sources: Huxiu

SpaceXAI designer Peng Zheng said people remain a bottleneck if they have to keep monitoring agents, so products should make delegation more automatic and reduce the need for repeated supervision [3].

Sources: Huxiu

Business Angle

  • Meta has proposed taking a small commission on transactions arranged through Muse, potentially paid by merchants; it has not reported income from the proposal [3].
  • Instinct’s founder said the product would not charge users and would rely on advertising; Howie Xu warned that early attention could fade after the novelty wears off [3].
  • Meta reported that more than 10 million people in the U.S. and Canada had set up a Muse agent, with about a quarter using it on a given day; the service’s long-term profitability remains unclear [7].
