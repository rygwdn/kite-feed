India and EU sign free trade agreement

Category: World

India and the European Union announced they have concluded and signed a long-negotiated free trade agreement, billed by EU leaders as the “mother of all trade deals,” after roughly two decades of talks [3][5][29][7]. The pact aims to sharply reduce tariffs across many sectors and create a vast combined market, while also deepening strategic ties between New Delhi and Brussels [7][30][41]. Multiple outlets say the timing reflects a desire by both sides to diversify partnerships amid U.S. tariff pressure and a more protectionist global trade environment under President Trump [29][43][44][5]. For businesses and consumers, supporters present it as a confidence-boosting step toward steadier rules and broader choice in a choppy world economy [1][22].

Key Points

  • EU-side cuts: India’s trade ministry said the EU will cut tariffs on 99.5% of goods traded over seven years, with zero tariffs planned for Indian marine goods, leather and textiles, chemicals, rubber, base metals, and gems and jewellery [34].
  • Carbon linkage: An Indian official said the EU did not grant India specific exemptions from its carbon rules, but agreed that any future CBAM relaxations it gives other countries would automatically apply to India [24].
  • Duty savings: The European Commission said Europe could save about €4 billion a year in import duties as India cuts or eliminates tariffs on 96.6% of EU goods exports, spanning autos, machinery, chemicals, pharmaceuticals and agri-food [10].
  • Business sentiment: A FEBI Business Sentiment Survey reported nearly 95% of European companies plan to expand in India, citing the FTA as a catalyst for manufacturing and supply-chain investment [8].
  • Consumer imports: With lower duties, items such as wine, olive oil and some processed foods are expected to become cheaper in India, while sensitive farm sectors remain protected under the agreement’s market-access approach [11].
  • Tech cooperation: The Hindu reported the FTA explicitly links the European AI Office with India’s National AI Mission and moves toward joint R&D on advanced semiconductor integration [6].
  • Market response: Indian equities rose as the agreement boosted sentiment, with Firstpost reporting the Nifty moving above 25,200 and metal and financial stocks leading gains [9].
  • Security track: Alongside trade, India and the EU also signed a Security and Defence Strategic Partnership, with RT reporting Indian firms could participate in the EU’s Security Action for Europe program [51][35].
  • Tariff removals: The deal cuts tariffs to zero for many industrial products, including iron and steel, plastics, chemicals and pharmaceuticals, according to the EU-focused coverage [39].
  • Tariff phase-downs: EU statements and related reporting say India will cut or eliminate duties on 96.6% of EU goods exports, including steep reductions on cars and alcoholic beverages over time [26][34].

Technical Details

  • Services access: Euronews said the pact opens up services alongside changes in goods and agriculture, indicating provisions beyond traditional tariff cuts [47].
  • Tariff liberalization: The deal’s core mechanism is reducing import duties, including zeroing many industrial tariffs, which can lower landed costs and improve cross-border price competitiveness [39][47].
  • Tariff schedule design: Reporting describes staged tariff elimination over several years, including sharp reductions on autos and alcohol as part of market-opening commitments [34][39].
  • Goods coverage: The European Commission said India will cut or remove tariffs on 96.6% of EU goods exports, signaling broad product coverage rather than narrow quota-based access [10].
  • AI office linkage: The Hindu said the deal formally connects the European AI Office and India’s National AI Mission to support coordination and joint work [6].
  • CBAM passthrough clause: India’s side said the EU made no India-specific CBAM concession, but any future relaxations for other countries would extend to Indian exporters [24].

Industry Impact

  • EU cost savings: The European Commission said the deal could save Europe about €4 billion a year in import duties as India cuts tariffs across most EU goods exports [26].
  • Export manufacturing: India’s commerce minister said exports worth about Rs 6.4 lakh crore could benefit via single-framework access to all 27 EU markets [8].
  • Manufacturing supply chains: European businesses see the agreement as supportive of manufacturing and supply-chain investment in India, with a survey reporting widespread expansion plans [8].
  • European exporters: The European Commission’s projected duty savings highlights potential margin relief for EU exporters that previously faced higher Indian tariffs on large categories like machinery, chemicals and autos [10].
  • Trade geopolitics: Multiple outlets linked the timing to US tariff pressure under President Trump, suggesting firms may use the pact to diversify markets and reduce exposure to US trade-policy swings [29][50][33].
  • Investor sentiment: Firstpost reported Indian equities rose on optimism tied to the agreement, with metals and financials among the leaders [23].
  • Food and beverage: Lower duties on products like wine and olive oil could change competitive dynamics for imported premium foods in India while still leaving protections for sensitive farm segments [11].
  • Tech supply chains: The deal’s emphasis on AI coordination and semiconductor R&D could support diversification of advanced-tech supply chains beyond single-region dependence [6][44].
  • Automotive trade: European automakers stand to gain improved access as India reduces car tariffs over time, which ACEA said could open a market of about 4 million passenger cars [39][34].

Historical Background

India-EU free trade negotiations have run for roughly two decades, with multiple outlets describing years of on-and-off talks before this week’s breakthrough [29][5][7]. In recent years, both sides have also pursued other major trade pacts, a trend several reports tie to a more fragmented global trade environment and concerns about tariff shocks [32][44].

Q&A

Q: Could the agreement take effect without every EU national parliament voting?

A: Firstpost reported the pact may avoid “mixed competence” provisions, which could let it bypass ratification by all 27 member-state parliaments [firstpost.com#24].

Q: Which Indian sectors do exporters expect to gain most quickly?

A: Indian industry groups pointed to textiles, leather/footwear, gems and jewellery, chemicals and marine products as likely early beneficiaries [17][34].

Q: How large do some reports say the combined market is in economic terms?

A: Al Jazeera described the agreement as creating a $27 trillion market spanning India and the EU [30].

Q: Which parts of agriculture are still protected despite wider market access?

A: Firstpost reports that while agri-food access expands and some imported foods may get cheaper, the deal keeps protections for “sensitive farm sectors” [11].

Q: Why did negotiations suddenly speed up after years of slow progress?

A: The Guardian reported talks accelerated in the past six months, and other outlets linked urgency to trade pressure from Washington under President Trump’s tariff approach [39][29][33].

Q: What does “opens up services” mean in practical terms?

A: Euronews describes the pact as opening services alongside agriculture and goods; in trade agreements, this typically refers to easing market entry and rules for cross-border providers rather than only cutting tariffs on products [47][common].
