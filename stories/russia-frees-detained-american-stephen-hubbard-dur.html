Russia frees detained American Stephen Hubbard during Ukraine peace talks

Category: World

Russia released Stephen Hubbard, a 74-year-old American jailed for more than four years, during U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff’s meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin in St. Petersburg on Wednesday. President Donald Trump said he had personally asked Putin to free Hubbard, who was later flown to Texas. Washington is seeking a negotiated end to Russia’s war against Ukraine [3][4][43]. Russia convicted Hubbard in a closed trial in 2024 of serving as a mercenary for Ukraine, an allegation he and his family disputed. The U.S. had designated him wrongfully detained. Trump said no prisoner swap was required, but later argued that his decision to suspend new sanctions helped prevent negotiations for Hubbard’s release from collapsing [7][3][22]. Witkoff said the discussions with Putin were constructive. The Kremlin said it was prepared in principle to resume U.S.-Russia-Ukraine talks between Nov. 10 and 19, while Putin aide Yuri Ushakov said the latest Ukrainian and European proposals offered little useful material for a settlement [6][18][22].

Key Points

  • Sentence details: The court sentenced Hubbard to six years and 10 months; Russian authorities said he admitted the charge, but his U.S. lawyer said he denied it [9][40].
  • Detention concerns: Hubbard’s former cellmate Ihor Chychko alleged that Hubbard was beaten, starved and subjected to electric shocks in custody; Hubbard’s sister also told the Associated Press that he suffered beatings and starvation [9][6].
  • Remaining detainees: Hubbard had been the only American in Russia formally designated by the U.S. State Department as wrongfully detained, though advocacy groups say other Americans remain imprisoned there [3][31].
  • Sanctions debate: Trump publicly linked the release negotiations to his sanctions pause, while U.S. lawmakers criticized the waiver and argued that easing pressure on Moscow could benefit Russia [3][16][6].
  • Talks agenda: Russian officials said future trilateral discussions should address postwar security arrangements for Russia and Ukraine; Putin also raised the restoration of bilateral U.S.-Russia economic cooperation [22][4].

Perspectives

Republican lawmakers: Senators Thom Tillis and John Cornyn rejected Witkoff’s description of Putin as a humanitarian; Cornyn called the Russian leader “a stone cold killer,” while Tillis called Witkoff’s comment embarrassing and outrageous [16].
