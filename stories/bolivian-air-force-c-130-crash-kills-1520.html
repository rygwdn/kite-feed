Bolivian air force C-130 crash kills 15–20

Category: World

A Bolivian Air Force Hercules C-130 cargo plane carrying newly printed banknotes crashed in El Alto, next to the capital La Paz, after veering off the runway during an attempted landing, according to officials and multiple reports [15][3][8][9][13]. The aircraft came to rest on or beside a busy avenue/highway, damaging about a dozen vehicles. Early reports put the death toll at least 15, while some outlets cited police updates that raised it to 20; about 30 injuries were also reported [1][5][10][15][6][7][8]. The scene quickly turned chaotic as bundles of cash scattered across the roadway and nearby ground. Crowds rushed in to grab banknotes while police tried to clear people out and secure the area; several reports said officers used tear gas [1][2][3][12][13][6]. Emergency crews worked to put out flames and search damaged vehicles for survivors, while authorities began investigating how the plane lost control. Some updates pointed to bad weather at the time [11][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Conflicting accounts of the phase of flight: Some reports said the C-130 veered off the runway during an attempted landing, while another placed the crash shortly after takeoff—an early sign that the sequence of events is still disputed [3][5][15].
  • Cash logistics detail: One report said the newly printed banknotes were being moved from Bolivia’s Central Bank to other cities, rather than described only as “cash” [10][11].
  • Aftermath handling of the cash: The Guardian reported authorities were later seen setting the scattered banknotes alight in a bonfire at the crash site [6].
  • Victim-location uncertainty: Fire Chief Pavel Tovar said it was still unclear whether those killed were on the aircraft, in vehicles on the avenue, or both [11][14].
  • Weather factor cited in some updates: AFP-cited police updates pointed to bad weather conditions at the time of the crash, which could matter as investigators piece together what happened [7][11].

Perspectives

Defence Minister Marcelo Salinas: He said the Bolivian Air Force’s C-130 Hercules was transporting newly printed currency and described the incident as a runway excursion at El Alto airport [3].

Sources: South China Morning Post

Fire Chief Pavel Tovar: He said authorities had not yet determined whether the victims were on the aircraft, in vehicles on the road, or both, as responders assessed the crash scene across the roadway and surrounding area [11][14].

Sources: RT, The Hindu

Scene management: The Guardian reported that after the initial scramble for scattered banknotes, authorities were later seen burning some of the money in a bonfire near the wreckage [6].
