Meta launches pro-AI ad campaign with Bowie song

Category: AI

Meta released an AI-themed advertising campaign that argues against fears that AI will make people less connected or leave them behind, with a voiceover saying the company is "betting on people" and that "the future is for everyone" [2][7][8][6]. Mark Zuckerberg posted the video Thursday and wrote in the caption that Meta believes the future should be broadly available as it enters "this next wave with AI" [7][8]. The ad drew attention because it uses David Bowie's "Five Years," a song whose lyrics describe Earth dying and humanity facing a five-year deadline [1][2]. The video pairs that soundtrack with scenes of people smiling, embracing, swimming, dancing, using Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook and Meta Ray-Ban glasses, and reconnecting with loved ones [2][7][8].

Primary Source: View original article

Call us optimists, call us dreamers, call us whatever the hell you want, but we’re betting on people, and we like those odds

— Meta ad voiceover (Fortune)

Key Points

  • Limited AI references: Engadget reported that the one-minute commercial makes few direct references to Meta's AI features, relying instead on general lifestyle footage [8].
  • Campaign scope: A Meta spokesperson told Engadget the ad starts a broader effort to offer an alternative to "AI doomers" and dystopian depictions of the technology [8].
  • Ad opening: TechCrunch said the ad starts with a black-and-white shot of an eye reading panicked AI headlines before shifting to color [2].
  • Timing: Fortune noted that the campaign launched in a week when an AI model went rogue and workers feared displacement [7].

Perspectives

Mark Zuckerberg: He wrote that Meta is focused on giving people tools to reach their full potential and distributing technology's benefits broadly.

Sources: Engadget

Historical Background

David Bowie's "Five Years" appeared on his 1972 album "The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars," which follows an apocalyptic rock-star narrative [common]. Meta traces its origin to Facebook, launched in 2004; Fortune described the ad's language as reaching back to the company's early social-networking pitch [common][7].

  • Meta's AI products would need to improve user engagement, ad performance or operating efficiency to affect long-term value [common].
