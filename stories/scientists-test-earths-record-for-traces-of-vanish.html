Scientists test Earth's record for traces of vanished civilizations

Category: Science

Astrobiologist Adam Frank of the University of Rochester and NASA scientist Gavin Schmidt developed the “Silurian hypothesis” after a 2017 conversation about whether advanced civilizations would inevitably trigger climate shifts like those driven by human fossil-fuel use [3][4]. They asked whether an advanced civilization that existed on Earth millions of years ago, used energy and disappeared would have left detectable traces in the planet’s record [3][4]. Frank and Schmidt published a paper on the idea in 2018, presenting it as a thought experiment rather than evidence that such a civilization existed [3][4]. Katharine Hayhoe, an atmospheric scientist at Texas Tech University who was not involved in the research, said the concept exposes global vulnerability as humans rapidly alter the climate by burning fossil fuels [3][4].

Technical Details

  • Geological incompleteness: The geological record is so incomplete that a civilization lasting 100,000 years — far longer than humans have lived in an industrial era — might leave almost no detectable trace [3].
  • Signal preservation: The clearest signal from human civilization would be a sudden spike in carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels, preserved in ice cores and ocean sediments, but over millions of years those signals would be smeared out and hard to distinguish from natural variability [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Anthropocene framing: The hypothesis links to the Anthropocene, the proposed geological epoch defined by human impact, by examining what traces human civilization could leave in the geological record [3].
  • Civilization impermanence: It examines how little evidence an industrial civilization might leave, even one lasting far longer than human history so far [3].
