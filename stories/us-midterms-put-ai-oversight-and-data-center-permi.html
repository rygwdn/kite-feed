US midterms put AI oversight and data center permits in focus

Category: AI

Voters in the U.S. midterm elections on Nov. 3 will determine control of Congress and choose governors in 36 states. The contests could shape scrutiny of artificial intelligence companies and permitting for data centers [4][7][9]. Wall Street forecasts point to a Democratic House, while Senate control remains uncertain. Whichever party wins, President Donald Trump retains his veto, limiting the prospect of sweeping federal legislation [4][6]. Democratic gains could bring congressional investigations into AI firms and proposals for model safety testing, incident reporting and greater accountability for data center developers. Analysts expect state and local decisions on power, water, land use and construction permits to affect projects more directly [4][7]. One bank analysis estimated that even heightened scrutiny would reach only about 15% of U.S. data center capacity, leaving the overall investment cycle largely intact [8]. Congress is scheduled to reconvene on Nov. 9, with federal funding negotiations due before a Dec. 11 deadline [8].

Conventional wisdom is that gridlock is good (for stocks).

— Ned Davis chief US strategist Ed Clissold (Reuters)

Key Points

  • Local ballots: Data center proposals appear on 41 local ballots in 10 states on Nov. 3; 28 measures would restrict development, and all six related votes earlier in 2026 favored limits [9].
  • Capacity distribution: The 15 states with the most data centers contain about 82% of U.S. capacity; Republican-governed states account for 62% and Democratic-governed states for 38% [8].
  • Power costs: A bipartisan bill that passed the House 417-3 in September would require state regulators to assess whether large data centers should pay for the electricity infrastructure they add. It failed to advance past a Senate procedural vote [13].
  • Investment scale: Morgan Stanley estimates Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Meta will spend more than $1 trillion combined on capital projects in 2027 [13].

Perspectives

JPMorgan strategists: They said continued Republican control would support AI infrastructure through policy continuity, possible fiscal expansion, deregulation and faster federal permitting.

Sources: Reuters

Industry Impact

  • Community sentiment: A CNBC poll cited by Cnyes found that 63% of registered voters oppose a data center within 10 miles of their home, while 13% support one [13].
  • Resource demand: An average 100-megawatt data center uses about 2 million liters of water daily and more electricity than 75,000 households [8].

Historical Background

Republicans controlled both chambers of Congress during President Trump's first two years back in office following his return to the White House in 2025 [12][4].

Business Angle

  • Include state politics in financing reviews: PIMCO says it has become more selective in evaluating transactions and pays closer attention to political factors [7].
  • Compare proposed AI expansion with construction activity: U.S. data center capacity under construction rose 48% from December 2025 to June 2026, according to a market analyst [9].

Q&A

Q: Could congressional gridlock create risks beyond AI oversight?

A: ICICI Bank sees a moderate risk of a partial government shutdown in late 2026 or 2027 and flags a renewed debt-ceiling standoff around mid-2027 [8].
