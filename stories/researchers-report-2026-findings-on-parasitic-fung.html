Researchers report 2026 findings on parasitic fungi and brain tissue preservation

Category: Science

Researchers documented unusual biological and planetary findings in 2026, including animal tissues that survived outside a body for years and mouse brain slices that recovered electrical activity after cryopreservation [3]. A study published in May found that fragments of scarlet sea cucumber tentacles and tube feet survived for more than three years in natural seawater. In March, researchers reported that neurons in rewarmed mouse brain tissue could fire and transmit signals across synapses [3]. The work did not show that memories survived or that a mouse could be revived. Separately, James Webb Space Telescope observations of the rocky exoplanet TOI-561 b showed that it reflects roughly three-quarters of incoming starlight, with silicate-mineral clouds likely contributing to the effect [4]. The planet orbits its star every 11 hours and reaches surface temperatures above 1,800 degrees Celsius. Researchers inferred the presence of an atmosphere from the planet’s reflected light and heat redistribution but have not established its composition. They say observations at longer wavelengths are a next step [4].

Did you know: The surviving sea cucumber tissue fragments absorbed dissolved amino acids from the surrounding seawater [3].

Key Points

  • Fungal parasite: Researchers described a Borneo fungus in the genus Pleurocordyceps growing in association with a fungus that infects ants, a case of one parasite exploiting another [3].
  • Spider disguise: A newly described Ecuadorian spider, Taczanowskia waska, has projections resembling the fruiting structures of spider-infecting fungi; possible benefits for predator avoidance or hunting remain untested [3].
  • Forensic traces: A forensic case study found six volatile compounds associated with decomposition in concrete sampled after a body had been removed; the chemicals alone cannot identify what happened [3].
  • Tidal locking: TOI-561 b keeps one hemisphere permanently facing its star, leaving a blistering dayside and a cooler nightside [4].

Timeline

  • January 2026: Forensic researchers reported decomposition compounds retained in basement concrete [3]
  • February 2026: Researchers described a spider that mimics a spider-infecting fungus [3]
  • March 2026: Researchers reported that vitrified mouse brain tissue recovered electrical activity after rewarming [3]
  • April 2026: Researchers described a new fungus associated with an ant-infecting fungus [3]
  • May 2026: Researchers reported that sea cucumber tissue fragments survived for years in seawater [3]

Technical Details

  • Vitrification: The mouse-brain protocol used protective chemicals and controlled cooling to place tissue in a glasslike state, limiting ice-crystal damage that can disrupt neural structures [3].
  • Long-term potentiation: Rewarmed hippocampal tissue displayed long-term potentiation, a persistent increase in synaptic signal strength used as a cellular model of learning; the experiment did not establish that memories were preserved [3].
  • Phase-curve observation: JWST observations spanning nearly four complete orbits allowed the team to view TOI-561 b’s nightside for the first time and assess how heat was redistributed [4].

Scientific Significance

  • Brain preservation: The findings concern tissue slices, not a complete brain, so they do not establish restoration of whole-brain function [3].
  • Limits of tissue survival: The study’s phrase “natural tissue immortality” describes the observed phenomenon, but does not demonstrate that the tissues can survive indefinitely [3].

Q&A

Q: What atmospheric compositions are researchers considering for TOI-561 b?

A: The atmosphere could resemble the carbon-dioxide-rich atmosphere of Venus or the sulfur-dioxide-rich environment of Io [4].
