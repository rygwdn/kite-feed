Conservatives seek emergency meeting over Trump tariff threat

Category: Canada

Conservative MP Shuv Majumdar, the party’s shadow minister for Canada-U.S. relations, asked Prime Minister Mark Carney to release Ottawa’s negotiation plan with President Trump after Trump threatened 50 per cent tariffs on a range of Canadian products within 30 days [2][1]. Majumdar called for the House of Commons Standing Committee on International Trade to hold an emergency meeting next week and said the government should table written plans at the hearings [2][3][1]. U.S. officials said the proposed levies respond to provincial bans on U.S. alcohol, Canada’s supply-managed dairy system and quotas on American cars [2].

Key Points

  • Opposition criticism: Majumdar said Carney campaigned on his ability to handle President Trump but has since conceded to many of Trump’s demands [2].

Historical Background

Canada’s supply management system for dairy, poultry and eggs uses production controls, import limits and administered prices; it has been a recurring point of tension in Canada-U.S. trade talks [common]. The Canada-U.S.-Mexico Agreement replaced NAFTA in 2020 and includes auto trade rules and dairy-market access provisions that shaped later bilateral disputes [common].
