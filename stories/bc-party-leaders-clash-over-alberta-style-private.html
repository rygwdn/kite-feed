B.C. party leaders clash over Alberta-style private health care

Category: Canada

British Columbia NDP Leader David Eby pledged to block what he calls U.S.-style health-care privatization, accusing the provincial Conservatives of supporting a model that could let doctors charge patients for medically necessary surgery. The Conservatives rejected the charge as fearmongering and said patients would continue to use their health cards [1][2]. The dispute centers partly on Alberta, where legislation passed late last year allows doctors to work in both public and private systems. Alberta says dual practice could attract doctors and provide more options; Eby argues it could draw staff away from public care. With the B.C. election set for Oct. 24, the parties also differ over health funding: the NDP proposes higher taxes on top earners, while the Conservatives promise a $50-million fund for rural practices and an expert panel on reducing wait times [1][2].

Key Points

  • Competing pledges: The Conservatives say their platform would keep emergency rooms open and recruit doctors and nurses. In criticizing Eby's record, they point to emergency-room closures and more than one million British Columbians without a family doctor [1].
  • Tax proposals: The NDP proposes a new tax bracket for people earning more than $1 million and increases to the top two existing brackets. The Conservatives argue higher taxes could drive doctors away [2].
  • Alberta's case: Alberta says its dual-practice model does not violate the Canada Health Act, and Premier Danielle Smith expects Ottawa to respect provincial jurisdiction over health care [1].
