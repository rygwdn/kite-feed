EU approves €90 billion Ukraine loan after pipeline restarts

Category: World

European Union envoys on April 22 gave preliminary approval to a €90 billion loan package for Ukraine after months of delay, and formal sign-off by the 27 member states is expected to follow quickly [6][8][12][4]. The breakthrough came after Ukraine said it had finished repairs and restarted the Druzhba pipeline segment that carries Russian oil through Ukraine to Hungary and Slovakia, ending a stoppage that had lasted since late January [11][7][18][9]. The deal reflects a difficult wartime trade-off. Kyiv gets badly needed financing for military and financial support in 2026-2027, while Hungary and Slovakia regain access to crude supplies they had demanded before dropping their objections [1][5][13][14]. The move also broke an intra-EU deadlock as the war stretches beyond four years, and several outlets said EU diplomats also advanced a new sanctions package against Russia alongside the loan decision [1][6][15].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: The Druzhba pipeline still runs through infrastructure built in the Soviet era, making a Cold War-era network central to a 2026 EU financing deal [13].

Key Points

  • Loan terms: NDTV reported that the package would be funded through EU borrowing on capital markets and offered as interest-free loans for 2026 and 2027, backed by EU budget headroom [5].
  • Transit timing: Hungarian company MOL said Ukrtransnafta told it that crude began entering Ukraine from Belarus around noon on Wednesday, with first deliveries to Hungary and Slovakia expected by Thursday [2][7].
  • Supply dispute: Ukraine said the January halt followed war-related damage to the pipeline, while Moscow rejected that explanation and Hungary's government accused Kyiv of deliberately blocking deliveries [18][2].

Perspectives

Ukraine: President Volodymyr Zelenskyy linked the completed pipeline repairs to progress on the EU support package and urged the bloc to unblock the funding once the infrastructure was restored.

Sources: AP News

Hungary and Slovakia: Both countries made renewed oil access through Druzhba a condition for dropping their objections, and their operators said they expected supplies to resume immediately after Ukraine reopened transit.

Sources: RFE/RL

European Union: EU diplomats framed the approval as a step toward keeping Ukraine financially afloat over the next two years while also letting the bloc move ahead with another sanctions package on Russia.

Sources: Deutsche Welle

Slovakia: During the debate over the loan and pipeline, Prime Minister Robert Fico also criticized the incoming Hungarian government, in comments The Guardian said signaled possible strain in the previously close Slovak-Hungarian alignment on energy issues.

Sources: The Guardian

Timeline

  • January 2026: Ukraine halted Druzhba transit after reported war-related pipeline damage [18][7][11]
  • 21 April 2026: Zelenskyy said repairs were complete and urged loan approval [2][17][18]
  • 22 April 2026: EU envoys approved loan after oil transit restarted through Ukraine [6][8][9]

Technical Details

  • Druzhba system: Druzhba is a Soviet-built cross-border pipeline system carrying Russian crude via Belarus into Central Europe, which is why repairs and transit permissions in Ukraine directly affect refineries in Hungary and Slovakia [3][13].
  • Budget headroom: NDTV said the loan would rely on EU borrowing backed by budget headroom, meaning unused margin in the EU budget framework supports the bloc's ability to raise funds on capital markets [5].

Industry Impact

  • Refining: The restart restores crude feedstock for Hungary and Slovakia, where operators said first shipments should arrive by Thursday after nearly three months of interruption [2][7].
  • EU finance: The approval unlocks a two-year support mechanism financed through EU market borrowing, extending the bloc's role as a large-scale funding source for Ukraine [5][12][6].
  • Sanctions policy: Several outlets said EU diplomats also advanced a new EU sanctions package against Russia alongside the loan decision [6][3].

Historical Background

The Druzhba, or 'Friendship,' pipeline is a Soviet-era oil network built to move crude from Russia across Eastern and Central Europe [13][common]. Since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, EU states have repeatedly clashed over how to cut dependence on Russian energy while still protecting countries with limited alternative supply routes [common]. Hungary and Slovakia remained among the EU members most dependent on pipeline oil delivered through Druzhba, making transit through Ukraine a recurring point of leverage in broader debates over aid to Kyiv [1][14].
