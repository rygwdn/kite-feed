Trump demands Iranian compensation as Hormuz talks stall

Category: World

President Donald Trump said Monday that the United States would demand compensation from Iran for people he said Tehran killed or wounded in wars, attacks and protests. The demand adds a new U.S. condition after Iran insisted Washington pay for damage from more than five months of U.S.-Israeli strikes before the Strait of Hormuz can fully reopen [3][8][9][16][22]. Trump said the demand would cover alleged Iranian actions over decades, including harm to U.S. forces, Iranian protesters and people in Lebanon, Syria, Yemen and Gaza [6][11][24]. Iran has said the strait will remain closed unless the United States lifts a naval blockade and sanctions, releases frozen Iranian assets, compensates Iran for war damage and ends military threats and attacks connected to Iran and its regional allies [1][7][26][37]. Tehran also said talks with Oman on navigation arrangements are separate from full reopening; Iranian officials said they were nearing a pact defining shipping lanes, while technical issues on a joint statement remained unresolved [10][49][19]. The strait carried about a fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas before the conflict and has been effectively blocked since U.S.-Israeli attacks on Iran began on February 28 [9][22][49]. Trump told Axios the United States was only “semi-negotiating” and would let economic pressure build on Tehran, while CBS News quoted him as saying the U.S. controls the strait but Iran “can make trouble” there [12][4][7].

Historical Background

Trump's compensation demand references a history of attacks blamed on Iran, including the 1983 Beirut barracks bombing that killed 241 U.S. service members, the 1996 Khobar Towers bombing, and the 2000 USS Cole attack that killed 17 sailors. While al-Qaeda claimed the Cole attack, a U.S. federal court later found Iran liable, a finding Tehran denied. Iran has also been accused of arming and funding groups responsible for roadside bombs that killed hundreds of U.S. troops in Iraq and Afghanistan [6][37].
