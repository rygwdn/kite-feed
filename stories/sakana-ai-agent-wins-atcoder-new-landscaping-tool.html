  • Heuristic contests: Heuristic contests reward good solutions under time/compute limits rather than guaranteed optimality, making them a practical testbed for search, planning, and decision-making systems [2][common].
  • Location awareness: In AI products, location awareness typically means using geographic context (and often local conditions) as an input feature so outputs can adapt to the user’s site instead of generic recommendations [1][common].
  • Agentic systems: An AI “agent” generally refers to a system that iteratively plans, takes actions (such as proposing candidate solutions), and evaluates outcomes to improve results over multiple steps [2][common].

Q: What kinds of inputs would make a “location aware” landscaping assistant most reliable?

A: Beyond a place name, practical inputs usually include sun/shade patterns, soil type, available irrigation, and size constraints; richer context typically improves recommendation relevance [common].

Q: How do heuristic contest objectives differ from classic competitive programming tasks?

A: Heuristic contests often grade solution quality on a spectrum under time limits, while classic tasks usually have a correct/incorrect outcome based on exact matching to an optimal or valid answer [common].

Q: How can readers pressure-test a “three bets” framework over time?
