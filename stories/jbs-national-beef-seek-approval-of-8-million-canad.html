JBS, National Beef seek approval of $8-million Canadian price-fixing settlement

Category: Canada

JBS and National Beef have reached proposed settlements worth $7,993,700 in Canadian class actions alleging price fixing and related conduct in the sale of beef and beef products in Canada [2][4][6]. JBS agreed to pay $7,498,700 and National Beef $495,000 for class members [2][4][6]. The lawsuits allege the companies participated in an unlawful conspiracy, beginning at least as early as Jan. 1, 2015, to limit the production or supply of beef and to fix, raise, maintain or stabilize prices for beef sold to purchasers in Canada [3][4]. The proposed class covers eligible customers who bought beef products for personal use or resale in Canada on or after Jan. 1, 2015, but excludes purchases by the food service industry, including restaurants [2][4]. Courts still must approve the settlements, and have not approved a claims-payment process or determined individual compensation amounts [2][6]. The agreements are not an admission of liability, fault or wrongdoing by JBS or National Beef. Class actions continue against Cargill Inc. and Tyson Foods Inc. [2][4][6].
