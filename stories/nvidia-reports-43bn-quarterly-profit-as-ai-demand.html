Nvidia reports $43bn quarterly profit as AI demand surges

Category: AI

Nvidia posted another record quarter, powered by booming demand for its data-center AI chips. Quarterly profit hit $43 billion, and fiscal-year profit reached $120 billion—up sharply from $4.4 billion three years ago [2]. Several outlets framed the results as fresh evidence that spending on AI infrastructure is still ramping up, with CEO Jensen Huang saying AI has reached an inflection point [5][16]. The market’s response, though, was mixed. Despite the strong numbers and upbeat guidance, Nvidia’s share price looked subdued as investors weighed how long the AI spending boom can last—and how much new capacity the industry can actually absorb [1]. For the broader AI ecosystem, the results offered a near-term boost: they helped ease fears of an AI slowdown and fed a rally in some Asian tech stocks. Still, some commentators warned that Nvidia’s story ultimately hinges on customers continuing to pour money into compute [7][3].

Key Points

  • Cash returns focus: Some coverage said investor questions about returning more cash to shareholders helped limit the market’s reaction, even after the earnings beat [35].
  • Key beats: Coverage highlighted that Nvidia beat consensus on revenue and earnings per share (EPS $1.62 vs $1.53), with data-center revenue also coming in above estimates [37].
  • Scale: The BBC said Nvidia reported record $215bn revenue, pushing back against broader worries about an AI slowdown [15].
  • Agentic AI: MarketWatch highlighted Huang’s view that “agentic AI” is a major force behind rising demand for chips [9].
  • Next chip cycle: CFO Colette Kress said Nvidia shipped its first “Vera Rubin” samples to customers, and the company emphasized better performance and energy efficiency as it looks beyond the current Blackwell generation [37][36].
  • Data center growth: CNBC said Nvidia’s data-center revenue rose 75% as the AI boom continued, with earnings and guidance coming in above expectations [12].
  • China constraint: Nvidia said it has not generated any revenue from its H200 chips in China and doesn’t know whether sales will be allowed, underscoring the uncertainty from U.S.-China tech frictions [37][38].
