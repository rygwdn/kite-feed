Alberta premier does not mention separation ahead of Oct. 19 vote

Category: Canada

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith marked Alberta Day at Calgary’s Heritage Park on Aug. 30 without mentioning separation. She said Alberta’s resources, skills and expertise are in demand and that Canada understands what the province contributes [1][2][9][12]. The address was part of provincial birthday events ahead of the Sept. 1 anniversary of Alberta entering Confederation [9][12]. Albertans are scheduled to vote Oct. 19 on 10 referendum questions, including questions on immigration and the Constitution, and one asking whether Alberta should remain in Canada or hold a second, binding referendum on leaving the country [4][2][10][12]. Smith has said she will vote for Alberta to stay in Canada and that the referendum gives voice to petitioners who supported either separation or remaining in Confederation [10][12]. On Aug. 29, hundreds attended a pro-separation rally at the Alberta Legislature, while the Alberta NDP hosted a rally urging voters to oppose separation [4][5][13][10].

Key Points

  • Turnout concern: NDP Leader Naheed Nenshi said polls showed separatist support at 17 to 20 per cent and warned that low turnout could give pro-separation voters disproportionate influence [10].
  • Conservative campaign: Former Conservative MP Monte Solberg’s Vote to Stay campaign aims to persuade 100,000 “soft separatists,” and its team includes former Alberta premier Jason Kenney [11].
  • Rally messaging: The Alberta Prosperity Project described its Edmonton event as a “gathering for a province in peril,” where attendees heard prayers and sermons in support of independence [13][5].
  • Provincial events: Alberta hosted celebrations at the legislature grounds in Edmonton and Heritage Park in Calgary, while supporting Alberta Day events in 69 municipalities [9].

Perspectives

Danielle Smith: Smith told Alberta Day attendees that “the world needs what Alberta has to offer” and spoke about the people and cultures that built the province.
