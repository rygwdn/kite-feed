Update: Israel orders attacks after Lebanon ceasefire breaches

Category: World

Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon killed six people on Saturday, according to Lebanon’s health ministry, after Israel said rockets were launched from لبنان and accused Hezbollah of violating a recently extended ceasefire [1][2][3][6][9]. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu then ordered the army to strike Hezbollah targets “vigorously” or “forcefully,” and Lebanese state media later reported more raids and shelling [2][3][4][5][9]. The Israeli military said some of those targeted were Hezbollah operatives, including people in a vehicle allegedly carrying weapons and another person on a motorcycle, while Hezbollah said it targeted an Israeli army vehicle in retaliation for one of the strikes [4][9]. The flare-up showed how fragile the truce remains, even after President Trump announced on Thursday that the 10-day ceasefire that began on April 17 had been extended by three weeks, keeping hopes for calm alive but clearly under strain [7][9].

Key Points

  • Strike locations: Lebanon’s health ministry said two strikes in Yohmor al-Shaqeef in Nabatieh district killed four people, while another airstrike on Safad al-Battikh in Bint Jbeil district killed two and wounded 17 [9].
  • Israeli account: The Israeli military said it had “eliminated” more than 15 militants in southern Lebanon earlier, and said some of the latest targets were Hezbollah operatives, including people in a vehicle allegedly carrying weapons and another person on a motorcycle [4][9].
  • Cross-border fire: Israel said it detected two projectiles launched from Lebanon and later intercepted another “suspicious aerial target,” calling the launches a breach of the ceasefire terms [6][9].

Perspectives

Israeli government: Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu told the military to attack Hezbollah targets vigorously after the army reported rocket launches from Lebanon and alleged truce violations.

Historical Background

Israel and Hezbollah fought a full-scale war in 2006 that left lasting instability along the Lebanon-Israel border [common]. Hezbollah later built up a major military presence in southern Lebanon, and cross-border exchanges escalated again during the wider regional conflict that intensified after October 2023 [common]. The current truce began on April 17, 2026, for 10 days and was extended by three weeks on April 24, 2026, after President Trump announced the extension [9][7].
