Scientists identify 66-million-year-old dinosaur adapted to eating fruit

Category: Science

Researchers identified Infernogallus jamesi, a turkey-sized, toothless dinosaur that lived about 66 million years ago and appears to have specialized in eating fruit and other soft plants. Its fossils, including a nearly complete skeleton, came from Montana and North Dakota. Its beak and jaw were adapted for mashing soft food, making it the first known dinosaur with evidence of a specialized fruit diet [1][2][3][5]. Paleontologist Greg Funston of Stony Brook University, lead author of the study published in Nature Communications, said the dinosaur's anatomy suggests a specialized feeding method. The researchers say it may have eaten berries and fleshy fruits; whether it helped spread large-seeded plants remains uncertain and requires more evidence [1][3][5].

This definitely expands the range of lifestyles we thought dinosaurs had, and it shows how adaptable theropods were

Key Points

  • Dietary flexibility: Funston said losing teeth and developing a beak can broaden an animal's food choices to include plants, provided it can digest them [1].
  • Name and discovery site: Infernogallus means “hell chicken,” a reference to its bird-like appearance and the Hell Creek formation where its fossils were found [1].
  • Shared ecosystem: Infernogallus lived on a subtropical coastal floodplain alongside Tyrannosaurus, Triceratops and Ankylosaurus [1].

Technical Details

  • Three-part feeding apparatus: The jaw-muscle arrangement powered forward-and-back chewing, a mobile joint allowed forward movement, and beak ridges and grooves helped mash and shear soft plants. Living birds and turtles have these features separately, but not all together [1][3].
  • Chewing versus gastroliths: Unlike many herbivorous theropods that ground food with swallowed stones called gastroliths, Infernogallus belonged to caenagnathids, which chewed food instead [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Cretaceous comparison: Mammals and birds probably also ate fruit, but they lacked the same specializations for a fruit-based diet; their small size also made them unlikely to spread very large seeds over long distances [1].

Historical Background

Flowering plants first appeared about 130 million to 140 million years ago and later spread across landscapes where conifers and ginkgoes had been prominent [1][3]. They introduced a new food source, which study co-author Thomas Dudgeon said had consequences for animals living alongside them [1].

Q&A

Q: What evidence remains incomplete about the dinosaur's appearance?

A: The known fossils did not preserve feathers, but researchers infer from fossils of close relatives that it was feathered [1][2][4].

Q: What fruits might it have eaten?

A: Researchers suggest berries and fleshy fruits, including fruits with central pits, such as peach- and cherry-like drupes [1][3].
