Canadian schools reopen with new rules, safety reminders

Category: Canada

Students are returning to classrooms across Canada with back-to-school changes and reminders focused on academics, health and safety. In Ontario, high school course marks will now include attendance and participation components, and most courses will require written exams as part of final grades under Ministry of Education changes introduced earlier in 2026 [1]. Food Allergy Canada says more than 600,000 children under 18 have a food allergy. Doctors are urging families and schools to prepare as students encounter new classroom environments and exposures in September [2]. Green Communities Canada is continuing School Streets pilot projects that use barricades and signs to move cars away from regular student drop-off and pickup zones, including earlier efforts at schools in Mississauga, Ont., and Edmonton [4]. Local road-safety reminders are also accompanying the return to class. Mount Pearl’s mayor has urged drivers to be patient, while police services and auto associations in several communities are promoting slower driving near schools [3].

Key Points

  • Grade weighting: Ontario students in Grades 9 and 10 will have 20% of final marks based on mandatory testing and 15% on attendance and participation, while Grades 11 and 12 students will have 25% tied to final evaluations and 10% to attendance [1].
  • Exam logistics: Ontario’s ministry says remote-learning and board-run online course exams must be written in person at a home school or another site unless a board can provide secure online proctoring [1].
  • School Streets pilots: CBC reported that volunteers redirected vehicles during drop-off and pickup periods while encouraging families to walk or cycle instead [4].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: The 'Slow It Down for School' campaign is underway in some communities, and police services, including Bracebridge OPP, are increasing patrols near schools. Drivers should slow down and watch for children at crossings, sidewalks and pickup areas [3].

Action Items

  • Check allergy plans: Parents of children with food allergies should review school procedures and make sure staff know the child’s triggers and emergency plan, after doctors warned about changing September exposures [2].
  • Confirm exam arrangements: Ontario families with online or remote high school courses should ask their school board whether exams will be written at the home school, another site or through secure online proctoring [1].
