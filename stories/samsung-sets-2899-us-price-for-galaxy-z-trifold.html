Samsung sets $2,899 US price for Galaxy Z TriFold

Category: Technology

Samsung confirmed that its Galaxy Z TriFold, a triple-folding smartphone that opens into a roughly 10-inch display, will go on sale in the US on January 30, 2026 [9][13][11][3]. The US price is $2,899, making it notably higher than other mainstream phones and even Samsung’s own foldables, and Samsung will sell a single configuration in the US with 512GB storage in black [9][3][14]. The launch highlights how quickly foldable designs keep evolving—while the price signals the technology is still in an early, premium phase that may become more accessible as manufacturing scales [9][15].

Primary Source: View original article

I kind of hate how much I like the Galaxy Z TriFold.

— Sam Rutherford (Engadget)

Did you know: The Galaxy Z TriFold is described as thinner at its thinnest point than the protrusion of its own camera bump, a quirky side effect of pushing thickness to the limit while still using large camera modules [4].

Key Points

  • Where to buy: Samsung said US sales will run through its own channels (Samsung.com and Samsung Experience Stores), with major carriers not offering the device directly, according to a report summarizing the rollout [14].
  • Hardware extremes: The TriFold is described as extremely thin at its thinnest point (about 3.9mm) and designed around two hinges that create a large internal screen when fully opened [14][19].
  • Durability claim: Samsung rates the device for roughly 200,000 folds over its lifetime, underscoring that hinge durability remains a key engineering focus for multi-fold phones [14].
  • In-box charger: Unlike many recent premium phones, the US TriFold package includes a 45W charging brick in the box, a notable accessory decision for a flagship device [18].
  • Early impressions: Hands-on and early review notes praised the expansive inner display for media use, while also flagging tradeoffs like weight, folded bulk, and software polish as areas that still need refinement [9][14].

Perspectives

Samsung (product positioning): Samsung is bringing its tri-fold model to the US market with a high-end configuration and pricing that positions it as a premium, first-generation form factor rather than a mass-market phone.

Timeline

  • Last month: Samsung unveiled the Galaxy Z TriFold and began initial regional launches [8].
  • December 12: Samsung launched the TriFold in South Korea first [14].
  • CES 2026: Engadget reported hands-on impressions and noted noticeable tradeoffs [9].
  • January 30: US sales begin online and in Samsung stores [9][11][3].

Technical Details

  • Tri-fold mechanism: A tri-fold uses two hinges so the display can fold twice, allowing a phone-sized device to open into a much larger tablet-like screen [13][19].
  • 10-inch internal display: Multiple outlets describe the fully opened TriFold as roughly a 10-inch AMOLED-class screen, aiming to blur the line between phone and small tablet [13][15].
  • Fold-cycle rating: A fold-cycle rating (here, 200,000 folds) is a durability metric for hinges and flexible displays that helps buyers estimate long-term wear under repeated opening and closing [14][common].

Industry Impact

  • Foldables competition: A US tri-fold release raises the bar for foldable experimentation and can pressure rivals to accelerate flexible-display roadmaps or differentiate with software and services [3][5].
  • Retail distribution: By keeping sales largely in-house rather than through carriers, Samsung can control the premium buying experience and messaging, but it may limit exposure compared with carrier-promoted flagships [14].
  • Accessories and charging: Including a 45W charger in the box stands out in a market where many premium phones ship without chargers, potentially nudging expectations for bundled accessories—at least at ultra-premium prices [18][common].

Historical Background

Foldables began as dual-screen designs that expanded phone screens into tablet-like canvases, but they remained niche because hinges, durability, and cost were hard problems to solve [common]. Samsung’s move to a tri-fold sold in the US suggests manufacturers are now exploring more complex folding geometries to chase “one device” computing—phone, tablet, and (with desktop modes) light laptop workflows [14][5].

Q&A

Q: Will there be other colors or storage options in the US?

A: Engadget reported that US buyers will only get a black model with 512GB storage at launch [9].

Q: How heavy is the TriFold compared with typical phones?

A: A report summarizing early reviews put the device at about 309 grams, which is heavier than many mainstream smartphones [14].

Q: How is Samsung trying to make the TriFold feel more like a computer?

A: SamMobile highlights Samsung DeX as part of the broader pitch for larger-screen foldables to act like light productivity devices when opened [5].

Action Items

  • Check if a tri-fold fits your workflow: Before buying, list your top two “tablet tasks” (reading, multitasking, video) and confirm the TriFold’s 10-inch unfolded format and DeX-style positioning match those needs [13][5].
  • Plan your purchase channel: If you want one at launch, look for availability via Samsung’s website or Samsung Experience Stores rather than carrier stores [14].
  • Budget for alternatives: If the price is hard to justify, compare the TriFold cost against buying a separate phone + tablet (and possibly a laptop), a tradeoff some coverage explicitly highlights [1][10].
