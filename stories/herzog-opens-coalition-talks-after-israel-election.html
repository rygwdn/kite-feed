Herzog opens coalition talks after Israel election leaves parliament deadlocked

Category: World

Israeli President Isaac Herzog accepted the certified results of the Oct. 27 parliamentary election and began consultations with party leaders on Nov. 4, after neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc nor opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot’s bloc won a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. Netanyahu’s bloc has 53 seats and Eisenkot’s has 48, while two Arab-led parties won a combined 19 [5][8][14]. The Central Elections Committee said its review found no irregularities that compromised the vote or changed the result. Netanyahu and his allies have alleged that Arab-led parties received foreign support but have not publicly substantiated the claims. A separate Shin Bet inquiry continues; the committee’s chairman said any suspected interference would have a limited effect, and Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara rejected a request to question Arab lawmakers without supporting evidence [2][4][10]. Herzog is consulting parties through Thursday and must choose by Nov. 11 whom to ask to form a government. The Arab party Ra’am has recommended Eisenkot, but opposition parties differ over cooperation with Arab factions. The lawmaker chosen gets 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension; failure to form a coalition could lead to another election early next year [20][8][15].

Key Points

  • Audit findings: Election officials reviewed 9,069 polling stations; the committee approved certification by a vote of 15 to two, with 14 members abstaining [14][7].
  • Coalition arithmetic: Ra’am’s eight seats could lift Eisenkot’s prospective backing to 56, but support remains uncertain given the Joint List alliance’s 11 seats and several opposition parties’ reluctance to work with Arab factions [8][25][12].
  • Shin Bet assessment: The agency described a widespread influx of substantial funds into Israel from foreign states, including hostile countries, but provided no evidence for the assertion [2][5].
  • Public cost: A new election could cost Israel more than $1 billion and prolong political gridlock, according to The Times of Israel [12].
  • Mandate rules: The president may choose the lawmaker he considers most likely to form a coalition, not necessarily the leader of the largest party [4].

Perspectives

Israeli President Isaac Herzog: Herzog urged parties to accept that they may have to set aside campaign promises to avert another election and condemned racist rhetoric aimed at Arab Israelis [3].

Historical Background

Israel held five elections between 2019 and 2022 after repeated coalition deadlocks. After the November 2022 election, Netanyahu took 58 days to form a government, despite Likud’s stronger showing then [7][26].
