Alberta urges 72-hour emergency kits as national alert test nears

Category: Canada

Alberta is urging residents to prepare emergency supplies ahead of a nationwide test of Canada’s Alert Ready system on Wednesday, Nov. 18. The test will reach television, radio and compatible wireless devices in every province and territory [1][2]. Alberta Public Safety and Emergency Services Minister Mike Ellis encouraged residents to download the provincial alert app and keep a kit with enough supplies to last at least 72 hours, including water, non-perishable food, flashlights, batteries and first-aid items [1]. The test message will identify itself as a test and say no action is required [2].

Primary Source: View original article

These alerts can be surprising, even jarring, but that’s the point.

— Mike Ellis, Alberta Minister of Public Safety and Emergency Services (Edmonton Journal)

Key Points

  • Alert volume: Alert Ready had issued more than 1,215 emergency messages across Canada in 2026, according to the system’s operator [2].
  • Phone compatibility: Wireless devices need to support public alerts, run current software and connect to an LTE network when the alert is sent or while it remains active [3].
  • Testing rules: The Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission requires broadcasters and wireless providers to issue at least one public test alert each year; tests typically occur in May and November [2].

Perspectives

Pelmorex, Alert Ready’s technical infrastructure operator, said Canadians cannot opt out of wireless alerts because the system is intended to warn them of imminent threats to life and property [2].
