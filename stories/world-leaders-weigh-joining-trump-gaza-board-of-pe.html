World leaders weigh joining Trump Gaza Board of Peace

Category: World

President Trump has proposed a new “Board of Peace” to help oversee governance and reconstruction in Gaza as the next phase of the ceasefire plan moves forward, and many countries say they have received invitations [1][8][13]. Several governments are still trying to understand how the body would operate and how it would relate to existing UN-backed frameworks, with some critics warning it could sideline the United Nations [1][13][12]. Early responses have been mixed: Morocco says it accepted an invitation to join as a founding member, while others—including France—have publicly declined, citing concerns about the board’s charter and scope [3][11][10][6].

Did you know: Singapore’s foreign ministry said it had not asked to be part of the Board of Peace but received an invitation anyway [20].

Key Points

  • Paid permanence: A U.S. official confirmed that a $1 billion contribution would secure permanent membership, while a three-year appointment would not require a contribution [19][18].
  • US leadership: The White House says the board would help implement a 20-point plan approved by the UN Security Council in November, and reported plans place senior US figures—including Secretary of State Marco Rubio, envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner—on the executive arm [5].
  • More invitations: Governments including Russia, Poland, Israel, China and Singapore have said they were invited, with several indicating they are assessing details before deciding whether to join [7][17][20][1].
  • UK engagement: UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer said Britain is discussing the initiative’s terms with allies and is prepared to support Gaza reconstruction efforts tied to the board’s second-phase work [7].
  • Davos target: President Trump has said he expects countries to sign on at the Davos economic forum in Switzerland on Thursday [5].

  • Thursday: Trump expects countries to sign on at Davos forum [5].

Technical Details

  • Board membership terms: A three-year appointment reportedly requires no contribution, while a $1 billion contribution buys a permanent seat, according to a U.S. official [19].
  • Transitional governance model: The proposal envisions the board working alongside a Palestinian technocratic administration during a transitional period, based on reported details of the plan [5].
  • Charter and scope: France said the charter extends beyond Gaza and therefore exceeds the UN-endorsed peace plan’s scope, which is central to some governments’ legal and diplomatic concerns [5][6].

Industry Impact

  • Humanitarian NGOs: Aid organizations may face parallel coordination channels if donors route decisions through the board while UN bodies continue operations, potentially increasing administrative complexity until roles are clarified [12][13].
  • Reconstruction contractors: If the board becomes a key gatekeeper for rebuilding projects, firms and donors may need to navigate a new approval structure—an uncertainty some governments cite as they review the charter and scope [1][10].
  • Diplomatic forums: With President Trump aiming for sign-ons at Davos, major economic gatherings could become venues for geopolitical buy-in as well as financial pledges tied to Gaza’s rebuilding [5].

Historical Background

Gaza has faced repeated cycles of conflict between Israel and Hamas over many years, and the territory has endured extensive damage and humanitarian strain since the latest war began in 2023 [common]. The current discussion builds on a ceasefire agreement reached last year and on a UN Security Council-approved plan that the White House says the board would help implement [5][8].

Q&A

Q: Who would hold final decision-making authority under the board’s charter?

A: France and other observers pointed to charter wording that appears to place ultimate decision-making power with President Trump, which some governments cite as a concern [10].

Q: How is Russia responding to its invitation?

A: The Kremlin said President Vladimir Putin received an invitation and that Russia is seeking to clarify “all the nuances” with Washington [7].

Q: What factors could make participation complicated for India?

A: Indian analysts note New Delhi is weighing its options and that joining could be diplomatically tricky given the board’s design and the range of invitees [16].
