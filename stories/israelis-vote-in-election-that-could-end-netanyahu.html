Israelis vote in election that could end Netanyahu’s rule

Category: World

Israelis began voting Tuesday in the first national election since Hamas’ Oct. 7, 2023, attack, choosing all 120 members of parliament as Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu faces former military chief Gadi Eisenkot and an opposition bloc seeking to replace him [7][36][13][71]. Final pre-election polls showed a close contest, with neither Netanyahu’s governing alliance nor the opposition expected to reach the 61 seats needed for a majority [9][62][75]. Netanyahu faces scrutiny over the security failures preceding the Oct. 7 attack and his leadership through three years of regional wars. Campaign issues included whether to investigate those failures, the burden of military service and Israel’s political direction. Eisenkot has pledged an independent state inquiry, while Netanyahu’s government has rejected that demand [35][15][17]. Israelis elect party lists rather than a prime minister directly. President Isaac Herzog will consult party leaders and ask the candidate he judges best placed to form a coalition to do so [36]. Reuters reported that the final tally was expected by Oct. 30 and official results on Nov. 4, after which coalition bargaining could take weeks [36].

Key Points

  • Coalition rules: Parties must win at least 3.25% of the vote to enter the Knesset; around a dozen lists were considered competitive, making small parties’ seat totals consequential [36][6].
  • Arab parties: Palestinian citizens of Israel number about 1.2 million, and Arab-led lists were projected to win 14 to 16 seats, potentially giving them a decisive role in coalition talks if other parties agree to cooperate [62][14][8].
  • First-time voters: About 330,000 young Israelis are eligible to vote for the first time, many facing compulsory military service as parties debate exemptions for ultra-Orthodox men [14][16][58].
  • Returning voters: An estimated 67,000 Israelis living abroad were expected to fly home to vote; one campaign group said their votes could amount to roughly one and a half Knesset seats [81][79].
  • West Bank stakes: Opposition parties have promised action against settler violence, but most oppose a two-state solution, and analysts say a new government may at best slow settlement-related policies [33][17].

Historical Background

Israel’s election is described as its first held on schedule since 1988, after decades in which governments failed to complete their four-year terms [62].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇸 United States: President Donald Trump endorsed Netanyahu before the vote and urged Israelis to re-elect him [9][63].
  • 🇮🇷 Iran: Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said the outcome would make no difference to Tehran and denounced the Israeli candidates [3].
