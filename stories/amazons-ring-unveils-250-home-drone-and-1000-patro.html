Amazon’s Ring unveils $250 home drone and $1,000 patrol robot

Category: Technology

Amazon-owned home-security company Ring unveiled two autonomous surveillance products on Nov. 10: the $249.99 Ring Drone Cam, an indoor flying camera for homes, and the $999.99 Ring Rover Cam, a wheeled patrol robot for businesses. The Rover is scheduled to ship in the United States on Dec. 16, 2026; the Drone is slated for Jan. 27, 2027, subject to Federal Communications Commission authorization [17][2][12]. Both devices use Ring’s AI Pilot navigation system to map indoor spaces, suggest patrol locations and navigate around obstacles. The Drone records 2K video and can respond to alerts from connected Ring devices; the Rover records up to 4K video and has an adjustable camera mast, siren and two-way audio [7][8]. The Drone’s camera switches off while docked, but the device depends on Wi-Fi and cloud processing for navigation and can fly for only five to eight minutes per charge. Ring says its deliberately audible propellers and recording indicators signal when it is operating; privacy concerns remain over mobile cameras mapping and recording indoor spaces [9][12][16].

Key Points

  • Cloud navigation: Ring shifted much of the Drone’s processing to the cloud-based AI Pilot system to reduce onboard sensors, weight and power needs; it can land safely if internet service drops, but cannot continue its patrol [9][16][5].
  • Operating limits: The Rover is limited to one floor and cannot climb stairs [30].
  • Subscription fees: Scheduled patrols and some recorded-video features require a compatible Ring Protect plan; a Ring spokesperson said plans cost $5 a month or $60 a year [2][27].
  • Commercial capacity: Ring says Rover can patrol business spaces up to 5,000 square feet [35].

Perspectives

Ring: Founder and chief inventor Jamie Siminoff said customers wanted views in places fixed cameras cannot reach and in rooms where they would not want permanent cameras installed [2][11].

Technical Details

  • Visual mapping constraints: AI Pilot builds a floor plan from the Drone’s guided setup tour or the Rover’s autonomous exploration; Ring cautions that reflective, transparent or visually featureless surfaces can disrupt navigation [12][30].
  • Drone charging: The Verge reported that each minute of flight requires about eight minutes of charging, limiting how frequently the Drone can patrol [47].
  • Encryption system: Ring says footage uses Throw Away the Key encryption; The Verge reported that encryption keys rotate every five minutes and Ring deletes its copies after 24 hours [47][23].

Industry Impact

  • Commercial security: Rover extends Ring’s business push beyond a $5,000 surveillance trailer announced in January and into a market that includes Knightscope and Cobalt AI [2].

Historical Background

Ring’s original Always Home Cam required owners to map flight paths in advance; the new Drone builds a floor plan during a guided setup tour [2]. Ring says feedback from customers of Amazon’s discontinued Astro for Business, who valued its roving-security feature, helped shape the Rover Cam [2].

Q&A

Q: Can the Drone Cam be controlled manually in flight?

A: No. Users can dispatch it on demand or set routes and patrol schedules, but it navigates autonomously and cannot be piloted directly [9][7].

Q: Will the Drone Cam be available outside the United States at launch?

A: Ring has announced U.S. launch plans; T3 reported that availability and pricing for the United Kingdom had not been announced [28].
