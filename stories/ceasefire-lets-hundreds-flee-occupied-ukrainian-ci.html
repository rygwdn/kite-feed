Ceasefire lets hundreds flee occupied Ukrainian city of Oleshky

Category: World

Hundreds of civilians evacuated from Russian-occupied Oleshky reached Ukrainian-held territory on Saturday, Oct. 31, after a local ceasefire allowed them to leave and aid to enter. U.S. First Lady Melania Trump mediated the pause, while Pope Leo XIV urged access for aid and evacuations. The ceasefire began Tuesday in the southern Kherson region and was scheduled to end Sunday evening [2][9][1]. Residents travelled by bus through occupied Crimea to Russia, then flew to Belarus and crossed into Ukraine’s Chernihiv region, where relatives and aid workers met them. Some evacuees said food supplies had run out and they had survived on garden plants. The ceasefire also allowed roads to be cleared of mines and food and medicine to enter Oleshky [7][16][15]. Ukraine and Russia have not disclosed how many people left. Melania Trump said the convoy could safely evacuate more than 750 people; an estimated 1,700 remained in Oleshky before the operation. Russia’s human rights commissioner said both sides had agreed to continue humanitarian missions. The truce was set to expire Sunday night [4][2][1].

  • Peace talks: Negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States were expected to resume talks in Abu Dhabi in early November after discussions were suspended in February [15][14].

Historical Background

Russia occupied Oleshky in 2022 [15][2]. The town was further devastated by flooding in 2023 after the destruction of the Kakhovka Dam [15].

Humanitarian Impact

The convoy’s youngest evacuee was just over one year old, and the oldest was 94. Some elderly residents were so weak they arrived on stretchers [7][4]. Before the operation, Oleshky lacked food and medicine; residents who stayed still had no gas, electricity or running water [15][1].
