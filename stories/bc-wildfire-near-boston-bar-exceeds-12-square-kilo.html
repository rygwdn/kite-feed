B.C. wildfire near Boston Bar exceeds 12 square kilometres

Category: Canada

The Brunswick Creek wildfire near Boston Bar, B.C., remained out of control after growing to more than 12 square kilometres, up from an earlier estimate of one square kilometre [1][4][5][6]. BC Wildfire Service first detected the fire on July 2 and says human activity likely sparked it [1][4][5]. Evacuation orders covered parts of the Fraser Canyon, including a Fraser Valley Regional District order for 120 properties in the North Bend area and an order for Fishblue Lake; the Boothroyd Indian Band and Boston Bar Indian Band also issued evacuation orders [1][5]. Smoke from the fire prompted air-quality warnings in the Fraser Canyon and eastern Fraser Valley, while poor visibility and steep terrain restricted aircraft use during firefighting operations [3][5][6].

Key Points

  • Air-quality warning: Metro Vancouver issued a yellow air-quality warning for Chilliwack, Agassiz and Hope, indicating a high health risk from particulate matter [5].
  • Resource request: Responders requested an incident management team as well as additional resources and aviation support for the fire [1][4].
