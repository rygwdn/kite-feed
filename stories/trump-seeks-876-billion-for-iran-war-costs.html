Trump seeks $87.6 billion for Iran war costs

Category: World

President Trump’s administration asked Congress on Wednesday to approve $87.6 billion in supplemental funding, most of it for the Iran war and expenses from Operation Epic Fury [16][7][6][35][2]. The Office of Management and Budget sent the request to House Speaker Mike Johnson one day after the Senate joined the House in passing a war powers resolution directing Trump to halt military action against Iran unless Congress authorizes it [35][20][7][53]. The package seeks about $67 billion for the Defense Department, including $21 billion for munitions and military capabilities, $17.3 billion for operational costs and $12.1 billion for classified programs [6][7][5][35]. It also includes money for American farmers, the Ebola response in Central Africa, embassy security near Iran, Energy Department work tied to Iran’s nuclear capabilities, and infrastructure projects in Washington, D.C., and New York City [12][17][16][13]. OMB Director Russ Vought wrote in his letter: “I urge the Congress to take action on these important and urgent requests as soon as possible” [3][16][23].

Key Points

  • Defense baseline: The military request would come on top of about $1 trillion appropriated last year and a $1.5 trillion defense budget Trump wants for next year [35][20][49].
  • Embassy security: The State Department would receive about $300 million for security and construction at U.S. diplomatic facilities in the Middle East and South Asia after some were attacked earlier in the war [6][7].
  • Ebola response: The request includes $500 million for Global Health Security and $800 million in international humanitarian assistance for the Central Africa Ebola response, including a quarantine facility in Kenya [12][14][51].
  • Senate risk: CBS News reported the request was likely to face Democratic opposition and could struggle in the Senate, where vulnerable Republicans would have to weigh a vote on the war before November midterm elections [7].

Industry Impact

  • Defense contractors: The Pentagon portion includes $21 billion to procure munitions, strengthen the U.S. industrial base and support critical military capabilities [12][35][14].
  • Agriculture: The package seeks $10 billion in temporary economic assistance for row and specialty crops planted in crop year 2026 and $1.1 billion for Florida farmers affected by recent storm damage [17][13].
  • Infrastructure: The request includes $500 million for restoration and construction projects around Washington, D.C., and $1 billion to help modernize Penn Station in New York City [17][13][44].
