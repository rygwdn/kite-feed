Meta removes AI-training language from business website-crawling terms

Category: Technology

Meta removed language from its commercial terms saying it could use content from websites linked to business accounts to develop, improve, research and train its products, after the provision raised concerns that it could cover publishers’ sites [1][2][8][9]. The clause had appeared in terms for businesses using Meta’s commercial products, including its advertising tools [4]. Meta said the revision clarifies that the crawling terms apply only to advertising and business products and does not change how its web crawlers operate [1][6]. The revised terms are scheduled to take effect on Oct. 30, and businesses accept them by continuing to use those products [4]. The News/Media Alliance, a U.S. trade group for publishers, said the change does not expressly rule out AI training or promise that Meta will follow websites’ crawler instructions [4][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Legal backdrop: Meta defeated a lawsuit over alleged copyright infringement last year, but other cases against AI companies, including Meta, continue [1].
  • Crawler scope: The terms described automated collection through scrapers, bots, spiders, crawlers and user agents, not only conventional web-crawler software [4][6].

Perspectives

News/Media Alliance: The publisher trade group argued that the revision limits how Meta may use publisher content, but not its ability to crawl and collect that content [4][6].

Sources: The Next Web

James Grimmelmann: The Cornell law professor warned that the original terms could force newsrooms to choose between allowing use of their reporting for AI training and risking the loss of their audiences on Meta platforms [3].
