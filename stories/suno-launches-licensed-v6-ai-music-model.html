Suno launches licensed v6 AI music model

Category: AI

Suno introduced its v6 AI music model family this week, saying partnerships with Warner Music Group, BMG and Believe supplied licensed music for the new systems and that they were not trained on the same data as earlier models [1][3][5][6][8][9][7]. The release includes three versions: the main v6 model for paying users, v6-wild for experimental outputs and v6-mini, a faster option available to all users [1][8][9]. The release follows copyright lawsuits in which record labels accused Suno of using copyrighted recordings without permission to train music-generating AI systems [1][2][3][8][9]. Suno settled with Warner Music Group last year, reached a deal with BMG last month and announced a Believe partnership covering artists and TuneCore clients who opt in [1][3][10]. Sony Music Entertainment and Universal Music Group’s lawsuit against Suno continues [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Trained from the ground up, with a new set of data that does not include the same data that our previous models were trained on.

— Jack Brody (The Verge)

Did you know: Gizmodo reported that Suno recently admitted using audio data from YouTube before rolling out the licensed v6 models [3].

Key Points

  • Model access: Digital Music News reported that free accounts can use v6-mini, but without download support or commercial rights [9].
  • Creation tools: Suno says v6 can edit part of a song based on a prompt or lyric word, use text, images or video as references, and isolate an instrument from a sample to create a new beat [1].
  • Artist opt-in: Warner’s settlement also created an arrangement allowing Warner artists and songwriters to lend music and likenesses to Suno’s platform if they choose [3].
  • Distribution path: Believe said tracks artists create with Suno’s new industry-partner model will become eligible for distribution through Believe and TuneCore [10].
  • User reaction: Digital Music News described early v6 reception as mixed, with some customers criticizing the models and others posting more positive assessments [9].

Perspectives

Suno CEO Mikey Shulman called the v6 models “faster, more expressive and higher quality” and described the launch as a model for AI and the music industry to build new product experiences together.

Sources: The Hollywood Reporter

Suno chief product officer Jack Brody said derivative works could create more revenue opportunities for stakeholders as Suno develops label-backed features around remixing and artist participation.

Sources: TechCrunch

Believe CEO Denis Ladegaillerie said the Suno partnership gives artists and labels a choice over participation, rights protection and sharing value created by AI tools.

Sources: Digital Music News

MusicRadar reported that v6 expands prompting beyond genre by letting users specify vocals, instrumentation, structure, mood, feel and non-musical references.

Sources: MusicRadar

  • August 2026: Suno struck a deal with BMG last month [1].
  • 8 September 2026: Believe announced a strategic partnership with Suno [10].
  • 9 September 2026: Suno rolled out the v6 model family [1][3].

Technical Details

  • Reference-conditioned generation: Suno says v6 can use text, images or video as references when generating tracks, broadening prompts beyond written descriptions [1].
  • Localized editing: The model can update a single lyric or part of a song without regenerating the full track, according to Digital Music News and TechCrunch [9][1].
  • Stem isolation: Suno says users can separate an instrument from a sample and use it to generate a new beat [1].

Industry Impact

  • Record labels: Warner, BMG and Believe supplied licensed music for the v6 models through partnerships with Suno [1][8][10].
  • AI music platforms: Suno plans to retire older models as v6 replaces prior systems, moving users to models built around licensed industry partnerships [1][8].

Business Angle

  • A licensed-data model may reduce copyright risk, but unresolved litigation with Sony and Universal means Suno still faces legal exposure [3].
  • Opt-in artist programs could turn labels from adversaries into suppliers, but compensation terms will shape margins [10][common].
  • If paying users prefer older models, retiring them could pressure retention even if the new system improves Suno’s legal posture [1][9].
  • TuneCore eligibility gives Suno-generated tracks a commercial route, but free v6-mini users lack downloads and commercial rights [10][9].

Action Items

  • Check commercial rights before exporting Suno tracks: If you use a free account, v6-mini lacks downloads and commercial rights, according to Digital Music News [9].
  • Review opt-in terms before contributing music: Artists and labels covered by Believe, TuneCore or Warner-related programs should examine participation, compensation and rights terms before allowing use of their music or likenesses [3][10].
