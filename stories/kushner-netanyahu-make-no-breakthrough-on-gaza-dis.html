Kushner, Netanyahu make no breakthrough on Gaza disarmament plan

Category: World

President Donald Trump’s envoy Jared Kushner ended two days of talks with Hamas and Israeli leaders without a breakthrough on Washington’s Gaza roadmap. He met Hamas political chief Khalil al-Hayya in Egypt on Aug. 16 and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in Jerusalem on Aug. 17 [6][10][44][13]. The U.S.-backed plan calls for Hamas to disarm and leave Gaza’s governance while Israeli forces withdraw and reconstruction begins under a new postwar arrangement [2][4][15]. The dispute centers on sequencing. Hamas says it accepts the roadmap but wants Israel first to halt attacks and withdraw, while Netanyahu says Israel will not pull back or allow reconstruction until Hamas fully disarms [27][44][103]. Netanyahu’s office said Israel and Trump’s Board of Peace agreed to form working groups on Gaza’s disarmament and on sanitation, clean water and public health, with demilitarization to be completed before reconstruction [1][2][62]. An Israeli official said Kushner and Netanyahu also discussed appointing an American general to oversee Hamas weapons decommissioning. Kushner told Fox News that weapons removal could begin in “as little as 30 days” and tunnel-filling could follow within 60 to 90 days [69][68][73][94]. No U.S. statement has confirmed a final Israeli commitment to the wider roadmap, and a spokesperson for Board of Peace envoy Nickolay Mladenov called it “a work in progress” [62].

Did you know: Kushner’s direct contact with Hamas was described as his second known meeting with the group, after an October 2025 meeting that helped clinch the Gaza ceasefire [37][31].

Key Points

  • Governance plan: CBS reported that Kushner’s Hamas meeting addressed transferring governing responsibilities to the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza and ensuring Hamas has no future governing role [2].
  • Security force: The U.S. roadmap envisions an international stabilization force working with a new Palestinian police force after Hamas disarms and Israeli forces withdraw [26][58].
  • Election pressure: Several reports said Netanyahu faces an October national election while his coalition trails in opinion polls, a factor diplomats linked to limited room for concessions [10][26][4].
