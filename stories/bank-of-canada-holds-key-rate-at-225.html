Bank of Canada holds key rate at 2.25%

Category: Canada

The Bank of Canada held its target for the overnight rate at 2.25% on September 2, with the Bank Rate at 2.5% and the deposit rate at 2.20%. It was the seventh consecutive decision without a rate change [3][2][7][15]. Governor Tiff Macklem said the governing council kept policy steady because recent data were largely in line with the July forecast, while policymakers weighed higher inflation risks from energy prices and uncertainty from new U.S. tariffs and Canadian countermeasures [4][7][10][12]. The central bank said Canadian GDP rose 3.3% in the second quarter after very weak first-quarter growth, with gains in consumption, housing, exports and business investment. Unemployment edged down to 6.4% in July, though indicators still pointed to excess supply in the economy [3][8]. Macklem said inflation was running at 3% and that the longer the Middle East conflict keeps oil prices elevated, the greater the risk that higher energy costs spread into other goods and services [7][15][9].

Key Points

  • Tariff scope: Washington's Section 338 tariffs took effect on August 22, imposing 50% duties on about 5% of Canadian exports, while Ottawa's counter-tariffs are scheduled to begin September 8 [5][17][16].
  • Market pricing: Before the decision, LSEG Data & Analytics figures cited by The Hub put the odds of another hold near 94% on Monday morning [5].
  • Economist forecasts: Desjardins moved up its call for two 2027 rate hikes totaling 50 basis points to the first half of next year, citing what Royce Mendes called a more hawkish policy tone [6].
  • Global backdrop: The Bank said U.S. growth remained solid on consumer spending and AI-related investment, euro-area growth exceeded expectations in the second quarter, and China's economy slowed [3][8].

Perspectives

Bank of Canada: The central bank said it would watch whether upward price pressures broaden beyond goods and services directly affected by energy costs and tariffs.

Industry Impact

  • Autos: President Trump threatened steeper levies on autos and auto parts beginning January 1, 2027, extending trade uncertainty into one of Canada's major manufacturing sectors [5].
  • Financial conditions: The Bank noted tighter financial conditions and higher long-term bond yields globally and in Canada, which could raise borrowing costs for businesses and households [3].
  • Hard-hit firms: Macklem said the Canadian government had announced new supports for businesses and workers affected by U.S. trade actions [4].

Business Angle

  • Uncertainty over trade rules could cause businesses to postpone investment and hiring, weighing on capital goods and services sectors [5][common].
  • Exporters exposed to the narrow tariff list face sector-specific pressure even if the Bank does not expect a large direct economy-wide hit [2][17].
  • Higher long-term bond yields can raise discount rates and financing costs, with the largest effects on leveraged firms and long-duration assets [3][common].

Action Items

  • Check variable-rate exposure: Review mortgages, lines of credit and floating-rate business loans against the unchanged 2.25% policy-rate setting and any lender-specific rate notices [3][common].
  • Review tariff-sensitive purchases: Businesses importing U.S. goods should check whether planned Canadian counter-tariffs taking effect September 8 apply to their inputs [5][16].
