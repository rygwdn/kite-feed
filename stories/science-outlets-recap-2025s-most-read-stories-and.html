Science outlets recap 2025’s most-read stories and records

Category: Science

Several science outlets are ringing in 2026 by looking back at what captivated readers in 2025, from approachable explainers and mysteries to headline-grabbing “superlatives” in research [1][2][3]. Popular Science says its year-end list mixes reader interest (traffic and social activity) with editorial selection, aiming to demystify everyday questions and strange phenomena [1]. Eos highlights a year that was “trying” for scientists but also featured visible public support for science and rapid-response science policy coverage, including its Research & Developments blog and an frequently updated Science Policy Tracker [2]. Science News spotlights record-setting discoveries and technical feats reported in 2025, including the longest lightning, AI-generated genomes, and a major black hole merger [3].

Key Points

  • Runes mystery: Popular Science describes an interdisciplinary effort to analyze a Canadian bedrock inscription that renders the Lord’s Prayer using more than 250 symbols from the oldest known runic alphabet, with findings made public after years of research and corroboration [1].
  • Radithor warning: One Popular Science retrospective pick revisits Radithor, a 1920s “radioactive ‘miracle water’” once marketed for ailments but later linked to deadly consequences for believers [1].
  • Go-bag shift: Popular Science notes that emergency preparedness has broadened beyond stereotypical “preppers,” arguing many people are overdue to assemble a practical go-bag [1].
  • Fire science series: Eos reports that two devastating fires in the Los Angeles area early in 2025 displaced thousands and caused millions in damage, and it profiled scientists who studied impacts on air, land, sea, and communities even while personally affected [2].
  • Odors and equity: Eos highlights reporting on air pollution, environmental racism, and how measurement and regulation challenges complicate efforts to address harmful odors [2].
