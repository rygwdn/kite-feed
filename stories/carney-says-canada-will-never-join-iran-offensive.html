Carney says Canada will never join Iran offensive

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney told the House of Commons that Canada is not involved in the U.S.-Israeli military offensive against Iran and “will never participate” [1][6][8]. After the Commons debate on the conflict, opposition parties kept pressing the government for a clearer account of Canada’s position and what comes next [5][6][2]. Ministers said their priorities include preventing Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons and pushing for de-escalation, but critics argued the government still hasn’t explained how it plans to reach those goals [5][2][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Absence fallout: Carney blamed scheduling conflicts for missing the March 9 Commons debate; Conservatives pointed to his empty seat and said he was “in hiding” [2].
  • Who defended the government: With the prime minister away, Foreign Minister Anita Anand opened the government’s response from his seat, calling Iran a “terrorist agent of disruption” while emphasizing non-proliferation [2].
  • Debate framing: The Walrus said ministers leaned hard on de-escalation during the debate, but didn’t lay out a concrete path for getting there [2].
  • Carney’s updated tone: After returning to Parliament, Carney spoke about seeking peace in Iran, according to Le Devoir [3].

Perspectives

Prime Minister Mark Carney: Carney shifted his schedule to make question period after opposition MPs criticized him for missing the previous day’s Commons debate on the Iran war, which his government had called [6].

Sources: National Observer

Defence Minister David McGuinty: In the Commons debate, McGuinty stressed de-escalation but didn’t offer a starting point or clear plan, according to The Walrus [2].

Sources: The Walrus

NDP (Don Davies): Davies called the government’s public messaging on the conflict “unprincipled, contradictory and incoherent” [7].

Sources: National Observer

Conservatives (Pierre Poilievre): Poilievre linked his criticism of the government’s Iran posture to domestic demands, including broader use of the One Canadian Economy Act, ending the industrial carbon price, and repealing the West Coast tanker ban [7].
