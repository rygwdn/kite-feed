Apple explores fitness features for future smart glasses

Category: Technology

Apple is exploring ways to turn future smart glasses and Vision headsets into health and fitness companions, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported. The features are unlikely to arrive on the first-generation glasses [1][3][4][5][6][7][8][10]. Reports describe the first model as focused on core smart-glasses functions, while later versions could use cameras and sensors to support wellness features [1][5]. The work follows an earlier Apple Fitness+ concept for Vision Pro that would have run inside the headset, shown workout classes, tracked user movement and used body-tracking analysis during exercise [3][8][10]. Apple did not release that concept after technical hurdles and the Vision Pro's weight made the headset impractical for most workouts [1][3][7][8][10]. Privacy remains a design issue because cameras on glasses sit on a user's face and can affect bystanders [1]. Apple has reportedly considered glasses without cameras, as well as camera systems that understand surroundings without taking photos or recording video [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Hiring signal: Apple's Vision Products Group is seeking a strategic product design leader to help define health, well-being and fitness experiences across vision products, with experience in digital health and sensor-driven features [1][3][9][10].
  • Workout uses: Reports describe possible functions including exercise-form monitoring, repetition counting, posture tracking and warnings when a user performs a movement incorrectly [1][5][7].
  • Interface constraint: Because the planned smart glasses are described as lacking a display, Apple would need a fitness interface different from Vision Pro's, potentially using audio prompts or links to iPhone and Apple Watch [3][8][10].
  • Existing inputs: Macerkopf said Apple could combine glasses with the heart-rate sensor already built into AirPods Pro 3 and the watchOS 26 Workout Buddy feature for training guidance [7].

Perspectives

Bloomberg's Mark Gurman: Gurman indicated that the first-generation glasses will likely focus on Siri AI and Apple Intelligence, with health and fitness features arriving in later models [9].
