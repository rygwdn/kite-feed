China confirms Trump, Putin and Xi summit on nuclear arms, Iran

Category: World

China confirmed Monday that President Xi Jinping will host U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen on Nov. 19 for their first three-way summit, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) leaders’ meeting [2][3][16]. The White House and Kremlin had already announced the meeting [11][1]. U.S. officials say Trump wants to discuss nuclear arms reductions and the war with Iran; Washington says Russia’s war in Ukraine is not on the trilateral agenda [15][3]. China’s Foreign Ministry gave no agenda details, while U.S. officials said they do not expect an agreement and described the talks as an opening conversation [2][15]. The discussions follow the February expiration of New START, the last major U.S.-Russia nuclear arms-control treaty, which set binding limits on strategic arsenals [18][14]. China has long argued that Washington and Moscow should cut their much larger arsenals before Beijing joins nuclear-reduction talks [3]. The White House schedule lists the meeting at Kylin Villa resort for 2:30 p.m. local time on Nov. 19; Trump is also scheduled to have lunch with Xi [11][17].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Arsenal disparity: In a commentary, Senior Colonel Zhou Bo cited an estimate that China could have 600 warheads by the end of 2025, while the U.S. and Russian arsenals together account for almost 90% of the world total [12].
  • Trade truce: The U.S.-China trade truce has been extended until January 2027 [1][32].
  • No separate Trump-Putin meeting: The Kremlin says no one-on-one meeting between Trump and Putin has been agreed, despite the leaders’ planned joint appearance with Xi [1][17].
  • European involvement: Russia-Ukraine negotiations are resuming under U.S. mediation, with representatives from France, Germany, Britain, Poland, Italy and the European Union set to join [1].

Perspectives

White House: Spokeswoman Olivia Wales said Trump considers nuclear proliferation dangerous [15].

Sources: KRDO (CNN)

Chinese expert: Qian Feng, director of the Research Department at Tsinghua University’s National Strategy Institute, said the summit’s main value could be opening a crisis-communication channel among the three powers to preserve space for diplomacy and reduce the risk of strategic miscalculations [13].

Sources: TASS

Ukraine: President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned in October that the three-power meeting could lead to decisions on Ukraine’s future without Kyiv, comparing that risk to a new Yalta-style division of influence [15][14].
