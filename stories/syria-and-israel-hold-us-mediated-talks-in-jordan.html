Syria and Israel hold U.S.-mediated talks in Jordan

Category: World

Syria and Israel held high-level, U.S.-mediated talks in Jordan on Sunday. Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani met an Israeli delegation that sources identified as led by Mossad chief Roman Gofman [7][13][14][9][2]. Syria’s state news agency SANA said the discussions focused on mechanisms to stop Israeli strikes, arrests and targeted operations in Syria, and on reviving negotiations toward a security agreement that could support a wider accord [5][10][1][68]. The meeting followed Israel’s strike on the Abu al-Duhur airbase in northern Syria, which Israel said was aimed at preventing a Turkish military deployment; Turkey denied having a military presence at the base [6][17][22][23]. Damascus also said it wanted to keep Syria out of tensions between Israel and Turkey, and Syrian officials repeated demands for Israeli forces to withdraw to positions held before December 8, 2024, and for full restoration of the 1974 disengagement agreement [13][15][16][50].

Key Points

  • Draft framework: Shaibani said the sides had agreed on paper to “almost everything” earlier this year, including security zones in southern Syria and a buffer zone staffed only by United Nations forces [5][19][68].
  • Israeli limits: Kan reported that Israel set red lines against Turkish air defense systems, drones or missiles in Syria that could restrict Israeli air force operations [6][63].
  • Coordination proposal: Sources cited by AFP said the sides discussed a U.S.-supervised “special operations room” in Jordan to prevent confrontation in Syria, including any involving Turkey [13][22][23][39].

Perspectives

Syrian government: Shaibani said Syria would continue relying on Turkish military training and cooperation, adding, “We will not dispense with this support.”
