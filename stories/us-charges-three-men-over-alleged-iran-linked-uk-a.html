US charges three men over alleged Iran-linked UK airbase plot

Category: World

The U.S. Justice Department charged three men on Nov. 10 over an alleged plot to attack RAF Fairford, a British air base used by U.S. forces, and their alleged involvement in other attacks. The defendants are Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi, a dual Iranian-Iraqi national; Mohsen Mohajeri, an Iranian national; and Mohammad Ali al-Hassani, an Iraqi national. Prosecutors allege they acted in support of Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and the Iraqi armed group Kata'ib Hezbollah [8][10][7]. The eight-count indictment says al-Hassani organized British associates, arranged for vans carrying fuel and made other preparations, while al-Saadi sent coded messages through relatives from a New York jail. Prosecutors allege the vans were meant to explode at the base; British police found no explosives but seized fuel. Only al-Saadi is in U.S. custody; prosecutors have not disclosed the other two men’s whereabouts [8][10][5]. The indictment also links the defendants to nearly 20 alleged attacks or attempted attacks against Jewish communities and other targets in Europe and North America. The U.S. withdrew its B-1 bombers from Fairford after the September incident; Iran has denied involvement [2][7][39].

Key Points

  • Vehicle preparations: Prosecutors say associates researched chemical accelerants, including potassium chloride, and made decals to disguise vans as fuel-delivery or swimming-pool-supply vehicles; at least two vans encountered a security roadblock [10][8].
  • Attack campaign: The Justice Department said a group calling itself Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiya claimed 18 attacks in Europe in spring 2026, seven of them in the United Kingdom [10].
  • London incidents: The indictment alleges the defendants watched a firebombing of a Jewish charity in London over a video call and directed a separate attack on a synagogue the following day [15][38].
  • British prosecutions: Two British men were charged in October in separate national-security cases: one with assisting a foreign intelligence service believed to be Iran’s, and the other with entering a prohibited site [13][1].
  • Second base: Flight-tracking data showed at least a dozen U.S. aircraft left RAF Mildenhall after a threat that The Wall Street Journal linked to Iran [1][2].

Perspectives

Iranian government: Iran has denied involvement in the Fairford incident; its embassy in London rejected claims linking Tehran to it as baseless and malicious speculation.
