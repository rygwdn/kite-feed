Alberta premier says she had no knowledge of separatists’ U.S. meetings

Category: Canada

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith’s office says she had no knowledge of reported meetings between separatist figures and U.S. Republican strategists and opposes Alberta separating from Canada or becoming a U.S. state [1][6][7]. The statement followed reporting that strategists met with Alberta separatists in Calgary to discuss software designed to identify and mobilize supporters, and that separatist representatives met with U.S. officials in Washington in 2025 [1][5]. Public Interest Alberta called on Smith to seek an Elections Alberta commissioner investigation into possible foreign interference in the campaign [5]. The report said separatist representatives offered U.S. officials access to fresh water and oil royalties in exchange for help with a transition to independence. Participants disputed that account, with Mitch Sylvestre denying that water or oil was discussed [1][8][9]. Albertans are scheduled to vote Oct. 19 on whether to remain in Canada or proceed to a second, binding separation referendum [1].

We have no authority to do any of that

— Mitch Sylvestre (Global News)

Key Points

  • Premier’s response: Smith’s office said provincial security officials monitor internal and external threats with national-security partners and refer concerns to the RCMP; two ministers were assigned to monitor influence campaigns [5].
  • Calgary meeting: Sylvestre said he was uncomfortable with the proposed software and objected to using the Alberta Prosperity Project’s membership list, citing legal and privacy concerns [1].
  • Public Interest Alberta’s allegation: Executive director Bradley Lafortune said Albertans with clear, proven ties to the UCP appeared to have colluded with American operatives [5].
  • Law proposal: Liberal MP Corey Hogan called for Canada to consider changing its laws to address unauthorized negotiations with foreign governments [2][8].

Perspectives

Alberta Premier’s office: Smith’s spokesperson Sam Blackett declined to say what, if anything, the premier would do about Sylvestre’s attendance at the Calgary meeting [1].

Mitch Sylvestre and Jeff Rath: Sylvestre said he attended only one of the three Washington meetings and doubted they were useful because Alberta was far from seceding; Rath, who attended all three, called the report’s account false [1].

Historical Background

Smith said in March 2026 that the RCMP did not brief her government on foreign-interference issues and that she wanted intelligence from other sources [8][9]. Her press secretary said on May 7, 2026, that Smith had received top-secret security clearance for national-security briefings [8][9].
