Trump expands Board of Peace as more nations join

Category: World

President Trump’s “Board of Peace,” initially presented as part of a Gaza ceasefire and postwar rebuilding framework, is being broadened into a wider diplomatic forum as his administration invites scores of countries to participate [1][5][4]. On Jan. 21, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Israel would join, even after earlier objections about the board’s Gaza-related executive committee [12][13][19]. Several Muslim-majority countries, including Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Qatar and the UAE, also announced decisions to sign on, framing their participation as support for implementing the Gaza plan and sustaining a permanent ceasefire and reconstruction effort [20][22][23]. Some European governments have responded cautiously or said they will not participate, and diplomats and critics warn the initiative could undercut the United Nations’ role in conflict resolution [3][4][2].

Prime Minister Netanyahu announced that he has accepted US President Donald Trump’s invitation and will join as a member of the Board of Peace, which will be composed of leaders of the world

Did you know: Reporting says invitations have gone to dozens of countries, with responses ranging from acceptance to outright rejection depending on national interests and views of the U.N. system [2][3].

Key Points

  • Membership mechanics: A charter described by AFP indicates members may pay up to $1 billion for a permanent seat, and the board would be chaired by President Trump, who would also “separately serve” as the U.S. representative [14].
  • Gaza executive group: President Trump also proposed a “Gaza Executive Board” operating under the wider body, with names reported to include Turkey’s foreign minister Hakan Fidan and Qatari diplomat Ali Al-Thawadi—an arrangement Netanyahu has objected to [14].
  • Cautious allies: Public reaction has been tentative in several capitals, with NPR reporting apprehension among some invitees and noting that a number of European countries have declined to join so far [3][12].
  • Unexpected yeses: Reuters reporting cited by multiple outlets says some states with long-strained ties to Washington, including Belarus, have accepted invitations, alongside U.S. partners such as Morocco [2][6][13].
  • Davos spotlight: President Trump is expected to discuss the initiative further at the World Economic Forum in Davos, where participating leaders and details of the expanded concept may be clarified [1].

Perspectives

Trump administration: The board began as a Gaza-focused mechanism but is now being pitched as a broader forum to broker conflicts beyond the Middle East [1][5].

Technical Details

  • Board charter: A charter seen by AFP outlines broad aims—stability, governance and “enduring peace”—and does not appear to limit the board’s role to Gaza [14].
  • Accession documents: A joint statement said participating states will complete joining through country-specific legal procedures by signing accession documents [22][20].
  • Gaza Executive Board: The initiative includes a proposed sub-body to oversee Gaza-related implementation, reported to include Turkish and Qatari representatives [14].

Industry Impact

  • Multilateral diplomacy: Diplomats cited in Reuters-based reporting warn the board could complicate U.N.-led conflict-mediation channels by creating a parallel venue for negotiations [2][6].
  • Aid and reconstruction governance: Because the board’s origins are tied to Gaza reconstruction oversight, participation and governance design could shape how reconstruction coordination is organized among regional states [5][20].
  • Global convening: Trump’s plan to discuss the initiative at the World Economic Forum suggests the board may become a recurring venue for leader-level diplomacy outside traditional institutions [1].

Historical Background

The board concept emerged from a Gaza-focused framework tied to a ceasefire plan and postwar governance and reconstruction discussions following the Israel-Hamas war that began after the Oct. 2023 Hamas attack on Israel [14][5]. The U.N. Security Council has remained a central venue for international conflict diplomacy, and some commentators now question whether a Trump-led forum could overlap with that role [4][2].

Q&A

Q: What legal authority would the Board of Peace have compared with the U.N. Security Council?

A: The articles describe it as a Trump-led initiative that critics say could rival or undermine U.N. work, but they do not report any treaty-based enforcement powers like the U.N. Security Council [4][2].

Q: How would the proposed Gaza Executive Board be staffed and selected?

A: Sources report proposed names for the Gaza Executive Board, including Turkey’s foreign minister and a Qatari diplomat, but do not provide a full staffing or selection process [14].

Q: Which countries have explicitly rejected joining so far?

A: Coverage notes that some U.S. allies have declined and specifically reports France signaling it will not join; other rejections are mentioned generally without a comprehensive public list in these articles [12][14][2].
