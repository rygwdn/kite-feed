Canada and China strike tariff deal on canola, EVs

Category: Canada

Canada and China reached a trade deal after Prime Minister Mark Carney met Chinese President Xi Jinping in Beijing, aiming to ease a tariff dispute that had strained key exports and EV trade [3][1]. Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe said the agreement is especially important as producers finalize 2026 crop seeding plans, and he credited Carney’s role in securing the outcome [1]. Federal Agriculture Minister Heath MacDonald said China is moving quickly to resume importing Canadian canola seed and beef shipments following the tariff-reduction agreement [2]. For Canadians, the development signals near-term export momentum for farm and food products alongside a clearer, if still debated, path for Chinese-made electric vehicles entering the Canadian market [3].

Key Points

  • Prairie timing: Moe framed the tariff agreement as time-sensitive for Saskatchewan’s export-oriented producers because it arrives while 2026 seeding decisions are being locked in [1].
  • Shipments resuming: MacDonald said canola seed and beef shipments to China should start moving again soon, describing Beijing as acting quickly after the deal [2].
  • EV trade-off: The Globe and Mail described the agreement as a truce in a painful Canada-China trade war that covers both canola and electric vehicles, linking farm access with vehicle-market terms [3].
  • Domestic politics: A Google News roundup highlights political pushback inside Canada, including Ontario Premier Doug Ford urging a “boycott” of Chinese EVs even as Moe publicly praised Carney’s negotiating role [4].
