Moon hides Jupiter during Sept. 8 skywatching event

Category: Science

The moon will pass in front of Jupiter on Sept. 8 in a daytime lunar occultation, briefly hiding the giant planet from observers where the event is visible [1]. Before dawn the same day, skywatchers can see the waning crescent moon close to Jupiter in the eastern sky; EarthSky said viewers should look as dawn begins to brighten [2][4]. The Guardian’s sky chart showed the moon and Jupiter low in the east from London at 5 a.m. Tuesday, with Mars farther away and identifiable by its reddish color [2]. The moon was expected to pass less than a degree north of Jupiter at 7:11 p.m. UK time, after both objects had set for UK observers [2]. EarthSky said the moon would continue waning toward new moon at 3:27 UTC on Sept. 11 [4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Viewing geometry: The Guardian said the moon appears about half a degree wide, so a separation of less than one degree would put Jupiter within roughly two lunar diameters [2].
  • Crescent phase: The lunar surface was about 10% illuminated before dawn on Sept. 8, making the crescent easy to identify near bright Jupiter [2].

Perspectives

Space.com: Space.com described the main event as a rare daytime occultation and advised readers to observe the moon-Jupiter pairing before the moon hides Jupiter from view.
