Google expands enterprise AI agents across Workspace and Chrome

Category: AI

At Cloud Next on April 22, Google rolled out a broader enterprise AI strategy built around Gemini-powered features across Workspace and Chrome, while partners including Atlassian and groundcover announced related Google Cloud integrations [4][8][9][1][2]. Taken together, the announcements show Google pitching enterprise AI less as a single chatbot and more as a coordinated system for multi-step work, drawing on shared context from emails, chats, files, meetings and business apps [4][6][7]. The immediate significance is practical: Google wants Gemini to become a workplace layer inside tools employees already use, from Gmail and Docs to Chrome, while also expanding enterprise tooling for agents and automation [8][9][10][5]. The updates also show how competition in enterprise AI is shifting toward platforms, observability, governance and partner ecosystems [2][1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Chrome automation: TechCrunch reported that Google is adding Gemini-powered 'auto browse' features to Chrome Enterprise, letting workers automate tasks such as research and data entry inside the browser [9][6].
  • Partner rollout: Atlassian said it is expanding its Google Cloud partnership to power agentic AI for teams, while groundcover announced AI observability support for agentic workflows in Google Cloud [1][2].

Perspectives

TechCrunch: TechCrunch said Google's new agent-building approach is aimed more at IT and technical users than at a purely no-code audience.

Sources: TechCrunch

Atlassian: Atlassian described its expanded partnership with Google Cloud as a way to bring agentic AI capabilities to teams worldwide through its enterprise software footprint.

groundcover: groundcover cast observability as a necessary layer for agentic workflows running in Google Cloud, underscoring that enterprises want monitoring alongside deployment.

Technical Details

  • Semantic context layer: Workspace Intelligence is described as a semantic layer that maps emails, chats, files, collaborators and active projects into shared context, so Gemini agents can retrieve and act on relevant information across Workspace in real time [4].

Industry Impact

  • Productivity software: Google is pushing Workspace beyond drafting assistance toward task execution across Gmail, Docs, Slides, Sheets, Drive and Chat, putting more pressure on enterprise productivity vendors to offer deeper workflow automation [4][8].
  • Cloud platforms: The addition of observability and related operational tooling shows that enterprise AI competition is expanding from model quality to governance and monitoring on cloud platforms [2][5].
  • Software partners: Google highlighted partner activity involving vendors such as Atlassian and groundcover, signaling that ecosystems are becoming a core part of enterprise agent adoption [1][2].

Historical Background

Google introduced Vertex AI in 2021 as its managed machine-learning platform for Google Cloud [common]. Google Workspace grew out of G Suite, which Google renamed in 2020 as it pushed a more tightly integrated productivity suite for business users [common]. The current announcements continue that broader shift by embedding AI more deeply into workplace software such as Workspace and Chrome [4][8][9].

Action Items

  • Review your organization's Workspace permissions: Check which emails, chats, files and project spaces Gemini agents could access before enabling shared-context features, since Workspace Intelligence is designed to pull context across those systems [4].
  • Pilot browser automation on low-risk tasks: Test Chrome's Gemini-powered automation on repetitive work such as research or data entry with a limited group before a broader rollout [9].
  • Map your existing AI tools to governance needs: Compare your current internal tools with Google's enterprise AI controls and monitoring options to see whether consolidation would reduce operational complexity [2][common].
