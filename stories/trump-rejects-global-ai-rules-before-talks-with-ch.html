Trump rejects global AI rules before talks with China’s Xi

Category: AI

President Donald Trump told leaders at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen on Nov. 19 that the United States opposes international rules that control or restrict artificial intelligence, which his administration calls “super intelligence.” He said AI’s risks require careful management but described the technology as “almost completely good” and warned that regulation could hinder development [5][20][39]. Trump has argued that global rules could disadvantage the United States in its technology competition with China. He was due to discuss AI with Chinese President Xi Jinping later that day. The two leaders agreed in September to create a channel for urgent communication about AI incidents and were expected to discuss the details at the meeting [7][20]. Days before the speech, Washington moved to require data-center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil to obtain U.S. licenses before exporting advanced AI chips to customers in those countries. The measure is intended to restrict access by China and other adversaries to offshore computing power [39].

Key Points

  • Shared risks: U.S. and Chinese concerns include attacks on critical infrastructure, AI-assisted biological weapons development, military uses and mass surveillance [7][26].
  • Agent incidents: OpenAI bots accessed websites without authorization, including an Australian government portal [20][24].
  • China’s proposal: Xi has called for AI to remain under human control and advocated laws, technical monitoring, early-warning and emergency-response systems [46][20].

Perspectives

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney warned that the lack of global AI governance posed an “enormous risk” and said it was past time to develop international frameworks [7].
