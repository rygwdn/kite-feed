Colombia quake rescuers search rubble as toll rises

Category: World

Rescuers, soldiers and volunteers searched collapsed buildings across western Colombia after a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck at 7:34 a.m. local time on Monday, Aug. 10, shaking Cali, Pereira, Manizales, Quibdó and communities near the epicenter around San José del Palmar in Chocó [12][22][52][46]. Colombian authorities lowered the death toll to 216 after local officials in Cali corrected earlier figures, while separate city-level counts cited by Reuters and others put the toll at 254, with Pereira and Cali among the worst-hit cities [3][27][16][2][26][60]. Search teams worked through the night using cranes, excavators, dogs, drones and hand-to-hand debris lines as families waited beside damaged apartment blocks for news of missing relatives [20][26][24][39]. President Abelardo de la Espriella said at least 2,595 people were injured and at least 195 remained missing, while civilian-run databases suggested the missing figure could be closer to 4,000 [21][24][49]. Crews pulled survivors from rubble on Tuesday, including 32-year-old Daniela Largo, whose mother said a local resident heard cries beneath the debris before neighbors and rescue crews reached her [3][11][62]. De la Espriella declared a state of emergency and announced economic support for affected areas as rescue operations continued in cities and rural zones where communications and access remained limited [12][26][43][68].

Did you know: Colombia’s two largest cities, Bogotá and Medellín, were spared the worst damage even though the quake was felt across hundreds of miles [22].

Key Points

  • Damaged housing: De la Espriella said more than 1,100 homes were destroyed and more than 8,000 were damaged, while UN-OCHA estimated about 5,000 damaged homes [12][26][43].
  • Hospital strain: In Cali, upper floors of a hospital, including pediatric areas, collapsed, forcing about 600 patients to receive treatment on a rubble-strewn street, hospital director Irne Torres Castro told Caracol television [34][36][47].
  • Aftershock risk: Nearly 100 aftershocks had been recorded by Tuesday morning, and many residents in Pereira and Cali slept outdoors because their homes were damaged or they feared further collapses [26][12][42].
  • Rural access: Indigenous communities in forested Chocó near the epicenter remained without power or basic services, complicating damage assessments and rescue work in areas reachable mainly by boat, jungle routes or aircraft [16][60][40][68].
  • Public order: Authorities imposed a nighttime curfew in Cali to prevent looting as emergency teams searched collapsed buildings in the city [22].

Historical Background

An earthquake in Colombia’s coffee-growing region killed almost 1,200 people in 1999, a disaster cited in local coverage of the latest quake’s impact on Pereira and surrounding cities [61]. Officials and aid workers said poverty, limited transport links and insecurity from armed groups in Chocó complicated damage assessments after Monday’s quake [40][68][57].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇸 United States: The U.S. allocated $15.5 million for emergency shelter, food, protection and damage assessments after the earthquake [26].
  • Inter-American Development Bank Group: The group activated $300 million in emergency financing for Colombia, according to a live update item carried by La Sexta [50].
  • 🇲🇽 Mexico: Mexico handled three consular protection cases and assisted a youth football team affected in Colombia, according to La Sexta’s live updates [50].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Aftershocks continue to pose a risk; avoid damaged buildings and sleep away from unstable structures if local authorities advise evacuation [26][12].
  • Transport: Pereira’s international airport remained closed, while roads and access routes in hard-hit areas were damaged or still difficult to assess [50][24]. Check airport and road status before travel to western Colombia.
  • Health: Medical services in Cali and nearby cities are strained; some hospitals have been damaged and may have reduced capacity [34][36].

Action Items

  • Donate through established relief channels: Use recognized disaster-relief organizations or official appeals that specify Colombia earthquake response, and verify the recipient before sending money [49].
  • Check on relatives through official and citizen lists: Families have posted missing-person details on citizen-run databases, but official figures differ sharply, so cross-check names with local authorities when possible [21][17].
