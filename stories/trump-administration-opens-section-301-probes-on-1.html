Trump administration opens Section 301 probes on 16 partners

Category: World

The Trump administration has opened new Section 301 investigations into what it calls “unfair trade practices” by 16 major U.S. trading partners, including the European Union, China, India, Japan and Singapore. The probes start a process that could eventually lead to new tariffs [1][3][5][8][9][10]. The move comes after a U.S. Supreme Court decision in February struck down President Trump’s earlier tariff approach. The administration is now looking for other legal routes to reapply trade pressure [1][2][3][5][9][10]. Several outlets say the administration is pitching the probes as a response to unfair foreign practices. Critics and analysts warn they could raise trade tensions and, if tariffs follow, increase costs for businesses and consumers [5][9][10][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • What the probes cover: The new Section 301 cases are described as manufacturing-focused investigations into foreign practices. The U.S. Trade Representative has launched them as the first step toward possible trade remedies [4][10][9].
  • Who’s under review: Reports name the EU and several major Asian economies — including China, India, Japan and Singapore — among the partners targeted in the new inquiries [4][8][3][1].
  • India exposure: Indian coverage says the probe could set the stage for new U.S. tariffs on India, depending on how the investigation unfolds [7][3].
  • Revenue rationale: Several outlets report the administration has pointed to replacing “hundreds of billions of dollars” in revenue it says was lost after the court ruling, as it searches for new tariff authority [3][4][10].

Perspectives

Trump administration: The White House says it is launching manufacturing-focused investigations into foreign practices, framing Section 301 as a way to rebuild U.S. tariff leverage.
