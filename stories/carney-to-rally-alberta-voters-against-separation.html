Carney to rally Alberta voters against separation before Oct. 19 vote

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney will attend “Forever Canadian” rallies in Calgary on Oct. 15 and Edmonton on Oct. 16, days before Albertans vote on Oct. 19 [2][4]. The ballot asks whether Alberta should remain in Canada or begin the process toward a later, binding separation referendum; it also includes nine questions on constitutional and immigration issues [2][4]. In his Thanksgiving message, recorded at his former Edmonton high school, Carney urged unity and argued that division would hinder projects including a proposed pipeline to the West Coast [4][5]. The campaign says it wants voters to keep Alberta in Canada. The Oct. 19 vote will gauge support for taking the first step toward a separate binding vote, not decide independence itself [4][5].

Key Points

  • Poll support: A poll cited by TV2 put support for Alberta independence at about 22%, though the article noted polls can be wrong [7].
  • Policy steps: Carney’s government scrapped the consumer carbon tax and designated the proposed Pacific Link pipeline a project of national interest on Oct. 1 [5].
  • Other speeches: Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre is expected to speak in Calgary on Oct. 16, with Alberta Premier Danielle Smith also scheduled to address the event [2][4].
  • Energy investment: Business Council of Alberta policy director Alicia Planincic said planned energy-sector investment for 2026 is 1.7% below the prior year [5].

Perspectives

Thomas Lukaszuk, Forever Canadian founder: Lukaszuk said Albertans want to hear from Carney and argued that cooperation and negotiation, not separatism, can address outstanding grievances [2].

Historical Background

A judge on Alberta’s Court of King’s Bench blocked a previously planned non-binding separation vote, ruling that consultation with Indigenous Peoples and the Crown was inadequate [5]. The Hub reported that the separatist push followed the Liberals’ April 2025 election victory and drew on grievances over equalization payments, federal review legislation and limited pipeline access [5].
