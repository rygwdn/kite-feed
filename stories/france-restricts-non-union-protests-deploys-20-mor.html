France restricts non-union protests, deploys 20% more police Saturday

Category: World

President Emmanuel Macron said France would ban demonstrations organized by groups he called “illegitimate” but allow union marches subject to route changes. Authorities are preparing for Saturday’s protests over living costs and public services [20][15]. The restrictions follow demonstrations that began over school conditions and escalated into clashes, looting and damage to public buildings on Oct. 17 [4][3]. Local authorities have barred all demonstrations in Lyon and Marseille this weekend, while Paris will allow a march from Bastille in the east but prohibit it from entering the city center [10][14]. A Paris court overturned a police ban on a student rally on Thursday, allowing thousands to gather. The demonstration remained stationary and passed without major incidents [12][1]. The Interior Ministry said Saturday’s police deployment would be 20% larger than last weekend’s, with additional military and specialist police support planned [15][3]. Macron was due to chair a defense council meeting on Friday, Oct. 23, to decide on further measures. The next nationwide demonstrations are planned for Saturday, Oct. 24 [10][15].

Key Points

  • Court challenge: A Paris administrative court overturned the police ban on Thursday’s student demonstration; student leader Coppelia Moreau said restrictions risk dividing protesters into “good” and “bad” groups and undermining the right to demonstrate [3][12].
  • Rapid prosecutions: Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin said 217 of about 2,700 people arrested had been jailed and roughly 1,000 were being prosecuted; defense lawyers criticized rushed case files and pressure for harsher sentences [10][18].
  • Police weapons: Two teenagers suffered life-changing injuries during the protests, including a 17-year-old girl who lost an eye and a 17-year-old boy who lost a hand; researchers and rights groups questioned the use of foam-round launchers and stun grenades [13].
  • Business disruption: The Louvre, Orsay and Picasso museums in Paris are due to close Saturday, while Marseille plans to suspend public transport and Art Basel Paris said it would close two days early [14][12][3].
  • Borrowing costs: France’s 10-year government bond yield reached 5.14% on Thursday, its highest level since July 2002, as investors weighed unrest alongside pressure on public finances [37][44].

Coppelia Moreau, university student leader: Moreau said the attempted Paris ban and other restrictions amount to a government effort to prevent demonstrations and violate the right to protest [3].

Historical Background

The current movement began with student demonstrations over school conditions and expanded to include complaints about living costs and public services [4]. The Yellow Vest movement, whose demonstrations over fuel prices shook France in 2018 and 2019, is among the groups planning to march this weekend [14][10]. The right to protest is constitutionally protected, but French authorities may ban demonstrations they consider a serious threat to public order. Organizers must notify authorities in advance [32].
