US jury convicts Rushdie attacker on terrorism charges

Category: World

A federal jury in Buffalo, New York, convicted Hadi Matar on Wednesday of terrorism charges tied to the August 2022 stabbing of author Salman Rushdie at the Chautauqua Institution in western New York [5][12][33]. The jury found Matar, 28, guilty on all counts, including engaging in an act of transnational terrorism and attempting to provide material support to Hezbollah, a Lebanese group designated as a terrorist organization by the United States [5][33][24]. Matar is already serving a 25-year New York state prison sentence for attempted murder in the same attack, which left Rushdie blind in his right eye after being stabbed or slashed 15 times as he prepared to speak on writers’ safety [5][12][20]. The federal conviction carries a possible life sentence, with sentencing scheduled for November 3 [2][33][27]. Federal prosecutors argued Matar acted in response to the 1989 call by Iran’s late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini for Rushdie’s death over “The Satanic Verses,” and linked the case to a 2006 speech by Hezbollah leader Hassan Nasrallah endorsing enforcement of the fatwa [5][12][24]. Defense lawyer Nathaniel Barone argued that the government had no proof of what was in Matar’s mind, and Matar did not testify [12][15].

Historical Background

Rushdie’s novel “The Satanic Verses” was published in 1988, and Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini issued a fatwa calling for Rushdie’s death in 1989 [12][24]. Rushdie lived under heavy protection for years after the threat, and Iran’s government later distanced itself from the fatwa in 1998 under President Mohammad Khatami [60].
