Justice Department backs OpenAI in Times copyright case

Category: AI

The Justice Department filed a statement of interest in Manhattan federal court supporting OpenAI and Microsoft in The New York Times' copyright lawsuit over the use of news articles to train large language models [4][5][24][30]. The department urged the court to reject the argument that training large language models on copyrighted text violates copyright law, saying the United States has a strong interest in developing a competitive AI industry and retaining global leadership in artificial intelligence [8][15][10][18]. The Times sued OpenAI and Microsoft in 2023, accusing them of using millions of newspaper articles without permission to train the systems behind ChatGPT and alleging that the chatbot can reproduce long portions of Times articles verbatim [7][11][15][14]. The Justice Department argued that AI training is protected by fair use because it is highly transformative, and it tied the issue to scientific research, economic mobility and national security [11][28][18][37]. The filing does not make the United States a party to the case and carries advisory rather than binding legal weight [18][21][15][34]. It appears to be the federal government's first formal position in a wave of AI-training copyright cases brought by authors, publishers, music labels and news organizations against AI developers including OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta [7][21][15][18].

  • Licensing debate: The Justice Department argued that mandatory content payments could limit competition by leaving only well-funded technology companies able to afford large-scale training data licenses [24][37].
  • Court filing: The government submitted a 20-page statement of interest, a mechanism federal agencies use to state their position in public-interest litigation without joining the case [8][18][38].
  • Competition argument: The Justice Department argued that requiring payment for training data could limit competition by leaving only well-capitalized technology companies able to build large language models [24][37].
  • Publisher claims: Court records show the Times case has been merged with related claims from several other newspapers against OpenAI and Microsoft [18][33].
