New research links fantasy, saliva and love

Category: Science

Recent science coverage suggests romance sits at the intersection of how we think and how our bodies respond—especially when it comes to kissing and passionate love. One study covered by PsyPost, published in Sexual and Relationship Therapy, found that people who engage more often in sexual fantasy tend to place greater importance on kissing for physical arousal and contact, linking cognitive tendencies to what people value in a romantic kiss [1]. In related reporting, a large survey of U.S. singles found that how often people experience passionate romantic love varies widely, and that it can show up in different patterns rather than a single, universal experience [2]. Another Valentine’s-Day-adjacent piece looked at the biology of kissing, noting that exchanging saliva could play a role in attraction or bonding—possibly through chemical cues and/or microbial exchange—though the mechanism remains unsettled [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Why test fantasy? The PsyPost-covered paper notes that earlier efforts to support the “arousal hypothesis” for kissing often found only weak links, prompting researchers to look at other factors, including sexual fantasy [1].
  • More than one purpose: The kissing research cited in the PsyPost piece describes romantic kissing as potentially serving mate assessment, pair bonding, and/or sexual arousal—several testable explanations rather than a single all-purpose one [1].
  • A key individual difference: The PsyPost report highlights that people who more readily imagine intimate scenarios also tend to value the arousal and physical-contact side of kissing, which may help explain why kissing feels more sexually catalytic for some than for others [1].

Perspectives

Study authors (Sexual and Relationship Therapy): They argued that kissing may not feel strongly arousing on its own for everyone, and that sexual fantasy could help explain why it acts as a bigger catalyst for some people than for others.

Scientific Significance

  • Scientific meaning: Taken together, the coverage shows how the same behavior—kissing—can be viewed through multiple lenses: proposed evolutionary functions (mate assessment, bonding, arousal), individual psychology (sexual fantasy shaping what someone values in a kiss), and possible biological pathways discussed in popular science (for example, saliva exchange as a contributor to attraction or bonding) [1][3].
  • Uncertainty and confidence: Much of the evidence here comes from surveys, self-reports, and suggestive biological ideas. The saliva-based mechanism is presented as plausible but not definitive, and the love-survey framing focuses on describing patterns rather than arguing for a single causal model [3][2][1].
  • Limitations and future work (inference): A logical next step is research that links mental-state measures with physiological data and/or longitudinal outcomes, to better tease apart when kissing functions mainly as bonding, assessment, or arousal [1][2][3].
