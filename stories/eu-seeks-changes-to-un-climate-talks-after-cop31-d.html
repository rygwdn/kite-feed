EU seeks changes to UN climate talks after COP31 deadlock

Category: World

The European Union will propose changes to the United Nations climate talks’ consensus rules after COP31 ended in Antalya without an overarching agreement, EU climate commissioner Wopke Hoekstra told the Financial Times. The summit’s proposed final text omitted a direct reference to moving away from fossil fuels, prompting the EU and other countries to reject it. Oil-producing states had resisted such language [4][3]. Hoekstra said Brussels wants a process that can implement commitments already made, while poorer countries criticized the lack of clear new climate-finance commitments. The EU says it contributed €32.3 billion in climate finance in 2025 and had proposed discussing a tripling of adaptation funding by 2035, but that proposal included no new commitments [4]. Any change to the rules also requires broad agreement. Countries are also pursuing climate action outside the formal talks: a fossil-fuel transition conference in Santa Marta, Colombia, drew 57 nations earlier this year, and another meeting is planned for 2027 [4][19].

Key Points

  • Science dispute: Saudi Arabia, India and Russia pushed to delay the next major Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change assessment until 2029, while European, African and Pacific nations wanted it before the 2028 Global Stocktake [23][8].
  • Indigenous rights: Indigenous Climate Action founder Eriel Tchekwie Deranger said COP mechanisms still lack effective ways to track whether states uphold Indigenous rights and include Indigenous participation in national climate policies [46].
  • Domestic scrutiny: Environmental groups warned that the federal government’s Bill C-39 could weaken protections for at-risk species, including southern resident killer whales in British Columbia [46].
  • Other decisions: Delegates adopted the Just Transition Work Programme and an Adaptation Fund decision despite failing to agree on the summit’s overarching political text [30].
  • Electrification pledge: More than 75 countries and 180 businesses backed a voluntary goal to raise electricity’s share of final energy use to 35% by 2035, but the goal was not included in the cover text [19].
  • Climate finance: Negotiators did not settle a baseline year for the proposed 2035 adaptation-finance target, leaving vulnerable nations without a clear measure of progress [30].
  • Temperature limit: Small island states and least developed countries regard the 1.5°C limit as existential and non-negotiable [23].
  • Separate pledge: More than 75 countries and 180 businesses endorsed a voluntary goal of raising electricity’s share of global final energy use from about 23% to 35% by 2035 [19].
  • Finance gap: Developing countries sought clarity on the baseline for tripling adaptation finance to $120 billion annually by 2035; adaptation needs in the Global South are estimated at about $500 billion a year [23][30].
  • Removal limits: The summit’s debate over exceeding the temperature limit included carbon removal, but the technologies proposed to bring temperatures back down have not been deployed at the scale discussed [23][54].
  • Carbon capture: A proposal to accelerate carbon capture and set specific deployment targets entered the draft on Nov. 16, drawing objections from countries that saw it as a way to prolong fossil-fuel use [26].
  • Warming projection: The 2026 Global Carbon Budget and a recent UNEP report project that long-term warming will likely reach 1.5°C around 2030 [23].
  • Separate decisions: Governments adopted decisions on adaptation and just transition, but the Mitigation Work Programme did not secure agreement [50].

Historical Background

Overarching cover decisions are additions to the formal negotiating process, not a requirement, and countries have regularly adopted them since the Glasgow meeting in 2021 [15]. Before COP31, countries last failed to reach an overarching political deal at the 2009 Copenhagen summit [3][16].

Q&A

Q: Where will climate talks resume after COP31?

A: Ethiopia is due to host COP32 in Addis Ababa in 2027, where organizers will need to address divisions left unresolved in Antalya [8].

Q: Did the breakdown prevent all climate decisions?

A: No. Governments adopted separate decisions on adaptation and the Just Transition Work Programme, but did not agree on the Mitigation Work Programme or the overarching political text [50][30].
