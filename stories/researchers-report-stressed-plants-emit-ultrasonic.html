Researchers report stressed plants emit ultrasonic click signals

Category: Science

Two science features highlighted plant research on stress responses and plant-electronics interfaces. One described evidence that stressed plants can emit faint ultrasonic clicks, while the other examined efforts to connect electronics directly to plant signaling systems [1][3]. Together, they portray plants not as passive organisms but as living systems that generate detectable responses, from ultrasonic emissions under stress to internal biochemical signaling that researchers hope to access more directly [1][3]. SciTechDaily reported on research suggesting that stressed plants can release bursts of ultrasonic sound at frequencies humans cannot hear, raising the possibility that these emissions could serve as measurable indicators of plant health [1]. The Hindu described “cyborg botany” as an effort to tap plants’ internal biochemical signaling and connect it with electronics, potentially turning living plants into environmental sensors or biohybrid devices [3]. Taken together, the stories point to a broader push to understand how plants respond to the world around them using new measurement tools [1][3].

Did you know: Humans cannot hear ultrasound, so a plant could emit detectable clicks nearby without anyone noticing unaided [common].

Key Points

  • Hidden acoustics: The reported plant sounds are ultrasonic clicks, not audible noise, so people would not hear them without specialized recording equipment [1].
  • Stress triggers: SciTechDaily said the sound bursts appeared when plants were under stress, including after physical injury or water-related stress [1].
  • Biohybrid goal: The Hindu said cyborg botany aims to access plants’ own biochemical signals at the source so researchers can connect living tissues with circuit-like systems [3].

Perspectives

SciTechDaily report: The article presents ultrasonic emissions as newly documented evidence that distressed plants produce measurable airborne signals that had previously gone unheard by humans.

  • Biochemical signaling: The Hindu describes plant communication as biochemical signaling, meaning internal molecular changes triggered by environmental conditions that researchers want to read directly with attached devices [3].
  • Biohybrid interfaces: Cyborg botany focuses on linking living plant tissue with electronics [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Scientific meaning: The reporting suggests plant responses may be more measurable from the outside than many people assume, whether through airborne ultrasonic clicks or internal biochemical activity. That could broaden how scientists study plant stress and signaling [1][3].
