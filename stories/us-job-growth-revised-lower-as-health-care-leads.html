US job growth revised lower as health care leads

Category: Business

New Labor Department revisions show hiring in 2025 was weaker than first reported, even as the economy kept growing and unemployment stayed relatively low [7][8]. Several reports said the updates meaningfully change the picture of 2025’s labor-market momentum, reinforcing the sense that finding a job has gotten harder despite ongoing expansion [7][8]. January’s payroll gain offered a brighter start to 2026, with job growth of about 130,000. But much of that increase came from health care and related services—areas supported by an ageing population and steady demand for care [2][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Revision mechanics: NPR described the change as an annual benchmark process that swaps earlier sample-based payroll estimates for more complete administrative counts. That can materially reshape past monthly readings even if the latest month looks steady [7].
  • Sector concentration: Firstpost emphasized that January’s hiring mix—dominated by health care—raises questions about how broad worker demand really is outside essential services tied to demographic needs [2].
  • Cooling beneath a low jobless rate: BBC reporting highlighted that what’s shifting is the flow of opportunities (hiring rates and openings). That can make job searches feel tougher even when unemployment stays relatively low [8].
  • Mixed interpretations: Some analysts read the latest month as a rebound, while others argued the revised 2025 baseline changes how strong that “rebound” looks in context [3][7].

Perspectives

Labor Department data revisions (as reported by NPR): NPR framed the new figures as a benchmark-driven reset that lowers the apparent pace of 2025 hiring compared with earlier monthly reports. The outlet cautioned that once you reset those back-period levels, the labor market’s recent momentum looks different [7].

Sources: NPR

Demographics-focused view (Firstpost): Firstpost argued that January’s hiring mix points to an ageing population as a key driver of job gains, with health care doing much of the heavy lifting.

Sources: Firstpost

Sector-imbalance concern (Quartz): Quartz cited experts who warn that health care is propping up the labor market while parts of the white-collar economy look weaker, suggesting a narrower base of growth than the headline figures imply.

Sources: Quartz

Market-rebound framing (Seeking Alpha): Seeking Alpha portrayed the January report as a stronger rebound after a softer 2025 labor market, leaning on a more upbeat read of the latest month.

Sources: Seeking Alpha
