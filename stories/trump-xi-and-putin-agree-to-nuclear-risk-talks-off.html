Trump, Xi and Putin agree to nuclear-risk talks, offer no arms deal

Category: World

U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin concluded their first three-way summit Thursday in Shenzhen with a declaration promising dialogue on nuclear risks, but no arms-reduction measures or timetable [1][40][2]. The meeting was the first time the three leaders had met together and followed separate Trump meetings with Xi and Putin [5][70]. The declaration also called for United Nations reform, increased production and supply of refined petroleum products, and respect for state officials’ immunity from international prosecution. It did not mention Russia’s war in Ukraine or the U.S.-led war in Iran and gave no details on what U.N. reform would involve [2][36][10]. After the summit, Putin said Russia would not negotiate a cease-fire. Trump told a Berlin meeting of Ukraine’s supporters that he had discussed a peaceful end to the war with Xi and Putin, according to U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff [9][62]. Trump said he would discuss the trip at a White House news conference on Friday, Nov. 20 [5].

Key Points

  • Arsenal disparity: The Stockholm International Peace Research Institute estimated China had 620 nuclear warheads in January 2026, compared with 5,042 in the U.S. arsenal and 5,420 in Russia’s [43].
  • Taiwan omitted: The declaration did not address Taiwan, despite China’s claim to the self-governed island and its stated willingness to use force if necessary [2][37].
  • No oil targets: The leaders set no production targets or implementation schedule for their petroleum pledge [61][31].
  • Format uncertain: Putin said the leaders had not agreed to meet regularly in the three-country format, though he said he wanted dialogue to continue in some form [26].
