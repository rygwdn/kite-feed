Google launches $179 Fitbit Edge with replaceable battery, GPS, OLED

Category: Technology

Google announced the Fitbit Edge on Oct. 12, a $179.99 fitness tracker with a screen and built-in GPS that sits between the screenless Fitbit Air and Pixel Watch 5 in its wearable lineup [9][2]. Preorders opened, with retail sales scheduled to begin Oct. 27; a Stephen Curry edition costs $219.99 [9][4]. The Edge has a 1.34-inch OLED display, a physical side button, ECG and heart-rate sensors, and a replaceable battery—the first Fitbit tracker designed for battery replacement [3][11]. Google rates battery life at up to seven days with the always-on display off and about two days with it on; the company says the screen offers 46% more viewing area than the Charge 6 [2][3]. The device works with Android phones and iPhones and uses Fitbit software rather than the full Wear OS system used by Pixel Watch [2][13]. It supports notifications, Maps directions and music controls, but lacks a microphone, speaker and NFC payments [9][2][12]. Google Health Coach and advanced Health Guardian insights require Google Health Premium after a three-month trial [9][16].

Key Points

  • Workout detection: Google is expanding automatic workout recognition on the Edge, Fitbit Air and Pixel Watch 3–5 from 10 activities to more than 100. The company says the new system will be nearly twice as accurate and cut missed workouts by more than half [20][34].
  • Health estimates: Health Guardian estimates monthly blood-pressure and insulin-resistance trends from wearable data. Google says the features are not diagnostic and the blood-pressure trend is not a substitute for a cuff measurement [32][31].
  • Paid coaching: Google Health Premium costs $9.99 a month or $99.99 a year after the included three-month trial and provides Health Coach and advanced health insights [16][33].
  • Device limits: The Edge cannot install additional apps or use on-wrist Gemini. Incoming calls appear as notifications; users cannot answer them on the tracker [2][16].
  • Market availability: Google’s announced launch-country list includes the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Japan, Australia and New Zealand, but not India [26].

Technical Details

  • Charging: A five-minute top-up provides about a day of use, while a full charge takes 90 minutes [3].
  • Interface: Users can customize watch-face complications in Google Health and rearrange the Edge’s tiles [3].
  • GPS design: The tracker has built-in GPS but does not use a dual-frequency GNSS system [64].

Historical Background
