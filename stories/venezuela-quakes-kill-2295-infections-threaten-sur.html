Venezuela quakes kill 2,295; infections threaten survivors

Category: World

Doctors and aid workers in Venezuela warned that the aftermath of the June 24 twin earthquakes could become a widening medical crisis, as untreated injuries, infections, unsanitary shelters and scarce clean water threatened survivors [2][3][6]. Venezuelan officials said at least 2,295 people had died and 11,267 had been injured. Thousands of displaced people were sleeping in crowded shelters or outdoors [2][3][34][58]. The magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 quakes struck northern Venezuela on June 24 and caused the worst destruction in La Guaira state, north of Caracas [4][29][83]. Medical staff said the disaster exposed a health system already weakened by shortages of doctors, underfunding and emigration, while aid workers warned damaged infrastructure could accelerate outbreaks in the worst-hit communities [2][3][6]. Acting President Delcy Rodríguez declared seven days of national mourning starting Wednesday evening, and National Assembly President Jorge Rodríguez said more than 4,000 rescue workers had pulled 6,461 people from rubble [11][58][34]. Rescue teams from seven countries were still trying to reach Hernán Gil, a 43-year-old security guard trapped under a collapsed building in Catia La Mar, late Wednesday into early Thursday [77][91][102].

Key Points

  • Morgue strain: The influx of bodies overwhelmed the forensic system, prompting authorities to use La Guaira’s port as a temporary morgue and refrigerated containers for victims [8][74][31].
  • Temporary camps: Authorities set up 25 temporary camps for displaced residents: 13 in La Guaira, eight in Caracas, two in Miranda, one in Carabobo and one in Yaracuy [34][49][58].
  • Rescue window: Gen. Francis Donovan said rescuers typically have three to seven days to find injured survivors in rubble [5].
  • Missing lists: An unofficial but widely used missing-persons list stood at 40,567, while U.N.-linked estimates cited roughly 50,000 people unaccounted for [34][72][112].
  • Food pressure: Survivors in La Guaira faced longer aid lines and worsening access to food and water as markets struggled to function after the quakes [120][122].

Perspectives

International Rescue Committee: The IRC said “the scale of the response does not meet the scale of humanitarian need,” as residents criticized the government’s pace and scope.

Industry Impact

  • Health services: UN agencies and partners reported damage or destruction to about 1,000 buildings, including hospitals, along with more than 400 schools and water systems, complicating care, shelter and child protection work [110][123].
  • Air logistics: The U.S. helped repair the runway at the damaged international airport near Caracas so humanitarian flights could land, according to U.S. officials cited by WZMQ and ZDF [114][32].
  • Local commerce: El Nacional reported that La Guaira’s local economy, especially small businesses, had been unable to restart one week after the quakes [106].

Historical Background

The June 24 disaster was a rare seismic doublet: two major quakes of magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 struck within seconds of each other, at shallow depths that can intensify surface damage [4][29]. Scroll reported the sequence was the most powerful recorded in the region in 126 years [29].

Humanitarian Impact

The United Nations counted about 1.8 million people needing humanitarian help, including roughly 680,000 children, while the World Food Programme sought $50 million to feed 500,000 people for three months in disaster zones [12][75]. Immediate needs include shelter, medical care, safe water, sanitation and food supplies [110][123].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇸 United States: U.S. Southern Command said about 2,000 U.S. service members were supporting search, recovery and supply delivery around Venezuela, and U.S. personnel helped fly in about 310 urban search-and-rescue specialists [5].
  • 🇮🇳 India: Indian medical teams continued relief work under Operation Amistad, and External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar publicly thanked them for their work during challenging operations in Venezuela [17].
  • 🇻🇳 Viet Nam: Viet Nam sent a 124-member rescue force and delivered emergency rations, generators, tents, medical bandages and medicines to Venezuela [137][136].

Action Items

  • Donate to child-focused relief: UNICEF said first flights with water, medicine and other supplies had reached Venezuela and asked for donations to reach more children and families [123].
  • Support food, water and shelter work: The Salvation Army said it was providing food, water, shelter and essential supplies to survivors and rescue workers on the ground [86].
