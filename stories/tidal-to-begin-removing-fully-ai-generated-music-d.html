Tidal to begin removing fully AI-generated music Dec. 9

Category: AI

Tidal says its systems flagged 43% of tracks delivered in the past 90 days as wholly AI-generated, with 94% of its AI-tagged catalog attributed to Suno, a music-generation platform [5][17]. The streaming service will begin removing fully AI-generated tracks on Dec. 9 and block new deliveries, while continuing to accept music made with AI assistance [6][7]. Tidal will use detection technology from Ircam Amplify, a French music-technology company, and screen submission metadata to distinguish fully generated recordings from work involving human creative input [12][4]. A Tidal spokesperson told Rolling Stone that most appeals it reviewed upheld the AI classification, while acknowledging that some false positives are inevitable [7]. Pitchfork reported that Ircam Amplify advertises 99% accuracy and a 1% false-positive rate, and estimated that applying that rate to the takedown could mistakenly flag about 290,000 songs [15]. The policy is stricter than the labeling systems used by Spotify and Apple Music. Bandcamp also bars AI music under a broader rule covering work made substantially with AI [26][22]. Tidal expects to finish removing the tracks by early 2027 [5].

Key Points

  • Low listening: Tidal said 96% of its AI-generated songs received zero plays in the previous 90 days [5][6].
  • Demand mismatch: Apple Music executive Oliver Schusser said more than a third of monthly uploads were fully AI-generated, while such music accounted for less than 0.5% of listening [28][32].
  • Daily rejections: Tidal expects to reject 80,000 to 85,000 songs a day once the ban takes effect [5][6].
  • Listener survey: An Ipsos survey across nine countries found 57% of respondents had fully AI-generated music in their playlists, while 74% thought they did not [16].
  • Rival policies: Spotify says it removed more than 75 million spam tracks from its catalog and 50 million from recommendations, while continuing to allow labeled AI music [5][42].
  • Impersonation removals: Tidal says it has already removed more than 65,000 tracks flagged for suspected artist-name impersonation [13][1].
  • Rival responses: Spotify said it removed 50 million spam tracks from recommendations and deleted 75 million spam tracks from its catalog, while retaining labels and other measures rather than a blanket ban [13][26].
  • Suno concentration: Tidal data cited by Digital Trends attributes 94% of its AI-badged tracks to Suno, a generative AI music platform [17].

  • Detection and metadata checks: Tidal plans to combine detection technology from Ircam Amplify with checks of distributor metadata. Metadata identifying a track as generated solely with AI can trigger rejection [4][46].
