Senate blocks Trump rule expanding White House research control

Category: Science

The U.S. Senate voted to block a rule that would give the White House more control over research, adding the language to a bill to fund the government until mid-December [1]. The provision targets President Trump's changes to federal research oversight and science-related funding [1][2][3]. El País profiled epidemiologist Luisa N. Borrell, whose research on racial health inequities is in a field where the Trump administration has cut resources [2]. Grist, republishing a Canary Media article through Climate Desk, reported that Trump administration actions have slowed Department of Energy funding for grid reliability and affordability projects, including terminated or delayed grants in Wisconsin, California and the upper Midwest [3].

Primary Source: View original article

In the United States, there are 197 words that disqualify you from research funding. I work with all of them

— Luisa N. Borrell (El País)

Key Points

  • Grid grants: Alliant Energy canceled a Wisconsin project aimed at reducing outages in disadvantaged and tribal communities after the Trump administration terminated the federal grant supporting it [3].
  • Delayed reimbursements: The Sacramento Municipal Utility District has not received DOE reimbursement since October for work tied to hundreds of thousands of advanced smart meters [3].
  • Transmission funding: A consortium of regional grid operators, utilities and state agencies in the upper Midwest is waiting for $464 million in DOE funds for high-voltage transmission lines, though the agency overseeing the project said the funding would soon be restored [3].

Perspectives

Trump administration: The administration said grants it moved to kill funded “the Left’s climate agenda,” according to Grist's account of the DOE actions.
