Japan second-quarter GDP slows to 1.1% annualized growth

Category: Business

Japan’s economy expanded at an annualized 1.1% in the April-June quarter, below the 2.0% median estimate in a Reuters poll and slower than an upwardly revised 1.9% expansion in the previous quarter, government data released Monday showed [3][14][15][22][28]. On a quarter-on-quarter basis, inflation-adjusted GDP rose 0.3%, missing forecasts for 0.5% growth, while the economy recorded its third consecutive quarterly expansion [10][14][23][33][35]. Domestic demand weakened. Private consumption was flat or down 0.02%, its first decline in eight quarters, and capital spending fell 1.2%, while net external demand added 0.5 percentage point to growth [14][23][25][27][34]. Exports rose 0.5%, supported by demand for Japanese hybrid vehicles and semiconductor-related equipment, while imports fell 1.5% as disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz temporarily reduced oil inflows, a decline that lifted GDP in national-accounting terms because imports are subtracted [10][14][27][36]. The Bank of Japan is weighing further rate increases while watching consumption and wage trends. Higher energy costs, a weak yen and Middle East-related supply strains continue to raise import costs for resource-poor Japan [4][9][14][10][15]. Overnight swaps implied about an 80% chance of a BOJ rate increase at the Sept. 18 policy meeting, according to Star Headline, while Bloomberg Economics’ Taro Kimura said the GDP report weakened the case for a September move [22][26].

Did you know: Spending by foreign visitors to Japan, counted as exports in GDP, fell 9.7% after June typhoon-related flight cancellations [36].

Key Points

  • Domestic drag: Domestic demand subtracted 0.2 percentage point from second-quarter growth after contributing positively for three quarters [23][30][36].
  • Government spending: Government consumption rose 1.6%; reports linked the increase to higher medical costs and expanded support for high-school tuition and elementary-school meals [10][16][27].
  • Market reaction: The yen briefly strengthened to 158.95 per dollar after the release, while the Nikkei 225 slipped 0.01% and the Topix fell 0.48% in early trading [22][37].
  • Price pressure: Japan’s corporate goods prices rose 7.2% from a year earlier in July, increasing operating costs and adding pressure that analysts said could pass through to consumers [22].

Perspectives

Taro Saito, NLI Research Institute economist: Saito told AFP that the GDP figures were worse than expected because consumption and capital investment were weak, and he attributed the growth to lower imports rather than a robust economy.

Sources: Infobae

Naoki Hattori, Mizuho Research Institute chief Japan economist: Hattori said steady wage growth, government subsidies and past U.S.-Japan coordination on currency intervention kept a September BOJ rate increase as the main market expectation.

Sources: Star Headline

Marcel Thieliant, Capital Economics economist: Thieliant said growth was acceptable and noted that government measures were still limiting the pass-through of higher energy prices to consumers, a pattern he expected to continue in the second half of the year.

Sources: Central News Agency

Wataru Akiyama, Nomura Securities equities strategist: Akiyama said rising short- and long-term rates tied to Middle East-driven inflation pressures could cap stock-market gains.

Industry Impact

  • Automakers and exporters: Global demand for Japanese vehicles, including U.S. demand for hybrids, supported exporters such as Toyota Motor and Honda Motor [10][14].
  • Financial markets: Japanese shares traded mostly lower after the GDP data, with 71 Nikkei 225 advancers, 151 decliners and three unchanged in early trading [37].
  • Household-facing sectors: Non-durable goods spending fell more than expected, while lodging and dining consumption also stayed weak, according to reports citing Japanese GDP component data [22][36].

Historical Background

Japan ended its negative interest-rate policy in 2024, starting a gradual shift away from years of extraordinary monetary easing [common].

Business Angle

  • Net external demand added to headline GDP growth, while domestic demand subtracted from it [23][30].
  • Higher short- and long-term rates can pressure equity valuations, especially for leveraged companies and long-duration growth stocks [37][common].
  • A weak yen can lift translated overseas revenue for exporters but raises the yen cost of imported inputs, including oil [31][15][common].
