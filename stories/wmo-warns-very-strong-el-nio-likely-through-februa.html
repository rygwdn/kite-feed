WMO warns very strong El Niño likely through February 2027

Category: Science

The World Meteorological Organization said El Niño is firmly established in the tropical Pacific and is expected to intensify into a very strong event over the coming months, with a near-100% probability of persisting through February 2027 [2][7][8][38]. The UN weather agency said the event is likely to peak toward the end of 2026 and could significantly alter rainfall and temperature patterns, increasing risks of floods, drought and extreme heat [7][8][26][62]. El Niño is a naturally recurring climate pattern and is not caused by climate change, but scientists expect it to add heat to a planet already warming from fossil-fuel emissions and to amplify weather extremes [16][26][38][22]. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said the world had entered a "danger zone of extreme weather," while WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo said the event could deliver "a massive blow" to communities and economies [2][16][26].

Key Points

  • Ocean readings: The Niño 3.4 index averaged 1.5°C above normal from May to July, reached 2°C in July, and posted weekly readings as high as 2.6°C between late July and mid-August [7][46][57].
  • Regional effects: El Niño has already been linked to worsening wildfires in Indonesia, weaker monsoon rains in India, higher fire risk in Australia and more intense flooding in South America [4][45][20].
  • Pacific cyclones: Hurricanes Lowell and Karina and Tropical Storm Marie were active in the Pacific on September 2, with Lowell reaching Category 5 in one report and both Lowell and Karina described as Category 4 storms in others [17][29][61][49].
  • Forecast outreach: The WMO said it made a major effort to circulate forecasts tied to possible El Niño-driven extremes to as many communities as possible [2].

Technical Details

  • Subsurface heat: WMO data showed subsurface ocean temperatures more than 8°C above normal in some areas during July and early August, a reservoir of heat that supports continued surface warming [7][25][53].
  • Intensity thresholds: SAWS said it classifies strong El Niño conditions above a 1.5°C three-month sea-surface temperature anomaly and very strong conditions above 2°C, with current forecasts potentially crossing 3°C [55].

Industry Impact

  • Agriculture: French reporting said products from El Niño-affected regions could rise in price in France, while South African forecasters warned hotter, drier summer rainfall conditions can affect farm yields [10][34].
  • Urban trees: UK horticulture experts warned that drought-weakened trees could become more brittle and vulnerable to storm winds during wetter, stormier autumn conditions [59].

Scientific Significance

  • Forecast confidence: RTÉ noted that the WMO's update was the first time an El Niño/La Niña forecast had been so unequivocal, citing the agency's near-100% probability of persistence through February 2027 [8][19][46].
  • Lagged warming: El Niño often peaks late in the year, while ocean heat transfers more slowly to the atmosphere and can push global temperatures higher the following year [26][38].

Historical Background

El Niño events recur irregularly every two to seven years and usually last about nine to 12 months [7][42][46]. South Africa’s weather service cited the 1982/83 and 2015/16 El Niño events as two of the most damaging for the region because both were associated with widespread drought [55]. A similar sequence of four Pacific cyclones occurred in 2015, another year marked by an intense El Niño [17].

Humanitarian Impact

The World Food Programme said in August that El Niño was likely to push about 50 million people into acute hunger [4][20][53]. Aid groups also warned that millions could be forced to move in the Horn of Africa as risks from fire, intense rain and food insecurity increase [37].

International Reactions

  • 🇵🇦 Panama: Panama declared an emergency over El Niño on August 25 after the phenomenon forced reductions in shipping through the Panama Canal [7].
  • 🇰🇪 Kenya: An official warned that 46 counties were likely to experience above-average rainfall as Kenya prepared for El Niño conditions [52].
  • 🇿🇦 South Africa: The South African Weather Service said the 2026/27 event could become the strongest El Niño in living memory if forecasts exceed the 3°C anomaly threshold [55].

Travel Advisory

  • Hawaii: Lowell and Karina were expected to pass away from Hawaii, but forecasts still allowed for stronger winds and increased rainfall in the archipelago [29][61][58]. Check National Hurricane Center and local emergency updates before coastal, boating or inter-island travel.

Action Items

  • Inspect drought-stressed trees in UK gardens: If a large tree shows damage after drought, arrange an inspection; for smaller trees, reduce dead weight in the crown where appropriate [59].
