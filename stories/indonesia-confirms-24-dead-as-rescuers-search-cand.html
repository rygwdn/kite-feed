Indonesia confirms 24 dead as rescuers search candy factory ruins

Category: World

Indonesian authorities on Monday confirmed 24 deaths and said about 30 workers remained missing after a weekend fire at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia candy factory in Cikande, west of Jakarta [2][3][5]. Rescuers continued searching the burned site, where unstable structures and lingering hot spots endangered crews; 29 people were confirmed safe, including five who were hospitalized [2][3]. The factory’s chief executive, Steven Erwin Wijaya, said about 300 people were at the complex for Saturday’s shift [3][2]. Presidential labor adviser Said Iqbal said investigators were assessing whether safety protocols had been followed. He said preliminary information pointed to a possible air-conditioner short circuit, but authorities have not established the cause [3][2]. The fire left many remains too badly damaged for visual identification, so police are collecting DNA samples from relatives and personal belongings. Identification could take two to three weeks [5][2]. Search operations and the safety inquiry are continuing [3][2].

Key Points

  • Rescue operation: More than 500 personnel, including soldiers, police, Red Cross workers and volunteers, searched an area of about 2,000 square metres [6].
  • Affected area: Officials estimated 63 people were in the hardest-hit section, mostly on the second floor, where the fire reportedly began near a cooling unit [6].
  • Company profile: The factory’s parent company employs about 5,000 people, and its Milkita candy products are exported to more than 40 countries [3][6].

Perspectives

Said Iqbal, Indonesian presidential adviser on labor issues: Iqbal said he would summon the factory’s chief executive [3].

Sources: The Hindu

Steven Erwin Wijaya, factory chief executive: Wijaya said the factory regularly held emergency and evacuation drills with local firefighters. He said evacuation routes and fire-protection systems met applicable standards and were working [5].

Sources: The Guardian

Tubagus Mahfud, Serang fire department operations chief: Mahfud said collapsed sections and poor lighting forced firefighters to proceed cautiously. Production materials could reignite, requiring cooling operations before crews could enter all areas, he said [5].
