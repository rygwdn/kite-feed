B.C. wildfire damages Clinton water and power systems

Category: Canada

Residents of Clinton, B.C., remained under evacuation order with no timeline for returning as the Pear Lake wildfire burned in the surrounding Cariboo region and damaged local infrastructure [1][2][6]. Clinton chief administrative officer Brian Doddridge said property owners would receive calls in the next few days telling them whether their homes survived, while officials assessed damage to the village watershed and possible effects on water quality [1][2][6]. The fire covered about 668 to 669 square kilometres, and CBC described it as the largest active wildfire in British Columbia on Wednesday [2][3][6]. BC Hydro senior field operations manager Gregg Jackson said the fire caused “unprecedented damage” to the area’s transmission system, destroying hundreds of power structures, including substations and high-voltage lines [1]. At an online meeting attended by about 350 people, Thompson-Nicola Regional District board chair Barbara Roden urged affected residents to seek help if they need it [1][2][6]. BC Wildfire Service crews also carried out planned ignitions near Highway 97 north of Clinton, which an incident commander said were intended to secure the fire’s edge and protect the village [3].
