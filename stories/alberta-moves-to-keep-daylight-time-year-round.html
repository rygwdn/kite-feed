Alberta moves to keep daylight time year-round

Category: Canada

Alberta has introduced Bill 31 to end its twice-yearly clock changes and stay on daylight time year-round, with the change set to begin on Nov. 1 if the bill passes [3][6][5]. The provincial government is calling the proposal "Alberta Time," and the bill marks the first formal legislative step toward ending seasonal clock shifts in the province [3][6]. The proposal matters beyond Alberta because neighboring provinces are watching closely. Saskatchewan, which does not currently change its clocks, said it may amend its own time law depending on what Alberta decides [1][2][5]. Coverage also points to trade-offs, including concerns about dark winter mornings, safety, and tourism [5].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Saskatchewan is unusual in Canada because it generally stays on the same time year-round instead of changing clocks in spring and fall [common].

Key Points

  • Regional ripple: Global News said other provinces are weighing their options as Alberta moves ahead with the change [5].
  • Legislative step: Thursday's tabling of Bill 31 was Alberta's first formal move toward ending the twice-yearly clock change after decades of following the seasonal schedule [3][6].
  • November target: If the bill passes, Albertans would not set their clocks back by one hour on Nov. 1, the scheduled date for the next autumn time change [6][5].
  • Saskatchewan option: Saskatchewan's government said it is watching Alberta's plan and could revise its own legislation [1][2].

Technical Details

  • Permanent daylight time keeps clocks one hour ahead of standard time throughout the year, eliminating the spring-forward and fall-back transitions provinces now use seasonally [common].
  • Interprovincial alignment matters because changes to time laws can leave neighboring provinces on different offsets during parts of the year, affecting transport, broadcasting, schools, and cross-border business operations [common].

Historical Background

Most Canadian provinces have long followed a seasonal clock-change system tied to daylight saving time, while Saskatchewan has largely stayed on a constant year-round system for decades [common]. Alberta's new bill would end decades of switching clocks twice a year in the province, according to the reporting [3][6].
