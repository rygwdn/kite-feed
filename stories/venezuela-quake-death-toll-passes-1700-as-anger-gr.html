Venezuela quake death toll passes 1,700 as anger grows

Category: World

Rescue teams searched collapsed buildings in northern Venezuela on Tuesday, six days after twin earthquakes of magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 struck the coastal region on June 24 less than a minute apart [5][18][12]. Venezuelan authorities put the toll at 1,719 dead and 5,034 injured, while the United Nations estimated that up to 68,000 people could be missing [32][18][46]. A NASA assessment based on satellite imagery suggested almost 60,000 buildings may have been damaged or destroyed [12][16]. La Guaira state, the hardest-hit area, remained the focus of rescue work after a 4.6-magnitude aftershock shook Caracas and La Guaira on Monday without immediate reports of new damage [5][30][32]. Salvadoran rescuers reached a 44-year-old man trapped under a shopping centre in Maiquetía early Tuesday after sending him water through a tube, while earlier rescues included a 21-year-old man pulled out after more than 100 hours [2][38][5][22]. Residents in several damaged areas accused authorities of responding too slowly, and BBC reporters in La Guaira saw people using crowbars, mallets and pickaxes to dig for relatives and neighbours [4][5]. A separate search focused on more than 100 Venezuelans who had been deported from the United States hours before the earthquakes and taken to a hotel in La Guaira that collapsed, according to survivors and ICE Flight Monitor data cited by the Associated Press [81][80][76][78]. Opposition leader María Corina Machado, speaking from Panama, accused the government of blocking her planned return to Venezuela by closing commercial airspace; reports said Maiquetía airport had been closed because of quake damage and partly reopened for humanitarian flights [101][106][104].

Key Points

  • Rescue window: Relief organizations say the first 72 hours after a disaster are usually the critical window for finding survivors, though trapped people can survive longer if they have access to food or water [17][25][29].
  • Foreign teams: Venezuelan authorities said support from 24 countries included more than 500 metric tons of supplies, more than 2,700 rescue and support personnel and about 86 canine teams [30].
  • Debris scale: The United Nations estimated the disaster generated roughly 1.2 million tonnes of debris in La Guaira state [16].
  • Power restoration: Jorge Rodríguez said electricity had been restored to 90% of La Guaira, while earlier government figures had put restoration at 75% [26][34].
  • Burial pressure: At Caracas' only public cemetery, the crematory ovens worked at full capacity and 60 to 70 burials were held each day between Friday and Sunday [16].

Industry Impact

  • National infrastructure: A UNDP preliminary satellite assessment put direct physical damage at about $6.7 billion, equivalent to roughly 6% of Venezuela's GDP [41].
  • Ports and logistics: The U.S. military repaired and reopened the Port of La Guaira on Monday, creating a route for aid deliveries after the disaster disrupted critical infrastructure [16][97].
  • Education: Delcy Rodríguez said school classes would be suspended for one more week while authorities assessed whether buildings were habitable [34].

Historical Background

The June 24 earthquakes were described as the strongest to hit Venezuela in more than a century [16][17]. Delcy Rodríguez has led the interim government since January, after reports said the Trump administration seized Nicolás Maduro [25][34].

Humanitarian Impact

Authorities reported 15,866 displaced people and 22,619 people receiving care in health centres, while aid groups warned that shelter, safe water, sanitation, food and medical access were urgent needs for people unable to return home [18][66].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇳 United Nations: OCHA activated emergency mechanisms and helped coordinate the arrival of 44 international urban search-and-rescue teams, including 2,245 specialists and 140 search dogs [41].
  • 🇮🇳 India: Venezuela thanked an Indian team for helping earthquake victims after the death toll crossed 1,700 on June 29 [3].
  • 🇺🇸 United States: A U.S. task force from Fairfax, Virginia, worked near the epicenter to assist in search and rescue [12].
