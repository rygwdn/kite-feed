US strikes three alleged drug-smuggling boats, 11 killed

Category: World

The U.S. military says it struck three small boats it suspected of drug smuggling in Latin American waters on Monday, killing 11 people in the Caribbean and the eastern Pacific [1][2][4][5][6][7][8][9]. U.S. Southern Command called those targeted “narcoterrorists,” a label several outlets say echoes the Trump administration’s framing of its campaign against maritime trafficking routes [1][4][5][6][7][9]. Multiple publications say the strikes push reported fatalities from the months-long effort to at least 145 since early September [1][4][5][6][7][9]. Supporters describe the operations as an attempt to disrupt dangerous trafficking networks at sea, while critics—and some coverage—focus on the human toll [5][4][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • How notable this round was: Several outlets described Monday’s attacks as among the deadliest single-day episodes of the months-long campaign, with strikes spanning both the Caribbean and the eastern Pacific [1][5][9][8].
  • Carrier departure and broader context: El País said the strikes came after the aircraft carrier Gerald Ford left the Caribbean, where it had operated since October as part of Operation Southern Spear. The outlet linked the mission to U.S. pressure on Venezuela in the run-up to the January 3 capture of President Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores [2].
  • Campaign scale: Since early September, the U.S. has carried out at least 42 strikes on alleged trafficking routes, according to AP figures cited by several outlets [1][5][4][7][9].

Perspectives

Timeline

  • October: The Gerald Ford deployed to the Caribbean for a counternarcotics mission [2].
  • Monday: The U.S. struck three boats in the Caribbean and the eastern Pacific [1][5][9].
  • Tuesday: The U.S. military announced the reported fatalities from Monday’s strikes and reiterated its broader campaign against alleged maritime traffickers [1][4][6].
