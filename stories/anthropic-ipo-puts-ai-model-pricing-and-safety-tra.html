Anthropic IPO puts AI model pricing and safety trade-offs under scrutiny

Category: AI

Anthropic’s prospectus warns that advanced AI models may become less differentiated as usage costs fall. Open models trail closed systems by about four months on average, and Moonshot’s Kimi K3.1 Pro costs half as much as Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5, according to research institute Epoch AI [58][71]. The Claude maker plans to price its shares Wednesday and begin Nasdaq trading Thursday [4]. Morningstar data put the cost of processing the same amount of text at comparable performance at 50 cents, down 97% from $17 at the end of 2024. Anthropic cut Claude Opus prices 20% in September, while OpenAI cut the price of one flagship model to one-fifth of its previous level in July [71]. Anthropic says its safety measures could slow capability gains compared with less-constrained competitors. It also says more than 80% of its revenue in the latest quarter came from models released in the preceding six months [58]. Cornell Tech Policy Institute director Sarah Kreps said the test of Anthropic’s safety commitments will come if public benefit becomes costly or conflicts with profit [41]. Anthropic says each new Claude version has boosted sales [58]. Investors are due to set the public-market price on Nov. 18, before the scheduled Nov. 19 debut [4].

Key Points

  • Developer adoption: On the Vercel developer platform, open models handled more than half of text requests in August, up from 13% in April, although Anthropic still accounted for nearly two-thirds of spending [71].
  • Cloud distribution: SemiAnalysis analyst Joey Brookhart said 48% of Anthropic’s revenue came through cloud platforms including Amazon Bedrock, Microsoft Foundry and Google Vertex, where customers can access Claude through existing providers [72].
  • New product lines: Anthropic has been testing Claude products for finance and law and has begun selling code scanning for vulnerabilities to existing enterprise customers, according to Brookhart [72].

Perspectives

Mark Levine, New York City chief financial and accountability officer: Levine said Anthropic was rushing toward an IPO without answering fundamental questions about its readiness as a public company, including how it would address risks it has described as potentially catastrophic or existential [74].

Technical Details

  • Trainium token throughput: SemiAnalysis analyst Joey Brookhart said throughput on Amazon’s Trainium chips was five to 10 times higher than a year earlier, reflecting gains in hardware, software and model-hardware coordination [72].
  • Compute allocation: Brookhart estimated that about 30% of Anthropic’s computing capacity generates inference-service revenue, while the remainder supports experiments, research, training and post-training [72].

Industry Impact

  • Infrastructure ties: Google and Amazon are simultaneously Anthropic shareholders, suppliers and resellers, linking their interests to the company’s growth [71].

Historical Background

Dario Amodei joined OpenAI in 2016, where he led its safety team and research into scaling laws. His sister Daniela joined OpenAI in 2018 and became vice president of safety and policy before later becoming Anthropic’s president [62].
