China’s AI-linked export boom lifts third-quarter growth to 4.5%

Category: AI

China’s economy grew 4.5% in the third quarter as demand for goods used in artificial intelligence infrastructure helped exports. Officials at the International Monetary Fund and World Bank meetings debated AI’s economic gains and risks [8][12][11]. Growth rebounded from the previous quarter but remained below the 5% recorded in January-March and at the lower end of Beijing’s 4.5%-5% annual target [8][12]. High-tech exports and manufacturing supported output while domestic spending and investment remained weak. Property investment fell more than 20% in the first nine months from a year earlier, and analysts cited weak consumer demand and the prolonged property slump as constraints [8][12]. At the Bangkok meetings, officials warned that growth could suffer if heavy AI investment fails to produce returns. IMF chief economist Silvana Tenreyro said AI-related products helped drive global trade growth [11]. China’s trade surplus is on course to exceed last year’s record $1.2 trillion, while trade tensions and uncertainty about AI spending pose risks to growth [12].

Key Points

  • Factory output: China’s high-tech goods sales rose 14.3%, while battery and industrial-robot production each increased by more than 29%, according to official figures [8].
  • EU trade gap: Chinese exports to the bloc rose 16% in the first nine months, while the EU ran a deficit of more than $1.1 billion a day [22][18].
  • Weak investment: Fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in January-September, while property investment dropped 20.6% and infrastructure investment contracted 4.2% [32][41].
  • Market uncertainty: Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh called AI a consequential economic change but warned that the transition would be bumpy because the market’s future structure remains unclear [11].
  • Policy support: Authorities allocated 550 billion yuan in unused government debt quotas and expanded access to an 800 billion yuan policy-financing tool, although infrastructure investment still contracted [32][41].
  • Smaller systems: The World Bank said “small AI” could help transform developing countries, according to The Guardian [6].
  • Global forecasts: A Financial Times survey of 94 economists pointed to AI-driven benefits extending beyond the United States and China [4].
  • September indicators: Industrial production rose 5.5% year over year in September, and retail sales increased 1.1%, their fastest growth since March [39][32].
  • Trade contribution: IMF chief economist Silvana Tenreyro said global trade grew 8.3% from 2023 to 2025 and would have risen 4.6% without AI-related products [11].
  • Trade balance: China recorded a $938 billion trade surplus in January-September after exports rose 36% in September, putting it on course to exceed last year’s record $1.2 trillion surplus [18][22].

Industry Impact

  • Automotive: Passenger-car exports exceeded 7.1 million units in the first nine months, more than 70% above the year-earlier period, while the EU-China agreement limits Chinese hybrid shipments to the bloc to 350,000 a year [22].
  • High-tech manufacturing: Official figures showed high-tech goods sales rose 14.3%, while production of batteries and industrial robots each grew by more than 29% [8].

Historical Background

China’s property slump followed years of overbuilding and excessive borrowing by private developers, leaving the sector a continuing drag on investment and consumer confidence [8][12]. Beijing announced support measures in September, including lower interest rates for some businesses and subsidies for property buyers, but economists said the measures had yet to show results [8][12].
