Trump starts Washington IndyCar race; Kirkwood wins Freedom 250

Category: World

President Trump waved the green flag on Sunday to start the Freedom 250 Grand Prix, a first-of-its-kind IndyCar street race through central Washington held as the finale of events marking the United States’ 250th anniversary [1][8][9][4]. Trump and first lady Melania Trump first took a slow ceremonial lap in the presidential limousine, known as The Beast, before drivers raced through downtown streets including stretches near the National Mall, Pennsylvania Avenue and the U.S. Capitol [1][9][19][31]. American driver Kyle Kirkwood won the race, with Christian Lundgaard second and Will Power third; CBS and The Guardian reported that the victory was Kirkwood’s second IndyCar win of the season, while the BBC and AFP-carried accounts described it as his seventh career IndyCar victory [1][14][21][23]. The event featured 25 drivers over 147 laps and a 250-mile course, with cars reaching speeds reported between about 185 mph and nearly 200 mph on a temporary circuit around Washington landmarks [1][4][8][52].

Did you know: Washington, D.C., has the lowest maximum speed limit in the United States at 55 mph, according to the BBC [1].

Key Points

  • Race result: Kirkwood crossed the finish line 3.09 seconds ahead of Lundgaard after leading 128 of 147 laps, while pole-sitter Alex Palou finished 20th after penalties and pit-lane problems, and Marcus Ericsson finished fourth [21][14].
  • Public access: Officials expected large crowds and made free tickets available, while private trackside club passes sold for $5,000 [10][31][32].
  • Airspace disruption: Event flyovers included a B-2 bomber and Air Force One [19][43].

Technical Details

  • Temporary street circuit: The course measured about 1.66 to 1.7 miles, had seven turns, and crossed or bordered major avenues around the National Mall rather than a permanent racing facility [4][19][52].
  • Street preparation: Workers prepared ordinary city infrastructure for racing by securing more than 200 manhole covers and sealing them with silicone caps [39].
  • Race distance: The 147-lap format produced a 250-mile race distance, making the event the second-longest street race in IndyCar history after 500km Cleveland races in 1982 and 1983 [4].

Industry Impact

  • Washington tourism: Bloomberg-carried reporting said the race was expected to draw 140,000 people and generate about $210 million in tourism revenue for Washington [27].
  • Race operations: Penske and IndyCar covered most of the reported $35 million cost, while Bud Denker said the two-day event drew 215,000 people [56].
  • Local transport: The event brought downtown street closures, adding operational constraints for local traffic [53].

Historical Background

The Freedom 250 was tied to the semiquincentennial of the Declaration of Independence, signed in 1776 [common]. CBS said it was the first major auto race in the capital in more than 24 years [52].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Security fencing, barricades and downtown street closures surrounded the temporary course during the event [39][53]. Avoid driving near the National Mall during similar events and use official city closure maps before traveling.
  • Air travel: Nearby Reagan National Airport experienced a flight pause linked to race flyovers and event security [53][56]. Check airline and airport notices before same-day travel through Washington-area airports during major federal events.
