Dell restores XPS brand, launches redesigned 14, 16

Category: Technology

Dell has brought back the XPS name at CES 2026 after retiring it in 2025 as part of a simplified “Dell Premium” style naming scheme that many reviewers said confused buyers [1][2][5][12][8]. The comeback starts with newly redesigned XPS 14 and XPS 16 laptops, which Dell and hands-on previews describe as thinner and lighter than the prior generation and aimed at premium Windows buyers [1][15][6][17]. Several outlets report Dell also reversed a key usability complaint by replacing the prior capacitive function row with physical keys, while keeping an “invisible” trackpad concept with clearer boundaries [1][11][12]. Early coverage frames the move as a customer-friendly reset: clearer branding, refreshed hardware, and an attempt to rebuild momentum in Dell’s high-end laptop line [2][3][18].

Primary Source: View original article

This complicated brand we called Dell last year was trying to cover this very large consumer space with lots of similar products

— Jeff Clarke (Slashdot (via Gizmodo))

Did you know: Dell said the new XPS laptops will carry an XPS logo on the lid for the first time, leaning into clearer visual branding [12].

Key Points

  • New silicon: The XPS 14 and 16 are built around Intel’s Panther Lake/Core Ultra Series 3 platform, paired with Intel Arc integrated graphics, positioning them as “AI PC”-oriented machines [4][11][9][16].
  • Display options: Configurations include OLED options (reported as 2.8K on XPS 14 and 3.2K on XPS 16) as well as a 2K LCD choice, and some coverage notes a variable refresh range up to 120Hz plus a low-power 1Hz option [9][15][11].
  • Memory and storage: Reported maximum configurations reach up to 64GB of LPDDR5x memory and up to 4TB of storage, with a 70Wh battery listed across models in at least one spec roundup [9].
  • Pricing and timing: Initial configurations are slated to go on sale January 6 with starting prices around $2,049 for the XPS 14 and about $2,199 for the XPS 16, with additional configurations expected in February including some priced under $2,000 [12][7].
  • Roadmap hint: Multiple reports say Dell plans to add an even lighter XPS 13 later in 2026 as a lower-priced entry point, though outlets note timing remains vague beyond “later this year” [9][10][11][12][13].

Perspectives

Dell leadership: Dell’s COO framed the 2025 naming approach as overly complicated and said the company is refocusing XPS as its premium consumer laptop identity rather than chasing every competitor product category [3].

Product reviewers (usability focus): Hands-on coverage welcomed the return of a physical function row and other refinements that address prior complaints, describing the redesign as more practical for everyday use [1][11][6].

Tech press (brand clarity): Commentary across outlets argued the XPS label carried strong recognition and that replacing it with generic tier names hurt how easily shoppers could understand Dell’s lineup [2][12][8].

Timeline

  • CES 2026: Dell announces XPS return and redesigns 14 and 16 [5][18].
  • January 6: Select XPS 14/16 configurations go on sale [12][9].
  • February: Additional configurations arrive, including lower-priced options [12][7].

Technical Details

  • Intel Panther Lake / Core Ultra Series 3: A new Intel laptop platform cited for the 2026 XPS models; coverage ties it to on-device AI capabilities and efficiency-focused “AI PC” positioning [4][9][16].
  • Tandem OLED: A display approach referenced for the new XPS laptops’ OLED options, highlighted by reviewers as a premium screen upgrade compared with standard panels [4][15].
  • Variable refresh rate (1–120Hz): A screen feature reported for some configurations, letting the panel dynamically adjust refresh rate to balance smoothness and power savings; one outlet also notes a 1Hz option [15][11].

Industry Impact

  • Windows laptop branding: The reversal underscores how much brand continuity can matter in PCs, where shoppers often rely on familiar sub-brands (like XPS) as a shorthand for design and build quality [2][5][8].
  • AI PC positioning: By pairing the rebooted XPS line with Intel’s Core Ultra Series 3/Panther Lake, Dell is aligning its flagship consumer laptops with the industry’s push toward on-device AI features rather than purely higher peak CPU performance [9][16].
  • Design ergonomics trend: The move away from capacitive function rows and toward physical keys echoes a broader premium-laptop emphasis on tactile usability, suggesting manufacturers are weighting day-to-day interaction more heavily than minimalist experimentation [1][11][12].

Historical Background

Dell announced a major PC naming overhaul around CES 2025, dropping long-running labels like XPS (and, in some coverage, Inspiron/Latitude/Precision) in favor of a tiered “Dell,” “Dell Pro,” and “Dell Premium/Pro Max” approach [12][3][8]. In 2026, Dell reversed course for its top consumer machines, reinstating XPS as the premium consumer brand while keeping the newer naming structure for other segments [3][8][18].

Q&A

Q: What changes did Dell make to repairability in the 2026 redesign?

A: One spec roundup says the new XPS 14 and 16 include improved repairability as part of the rebuild [9].

Q: Will Dell keep the old names for Inspiron and Latitude too?

A: Reporting indicates Dell is not broadly restoring all legacy names; instead, it is primarily bringing back XPS while keeping other tiers like Dell Pro that it says “still tests well” [3][8].

Q: Are these XPS models expected to use discrete GPUs?

A: Early coverage emphasizes Intel Arc integrated graphics alongside Core Ultra Series 3 processors, and does not foreground discrete GPU configurations in the initial announcements [9][15].

Action Items

  • Compare OLED vs LCD configurations before ordering: If you prioritize image quality, look for the OLED options; if you want longer battery life, consider the 2K LCD configurations highlighted as more power-efficient in early coverage [15][9].
  • Wait for February configurations if price-sensitive: Several outlets say more variants arrive in February, including lower-priced options under $2,000, so waiting may broaden choices beyond launch-day SKUs [12][7].
