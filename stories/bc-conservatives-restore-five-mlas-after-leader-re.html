B.C. Conservatives restore five MLAs after leader resigns

Category: Canada

British Columbia’s Conservative caucus readmitted five legislators after former leader Kerry-Lynne Findlay resigned Sunday, Sept. 20, following weeks of defections, expulsions and internal conflict. Former leader John Rustad, former interim leader Trevor Halford, and MLAs Linda Hepner, Scott McInnis and Claire Rattée rejoined the caucus after interim leader Lorne Doerkson invited them back. Thirteen legislators had left or been expelled from the caucus during the upheaval [1][4][6]. The governing NDP caucus met Sunday to discuss the possibility of an early election. Findlay won the Conservative leadership in May but resigned less than four months later, and the party board appointed Doerkson interim leader. An election could take place while the Conservatives lack a permanent leader, and Findlay remains a candidate in the Abbotsford-Mission byelection scheduled for Sept. 26 [12][10][6].

Did you know: The Conservatives’ 2024 vote share was only 1.6 percentage points below the NDP’s despite the party holding 44 seats [13].

Key Points

  • Unity negotiations: Rustad said high-level talks were underway to reunite the Conservatives with the breakaway CentreBC party and said there was a 95 per cent chance of success [6][11].
  • Leadership constraint: The party constitution bars an interim leader from seeking the permanent leadership, although Rustad said Doerkson could still lead the party into an election [5][11].
  • Ballot controversy: The Abbotsford-Mission byelection was described as tainted by bribery accusations [10].
  • Near miss: The party came within three seats of forming government in 2024 [13].
