US and Cuba confirm rare reform talks in Havana

Category: World

The United States and Cuba have confirmed that officials from both governments met in Havana on April 10, opening a small diplomatic channel even as relations remain strained [2][3][4][5][6][7][9]. US officials pressed for reforms, including the release of political prisoners and compensation for assets seized after Cuba's 1959 revolution. Cuba said its top priority was ending the US energy blockade, which has deepened the island's economic and power crisis [1][8][4][5][6]. Accounts differ on how hard Washington pushed its message. El Pais reported that US officials gave Cuba a two-week ultimatum to free artist Luis Manuel Otero Alcantara, rapper Maykel Osorbo, and more than 1,000 political prisoners as a gesture of good faith [1]. Cuban officials, echoed by other reports, said the exchange was respectful and professional, and that neither side issued threats or formal deadlines [3][4][6][7]. Even with those differences, the confirmed contact suggests both sides still see some value in direct talks during a tense period, offering at least a narrow opening for future dialogue [2][3][4].

Key Points

  • US delegation: Cuba said the US side included assistant secretaries and other deputy-level State Department officials, while Havana was represented at the deputy foreign minister level [3][8].
  • Named prisoners: The two most prominently named detainees in the US push were artist Luis Manuel Otero Alcantara and rapper Maykel Osorbo, both of whom have been imprisoned for about five years, according to El Pais [1].
