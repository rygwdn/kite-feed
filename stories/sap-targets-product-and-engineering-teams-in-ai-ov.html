SAP targets product and engineering teams in AI overhaul

Category: AI

SAP has asked teams in product development, engineering and sales to identify potential reductions as it reorganizes its enterprise software business around artificial intelligence. People familiar with the discussions said earlier attempts to introduce AI products faced problems [2][13]. The plan under consideration would eliminate at least 10,000 jobs, but the final figure could change after labor negotiations [2][1]. At a May user conference, SAP introduced Joule Work, an interface intended to give employees a single point of access to its applications, along with Joule Assistants and Industry AI for sector-specific workflows [3]. SAP said its assistants would use more than 200 subagents to automate repetitive business tasks [3]. SAP has not confirmed the proposed cuts [2]. In the United States, employers cited AI in 121,401 job-cut announcements through October 2026, about 20% of the total, according to Challenger, Gray & Christmas [5]. SAP is expected to provide more detail with its full-year results in January [9].

Companies are spending billions of dollars on AI infrastructure, software and talent. At the same time, many are trimming roles viewed as overlapping, manual or less critical to future growth

— Krishna Iyer, corporate governance expert (NDTV)

Key Points

  • AI investments: SAP completed four acquisitions in 2026—Reltio, Dremio, Prior Labs and TechWolf—and made strategic investments in AI-focused firms Parloa, n8n and Conduct [5].
  • Previous overhaul: SAP’s 2024 restructuring relied mainly on voluntary departures and retraining; hiring for new roles kept overall headcount relatively flat [10].
  • German protections: An agreement negotiated by SAP’s German employee representatives protects employment through 2028 and provides alternative roles, training and preserved salary levels for affected employees [4].
  • Leadership changes: Two of SAP’s six executive board members are due to leave in March. Chief Executive Christian Klein took over most of product lead Muhammad Alam’s duties, while sales chief Thomas Saueressig plans to leave for Ionos [10].

Perspectives

SAP: The company said it regularly reviews its organization to keep it aligned with its strategy and customer needs. It declined to confirm whether cuts are planned [2].
