November Beaver Moon begins series of close full moons through February

Category: Science

November’s full Beaver Moon peaks at 9:53 a.m. Eastern time on Tuesday, Nov. 24, when it will be below the horizon in North America. Observers there can look for it rising around sunset. The moon will be about 224,170 miles from Earth, close enough to qualify as a supermoon under some definitions, and should appear only slightly larger and brighter than an average full moon [2][1][3]. The next full moon falls on Dec. 24 and is expected to be the closest in the sequence, at about 221,667 miles. Further full moons are due Jan. 22 and Feb. 20. Sources describe the sequence as either three or four consecutive supermoons [2][1]. Near moonrise, the moon may look especially large because of the horizon-related “moon illusion,” not because it is closer at that moment [3][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Pleiades pairing: On Nov. 24, the moon will pass about 0.9 degrees from the Pleiades star cluster, though its brightness may obscure the cluster’s fainter stars [3].
  • Best viewing: The moon will appear full to the naked eye on Monday and Wednesday as well as Tuesday [2][7].
  • Coastal tides: People’s Daily Digital reported coastal preparations with sandbags, berms and pumps in Laguna Beach and other shoreline areas ahead of possible high-tide flooding [5].
  • Other planets: On the night of Nov. 24-25, Mars and Jupiter are expected to be visible in the eastern sky, Saturn in the southwest, and Venus before dawn [6].

Timeline

  • 24 December 2026: Cold Moon expected to pass about 221,667 miles from Earth [2][9]
  • 22 January 2027: Wolf Moon expected as another close full Moon [2][1]
  • 20 February 2027: Snow Moon forecast to complete the four-month sequence [1][7]

Action Items

  • Look for the moon’s orange tint shortly after moonrise. Near the horizon, its light travels through more of the atmosphere, where blue wavelengths are scattered more strongly [9].
