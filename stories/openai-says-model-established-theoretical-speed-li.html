OpenAI says model established theoretical speed limit for matrix multiplication

Category: Science

OpenAI said on Nov. 25 that an internal artificial intelligence model had established a theoretical speed limit for multiplying matrices, a question that had occupied mathematicians and computer scientists for more than half a century [4]. The previous week, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge announced a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis [4][5]. The Riemann proof is encoded in more than 17 million lines of Lean, a proof-checking language. Human review continues, and a mathematician interviewed by NPR said the code passed checks despite its scale [7][5]. Some mathematicians warn that mass-producing results could strain research and education; others say AI lets them explore unfamiliar mathematical techniques more quickly [1][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Even though I have no understanding of the proof, and nobody does, I am more confident that this proof is correct than any proof I have ever encountered.

— Andrew Sutherland, MIT mathematician specializing in Lean (NPR)

Key Points

  • Cost of AI-generated results: OpenAI estimated that finding its Navier-Stokes solution cost $15 million, leaving a potential $14 million net loss even if it received the Clay Institute prize [1].

Perspectives

Kurusch Ebrahimi-Fard, mathematics professor and researcher at Simula’s SURE-AI center: He said researchers should neither ignore, mystify nor demonize AI [9].

Sources: forskning.no

Thomas Surowiec, deputy head of Simula’s SURE-AI center: He said students who let AI do their thinking learn little and called for broad mathematical understanding and skepticism when using the tools [9].

Sources: forskning.no

Association for Human Mathematics: Its members have committed to AI-free research that prioritizes human thinking and judgment in the mathematical sciences [1].

Timeline

  • October 2026: OpenAI published more than 700 papers on 372 open problems [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Implications of confirmation: Mathematicians have developed many results that assume the Riemann hypothesis is true; confirming it would establish those results as true, NPR reported [5].
