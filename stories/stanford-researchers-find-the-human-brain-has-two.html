Stanford researchers find the human brain has two developmental origins

Category: Science

A Stanford Medicine-led study published in Nature Neuroscience found that the human brain develops from two distinct groups of embryonic progenitor cells rather than a common source. One lineage produces the forebrain and midbrain, while the other produces the hindbrain, including the brain stem; researchers said the two systems evolved independently over hundreds of millions of years before becoming integrated [6][8][14]. Researchers identified the split by studying mouse embryos during gastrulation, an early stage of development when the body’s basic structure forms. They found that the two cell populations followed separate developmental paths [8][13]. The finding offers an explanation for why laboratories have struggled to produce hindbrain neurons and provides a model for studying brain-stem diseases, including spinal muscular atrophy and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis [6][4].

Perspectives

Stanford Medicine researchers performed lineage tracing in mouse embryos and assessed the finding’s relevance to humans through stem-cell experiments rather than direct tracing of human embryos [6][14].
