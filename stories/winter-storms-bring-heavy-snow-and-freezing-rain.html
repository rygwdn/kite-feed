Winter storms bring heavy snow and freezing rain

Category: Canada

Canadians across parts of the Prairies and Ontario are gearing up for a mid-week return to wintry weather. Environment Canada warnings point to heavy snow, falling temperatures, and tough travel conditions. On the Prairies, forecasts call for significant accumulations—up to about 40 cm near Edmonton—with driving expected to be hazardous in spots as vehicles get stuck and roads turn treacherous. [3][2][4] In Ontario, the concern is a messy mix: rain shifting to freezing rain and snow, with some areas expecting up to about 15 cm and wind gusts around 60 km/h. In Toronto, local coverage says freezing rain, snow, or ice pellets could complicate Wednesday’s commute. [1][5][4]

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Edmonton-area outlook: Environment Canada said the heaviest snow near Edmonton should taper later Tuesday, but colder air and hazardous roads remain a concern. [3]
  • Saskatchewan whiteouts: Advisories in Saskatchewan warn of whiteout conditions, with roughly 25–30 cm possible in some areas, and urge people to stay off the roads. [6]
  • Ontario timing: The storm in Ontario is expected to arrive around 3–4 a.m. Wednesday and last through much of the day, setting up a tricky morning commute. [5]
  • Snap back after mild spell: Toronto-area coverage notes the midweek wintry mix follows a stretch of milder weather, which can catch commuters off guard when conditions change quickly. [1]
  • Broader swath: The Globe and Mail reported winter weather warnings across parts of the Prairies, along with areas of Ontario facing snow and freezing rain. [4]

Perspectives

Environment Canada: Federal forecasters warn of a sharp return to winter across Prairie regions, with conditions likely to make travel worse in areas under warning. [3].

Timeline

  • This weekend: Toronto saw milder temperatures before winter returned midweek. [1]
  • Tuesday: The Prairie storm left vehicles stuck and roads treacherous in places. [3]
  • Wednesday: Ontario’s storm was expected to arrive around 3–4 a.m. and last through much of the day, raising commute concerns. [1][5]
  • Thursday morning: Snow and travel concerns were expected to linger in parts of Saskatchewan into Thursday morning. [4]

Action Items

  • Adjust Wednesday travel plans: If you’re in southern Ontario or Toronto, plan for freezing rain and snow, and consider skipping optional driving during the worst period. [1][5]
  • Prepare for Prairie driving disruptions: In affected Prairie areas, delay road travel when whiteouts are expected, and keep up with local warning updates. [6][3]
