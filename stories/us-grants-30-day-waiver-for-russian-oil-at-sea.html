US grants 30-day waiver for Russian oil at sea

Category: World

The United States issued a temporary, 30-day sanctions waiver that allows the delivery and sale of Russian oil and petroleum products already loaded onto vessels before March 12, a narrow easing that runs until April 11 [2][4][9][1][5]. US officials said the goal is to steady global energy markets after oil prices jumped following the Iran war [1][5][4][7]. Several outlets said the waiver opens a short window for cargoes that were effectively stuck at sea to be sold and delivered [2][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • India-like carve-out: Some reports described the move as expanding a carve-out previously linked to India to other buyers, as long as the Russian cargo was already at sea [3][7].
  • Economic backdrop: NDTV connected the waiver to broader financial strain, saying the Iran war has pushed up oil prices and rattled markets [6].
  • Cargo volume estimate: Around 19 million barrels of Russian crude and fuel on roughly 30 tankers in Asian waters were cited as potentially available for purchase [8].

Perspectives

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent: Bessent said the waiver is meant to help stabilize energy markets unsettled by the Iran war.

Sources: The Straits Times

US Treasury Department: The department issued a time-limited license that permits the delivery and sale of Russian oil already loaded onto vessels before March 12, effective for 30 days.

Sources: DW

The Hindu: The outlet said the license follows other steps aimed at easing energy pressure, including a Department of Energy announcement of a strategic petroleum reserve release.
