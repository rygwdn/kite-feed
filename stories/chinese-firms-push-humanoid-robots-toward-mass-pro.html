Chinese firms push humanoid robots toward mass production

Category: Technology

Chinese robotics companies are moving humanoid robots from demonstrations toward commercial products, led by UBTech’s U1, a full-sized humanoid unveiled in Shenzhen on June 30 with silicone skin, blinking lashes, manicured nails and male and female versions [1][6][5]. UBTech said the U1 had more than 13,000 orders by the end of its launch day and that deliveries would begin in September [1]. UBTech is marketing the U1 as a companion robot for single people and older users. Cameras in its eyes, chest sensors and microphones support conversation, listening and hand-holding functions [6][5]. The basic model costs 119,800 yuan, or about $17,600, while an “Ultra” version costs 990,000 yuan, and higher-priced versions can be customized to resemble a loved one, celebrity or imaginary character [6]. Chinese start-ups are also racing to build dexterous robotic hands, a technical barrier that researchers and companies see as necessary for humanoids to perform practical tasks rather than staged demonstrations [2]. China is promoting “embodied AI” while facing an aging and shrinking workforce, and Tesla’s Optimus robot remains behind earlier production targets described by Elon Musk [2][1].

Historical Background

Tesla introduced the Tesla Bot concept in 2021 and later moved through several production-readiness targets that slipped into 2025 and 2026 [1]. In China, public attention accelerated after Unitree’s dancing humanoids appeared at the 2025 Spring Gala, the nationally televised Lunar New Year variety show [2].

Business Angle

  • Companies that can ship reliable robots at scale may build a manufacturing advantage, but launch-day orders alone do not prove durable demand [1][common].
  • The U1's wide price range suggests early humanoid markets may begin with affluent consumers or specialized buyers rather than mainstream households [6][common].
  • Robotic hands could become a key competitive bottleneck because useful humanoids need dexterous manipulation, not just speech or mobility [2].
