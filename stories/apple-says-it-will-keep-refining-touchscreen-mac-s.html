Apple says it will keep refining touchscreen Mac software after launch

Category: Technology

Apple says it is only beginning to adapt macOS for touch as its first touchscreen MacBook Pro approaches its Nov. 6 release. The 14- and 16-inch laptops, announced Oct. 28, pair touch input with tandem OLED displays and M5 Pro or M5 Max chips [4][26][45]. The software keeps the keyboard and trackpad central: macOS can treat many touches as clicks, while interface controls such as menus and window buttons enlarge when users touch them. Apple said it plans further refinements but did not announce a schedule [8][15][4]. The touchscreen models start at $2,899, while Apple continues to sell non-touch MacBook Pros with the same M5 Pro and M5 Max chip options [25][7]. A Wall Street Journal reviewer found touch helpful for panning a map and moving a video-editing timeline, but reported arm fatigue and small targets during general use [65]. Preorders are open, and Apple says customer deliveries and store availability begin Nov. 6 [86].

Key Points

  • Touch controls: A swipe up from the screen’s bottom edge reveals the Dock [15].
  • Optional input: Users can switch off touch input in Accessibility settings while continuing to use the keyboard and trackpad; a separate setting enables pinch-to-zoom across macOS [84][85].
  • Design changes: The redesigned 16-inch model weighs about 1.82 kilograms, roughly 320 grams less than its predecessor, and is nearly a millimeter thinner [19].
  • Price ceiling: A configured 16-inch model with M5 Max, 128GB of memory and 16TB of storage costs $14,549 [24].
  • Developer use: A Digital Trends demonstration showed a developer interacting directly with a touch-based iPhone app simulator in Xcode while keeping the development environment visible [28].

Technical Details

  • Integrated touch sensor: Apple integrated the multi-touch sensor into the display panel rather than layering it on top, a design intended to reduce latency and bulk [25].
  • Dynamic Island: On the new MacBook Pro, users can pull down on the Dynamic Island to open an expanded floating widget with access to Siri responses and Spotlight search [15].

Industry Impact

  • Touch-enabled PCs: Computerworld cited IDC data saying enterprises buy 66% of touch notebooks, identifying business buyers as a major segment for touch-enabled laptops [60].
  • PC demand: Apple’s PC shipments also declined in the third quarter, but the drop was less severe than the industry average [48].

Historical Background

Apple had already offered an indirect route to touch through Sidecar, which turns an iPad into a wireless external display for a Mac; macOS 27 adds finger input on that screen [9].
