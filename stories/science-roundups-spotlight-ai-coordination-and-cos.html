Science roundups spotlight AI coordination and cosmic object

Category: Science

ScienceAlert’s Aug. 15 weekly science roundup highlighted a study in which 1,000 AI agents, given a choice between two meaningless options, sometimes converged on the same answer without rewards, cooperation instructions or a leader [1]. The roundup also listed items on a NASA rover finding an unexpected gemstone-like signal in a Martian rock, dolphins filmed using shells while hunting off eastern Australia, declining vegetable diversity, an early food-allergy treatment trial and drought risks for some red wines [1][2]. SingularityHub’s Aug. 15 technology roundup linked to reports on researchers and startups looking beyond transformer-based large language models; a proposed solar-system-sized, red-glowing “black hole star”; and Quanta Magazine’s account of cell biologist Junyue Cao’s view that aging may unfold in discrete molecular stages rather than as simple wear and tear [3]. The roundup said measurements of the proposed black hole star showed energy output 100 billion times greater than any known star can produce, closer to black holes than stars [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Transformers are starting to show their age. Many of the recent advances in LLMs, such as the development of so-called reasoning models and their ability to handle large amounts of input at once, are not neat extensions of that core technology but workarounds that patch over some of its fundamental flaws.

— Will Douglas Heaven (MIT Technology Review via SingularityHub)

Key Points

  • AI behavior: ScienceAlert said the AI-agent finding could be useful or dangerous because large agent groups may coordinate without central control [1].
  • Aging stages: Quanta Magazine’s reported account, summarized by SingularityHub, said Cao used systemwide mouse data to define aging stages by molecular signals and changing cell populations [3].

Scientific Significance

  • AI coordination risk: The AI-agent result tests whether coordination can arise without explicit incentives as autonomous systems interact in shared environments [1].
