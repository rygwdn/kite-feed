T. rex fossil Gus sells for record $50.1 million

Category: Science

A 67-million-year-old Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton nicknamed “Gus” sold for $50.1 million including fees at Sotheby’s in New York on Tuesday, setting a record for any dinosaur fossil sold at auction [8][20][10][3]. Sotheby’s did not identify the buyer; news reports said the winning bid came by phone after about 10 minutes of bidding involving seven bidders [20][10][32][3]. The fossil was found in 2021 on a cattle ranch in Harding County, South Dakota, and named for landowner Gary “Gus” Licking [20][15][3]. Sotheby’s described it as one of the largest and most complete T. rex specimens known, about 38 feet long and 12.5 feet tall, with 183 fossil bone elements and roughly 61% completeness by bone count [10][15][32][3]. The sale topped the previous fossil auction record set in 2024 by the stegosaurus “Apex,” which sold at Sotheby’s for $44.6 million [8][10][15][3].

Key Points

  • Estimate surpassed: Sotheby’s had estimated Gus at $20 million to $30 million before the auction, but the final price far exceeded that range [20][10][15][32].
  • Preparation work: The bones were excavated during three summers from 2021 to 2023, then required about three additional years of laboratory preparation and assembly [8][31][56].
  • Research access: The Conversation cited a 2025 study that counted 61 T. rex fossils in public trusts and 71 privately held, a split relevant to debates over scientific access [13].

Perspectives

Sotheby’s: Cassandra Hatton said the price reflected the time and resources required to uncover, document and prepare Gus, including excavation over multiple summers and years of laboratory work.

Technical Details

  • Completeness metrics: Sotheby’s described Gus as about 61% complete by bone count but 75% to 80% complete by estimated original bone mass, reflecting different ways of measuring specimen completeness [15][32].
  • Gastralia preservation: The specimen includes 30 gastralia, or belly ribs, which are often less represented in mounted dinosaur fossils and useful for reconstructing body shape [10][common].
  • Skull mounting: The original skull was too heavy for Sotheby’s to mount on the displayed skeleton, so the real skull was shown separately while a lightweight reproduction sat on the mount [22].

Industry Impact

  • Museum collecting: Stephen Brusatte of the University of Edinburgh told The Guardian he worried museums and academic institutions cannot compete with prices reached at fossil auctions [9][58].
  • Public display: Apex, the previous record-setting stegosaurus, was bought by Ken Griffin in 2024 and loaned to the American Museum of Natural History, a precedent cited while Gus’s destination remains undisclosed [15][31].

Scientific Significance

  • Research value: Gus preserves anatomical and pathological evidence from a large adult T. rex, including skull bite marks and healed rib injuries that can inform studies of behavior, trauma and survival [8][32].
  • Unknown access: The buyer has not been disclosed, so researchers and the public do not yet know whether Gus will enter a museum, remain privately displayed, or be made available under loan terms [8][20][3].

Historical Background

T. rex fossils have drawn record auction prices in recent years. “Stan” sold for nearly $32 million in 2020, before Gus exceeded that mark. The Hell Creek Formation, which spans parts of Montana, Wyoming and the Dakotas, has yielded many T. rex fossils; one of the first T. rex skeletons was found there in 1902, and the species name was based on fossils from that region [15].
