B.C. nurses end strike, prepare for wage arbitration

Category: Canada

British Columbia's unionized nurses are ending provincewide job action after the Nurses Bargaining Association and the province accepted settlement recommendations from mediators Amanda Rogers and Vince Ready after just over a week of mediation [5][6]. The BC Nurses' Union represents 55,000 nurses, and the union said all job action would cease as of midnight Friday while members review the offer [1][5][4]. Compensation remains unresolved; the issue is expected to go to binding arbitration this fall, with the province offering 12 per cent over four years and the union seeking 32 per cent [5][6][3]. The union said the non-binding recommendations address some major concerns raised when members rejected the last deal, including workplace violence and non-nursing duties; the B.C. government said mediators will keep working with the parties before the next steps in the process [5].

Key Points

  • Rejected deal: Job action began at the start of July after union members rejected a tentative contract reached in May with the Health Employers Association of B.C. [4][5].
  • Service limits: Essential services remained in place during the dispute, while nurses refused certain overtime and non-nursing duties [5].
  • Intimidation complaints: Union president Adriane Gear said nurses filed thousands of complaints alleging employers intimidated them when they took job action [5].

Industry Impact

  • Hospital staffing: The mediation recommendations call for a Health Ministry review of work currently done by nurses, with the stated goal of maximizing time spent on direct patient care rather than tasks such as portering, transport, clerical work, administration, housekeeping and maintenance [6].
  • Workplace safety: Mediators recommended a study to identify where nurses and other health-care workers face the greatest risk of violence, including whether hospital relational-security officers should become special constables and whether a flagging system for violent patients should be assessed [6].
