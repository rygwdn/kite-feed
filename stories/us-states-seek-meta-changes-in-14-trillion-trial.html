US states seek Meta changes in $1.4 trillion trial

Category: Technology

A federal trial scheduled to begin Tuesday in Oakland, California, will test claims by U.S. states that Meta designed Facebook and Instagram features to keep children and teenagers on the platforms and collected data on children under 13 without parental consent, allegedly violating federal law [3][5][7]. The case grew out of a 2023 lawsuit by 30 states, including California and New York [4][2][3]. The states seek financial damages, which AP reported could total as much as $1.4 trillion, along with court-ordered changes to Facebook and Instagram [3][5][7]. Requested changes include ending public like counts, infinite scrolling feeds, autoplay videos, certain appearance-changing image filters, multiple-account creation and disappearing posts such as Instagram Stories, while adding parental verification for teen users [4][2][9][13]. Meta disputes the allegations [8]. The Los Angeles Times reported that a federal judge dealt the company a setback over evidence tied to a key witness days before trial [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Product demands: The states also want parental verification for teenage users, removal of appearance-altering image filters, an end to video autoplay, restrictions on multiple accounts and removal of disappearing posts such as Instagram Stories [4][2][17][9][13].
  • Prior ruling: BBC reported that Meta has lost successive court challenges over claims involving how its platforms targeted and harmed young users [4].
  • New Mexico: A separate New Mexico case resulted in a $942 million fine against Meta and a finding that the company created a public nuisance [2][10][14].
  • Data claim: The lawsuit alleges Meta routinely collected data on children under 13 without parental consent, which the states say violated federal law [3][16].
  • Design claims: The lawsuit says Meta used powerful technologies to entice and ensnare young users while pursuing profit [3].

Perspectives

Meta: The company denies the allegations and said it would show that it has long prioritized support for young people.

State plaintiffs: The states argue Meta chose to exploit young users and made it harder for them to reduce use through repeated notifications designed to draw them back into the apps.

Industry Impact

  • Social media design: A state victory could pressure platforms beyond Meta to reassess infinite scroll, autoplay, likes, Stories-like formats and youth recommendation systems because the states seek remedies targeting those features [4][2][13].
  • Platform design: A ruling requiring Meta to remove features like infinite scroll and autoplay for young users could force other social media companies to reevaluate their own engagement mechanics for youth safety [4][2][13].
  • Digital advertising: If youth-facing engagement features are restricted, Meta may face lower usage time among younger audiences, which could affect ad inventory on Facebook and Instagram [4][2].
  • Litigation risk: AP reported Meta faces thousands of child-safety lawsuits, with this California trial described as potentially the most consequential among them [3][5].
  • Youth data: The complaint alleges Meta collected data from children under 13 without parental consent, a claim that could shape how platforms verify age and obtain permission for young users [3][12].

Business Angle

  • A ruling that treats youth-safety design as a public-nuisance or privacy-law issue could raise compliance costs for other social platforms that use similar engagement features [2][common].
  • Court remedies that reduce time spent on Facebook and Instagram could affect an advertising business that relies on user attention and ad inventory [4][common].
  • If trial evidence supports Meta’s account of its youth-safety work, the company may limit operational remedies even if it still faces continued state-by-state litigation [3][16].
