Former Anthropic researcher to testify at New York AI safeguards hearing

Category: AI

Former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon is set to testify Monday, Oct. 5, at a New York City Council hearing on AI, alongside representatives of Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta [13][9][14]. Council Speaker Julie Menin requested his testimony as lawmakers consider bills to establish safeguards for the technology [13][14]. Coxon left Anthropic in September and warned that increasingly powerful AI could become impossible to control. He accused Anthropic and OpenAI of “gambling with our lives” [13][15]. Former Google DeepMind researcher Alex Turner and Daniel Kokotajlo, a former OpenAI researcher who leads the AI Futures Project, are also expected to testify [13]. New York City is seeking a larger role in regulating an industry that has expanded in the city, while much AI regulation remains at the federal and state levels [13]. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google agreed to attend after Menin’s office warned they could be subpoenaed; the council subpoenaed SpaceX’s AI unit after it did not respond to requests to appear [13].

Key Points

  • Proposed penalties: A bill sponsored by Menin would give whistleblowers a share of fines or penalties recovered from AI companies after reported violations [13].
  • Legal claims: Other proposals would let people harmed by AI systems sue developers in certain circumstances and require independent validation of some systems before the city deploys them [13].
  • Company witnesses: The companies plan to send policy and safety officials; Turner confirmed he would attend, while Google declined to comment [13].
  • Earlier commitment: Meta had agreed to attend before the other companies committed following the warning from Menin’s office [13].
