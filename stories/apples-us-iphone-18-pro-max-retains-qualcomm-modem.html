Apple’s U.S. iPhone 18 Pro Max retains Qualcomm modem

Category: Technology

A TechInsights teardown found that Apple’s U.S. iPhone 18 Pro Max uses Qualcomm’s Snapdragon X80 5G modem rather than Apple’s in-house C2 modem. The U.S. model, A3473, differs from the smaller iPhone 18 Pro, which uses C2 in the United States, and from Pro Max models sold outside the United States, which also use C2 [1][2][3][5]. Apple’s U.S. specifications page lists C2 for the iPhone 18 Pro but leaves the Pro Max modem field blank. Specifications for Canada, the United Kingdom, France and India list C2 for both Pro models [1][2][5]. The reason for the regional split is unclear [1][2].

Key Points

  • The iPhone 18 Duo uses Apple’s C2 modem worldwide, extending the company’s in-house modem beyond the two Pro models [5][8].

Perspectives

TechInsights identified Qualcomm’s SDR875 RF transceiver alongside the SDX80M modem in the U.S. unit [1][3].

Sources: The Mac Observer, Digital Trends

Technical Details

  • Model-specific RF design: The U.S. Pro Max pairs the Qualcomm SDX80M modem with an Apple-designed mmWave antenna module [1][8].
  • Wireless connectivity: Apple’s N1 wireless combo module handles Wi-Fi separately from the cellular modem in the U.S. handset [7].

Industry Impact

  • The iPhone 18 Pro is the first Pro iPhone to use an in-house modem worldwide, while the Pro Max uses different modems by market [8][5].
  • Component sourcing: The teardown identified components from Skyworks, Broadcom, Qorvo, Texas Instruments and NXP alongside Apple-designed chips [2][7].

Action Items

  • Before buying a U.S. Pro Max, review regional technical specifications or confirm the exact model number, as U.S. and international versions use different cellular modem hardware [5][2].
  • Check mmWave requirements separately: The U.S. Pro Max has an Apple-designed mmWave antenna module despite using a Qualcomm cellular modem, so buyers should verify carrier and local support for the bands they need [1][8].
