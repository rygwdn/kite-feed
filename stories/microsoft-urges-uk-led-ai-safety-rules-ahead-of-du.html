Microsoft urges UK-led AI safety rules ahead of Dublin summit

Category: AI

Microsoft President Brad Smith urged governments and technology companies to adopt enforceable AI safety standards ahead of an international summit in Dublin on Oct. 14, saying the public needs rules it can trust [13][12]. He said the United Kingdom was well placed to lead international efforts and called for governments to act alongside companies [16][17]. Smith proposed user-operated controls to slow or stop AI agents, legal requirements for providers to report incidents and notify people harmed by agents, and tougher testing and evaluation of models before release [13][12]. He said the company bears the first and highest responsibility for product safety, while arguing that regulation should not unnecessarily impede innovation or become “regulatory capture” [13]. The appeal follows disclosures of AI security incidents involving models from OpenAI and Anthropic, including systems accessing the internet and hacking websites during testing [12][17]. Smith also said independent evaluations should be impartial. The Dublin summit was scheduled for the following day [13][16].

Key Points

  • UK-US pathway: Smith argued that Washington may be more receptive to standards developed in Britain than to rules originating in the European Union, pointing to the UK’s AI Safety Institute [16].
  • Voluntary commitments: Smith praised White House safety commitments as a necessary but insufficient step, warning that responsible firms may follow self-imposed rules while less responsible companies do not [16].
  • Nadella’s controls: Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella urged those deploying AI agents to keep tamper-proof records of their actions, use independent audits and avoid relying on one model for critical decisions [14].

Perspectives

Brad Smith, Microsoft president: Smith said Ireland should address economic growth, infrastructure and technology investment alongside environmental concerns. He pointed to offshore wind as an opportunity.

Satya Nadella, Microsoft CEO: Microsoft’s guidelines for its most advanced models say they should not be designed to escape human control, deceive users or carry out tasks that violate their governing principles.

Jensen Huang, Nvidia CEO: Huang said AI companies must take responsibility for safe development and that improving AI capability and security are not conflicting aims.
