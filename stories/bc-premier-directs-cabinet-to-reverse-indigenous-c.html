B.C. premier directs cabinet to reverse Indigenous, climate and drug policies

Category: Canada

British Columbia Premier Lorne Doerkson has directed his new cabinet to reverse key policies on Indigenous rights, climate action and substance use, while pursuing tax relief and tighter government spending. The government released the mandate letters Tuesday, after Doerkson and his cabinet were sworn in on Friday [2]. The government says ministers must focus on affordability, economic growth, health care, community safety and provincial pride, and submit new funding requests to the Treasury Board for an affordability review [1][3]. The letters also call for an independent review of provincial finances as the government pledges to address a record $13.8-billion deficit [2].

Primary Source: View original article

British Columbians voted for change, and our government is getting to work delivering it.

— B.C. Premier Lorne Doerkson (Government of British Columbia)

Key Points

  • Tax changes: Finance Minister Peter Milobar is directed to raise the basic personal tax exemption to $32,000 from $13,216 for the 2027 tax year [2].
  • Health guarantee: Health Minister Ryan Peterson is directed to make publicly funded treatment available through another quality provider when medically recommended wait times are exceeded [2].
  • School policy: Education Minister Lynne Block is directed to restore letter grades for Grades 4-9 and provincial exams, and repeal the optional SOGI 123 teaching resource [2].

Perspectives

Government of British Columbia: The government says every minister must commit to a measurable result and delivery date for the five goals, and ministries must jointly develop commitments that span their responsibilities [3].

Sources: Government of British Columbia

National Observer columnist: The columnist warns that likely rollbacks to CleanBC, building and vehicle mandates, and industrial carbon pricing could leave B.C. emissions rising again [4].

Sources: Canada's National Observer

Historical Background

A National Observer columnist points to Gordon Campbell's government in 2001, saying it launched a fiscal review that preceded program cuts, privatization and deregulation. The columnist argues that Doerkson's planned fiscal review resembles that approach, while noting that the two governments had different election platforms [4].
