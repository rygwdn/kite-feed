Early polar-vortex disruption raises risk of colder European winter

Category: Science

Meteorologists have detected an unusually early disruption of the Arctic polar vortex, about three weeks ahead of the earliest comparable events in modern records. The disruption could bring colder weather and more snow to parts of Europe in December [1][3][7]. A weakened vortex can alter atmospheric circulation and allow Arctic air to move farther south, but it does not guarantee a cold spell at the surface [3][6]. Seasonal simulations suggest below-normal temperatures and increased snowfall across much of Europe in December, while January’s colder weather may shift toward western, central and northern Europe [2][3]. A very strong El Niño could alter or counteract the vortex-related pattern, and meteorological agencies say the outcome remains uncertain [2][3][5][7]. The UK Met Office says the early timing could also make the effects less pronounced because the Arctic and Siberian regions that supply the cold air are not yet as cold as they are later in winter [7].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Stratospheric signals: Winds roughly 30 kilometers above Earth have weakened sharply. ECMWF and GEFS forecasting models indicate a possible wind reversal and temperatures more than 27°C above seasonal norms in some stratospheric regions [3][2].
  • January snowfall: Simulations indicate heavier snowfall in western, central and northwestern Europe, where cold air could meet moisture from the Atlantic and Mediterranean. Above-normal temperatures are possible in eastern Europe [3][2].
  • Seasonal cycle: The vortex normally strengthens through autumn and peaks around January. Major disruptions are most common from January to March [5][6].
