Chinese graduates face record hiring squeeze as AI spreads

Category: AI

China’s 2026 graduates are entering a strained labor market as about 12.7 million people leave college and graduate school, a record cohort, while employers adopt artificial intelligence in more functions [1][10]. China’s urban unemployment rate for people ages 16 to 24 was 17.9% in July, according to National Bureau of Statistics data cited by EToday, and job supply has not kept pace with the rising number of graduates [10]. Outside China, UBS requires graduates and interns applying for roles at the Swiss bank to show they can use AI to improve outcomes and efficiency, while NZZ reported that AI competence has become career-relevant at the bank [6][9]. A Peterson Institute event scheduled for November 17-18, 2026, says AI can create jobs and raise productivity while automating some tasks and eliminating some existing jobs [8].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Applicant strain: A 26-year-old Hong Kong University graduate from northern China applied to 150 internships and entry-level jobs in Hong Kong and mainland China without success, EToday reported [10].
  • Skills demand: Mixnews described AI engineering as a growing field that requires programming, mathematics, algorithms, data work, problem-solving ability, English and continuous learning [7].
  • Demographic link: PIIE said aging populations can push firms toward labor-saving technologies, while the fastest population growth is expected in places where digital connectivity and AI-leverage potential remain lowest [8].

Perspectives

Peterson Institute conference organizers: The IMF, OECD, PIIE and World Bank conference agenda links AI and demographic change, saying they may alter specialization patterns, labor allocation and policy needs.

Sources: Peterson Institute for International Economics

Industry Impact

  • Education providers: EToday reported that one graduate paid $400 for an AI training course to improve competitiveness, showing demand for AI credentials among job seekers [10].
  • Media production: EToday cited a broadcasting graduate who noticed more job listings connected to AI-produced dramas and programs [10].

Historical Background

China has expanded university enrollment for years, increasing the number of graduates entering the job market, while job creation for graduates has not kept pace, according to EToday’s account of the New York Times report [10]. Rapid AI adoption has added to that employment imbalance, with government support and public enthusiasm helping spread the technology quickly in China [10].

Business Angle

  • Banks that require practical AI proficiency may gain efficiency if the tools increase analyst output without lowering review quality [6][common].
  • AI-engineering demand favors firms and education providers that teach implementation skills, not only basic tool use [7][common].

Action Items

  • Build a work sample: Create a small AI-enabled project tied to your target role, because UBS and other employers are asking candidates to demonstrate practical AI use rather than general familiarity [6][9].
  • Prioritize implementation skills: Study programming, data analysis, algorithms and mathematics if pursuing AI engineering, since Mixnews identifies those as core requirements for building deployable AI systems [7].
