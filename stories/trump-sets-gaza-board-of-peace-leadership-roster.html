Trump sets Gaza ‘Board of Peace’ leadership roster

Category: World

The White House has announced a “Board of Peace” that President Trump will chair to supervise a transitional period in Gaza focused on governance, reconstruction and investment, as part of a second phase of a U.S. plan following an October ceasefire arrangement [2][3][4][13][8][9]. Named members include US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, former UK prime minister Tony Blair, Jared Kushner, US envoy Steve Witkoff, World Bank Group president Ajay Banga, Apollo Global Management CEO Marc Rowan and others [3][4][5][9][16][12]. In parallel, a Palestinian technocratic committee intended to help administer Gaza under U.S. supervision held its first meeting in Cairo, with its leader Ali Shaath saying the group aims to move quickly on urgent needs like shelter and anticipates reconstruction taking about three years [10][5][3]. While supporters portray the initiative as a practical step toward stabilizing and rebuilding a war-damaged territory, critics in the region and beyond object to some appointments and to the U.S.-led oversight model for Gaza’s future governance [7][6][3].

Today, on behalf of President Trump, we are announcing the launch of Phase Two… moving from ceasefire to demilitarization, technocratic governance, and reconstruction.

— Steve Witkoff (Dawn)

Did you know: Le Monde notes Tony Blair worked on the Israeli-Palestinian issue for years after leaving office as a representative tied to the international ‘Middle East Quartet’ framework [6].

Key Points

  • Security force: President Trump also appointed a U.S. officer to lead a nascent security force envisioned for post-war Gaza, according to reports on the plan’s security track [1][6][14].
  • Phase two focus: Envoy Steve Witkoff described the shift as moving “from ceasefire to demilitarization, technocratic governance, and reconstruction,” and warned Hamas of “serious consequences” if it does not meet obligations, including returning a final deceased hostage [4].
  • Ceasefire context: Reporting notes Gaza has still seen deadly violence despite a ceasefire that took effect in October, underscoring the fragility of the security environment the plan aims to address [15].
  • Regional mediators: Witkoff publicly credited Egypt, Turkey and Qatar as “indispensable” in advancing the earlier ceasefire-and-exchange agreement that preceded this new governance and rebuilding push [4].
  • Board remit: The White House described the board’s portfolio as spanning governance capacity-building, regional relations and reconstruction financing, including investment attraction and large-scale capital mobilization [3][4].

Perspectives

Timeline

  • early October: Ceasefire and hostage exchange agreement reached, launching initial phase [3].
  • Thursday: NCAG-led Palestinian technocratic body held its first meeting in Cairo [3].
  • Friday: White House announced ‘Board of Peace’ members and phase-two launch [4][13].

Technical Details

  • Transitional technocratic administration: The plan envisions a Palestinian-led technocratic body—the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG)—as a transitional governing mechanism under the broader U.S.-supervised framework [4][3].
  • Demilitarization objective: Phase two is described as aiming for “full demilitarization,” including disarming “unauthorized personnel,” linking governance and reconstruction to security arrangements [4].
  • Capital mobilization: The board’s mandate explicitly includes attracting investment, arranging large-scale funding and mobilizing capital for reconstruction, with involvement from finance and development leaders [3][9][16].

Industry Impact

  • Reconstruction finance: By pairing political oversight with institutions and executives tied to development and private capital, the plan signals a push to blend public-sector coordination with investment-led rebuilding [3][9][16].
  • Humanitarian logistics: Witkoff’s account of phase one emphasizes large humanitarian aid flows, and phase two’s stated pivot suggests aid delivery may increasingly link to governance capacity and security compliance [4].
  • Diplomacy ecosystem: The continued role of regional mediators (Egypt, Turkey, Qatar) highlights how third-party diplomacy remains central to sustaining agreements and enabling reconstruction planning [4].

Historical Background

Several outlets frame the new board as “phase two” of a broader U.S. plan that follows an October ceasefire arrangement and hostage exchange framework [4][3][15]. Tony Blair has been involved in Israeli-Palestinian diplomacy before, including years working on the issue after leaving office as part of an international Quartet structure, which helps explain why some see him as experienced while others remain skeptical [6].

Q&A

Q: What specific portfolios or responsibilities will each named board member oversee?

A: The White House statement describes broad portfolios—governance capacity-building, regional relations, reconstruction, investment attraction, funding and capital mobilization—but does not spell out a detailed division of labor in the reporting provided [3][4].

Q: What criteria will the plan use to judge whether demilitarization is being achieved?

A: Witkoff describes demilitarization as including disarmament of “unauthorized personnel,” but the public accounts here do not provide measurable benchmarks or verification mechanisms [4].

Q: How will the transitional Gaza administration relate to existing Palestinian political institutions?

A: Sources describe a technocratic Palestinian committee/NCAG intended to govern during a transitional period under U.S. supervision, but they do not detail formal institutional links to other Palestinian bodies in the reporting provided [3][5][10].
