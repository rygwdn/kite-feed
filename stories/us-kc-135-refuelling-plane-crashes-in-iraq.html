U.S. KC-135 refuelling plane crashes in Iraq

Category: World

A U.S. Air Force KC-135 aerial refuelling aircraft crashed in western Iraq on Thursday while supporting U.S. operations against Iran, U.S. Central Command said. Centcom added that another aircraft involved landed safely [1][6][8][10][11]. The military said the incident took place in “friendly airspace” during Operation Epic Fury and was not caused by hostile fire or friendly fire [1][4][5][9][10]. As rescue efforts continued, the U.S. military later confirmed that four people were killed in the crash [7][12][14]. The crash comes as the broader U.S.-Israel war with Iran, which multiple outlets say is in its second week, continues [10].

Primary Source: View original article

The situation with Iran is moving along very rapidly,

— President Donald Trump (Politico)

Key Points

  • Crew reports: A U.S. official told CBS News the crew’s status was initially unknown after the tanker went down in western Iraq [2].
  • Competing claims: The Islamic Resistance in Iraq said it shot down the KC-135 “in defence of our country’s sovereignty and airspace,” a claim that conflicts with Centcom’s statement that the crash was not due to hostile fire [5][9].
  • War losses: The Hindu said the KC-135 was at least the fourth U.S. military aircraft lost in the conflict, after three U.S. F-15s were shot down by friendly fire over Kuwait [4]. Politico reported that all six crew members from those fighter-jet losses ejected safely [10].
  • Trump remarks: President Donald Trump has offered shifting public assessments of the campaign’s progress in recent days, including saying the war was “very complete” in a CBS interview and later that it was “moving along very rapidly” at a White House event [10].

Perspectives

U.S. Central Command: In its initial statement, Centcom called the downed KC-135 “a loss” but did not describe the crew’s condition or report casualties as rescue efforts were under way [1].

Sources: South China Morning Post

Islamic Resistance in Iraq: The group said it brought down the aircraft and framed it as a move to defend Iraq’s sovereignty and airspace [5].

Sources: Dawn

Pentagon (as cited by Politico): The Pentagon says seven U.S. service members have been killed and about 140 wounded in the war so far, adding broader context for the crash [10].
