Update: US probe blames outdated data in Iran school strike

Category: World

A preliminary U.S. military inquiry has found that U.S. forces were likely responsible for the Feb. 28 Tomahawk missile strike that hit Shajarah Tayyebeh Elementary School (a girls’ school) in Minab, southern Iran, killing more than 150 people—many of them children [11][1][13][3][15][12][2]. Multiple reports say the strike may have stemmed from outdated targeting data that wrongly identified the site as a military facility [11][1][3][12][2]. The incident has become a focal point for broader concern about civilian deaths during the U.S.-Israeli attacks on Iran that began Feb. 28 [2][5][15]. Investigators are still reviewing how older intelligence stayed in the system and was used in planning [11][3][2]. President Trump initially suggested Iran might be responsible. He later said he could “live with” whatever the investigation finds, and when asked afterward about the report, he said he did not know about it [3][11][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Operational context: The strike came in the opening wave of attacks in the Minab area aimed at facilities tied to the IRGC navy. Reporting says the school sits on the same block as IRGC-linked buildings [2][11][3].
  • Scale of incident: Some coverage calls it the deadliest single strike of the campaign so far, intensifying scrutiny of early-war targeting decisions [2][5].
  • Human error emphasis: Preliminary findings cited in the New York Times said the mistake was unlikely to be mainly driven by newer AI tools, and instead pointed to people relying on outdated intelligence [2].

Perspectives

US Senate Democrats: Senate Democrats urged Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to move quickly, investigate fully, and explain whether U.S. actions caused civilian harm.

Sources: Al-Monitor (Reuters)

US military investigators (as described): Investigators are looking at how outdated Defense Intelligence Agency data was used—and why standard review steps did not catch or revalidate it.

Sources: The New York Times
