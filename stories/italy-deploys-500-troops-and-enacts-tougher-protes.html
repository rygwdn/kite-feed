Italy deploys 500 troops and enacts tougher protest laws after Milan riots

Category: World

Italy’s cabinet approved a decree on Tuesday deploying 500 soldiers to Milan through the end of 2026 and introducing tougher penalties after cost-of-living protests turned violent in the city and other northern towns. The decree took effect immediately, but parliament must convert it into law within 60 days [35][22][1]. The decree creates an offence for people in groups of five or more who endanger public safety during a demonstration, punishable by up to five years in prison and a €20,000 fine. It also makes parents liable for damage caused by their children and presumes that people aged 14 and over understand their actions unless proven otherwise [35][22]. Police said more than 100 officers were injured over the weekend, when young people looted shops and set fires. Demonstrators cited fuel and energy costs, rents, low wages and poor public services [22][1]. Separately, truckers in southern Italy have blocked roads over diesel prices, disrupting supply chains and forcing some pasta makers to halt production. A new round of demonstrations is planned for Saturday, Oct. 24. Parliament will decide whether the measures become permanent law [35][7][13].

Key Points

  • Detentions and arrests: Milan police detained 271 people during the unrest; 32 were arrested, including 23 minors, and another 28 were reported to judicial authorities [6][4].
  • Shop precautions: Chanel, Tiffany and Dior removed merchandise from their Milan shop windows, while some fashion-district stores stayed closed over fears of further violence [6][4].
  • Troop limits: The final decree does not grant soldiers police powers, after President Sergio Mattarella objected to the proposal. The soldiers will support security patrols [31][1].
  • Haulier aid: Transport Minister Matteo Salvini promised €480 million in funding after meeting union representatives, but some union officials said blockades would continue until the government took concrete action [36].

Historical Background

Since taking office in 2022, Meloni’s government has approved successive security measures, including a 2022 decree targeting illegal rave gatherings and a 2023 decree that expanded pretrial custody for minors. A 2025 security decree added offences and tougher penalties for acts including road blockades during protests. Government data cited by Reuters show Italy’s prison population rose by more than 17%, or nearly 10,000 people, over four years [11][35].
