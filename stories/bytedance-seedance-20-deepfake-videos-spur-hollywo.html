ByteDance Seedance 2.0 deepfake videos spur Hollywood pushback

Category: AI

A new wave of highly realistic AI-generated videos is spreading online. One viral clip shows convincing lookalikes of Tom Cruise and Brad Pitt fighting on a damaged bridge—footage that outlets have linked to ByteDance’s Seedance/Seedance 2.0 video-generation tools as an example of how quickly the technology is improving [2][1]. The speed and quality of these clips has renewed concern in the entertainment industry about how easy it’s becoming to produce convincing, movie-like scenes without permission [2][4][1]. Hollywood organizations have pushed back, criticizing Seedance 2.0 as a fast-emerging tool for “blatant” copyright infringement and calling for stronger guardrails and enforcement [4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Viral exemplars: Seedance-generated clips that spread widely included faux “Lord of the Rings” style footage and a fabricated Mandarin-language scene featuring Kanye West and Kim Kardashian [2].
  • Realism markers: Futurism said recent text-to-video demos are adding more lifelike details beyond visuals, including an updated “Will Smith eating pasta” clip with a soundtrack of “unnerving squelching and chomping sounds” [2].
  • Company context: Futurism described Seedance as a ByteDance product—the Chinese company that owns TikTok—and framed Seedance 2.0 as the latest leap in its text-to-video capabilities [2].

Perspectives

Hollywood organizations (unnamed): Groups told TechCrunch they see Seedance 2.0 as a tool for “blatant” copyright infringement.
