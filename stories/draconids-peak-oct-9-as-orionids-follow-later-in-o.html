Draconids peak Oct. 9 as Orionids follow later in October

Category: Science

The Draconid meteor shower is forecast to peak on the evening of Oct. 9, followed by the Orionids overnight Oct. 21-22 [8][4]. The Draconids are active Oct. 6-10 and come from debris shed by comet 21P/Giacobini-Zinner; astronomers forecast an average of five to 10 meteors per hour at the peak [8]. The Orionids come from debris shed by Halley’s Comet and appear to radiate from near Orion, although meteors can streak across any part of the sky [4]. A bright waxing gibbous Moon could obscure dimmer meteors, making the final hours before sunrise, after moonset, a better time to watch [4]. Observers can watch the Draconids with the naked eye; binoculars and telescopes offer no advantage, the Royal Museums Greenwich advises [8].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Variable activity: NASA describes the Draconids as episodic: activity can surge into a meteor outburst or drop so low that casual observers see few or no meteors [8].
  • Meteor speed: Draconid particles travel at about 65,000 km/h; their relatively slow speed means brighter meteors generally require larger particles [8].
  • Viewing conditions: To watch the Draconids, avoid direct lights and scan as much of the sky as possible while your eyes adjust to the dark [8].
