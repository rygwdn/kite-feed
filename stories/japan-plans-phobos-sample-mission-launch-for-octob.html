Japan plans Phobos sample mission launch for October 20

Category: Science

Japan’s Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) spacecraft is scheduled to launch on October 20 from Tanegashima Space Center to collect material from Phobos, one of Mars’s two moons, and return it to Earth. Led by the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA), the mission aims to help determine how Phobos and Deimos formed and study material that may preserve clues about the early solar system [4][7][9]. MMX is expected to reach the Mars region about a year after launch, study Phobos for up to three years and return a sample capsule in 2031; another report puts the return in the Japanese fiscal year ending March 2032. The mission also plans to observe Deimos. Japan is working with U.S. and European space agencies, and the spacecraft will carry a French-German rover to survey Phobos before sampling [6][7][1].

They are known as the most important unclimbed mountains in the solar system.

— Yasuhiro Kawakatsu, MMX project manager (The Korea Times)

Key Points

  • Sampling plan: JAXA aims to collect at least 10 grams in two attempts, using a NASA device that lifts surface dust with pressurized gas and a Japanese corer that reaches about 2 centimeters below the surface [6][1].
  • Mission cost: The mission’s development cost is 55.3 billion yen, nearly $350 million, according to Anadolu Agency [7].
  • Mars material: JAXA estimates that material blasted from Mars could make up about 0.1% of the Phobos sample [6].
  • China’s plan: China plans to launch its Tianwen-3 Mars sample-return mission around 2028 and aims to return material as early as 2031 [1][9].

Perspectives

Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency: JAXA says the mission will assess whether Phobos could serve as a “natural space station” and help establish requirements for future crewed Mars missions [1].

Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency scientists: Phobos may yield a wider variety of samples than Mars itself, particularly if the moon formed after a collision with the planet [1].

Sources: Asharq Al-Awsat

Timeline

  • 20 October 2026: An H3 rocket is scheduled to launch MMX from Tanegashima [9][7].
  • 2027: The spacecraft is expected to reach the Mars region about one year after launch [6][7].
  • 2029: The French-German Idefix rover is expected to survey Phobos for about 100 days [6].
  • 2031: MMX aims to return the Phobos sample to Earth [6][4].

Technical Details

  • Low-gravity landing: Phobos has about 1/2,000 of Earth’s gravity. MMX uses four-legged, shock-absorbing landing gear to reduce bouncing, and sampling operations must be completed within about two hours [6].
  • The rover’s wheels are designed to gain traction in Phobos’s low gravity. It can right itself if it tips over and is expected to move very slowly [6].

Scientific Significance

  • Competing origin theories: The samples could help test whether Mars’s moons formed from debris after a large impact on Mars or were asteroids captured by its gravity; JAXA chose Phobos partly because it is closer to Mars than Deimos and may therefore provide more information about the planet [6][10].
  • Limits of life-related claims: The stated scientific goal is to study the origins and transport of materials linked to habitable environments; the articles describe possible clues to life’s origins, not evidence that life existed on Phobos [1][4].

Historical Background

Japan has previously returned asteroid samples through its Hayabusa missions: the first returned material in 2010, and Hayabusa2 returned 5.4 grams in 2020 [6][1]. MMX will attempt a sample return from the Mars system, where Japan’s project manager says the spacecraft must travel close to Mars and then escape its gravity to return to Earth [1].
