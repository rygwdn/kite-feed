Tourist plane crash in Peru kills 13 near Nazca

Category: World

A small tourist plane crashed in southern Peru on Saturday, Aug. 1, while carrying visitors on a sightseeing flight over the Nazca Lines, killing all 13 people on board [1][3][5][7][20]. Peru’s transport authorities said the victims included 11 foreign tourists from Italy, Germany and Spain, plus pilot Américo Salazar Cuellar and copilot Irenka Del Carpio [5][20][25]. The aircraft, identified in several reports as a Cessna Caravan or Cessna 208 Caravan, had taken off from Pisco airport before going down near the Nazca area, where tourists commonly fly to view the desert geoglyphs from above [3][9][17][55]. Authorities said the cause was not immediately known, and Peru’s civil aviation and air accident authorities opened an investigation [1][5][9][25].

Key Points

  • Flight timeline: Police said the plane lost contact with the control tower minutes after takeoff and crashed at about 1:20 p.m. local time [5][9].
  • Victim details: Peru’s Transportation Ministry identified seven Italian victims aged 18 to 54, two Spaniards aged 52 and two Germans aged 77 and 78 [7][20].
  • Aircraft operator: Nazca’s government said the aircraft was operated by Aerodiana, whose website says it has run Nazca Lines scenic flights for 14 years using Cessna Grand Caravan planes [1][9].
  • Evidence collection: Peru’s transport ministry said civil aviation and air accident authorities would investigate, with agents already collecting evidence at the scene [25][55].
  • Weather context: Nazca Lines flights from the nearby Maria Reiche airport had been suspended the previous day after winds above 40 km/h lifted dust and reduced visibility [42][60].

Perspectives

Peru’s Ministry of Transport and Communications: The ministry said the aircraft carried German, Italian and Spanish visitors and two crew members, and located the crash in Socos, about 6km from Nazca and roughly 19km from the Nazca Lines site.

Historical Background

The Nazca Lines were designated a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1994, reflecting their archaeological significance in southern Peru [64][common]. Previous fatal sightseeing crashes have occurred in the area, including a 2022 crash that killed seven people and a 2010 crash that killed four British tourists and two Peruvian crew members [38][41].

Business Angle

  • Authorities identified Aerodiana as the operator of the crashed sightseeing aircraft [1][3][9].
  • Any temporary suspension at Pisco airport could disrupt Nazca Lines scenic-flight operators that depend on short aerial tours for foreign tourism revenue [7][25].

International Reactions

  • Germany, Italy and Spain: Peru’s Trade and Tourism Ministry said it would provide necessary support to the embassies of the countries of origin of the tourists who died [29][49].
