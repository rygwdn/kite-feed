AfD wins Saxony-Anhalt vote, straining Merz coalition

Category: World

Germany's far-right Alternative for Germany won Saxony-Anhalt's state election with 43.8% of the vote, while Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democratic Union finished at about 17% in the eastern state, according to final results cited by Euronews and figures reported by RT [3][7]. The result put AfD within reach of state power in Saxony-Anhalt, a state of 2.1 million people, and The Week said the party fell three seats short of the majority needed to form a government [3][9]. Merz said he was "deeply shocked" by what he called the CDU's worst defeat in years or decades, but argued his government should press ahead with reforms while communicating them better [9][7][12]. Politico reported early signs that the Social Democratic Party, Merz's center-left coalition partner, was turning against parts of the reform course, while DW reported economists warning about risks for business confidence but noting that most tax, labor-market and social-security decisions remain federal responsibilities [12][1]. President Trump hailed the result as a "really big night" and criticized Germany's immigration regulations [4].

Key Points

  • Governing options: AfD leaders said they would seek to win over CDU lawmakers or work with the far-left BSW to secure the three seats needed in Saxony-Anhalt [9].
  • Voter drivers: The Globe and Mail linked the result to voter frustration over bureaucracy, living costs and economic insecurity, while the Financial Times described the Saxony-Anhalt outcome as national AfD trends “but on steroids” [2][5].
  • Street protests: Thousands of protesters rallied in Cologne against AfD after the party won nearly 44% in Saxony-Anhalt [10].
  • Government stakes: DW noted that AfD could now put forward the state's minister-president [1].

Historical Background

Germany's mainstream parties have maintained a postwar “firewall” against cooperation with far-right parties, a strategy the New York Times described as part of the country's postwar approach to safeguarding democracy [11][9]. The CDU dominated Saxony-Anhalt politics for much of the period after German reunification before this defeat [7]. DW described AfD as considered far-right extremist in parts, a label that affects how other parties and institutions treat potential cooperation with it [1].
