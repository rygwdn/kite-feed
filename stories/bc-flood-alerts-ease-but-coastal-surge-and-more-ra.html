B.C. flood alerts ease, but coastal surge and more rain pose risks

Category: Canada

British Columbia downgraded or lifted many flood and rainfall alerts on Friday after days of heavy rain, but officials warned that more rain, unstable riverbanks and coastal flooding still posed risks through the weekend. The province’s River Forecast Centre kept high-streamflow advisories in place for the Sea-to-Sky, Howe Sound, North Shore and parts of Vancouver Island, while Environment Canada warned that high tides could exceed predicted levels in exposed coastal areas [5][2]. Pemberton lifted an evacuation order for its Airport Road area and eased a shelter-in-place advisory, but the village remained under an evacuation alert. The Líl̓wat Nation lifted orders and alerts for most areas. Earlier flooding had blocked roads and prompted evacuation measures, including in Zeballos. Environment Canada reported 307 millimetres of rain in Port Mellon between Tuesday morning and Thursday at noon [5][4].

Key Points

  • Coastal hazards: The coastal surge was forecast to peak from 10 a.m. to noon [2].
  • Island rainfall: Rainfall warnings remained for western and inland Vancouver Island, where 50 to 100 millimetres were expected, with some areas possibly receiving 120 millimetres [3].
  • Road flooding: Floodwater covered part of Highway 99, while sections of Lillooet Lake Road had up to a foot of water [4].

Perspectives

District of Squamish: The district said BC Hydro’s release of water from Cheakamus Dam would create room in the reservoir for more rain, while warning residents that the discharge could flood nearby roads and properties [4].

Historical Background

British Columbia was marking the fifth anniversary of heavy rains that flooded the province’s southern half, washed out key transportation routes, damaged homes and farmland, and killed several people [5]. Environment and Climate Change Canada meteorologist Bobby Sekhon said that event and other severe-weather episodes helped lead to the agency’s current colour-coded forecasting system [5].

Humanitarian Impact

The search for a canoeist missing off northern Vancouver Island was suspended after three days because of difficult weather [4].

Travel Advisory

  • Road safety: Water may pool on roads; watch for washouts near rivers, creeks and culverts, and expect possible travel delays [3].
  • Coastal conditions: Coastal flooding statements were in effect for Metro Vancouver, the Southern Gulf Islands and parts of Vancouver Island amid high tides and storm surge [5][2].
