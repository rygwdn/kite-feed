Arbitrator imposes Amazon’s first North American union contract in B.C.

Category: Canada

An independent arbitrator imposed a three-year collective agreement covering workers at Amazon’s warehouse in Delta, British Columbia, on Nov. 13, establishing the first binding union contract for Amazon workers in North America. Unifor, the union representing about 800 workers, says the deal raises hourly pay to $29 by the end of the agreement and adds severance protections and vacation time for long-serving employees [2][3]. Amazon says workers had no vote on the terms and called the arbitration process unfair. A company vice-president said Amazon would challenge the contract in court if necessary. British Columbia’s Labour Relations Board ordered binding arbitration in August after negotiations stalled, despite Amazon’s opposition [3][1].

Key Points

  • Pay schedule: The agreement sets wage increases of 3.25% in October 2027 and 3.5% in October 2028, according to Les Echos [4].
  • Union dues: Amazon says dues could total as much as C$1,064 in the first year and argues that deductions could leave workers with less take-home pay [4][3].
  • Previous wage: Amazon said the average hourly wage for a warehouse employee was C$26.27 after a 4% increase in September [4].
