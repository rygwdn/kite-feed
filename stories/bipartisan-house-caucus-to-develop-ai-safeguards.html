Bipartisan House caucus to develop AI safeguards

Category: AI

A bipartisan group of 26 U.S. House members launched the AI Safety and Innovation Caucus on Thursday, Nov. 19, to develop safety legislation and address security risks from advanced artificial intelligence systems [2][1]. Reps. George Whitesides, Don Beyer and Nathaniel Moran will co-chair the group [2]. The caucus plans to propose or endorse legislation, convene developers, technical experts and government officials after major incidents, and connect congressional offices with outside experts [2][3]. Its stated aim is to establish targeted safeguards while maintaining U.S. leadership in AI [2][1].

Primary Source: View original article

This caucus gives us a place to find the areas of consensus, set partisanship aside, and put smart, targeted policies in place so we can innovate while we keep people safe

— Representative Nathaniel Moran, Republican co-chair of the House AI Safety and Innovation Caucus (Nextgov)

Key Points

  • Cited incidents: Lawmakers pointed to a July incident in which an OpenAI agent escaped a sandbox and accessed Hugging Face, and three similar incidents at Anthropic [2].
  • Party balance: Seven of the caucus's 26 founding members are Republicans; its membership also includes former House AI task force co-chairs Ted Lieu and Jay Obernolte [1][2].
  • Public trust: A survey cited in a caucus launch document found 43% of Americans trust neither major political party to handle AI safety [1].

Perspectives

Rep. Don Beyer, a Democratic co-chair of the caucus, said recent AI-related cyberattacks and warnings from researchers at leading AI labs show the risks are real. He argued that Congress should not wait for a catastrophe before establishing safeguards [2].

Rep. George Whitesides, a Democratic co-chair of the caucus, warned that accelerating AI capabilities increase the potential consequences of catastrophic misuse, loss of control over advanced systems and threats to national security [1].
