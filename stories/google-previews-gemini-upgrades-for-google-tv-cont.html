Google previews Gemini upgrades for Google TV controls

Category: AI

Google used CES 2026 previews to show new Gemini features coming to Google TV, aiming to make the TV more interactive through conversational help and on-screen AI experiences [2][3][1]. The demos include letting people ask Gemini to adjust TV settings by voice and to pull up and work with personal photos from Google Photos, including edits via a Photos Remix feature [1][3]. Some coverage frames this as a push to get viewers to consume more AI-generated content directly from the couch, which could reshape what “watching TV” means on supported devices [5].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Modern TVs often expose controls like picture mode, brightness, and input selection through software settings menus that can be navigated by voice assistants on some platforms [common].

Key Points

  • Photo workflows: Google TV is expected to let users ask Gemini to find specific pictures in Google Photos and then edit them on the TV, such as with Photos Remix [3][1].
  • Hands-free tuning: In Google’s preview, Gemini can handle routine setup tasks like changing TV settings through natural language, reducing the need to dig through menus [2][1].
  • AI viewing push: Gizmodo describes the update as an attempt to see whether people will watch AI-created outputs on television, positioning Gemini as a new kind of living-room content layer [5].
  • CES showcase: The features were presented as part of Google’s CES 2026 announcements for Google TV, indicating a near-term product update cycle rather than a distant research demo [2][1].

Perspectives

Google: Google positions the Gemini additions as making Google TV “more helpful” and enabling new ways to engage with the TV through Gemini-powered features.

Sources: Google blog

TechCrunch: TechCrunch highlights practical utility: Gemini on Google TV can help with tasks like adjusting settings and finding/editing photos, turning the TV into a more capable assistant surface.

Sources: TechCrunch

Gizmodo: Gizmodo frames the move as a deliberate bid to encourage watching AI outputs on TV, suggesting the update is as much about content behavior as it is about convenience.

Sources: Gizmodo

Technical Details

  • Gemini on-device actions: The previewed Google TV experience uses Gemini as a conversational layer that can trigger system actions—like changing TV settings—based on spoken requests, turning natural language into device control [1][2].
  • Google Photos integration: Gemini on Google TV is shown accessing a user’s Google Photos library so people can search for specific pictures by asking for them, then bring them up on the TV [3][1].
  • Photos Remix feature: Photos Remix is presented as an AI-assisted editing capability for pictures from Google Photos that can be invoked through Gemini workflows on the TV [1].

Industry Impact

  • Smart TV UX: Voice-driven settings changes and conversational help could push TV makers and platform rivals to simplify navigation and compete on assistant quality, not just app availability [2][1][common].
  • Living-room creation: Bringing photo search and AI edits to the biggest screen in the home may encourage more “lean-back” creative use cases (family photo viewing and quick edits) that traditionally happen on phones or laptops [3][1][common].
  • Content ecosystem: Positioning Gemini as a pathway to on-screen AI outputs suggests a potential new category of TV experiences that blend entertainment with generated media, which could affect how streaming platforms think about discovery and interactivity [5][common].

Historical Background

Google TV is Google’s smart-TV platform layered on Android TV, and it has increasingly emphasized voice interaction as streaming catalogs and settings have grown more complex [common]. Gemini is Google’s newer AI assistant brand, and the CES 2026 previews extend it from phones and PCs into the living room, reflecting a broader trend of placing generative AI into everyday consumer devices [2][common].
