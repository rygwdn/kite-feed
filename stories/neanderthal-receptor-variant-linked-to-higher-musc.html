Neanderthal receptor variant linked to higher muscle mass

Category: Science

A new study links a Neanderthal-derived growth hormone receptor variant to higher lean muscle mass in some people living today. Science reported that carriers tend to have more lean muscle mass, while Phys.org described the receptor as increasing muscle mass in people today [1][6]. Nautilus, Scientific American and Discover Magazine also described the finding as a connection between a Neanderthal hormone receptor or gene variant and greater muscle mass in modern humans [2][3][5]. The finding focuses on DNA inherited from Neanderthals, who interbred with ancestors of present-day humans. Scientific American noted that most of the world’s population carries at least a little Neanderthal DNA [3][common]. Phys.org reported that Neanderthals were generally more strongly built than most people living today, with bones showing signs of large muscles, prominent brow ridges and relatively short tooth roots [6].

Primary Source: View original article

Historical Background

Neanderthals lived in Eurasia before disappearing about 40,000 years ago, and genetic studies have shown that they interbred with ancestors of some present-day humans [common]. That interbreeding left Neanderthal-derived variants in modern human genomes, which researchers continue to test for effects on traits such as immunity, metabolism and physical form [common].
