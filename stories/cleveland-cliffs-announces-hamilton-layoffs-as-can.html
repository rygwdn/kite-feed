Cleveland-Cliffs announces Hamilton layoffs as Canada, Ontario unveil aid

Category: Canada

U.S.-owned steelmaker Cleveland-Cliffs announced layoffs at its Hamilton, Ontario, plant as Prime Minister Mark Carney and Ontario Premier Doug Ford unveiled a C$8.7 billion support package for steel, auto and critical-mineral businesses [1][8][13]. The plan also includes up to C$1 billion in federal funding, matched by Ontario, for roads serving the mineral-rich Ring of Fire in northern Ontario [6][7]. The U.S. Embassy in Canada separately accused Canadian politicians of trying to influence the Nov. 3 U.S. midterm elections, citing remarks about retaliatory tariffs and political meetings. Carney and Ford rejected the allegation. The embassy also cited 11 Canadians charged with voting illegally but did not link them to the Canadian government [1][9]. A parliamentary petition calling for U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra’s removal had gathered more than 275,000 signatures [6][7].

Key Points

  • Export diversification: Carney has set a goal of doubling Canadian exports to other markets over the next decade [1].
  • Additional allegation: The embassy’s fact sheet cited Liberal MP Anthony Housefather saying he had campaigned in Florida for Democratic Representative Debbie Wasserman Schultz before her August primary [9].
  • Auto industry: Carney said an integrated North American auto industry would benefit both countries, while acknowledging it may no longer be a priority for Washington [1].

Perspectives

U.S. Embassy in Canada: The embassy said Canadian officials were publicly describing plans to influence U.S. politics and called on Canadians to refrain from participating in or influencing U.S. elections [9].
