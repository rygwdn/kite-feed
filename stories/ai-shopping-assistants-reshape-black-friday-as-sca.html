AI shopping assistants reshape Black Friday as scams and errors rise

Category: AI

Consumers are using artificial intelligence chatbots to compare prices and find gift ideas, while newer AI agents can browse stores, track deliveries and make purchases. The U.S. National Retail Federation says 59% of consumers plan to use AI for holiday shopping; Black Friday is Nov. 27, followed by Cyber Monday on Nov. 30 [7][16]. In Australia, a separate survey found one in four shoppers planned to use an AI chatbot for research, and 72% of that group said it would be their first time [17]. Chatbots can invent products or prices, recommend unsuitable items and expose data shared to personalize results. Visa testing found AI models failed to recommend a product matching a customer’s needs in 42% of test runs, while cybersecurity experts warn that malicious website instructions can manipulate agents with access to email or payment details [7][8]. Retailers are also using AI to set discounts and influence which products shoppers see. Adobe data cited by retailers showed traffic to online shops from AI-tool links more than doubled this November from a year earlier, while marketing teams are adjusting product listings to appear in chat recommendations [5][3].

Key Points

  • Accuracy gap: A survey by online-reputation company Custplace found AI gave incorrect product answers 64% of the time, with fluctuating prices a frequent source of mistakes [18].
  • Retail discounts: Salesforce said November discounts before Black Friday averaged 8% deeper than a year earlier, while Adobe expected TV discounts to average 23% on Black Friday versus 11% earlier in the month [5].
  • Data exposure: TrendLife data showed 76% of Australians already using AI agents had granted access to personal accounts or data, and 15% were willing to let agents complete purchases [8].
  • Shopping patterns: Adobe reported online sales of personal-hygiene goods from Nov. 1-22 were 40% above October’s average, while cleaning products rose 33% and clothing basics 27% [5].
  • Consumer losses: Australia’s Scamwatch recorded $22.2 million in losses from buying-and-selling scams during 2026, as authorities warned criminals may exploit AI shopping agents [8].

Industry Impact

  • Retail cybersecurity: Ransomware attacks on retailers are 50% more frequent in November, Semafor reported, and the sector has low levels of cyber insurance [10].

Q&A

Q: Why are some retailers blocking outside shopping agents?

A: Amazon says Meta did not notify it before Muse began browsing and that the agents did not identify themselves; Amazon also operates its own shopping agent [7][17].

Q: How can shoppers tell whether a sale price is genuine?

A: Greek consumer guidance says the displayed reference price should be the product’s lowest price in the previous 30 days [16].

Action Items

  • Be specific about intended use and constraints—for example, ask whether a bag will fit under an airplane seat—rather than expecting a vague prompt to capture your needs [7].
  • Avoid sharing identifying details about friends or relatives when brainstorming gifts; AI systems may use those details to build profiles of people you shop for [7].
  • Check independent reviews and the seller’s return policy, including where items must be shipped and who pays return shipping, before placing an order [1].
