Unlicensed Colombian gold mine collapse kills 14, injures seven

Category: World

A collapse at an unlicensed open-pit gold mine near Santa Bárbara de Iscuandé in Colombia’s Nariño department killed 14 people and injured seven on Monday, Oct. 19. Search operations have ended [11][4][18]. Four of the injured were in serious condition, and the Nariño governor’s office worked with the army to arrange their evacuation by air to Tumaco [18]. Rescuers faced difficult terrain and the presence of armed criminal groups in the remote area, which is accessible only by boat or small aircraft [10][6]. Illegal gold mining is a major source of income for armed groups in Nariño, alongside drug trafficking and extortion, and the regional government says communities have few economic alternatives [17][10]. The collapse was the second fatal mine disaster in Nariño in less than three months: 13 people died in a similar accident in August [11][8]. Nariño Governor Luis Alfonso Escobar has urged the national government, the National Mining Agency and the Mines and Energy Ministry to speed up the formalization of small-scale mining to improve safety [8][2].

Did you know: Nariño peace official Álex González said a kilogram of gold could fetch more than 400 million Colombian pesos, compared with about 4 million pesos for a kilogram of cocaine base paste [17].

Key Points

  • Environmental damage: Illegal gold extraction contributes to deforestation and can contaminate rivers with mercury, according to reporting on mining in Colombia [17].
  • Unapproved sites: A Colombian government and U.N. drug agency report found that three-quarters of recorded areas used to extract gold from river deposits in 2025 were not authorized for that activity [21].

Timeline

  • August 2026: A similar mine accident in Nariño killed 13 people [8]
  • 19 October 2026: A collapse at the Chambero mine killed workers and injured seven [11][4]
  • 19 October 2026: Nariño authorities said search and rescue operations ended Monday night [11][4]

Historical Background

Hundreds of small mines in Colombia operate without government permits, and some are controlled by rebel groups [11]. The Jacobo Arenas Block includes former Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia members who rejected the 2016 peace agreement [6].

Humanitarian Impact

The Ombudsman’s Office urged armed groups to allow humanitarian aid to reach the town [6].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇳 UN Human Rights Colombia: The U.N. office urged Colombian authorities to support the injured and affected families and to pursue prevention, investigation and sanctions tied to illegal mining [18].

Q&A

Q: Has the cause of the collapse been established?

A: Initial reports did not establish the precise cause. Nariño official Álex González pointed to weak regulation of mining in Colombia’s Pacific region [21].
