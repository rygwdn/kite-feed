Grand Canyon flash flood leaves one dead, about 15 missing

Category: World

One person died and about 15 people remained missing or unaccounted for after a flash flood swept through Bright Angel Canyon and the Phantom Ranch area of Grand Canyon National Park in Arizona at about 2:30 p.m. local time Saturday, the National Park Service said [2][3][4][6][9][1][16][17][20][25]. The body of a 46-year-old man was recovered Sunday evening near Crystal Rapids along the Colorado River, and coroner's officials responded to the scene [2][3][6][9][16][17][46]. The flooding followed heavy rain on the Kaibab Plateau, where reports said roughly 0.5 to 1 inch fell in about 20 to 30 minutes in steep terrain [2][6][33]. Officials evacuated at least 62 people from the canyon, including hikers and campers near Phantom Ranch and the lower North Kaibab Trail, and the Arizona Department of Public Safety assisted with continuing evacuations [3][4][17][19][16]. Floodwaters destroyed or damaged footbridges over Bright Angel Creek, swept boulders, metal structures and other debris into the Colorado River, and knocked out the sole water pipeline serving tourists and residents in the park [3][1][17][25][31]. Starting Monday, park officials barred tourists from overnight stays at lodges and hotels inside the park because of limited potable water, while year-round residents were asked to conserve water [3][8][17][35][46].

Key Points

  • Missing estimate: The park service first treated more than 20 people as possibly missing before revising the working estimate to about 15 as officials gathered more information [2][10][19][28][56].
  • Closed areas: Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel Campground, the Phantom Ranch Canteen and cabins, and the entire North Kaibab Trail from the trailhead to Phantom Ranch were closed until further notice [9][3][19].

Perspectives

National Park Service: The agency asked the public to provide information about hikers, backpackers and campers who may have been in the Bright Angel Creek corridor, Phantom Ranch or related backcountry areas when the flood struck.

Historical Background

Phantom Ranch opened in 1922 and can be reached only on foot, by raft or by mule, making inner-canyon evacuations dependent on trail conditions and aircraft when flooding cuts crossings [19][48]. The damaged water line is tied to a $208 million rehabilitation project for the park's pipeline and water delivery system that began in 2023 and was expected to finish next year [25].

Travel Advisory

  • Additional thunderstorms and heavy rain were possible through Monday, and officials warned of potential flash flooding, debris flows, rockfall and changing trail and river conditions [26][42]. Avoid closed inner-canyon routes and check National Park Service alerts before entering backcountry areas.
