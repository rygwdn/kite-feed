NASA studies show LISA could detect planets through gravitational waves

Category: Science

Two studies led by NASA astrophysicists describe how the planned Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) could detect exoplanets orbiting pairs of white dwarfs through the planets’ effects on gravitational-wave signals [1][2][3]. Such detections would reveal a population not yet observed: planets around binary white dwarfs [1][3]. LISA is scheduled to launch in the mid-2030s, and researchers estimate the Milky Way contains about 10 million double-white-dwarf systems with orbital periods under an hour [1][3]. The studies also describe how NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope could help verify candidate planets. The findings appeared in The Astrophysical Journal [1].

Primary Source: View original article

These joint detections will allow us to establish that the gravitational signals correspond to genuine planets rather than false positives.

— Mary Anne Limbach, assistant research scientist at the University of Michigan (NASA)

Key Points

  • LISA’s expected reach: Researchers estimate the observatory could resolve about 10,000 individual double-white-dwarf systems in the Milky Way [1][3].
  • Webb confirmation: For a test system 500 light-years away, the researchers estimate Webb could detect a planet about five times Jupiter’s mass, while LISA could detect a planet less massive than Jupiter under ideal conditions [1].

Technical Details

  • Doppler sidebands: The planet’s tug shifts the binary’s gravitational-wave signal into secondary frequencies above and below its main frequency; researchers compare the effect with sidebands in radio transmissions [1].
  • LISA array: Three spacecraft will form a triangle with sides 1.6 million miles (2.5 million kilometers) long and use infrared laser beams to measure tiny changes in their separation [1][3].
