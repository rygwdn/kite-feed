France unveils €54 billion budget cuts as bond yields reach highest level since 2002

Category: Business

France’s government on Oct. 1 unveiled a 2027 budget plan with about €54 billion in savings, aiming to reduce the deficit to 5% of gross domestic product as public debt is forecast to approach 122% of GDP [5][15][18]. The yield on French 10-year bonds climbed to 4.96%, its highest level since 2002, and the yield gap over German debt reached 141 basis points, its widest since 2012 [24][15]. Proposed measures include pension savings, a freeze on public-sector salaries and changes to state-funded sick leave. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said in September that without savings, the deficit could reach 6.5% of GDP next year [5]. A global repricing of government debt, driven by higher oil prices and inflation concerns, has also contributed to the bond sell-off, while investors question France’s ability to pass the plan [8][24]. The government lacks a clear parliamentary majority and must formally submit the Finance Bill to the National Assembly by Oct. 6 [5]. Jérémie Peloso of BCA Research said negotiations could continue into the first half of 2027, prolonging uncertainty over the fiscal outlook [5].

Key Points

  • Long-term debt outlook: Goldman Sachs analysis cited by Cnyes said France’s debt-to-GDP ratio could reach 125% in the early 2030s [15].
  • Investor sentiment: A Bank of America survey of European fund managers ranked France as their least-preferred market for the next 12 months [25].
  • Bond repricing: The yield on France’s 10-year bonds had risen by more than one percentage point since June, marking the bonds’ weakest quarterly performance since the euro was introduced [25].
  • Economic backdrop: France’s economy was barely growing, while debt strained public finances and the government faced demands over public services [22].

Perspectives

French government: Budget minister David Amiel argued that spending cuts were necessary and said the government could not keep postponing action on public debt [18].

Sources: Danas

Jean-Luc Mélenchon and Marine Le Pen: Mélenchon proposed cancelling French government bonds held by the European Central Bank to free funds for public investment; right-wing representatives called the proposal unrealistic, while Le Pen advocated reforms to public finances [18].

Sources: Danas

French unions and demonstrators: Protesters included firefighters seeking additional resources, alongside public employees and students; the wider dispute also involves wages, purchasing power and public services [22][23].

Timeline

  • 29 September 2026: Firefighters and other groups began demonstrations and strikes [22]
  • 1 October 2026: The government presented its 2027 budget plan as bond yields surged [5][24]
  • 6 October 2026: Deadline for formally submitting the Finance Bill to parliament [5]

Technical Details

  • France-Germany spread: The 10-year yield gap reached 141 basis points, a measure of the additional return investors demanded on French debt relative to German bonds [15].
  • Credit-default swaps: French sovereign CDS prices rose by more than 100% in a month, signaling a sharp increase in the market price of insuring against credit risk [15].

Industry Impact

  • French government borrowing: The 10-year bond yield reached 4.96%, raising the rate France would pay on newly issued long-term debt [24].
  • European sovereign bonds: During the sell-off, investors shifted from French and other euro-area peripheral bonds into German government debt, widening differences in national financing costs [15].

Business Angle

  • The deficit plan combines spending restraint with measures to raise tax revenue [7].

Q&A

Q: How large is France’s public debt relative to its economy?

A: France’s public debt stood at about 119% of GDP, with the government projecting it could approach 122% in 2027 [18].
