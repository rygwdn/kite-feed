Japan launches Phobos sample-return mission targeting 2031 Earth arrival

Category: Science

Japan launched its Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) spacecraft on Oct. 19 to collect material from Phobos and return it to Earth in 2031. The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) said the probe’s solar power, communications and attitude-control systems passed initial checks after launch. The spacecraft is expected to reach Mars in August 2027 [2][7][8]. MMX will deploy the Idefix rover to assess the surface before attempting to land and gather about 10 grams of material. The samples could help determine the moons’ origins, including whether Phobos is made from material ejected from Mars [2][1]. The mission will also observe Deimos. The European Space Agency’s Mars Express spacecraft is expected to help observe and image the planned landing site when MMX arrives. Its current mission extension runs through 2029 [8].

Primary Source: View original article

There hasn't yet been a successful mission to go to Phobos and bring a bit back to Earth. I think this is an incredibly ambitious and fun mission.

— Prof. Sara Russell, MMX deputy for sample analysis (Only Good News Daily)

Did you know: On Phobos, the Idefix rover’s Earth weight of about 25 kilograms would correspond to roughly 15 grams [2].

Key Points

  • Sampling system: The lander carries a drill and a pneumatic sampler. Honeybee Robotics executive Kris Zacny said the pneumatic sampler is designed to collect surface material in seconds [2].
  • Rover conditions: Idefix will test whether wheeled travel is viable on Phobos, which may be covered in fine regolith [1].
  • Mars Express: In orbit around Mars since 2003, the spacecraft received a software update designed to reduce gyroscope wear and potentially extend its operations [8].
  • Sample destination: The return capsule is expected to land in Australia; Only Good News Daily identifies the Woomera desert in South Australia as the planned landing area [2][5].

Perspectives

Sonia Fornasier, a Paris Observatory astronomer and MMX instrument project scientist, described MMX as a transformative step in studying the Martian system and said it ranks highly among missions sent to Mars [2].

Sources: Live Science

A former Mars Express team member told The Register that any further extension of the spacecraft’s mission would depend on funding and scientific return. Discussions could begin in 2028 or 2029 [8].

Sources: The Register

Sara Russell, a Natural History Museum researcher and MMX deputy for sample analysis, said Phobos’s weak gravity and proximity to Mars make placing a rover on its surface harder than the moon’s low gravity alone might suggest [5].

Sources: Only Good News Daily

Timeline

  • 19 October 2026: MMX launched aboard an H3 rocket from Japan [8][2]
  • 21 October 2026: JAXA reported completion of initial critical spacecraft operations [8][2]
  • August 2027: MMX is expected to reach Mars and begin its moon studies [2][7]
  • July 2031: JAXA plans for the spacecraft to return to Earth [7]

Scientific Significance

  • Phobos’s interior: Existing data suggest the moon may not be solid rock; large voids and possible ice deposits could lie beneath its surface [1].

Historical Background

American astronomer Asaph Hall discovered Phobos and Deimos in 1877 [2]. Observations suggest Phobos may not be solid rock, with large voids beneath its surface and possible deposits of ice [1].
