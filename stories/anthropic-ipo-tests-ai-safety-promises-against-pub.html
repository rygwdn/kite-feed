Anthropic IPO tests AI safety promises against public-market pressure

Category: AI

Anthropic, the maker of Claude and described as the first pure AI lab to go public, began trading on Nasdaq on Nov. 19. Its shares fell the next day during a wider technology selloff as the company faces pressure to fund costly computing needs and sustain growth [41][24][13]. Critics cited by NPR say demands for shareholder returns could weigh on decisions such as delaying a risky model for additional safety testing. Anthropic’s prospectus warns that safeguards may prove inadequate or be circumvented, and says the company could ultimately lose control of its models [16]. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has said safety and public benefit are built into the company’s mission [16]. Anthropic has made large, long-term infrastructure commitments and uses chips from Nvidia, AMD, Google and Amazon for model training and inference, according to reporting on its filings and supplier relationships [2][26]. Its Long-Term Benefit Trust can appoint four of seven directors, and the company markets its safety approach to enterprise and government customers [16][11].

Key Points

  • Supplier investment: Seven semiconductor and technology suppliers bought non-voting shares in Anthropic’s private placement, giving them a financial stake in a major customer [2].
  • Share supply: About 300 million additional Anthropic shares are due to become tradable on Dec. 15, which some investors said could add pressure to the stock [34].
  • Customer stakes: Anthropic markets its safety approach to enterprise and government customers concerned with security, data handling and predictable model behavior [11].
  • Safety governance: Anthropic is a public benefit corporation; its Long-Term Benefit Trust can appoint four of seven directors, while the founders control appointments to the other three [16].
  • Trust dissolution: Legal experts cited by NPR said the founders and shareholders with voting rights could dissolve the trust, potentially allowing them to override its influence during a major safety dispute [16].
  • Enterprise demand: Anthropic reported more than 11,900 customers generating at least $100,000 in revenue over the preceding 12 months as of September, compared with about 1,500 in 2025; its revenue run rate reached $81 billion by Oct. 31 [58].
  • Index buying: Intropic estimates funds tracking stock indexes could buy as much as 30% of Anthropic’s shares by year-end, though those purchases are not guaranteed [40].
  • Bank fees: The deal generated $550 million in fees for Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and 20 other banks, according to Bloomberg data cited by The Business Times [58].
  • Options trading: Options on Anthropic shares were expected to start trading the following week, giving investors another way to bet on or hedge against price swings [40].

Jonathan Golub, chief equity strategist at Seaport Research Partners, said the AI-stock selloff looked excessive and demand for computing power remained strong. He identified financing hundreds of billions of dollars in new infrastructure as a challenge [35].

Technical Details

  • Direct procurement: Under its “Anthropic Direct” strategy, Anthropic contracts separately for accelerators, CPUs, memory and networking equipment, and leases data-center space and operations, rather than bundling them through public-cloud providers [2].

Industry Impact

  • Venture capital exits: Anthropic’s listing delivered large paper gains to early backers: at the IPO price, Menlo Ventures and Lightspeed Venture Partners each held stakes worth about $25 billion, CNBC reported [21].
  • Enterprise software: Anthropic’s efforts to sell Claude for coding and other corporate tasks depend on expanding beyond coding tools and chatbots, which account for roughly three-quarters of corporate spending on generative-AI application services [4].
  • Public-market benchmark: Anthropic is the first frontier AI model maker to list, so its disclosures could give investors a direct reference point for evaluating AI developers rather than relying only on chipmakers and data-center suppliers [58][59].

Historical Background

The listing followed a prolonged slowdown in venture-backed IPOs: technology companies raised $73 billion in 2021, compared with $39 billion over the following four years, according to data from University of Florida emeritus professor Jay Ritter cited by CNBC [21]. The number of technology companies going public fell from 126 in 2021 to 34 in 2025 [21].

Q&A

Q: How can the safety trust be removed?

A: The Long-Term Benefit Trust can be dissolved only with approval from all seven co-founders and a majority vote among shareholders with voting rights [16].

Q: How quickly are Anthropic’s computing costs rising?

A: Anthropic spent nearly $9 billion on computing capacity and infrastructure in the first half of 2026, then reported another $5 billion in computing expenses in the third quarter [3].

Q: Was there significant investor interest during Anthropic’s debut despite the later retreat?

A: Retail investors traded about $772 million of Anthropic shares on Thursday, the most for an IPO debut since Vanda Research began tracking the data in 2012 [40].
