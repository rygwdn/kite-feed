Finland and Norway call Putin as Europe splits over Ukraine talks

Category: World

Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre held a 90-minute call with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Oct. 27, pressing Ukraine’s position ahead of planned Russia-Ukraine-U.S. talks in Abu Dhabi in early November [1][4][3]. The leaders said they initiated the call in consultation with Kyiv; Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said they urged concrete steps to de-escalate attacks on Ukraine’s energy facilities and infrastructure [2][15]. Neither side announced a shift in position. The Kremlin said Putin argued that a lasting settlement required Russia’s interests to be taken fully and unconditionally into account and blamed European Union and NATO policies for prolonging the war; Støre said the conversation exposed “big differences” [1][6]. The leaders agreed to keep speaking directly by phone, while making clear they spoke for their own countries, not Europe as a whole [4][17]. The call drew a public rebuke from Denmark’s Mette Frederiksen, who said it was not coordinated with other Nordic leaders; Sweden’s Ulf Kristersson warned that separate initiatives could divide Europe’s voice [5][36]. Ukraine backed the contact. The next trilateral meeting is expected after Nov. 4 in Abu Dhabi [3][2].

Key Points

  • Oslo groundwork: The call followed an Oct. 20 meeting in Oslo attended by Stubb, Støre, Zelensky and U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff; Jared Kushner joined remotely [4][6].
  • Why now: Stubb said tense U.S.-Ukraine relations helped persuade the leaders to open a direct channel [6].
  • Presidential-level talks: Sybiha said Zelensky was ready to meet Putin, arguing that key decisions require leaders and that a presidential meeting could give negotiations new momentum [3].
  • Negotiator debate: Reporting has identified Stubb, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen, Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni and Czech President Petr Pavel as potential European representatives [2].
  • Kremlin response: Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov described the call as “very constructive and practical” [1].

Perspectives

Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre: Støre rejected criticism that the two countries were acting alone, arguing that European countries should have a direct channel to Moscow rather than leaving communication solely to the U.S. administration [4][17].
