Studies test Enceladus microbes and plume-particle chemistry

Category: Science

Two studies published in Science Advances examined whether a methane-producing Earth microorganism could remain active under laboratory conditions simulating Enceladus’s subsurface ocean, and how ocean material becomes ice grains in the moon’s plumes [4][5]. The microorganism survived, grew and produced methane in the simulated chemistry; the findings do not show that life exists on Enceladus [2][9]. The second study found that ocean spray can sort and concentrate compounds into individual ice grains as it freezes and fragments. The process could also concentrate organic molecules and potential biosignatures [5][6][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Future spacecraft will have to analyze many individual ice particles in the plume. But if they come across one with microbial material in it, they could identify biosignatures in the particle relatively easily with already available technology.

— Frank Postberg (Slashdot)

Key Points

  • Cassini data: An analysis of mass spectra from Enceladus plume ice grains found greater compositional diversity than previously recognized [6].
  • Microbe habitat: The tested methane-producing microorganism is normally found near deep-sea hydrothermal vents on Earth [2][8].
  • Plume sampling: Enceladus’s south-polar plumes send water vapor and ice particles into space, allowing spacecraft to sample material from the ocean [4].
  • Internal heating: Saturn’s gravitational pull flexes Enceladus, producing tidal heating and hydrothermal activity at the core-mantle boundary [7].
