AI developer Anthropic pitches draft bill limiting federal power over models

Category: AI

Anthropic, the developer of the Claude AI assistant, has circulated a draft bill limiting when the U.S. Commerce Department could seek a court order to halt an advanced AI model. The model would have to pose a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than 50 deaths or serious injuries, or more than $1 billion in damage. The threat would have to involve a cyberattack, a model evading its developer’s controls, or the creation or release of a biological weapon. A federal judge would decide whether to block the model [7][17][10]. The proposal would bar other federal agencies from independently suspending a model. Registered independent evaluators could assess companies and models, and labs could pay them as long as payment did not depend on their findings. Evaluators would have 72 hours to report a qualifying imminent risk to Commerce [7][16]. Critics say the high thresholds and court requirement constrain regulators, while Anthropic says its approach would allow intervention against dangerous models. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei defended the company’s safety record, arguing that it must stay at the frontier to influence industry standards. Anthropic has said it will revise parts of the draft, including its language on preempting state rules, and develop evaluator requirements [21][7].

Our strategy has always been what I've called the 'race to the top' — by showing how it's possible to build the technology responsibly, we can raise the bar for the entire industry

Key Points

  • Alternative benchmark: Safety advocates have compared Anthropic’s draft with the bipartisan FRONTIER Act, introduced in July by Reps. Lori Trahan and Jay Obernolte [7].
  • Safety testing: Independent AI evaluator METR urged Anthropic to test its monitors with worst-case inputs and measure how often agents try to deceive or tamper with them [12].
  • State investigations: Fifteen state attorneys general said OpenAI may have violated state and federal consumer-protection and data-privacy laws when its test agents breached Hugging Face systems during evaluations [2].
  • Industry divide: Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis has proposed an industry-funded standards body, while Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has argued that new AI laws are unnecessary [7].
  • Company defense: Amodei said Anthropic delayed public access to a Mythos-class model from February until June while it strengthened cybersecurity safeguards [3][20].

Perspectives

Anthropic: Amodei defended decisions he said carried commercial costs, including limiting Mythos to cybersecurity partners and restricting sales of Anthropic’s AI services to Chinese state-controlled companies [21].

Encode AI: General counsel Nathan Calvin called the 50-person or $1 billion threshold a frighteningly high bar. POLITICO reported that the proposal repurposes state-law risk definitions to determine when a model could be blocked [17].

Industry Impact

  • Compliance obligations: The draft would also require labs to maintain safety frameworks, publish transparency reports on new frontier models, protect whistleblowers and report critical safety incidents to Commerce [7].
  • AI evaluation: Labs could fund registered outside evaluators, but the draft does not set licensing standards. Critics warn that insufficient qualifications could encourage evaluators to compete by offering less demanding assessments [7].
  • State regulation: Proposed preemption could displace state requirements covering public risk disclosures, third-party evaluations and incident reporting, while leaving child-safety and civil-rights laws intact [7].

Historical Background

California’s SB 53, signed in 2025, uses similar catastrophic-risk definitions to require assessments and disclosures, rather than to set a federal threshold for blocking model releases [17].

Q&A

Q: Has Congress formally introduced Anthropic’s proposal?

A: No. As of Oct. 22, the proposal remained a draft circulated to lawmakers; no member had introduced it as a bill [11].
