United blocks AI agents from airline transactions, leaving search available

Category: AI

United Airlines is barring unauthorized AI assistants from booking, changing or servicing tickets through its website, app, travel agencies and aggregators while allowing them to display flights and fares [11][3][7]. The Chicago-based carrier revised its booking and distribution terms on Nov. 11; the terms allow it to cancel reservations made or managed by an AI agent, even when a customer approves the action [7][15]. United says an AI agent could expose login or payment information, make unwanted purchases or use MileagePlus miles, and might complete regulatory acknowledgments that require a traveler’s personal confirmation [3][4]. United also says its back-end systems can identify whether a booking came from a consumer, travel agency or AI agent, and that bookings by automated assistants remain minimal [2]. Delta allows authenticated assistants to book under safeguards, while Southwest has opened flight search through ChatGPT; United has not announced any authorization agreements for third-party agents [27][7]. JetBlue President Marty St. George said airlines should respond if customers adopt the tools, but warned that intermediaries could add costs to an industry that struggles to turn a profit [13].

Key Points

  • Sparse usage: Uber said AI agents generated 500 food orders and four ride bookings out of 320 million trips and deliveries during a peak week [2][25].
  • Search costs: Aviation analyst Henry Harteveldt said automated flight searches cost airlines money, so a surge in agent queries could raise expenses without producing additional bookings [3][15].
  • Pricing scrutiny: The Federal Trade Commission ordered United, Delta, JetBlue and other carriers to provide information on how AI tools influence individual fares and how they use customer data [10].
  • Travel AI push: OpenAI acquired travel-booking startup Odessia, while Meta has identified travel planning as a central use for its Muse assistant [2][24].
  • Policy boundary: United’s revised ticketing rule covers standalone assistants and AI-enabled browsers, not just branded chatbot apps [15].

Perspectives

United Airlines: The airline urged other companies to adopt measures that protect customer data and safety, and said it would update its Digital Trust Center with guidance for customers [16].

Technical Details

  • Contract clauses: Rule 6.J.6 bars AI agents from making, modifying or servicing tickets or purchasing products or services; Rule 5.H allows United to cancel reservations made or managed by agents without notice [15].

Industry Impact

  • Distribution partners: United’s rules require travel agencies and booking platforms to obtain express written consent before using AI agents to search, display, book or service its flights; violations can cost them authorization to issue United tickets [7].
  • Future access: United has indicated it may reconsider restrictions as third-party agents advance, after establishing protections for travelers [22].

JetBlue had about $8.4 billion in debt and finance lease obligations and had not posted an annual profit since 2019, according to Reuters [13].
