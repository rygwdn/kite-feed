Nova Scotia NDP seeks emergency debate on $10-billion AI centre

Category: Canada

Nova Scotia’s NDP renewed its call for an emergency debate on a proposed $10-billion artificial-intelligence data centre in Cape Breton, with questions about environmental safeguards and accountability unresolved after two public meetings [3][1][2]. Parents separately raised concerns about power bills at an NDP news conference [3]. EverWind Fuels plans an 18,600-square-metre facility in Point Tupper; U.S. AI company Anthropic has signed a 20-year lease, with operations targeted to begin by the end of 2027 [2][1]. Residents at the meetings questioned the lack of an environmental assessment and sought answers about accountability [2][1]. NDP Leader Claudia Chender called for written commitments on power rates and environmental protections, saying verbal assurances were insufficient [2][3]. Premier Tim Houston says existing approval processes have guidelines and has not determined whether new legislation is needed. EverWind says it will hold more consultations as the project advances [1][2].

Primary Source: View original article

Is there going to be a tax levy? Will there be additional income? Will our environment be protected? Show us the regulations that prove that. Will ratepayers be protected? Put it in law.

— Claudia Chender, Nova Scotia NDP Leader (Global News)

Key Points

  • Power demand: The first phase is planned to use 95 megawatts of electricity, with a 325-megawatt expansion expected in mid-2028 [1][2][3].
  • Rate assurances: Nova Scotia’s Energy Department said the province, other levels of government and EverWind have committed to avoiding negative effects on electricity rates; EverWind also says it will use a closed-loop water system [3].
  • Investor details: EverWind representatives declined to discuss the project’s private investors when resident Teresa MacNeil asked who would finance it [1][2].

Perspectives

Bible Hill resident Cassie Kiftle: She said she opposed the proposal because of Anthropic, the American company leasing the centre, and raised concerns about Canadian data security [1][2].
