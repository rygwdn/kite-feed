Alberta tables bill for 91-seat map ahead of 2027 election

Category: Canada

Alberta’s government introduced legislation on Nov. 16 to adopt a proposed 91-seat electoral map for the provincial election scheduled for Oct. 18, 2027, replacing the current 87-seat map [1][2][3]. The opposition New Democratic Party says it will challenge the boundaries in court if the bill receives royal assent, while Justice Minister Mickey Amery expects the legislation to pass by year’s end [2][1]. The proposed map emerged from a new boundary-drawing process after the government rejected an independent commission’s 89-seat plan in April [3]. Elections Alberta warned that changing boundaries close to an election could complicate preparations; the agency said in November it could still run the vote using the proposed map [3].

Primary Source: View original article

If this passes, we’re challenging it in court.

— Naheed Nenshi, Alberta NDP leader (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Riding populations: Under the proposed map, rural riding populations would average nearly 11% below the provincial average of 53,722, while Calgary and Edmonton riding populations would be about 6% above it [1].
  • Legal uncertainty: A constitutional challenge could seek an injunction to keep the existing boundaries in force, and Elections Alberta may need to prepare to use either map [2].

Perspectives

Alberta government: Justice Minister Mickey Amery defended combining urban and rural communities in some districts, saying the approach reflects the connections between those communities [1][2].

Sources: The Globe and Mail

Alberta New Democratic Party: Leader Naheed Nenshi called the map gerrymandered and urged members of the governing UCP to vote against adopting it, saying there was still time to reject the proposal [2].

Sources: CBC News

Elections Alberta: Chief electoral officer Gordon McClure previously asked the government to retain the current boundaries or delay the election, citing the agency’s usual 12-to-18-month preparation period [3].
