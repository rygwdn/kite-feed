Israel strikes southern Lebanon as ceasefire strains

Category: World

Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon killed 14 people and wounded 37 on Sunday, according to Lebanon's health ministry, making it the deadliest day since a ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah took effect more than a week earlier [1][3][4][5][7][9]. The dead included two children and two women [3][4]. Israel also said one of its soldiers was killed as fighting along the border flared again [3][4]. The escalation came as Israel warned residents in seven towns north of the Litani River to evacuate and said it was acting against Hezbollah. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the military was targeting the group "vigorously" and accused it of undermining the truce [2][1][6][9]. Hezbollah rejected that accusation and said it would keep responding to what it called Israeli ceasefire violations, underscoring how fragile the extended truce remains even though it was meant to reduce cross-border hostilities [1][2][7][6].

Did you know: The Litani River has long served as a strategic reference line in southern Lebanon's security arrangements, making it more than just a geographic landmark [common].

Key Points

  • Wider toll: Israeli strikes have killed at least 36 people in Lebanon since the truce began, according to an AFP tally cited by The Hindu [5].
  • Buffer zone: Israel warned civilians to leave seven towns beyond the "buffer zone" its forces occupied before the ceasefire, a sign that military operations were reaching beyond previously held positions [3][9].
  • Truce terms: Under the extended ceasefire, Israel says it reserves the right to act against "planned, imminent or ongoing attacks," a clause it cites to justify near-daily strikes in south Lebanon [6].
  • Daily pressure: Multiple reports said Israeli forces have carried out repeated, and in some cases near-daily, strikes in southern Lebanon despite the truce, showing the agreement has not fully halted hostilities [1][6].

Historical Background

The current truce followed a more recent round of fighting along the Israel-Lebanon border and was later extended, but the articles say it has not fully stopped hostilities [6][1].
