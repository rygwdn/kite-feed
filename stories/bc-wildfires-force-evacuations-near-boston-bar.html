B.C. wildfires force evacuations near Boston Bar

Category: Canada

Two out-of-control wildfires near Boston Bar in British Columbia’s Fraser Canyon prompted expanded evacuation orders and alerts, and officials grouped the Brunswick Creek and Ainslie Creek fires as the Brunswick Complex [9][4]. The Canadian Press reported that the fires covered more than 114 square kilometres combined, with evacuation orders covering 222 properties and alerts covering 188 nearby properties, including Boston Bar itself [9]. The Ainslie Creek fire was estimated at more than 88 square kilometres, while the Brunswick Creek fire was more than 26 square kilometres; wildfire officials believe human activity caused both [1][8][9]. Officials said high winds and hot, dry conditions drove fire growth, and warned that more wind could further increase fire behaviour despite lower temperatures in the forecast [8][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Fire response: BC Wildfire Service said ground crews, heavy equipment and helicopters were working the Brunswick Creek fire, with crews attacking the northern flank and building a fuel break south of the fire [3].
  • Smoke impacts: Smoke from the Brunswick Creek fire darkened skies over Kamloops on Tuesday evening, while Kamloops Airport reported scheduled arrivals and departures remained on time as of 7 p.m. [10].
  • Regional fire status: The Brunswick Creek and Ainslie Creek fires were the only fires of note among about 20 active blazes burning across British Columbia [9].

Perspectives

Fraser Valley Regional District: The district upgraded the Canyon Alpine area from an evacuation alert to an evacuation order, telling residents they must leave immediately because of danger from the Brunswick Creek and Ainslie Creek fires.

Sources: Vancouver Sun

Timeline

  • 2 July 2026: Brunswick Creek wildfire began near Boston Bar [1][8].
  • 4 July 2026: Ainslie Creek wildfire ignited across the Fraser River [1].
  • 6 July 2026: Metro Vancouver issued yellow air warning for Fraser Valley communities [1].
  • 7 July 2026: Air warning ended at noon after Monday’s smoky conditions [1].
  • 8 July 2026: Spius Creek-area alert covered about 61 properties Wednesday evening [8].

Industry Impact

  • Transportation: The Trans-Canada Highway closure cut traffic through a key Fraser Canyon route, with Highway 1 shut between Boston Bar Station Road and Ainslie Road North [3][8].
  • Utilities: About 230 B.C. Hydro customers in and around the Boston Bar evacuation zone lost electricity beginning Tuesday afternoon [8].

Historical Background

Lytton, in the Fraser Canyon, was largely destroyed by wildfire in 2021 after Canada recorded its highest-ever temperature there during a heat dome [common]. The Vancouver Sun noted that Lytton’s recovery remains unfinished as the new evacuations near Boston Bar expanded in the same broader canyon region [1].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: DriveBC said the Brunswick Creek fire forced Highway 1 to close near Boston Bar [3]. Avoid the closed Highway 1 corridor.
  • Health: Interior Health advised higher-risk residents, including older adults, children and people with pre-existing conditions, to take extra precautions and limit smoke exposure where possible [10]. Reduce smoke exposure if you are in an affected Interior community.

Action Items

  • Check your evacuation status: Use Fraser Valley Regional District and First Nation emergency notices for the latest order or alert boundaries before travelling near Boston Bar [1][8].
  • Prepare to leave if under alert: Evacuation alerts mean residents should be ready to leave at short notice, including Boston Bar properties covered by the latest alerts [9].
