Yemen government launches Hadramout operation as Saudi jets strike

Category: World

Yemen’s Saudi-backed government says it has launched an operation in the eastern province of Hadramout to retake military sites from the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council (STC), framing the move as an effort to restore security and avoid chaos [1][23][25]. The STC and allied officials say Saudi-led coalition air strikes hit STC positions after the operation began, and STC-linked officials reported fatalities and injuries from strikes near the Saudi border area [8][12][19][aljazeera.com#5]. The flare-up highlights widening strains between Saudi Arabia and the UAE—partners in the coalition formed to fight Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthi movement—but which back different factions in the south [2][24][15][16]. Civil aviation has also been disrupted, with flights from Aden airport halted amid competing claims over new routing requirements and landing permissions [6][aljazeera.com#6][13][18].

This operation is not a declaration of war, nor an attempt to escalate tensions

— Salem Al-Khanbashi (The New Arab)

Did you know: Hadramout borders Saudi Arabia and is described as oil-producing, making it strategically important within Yemen’s fractured political map [23].

Key Points

  • Camp reclaimed: Hadramout authorities said their forces took control of the Al-Khasha’a camp, described as the province’s largest and most important base, after government armoured vehicles advanced toward it [23].
  • Command reshuffle: Yemen’s government said Hadramout’s governor, Salem Al-Khanbashi, was appointed to command the Saudi-backed “Homeland Shield” (National Shield) forces in the province with broad military, security and administrative authority [23][9].
  • Airport dispute: Saudi Arabia’s ambassador to Yemen said the STC leader refused permission for a plane carrying a Saudi delegation to land in Aden, while STC-linked officials publicly objected to Saudi demands affecting flights between Aden and the UAE [9][13][18].
  • UAE drawdown: The UAE announced a withdrawal of its troops from southern Yemen this week, which analysts described as a de-escalatory step even as Saudi-UAE negotiations over influence in Yemen remain unresolved [10].
  • Local significance: Hadramout’s oil-producing status, border with Saudi Arabia, and cultural links—many prominent Saudis trace origins to the province—are cited as factors raising the stakes of control there [23][20].

Perspectives

Hadramout governor and Saudi-backed local forces: They describe the campaign as a “peaceful” handover of military sites aimed at restoring security and preventing Hadramout from “sliding into chaos,” and say it does not target civilians or political groups.

Southern Transitional Council (STC): STC officials say the operation was not peaceful and accuse Saudi Arabia of bombing their forces; STC figures describe the confrontation as “existential” and say their forces are on full alert.

Technical Details

  • Saudi-led coalition: A military coalition formed in 2015 to fight the Iran-aligned Houthi movement; it nominally includes the UAE even as Riyadh and Abu Dhabi back rival local partners in Yemen’s south [8][2].
  • Southern Transitional Council (STC): A UAE-backed separatist movement that controls parts of southern Yemen and has recently expanded in Hadramout and Mahra, challenging Saudi-backed government forces [2][8].
  • Homeland/National Shield forces: Saudi-supported forces operating in government-held areas; Yemen’s government says Hadramout’s governor was given overall command authority in the province during the current operation [23][9].

Industry Impact

  • Regional shipping risk: Analysts warn that instability in eastern Yemen can ripple into the Red Sea and nearby waterways, a key global chokehold zone, raising security and insurance concerns for trade routes [10][20].
  • Energy infrastructure exposure: Fighting in oil-producing Hadramout and competition over bases and territory increase the risk of disruptions around energy assets and transport corridors in the province [23][20].
  • Air connectivity: The halt in Aden airport flights and the dispute over routing to the UAE can disrupt passenger travel and cargo movement, adding friction to commerce in the south [aljazeera.com#6][13].

Historical Background

Yemen’s conflict escalated after the Houthis seized the capital Sanaa in 2014 and forced the internationally recognised government out, prompting a Saudi-led coalition intervention in 2015 [8][2][17]. Over the years, Saudi Arabia and the UAE have backed different armed and political factions in areas nominally controlled by the government, including the STC in the south, creating parallel chains of command and recurring tensions [2][24][15]. Despite years of fighting, the Houthis remain entrenched in Yemen’s most populated areas, leaving the country fragmented and prone to new rounds of intra-coalition conflict [17][2].

Q&A

Q: What territory does each major Yemeni faction control today, beyond Hadramout?

A: Several sources outline a patchwork: the Houthis hold much of the north and the capital area, while government-aligned and STC-aligned forces control different parts of the south and east [2][3][17].

Q: What specific conditions led to Aden airport flights being suspended?

A: Reports cite a dispute involving landing permissions for a Saudi delegation and separate claims about whether flights to the UAE must stop in Saudi Arabia; the parties contest each other’s accounts [18][aljazeera.com#6][13].

Q: Why are Saudi Arabia and the UAE described as competing powerbrokers in Yemen’s south?

A: Coverage says both countries have supported different factions and interests in Yemen’s government-held areas for years, and the STC’s recent advances have sharpened that divergence [15][24][16].
