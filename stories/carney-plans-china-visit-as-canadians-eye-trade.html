Carney plans China visit as Canadians eye trade

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney is set to visit China next week, and his office says he wants to “recalibrate” Canada’s relationship with Beijing while exploring economic opportunities for both countries [1]. The timing comes as a new Ipsos poll suggests a small majority of Canadians would like to expand trade with China, with Ipsos CEO Darrell Bricker framing the result as driven largely by economic pressure from the United States’ trade war rather than a sudden surge of enthusiasm for China [3]. Together, the coverage highlights a pragmatic debate for Canadians: how to broaden markets for Canadian exporters while managing longstanding concerns about values and national interests in the Canada–China relationship [1][3].

Key Points

  • Political messenger: Carney’s intent to revisit the relationship was conveyed publicly by his parliamentary secretary, MP Kody Blois, who said the goal is to look for “economic opportunities” alongside a diplomatic reset [1].
  • Public mood: Global News reports the Ipsos findings as a narrow-tilt result toward “more trade,” indicating Canadians appear open to diversification even if views are not overwhelmingly unified [3].
  • US shadow: Ipsos’ Darrell Bricker said the poll outcome reflects “more about the United States,” pointing to the economic realities created by the U.S. trade war as a key driver of Canadian attitudes [3].
  • Values warning: Opinion commentary linked in the Global News roundup includes former diplomat Michael Kovrig urging Carney to defend Canadian values while pursuing trade during the China visit [2].
  • Stronger criticism: A separate opinion column highlighted in the same roundup argues Carney’s approach risks aligning Canada with actors hostile to Canada’s interests, underscoring how polarized the China file can be at home [2].
