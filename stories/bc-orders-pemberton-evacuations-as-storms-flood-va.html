B.C. orders Pemberton evacuations as storms flood Vancouver Island

Category: Canada

British Columbia upgraded flood watches to warnings, and Pemberton ordered evacuations along Airport Road as a series of storms brought flooding and heavy rain to the South Coast. The evacuation order covers homes, community facilities and Pemberton Regional Airport. Port Mellon recorded 214 millimetres of rain in 24 hours, and officials reported flooding near One Mile Lake [1][2][3]. The Lil’wat Nation also ordered evacuations at some properties, while the Squamish-Lillooet Regional District issued alerts in flood-risk areas [2]. About 13,000 customers remained without electricity across B.C. on Wednesday afternoon, and a mudslide and flooding closed Bamfield Road on Vancouver Island [2]. Forecasters expected a brief lull Thursday or early Friday, followed by another strong system from Friday through Sunday. They said conditions may turn drier around Nov. 15 or 16 [1][3].

The weather is not cooperating and the flooding at Sarita river is something that needs to be taken very seriously.

— John Jack, Huu-ay-aht First Nations Chief Councillor (CBC News)

Key Points

  • Power outages: More than 400,000 B.C. Hydro customers lost power at some point from Friday through Monday [2].
  • Communications: Downed power lines disrupted phone and cell service in parts of Vancouver Island and northwest B.C. [2].
  • Wind damage: A Prince George resident said a fallen tree remained on her home for days because continuing high winds prevented contractors from removing it. Environment Canada warned of gusts up to 90 km/h in the area [8].
  • Rainfall forecast: Forecasters warned of rainfall rates up to 20 millimetres an hour on western and inland Vancouver Island and as much as 160 millimetres along the Sea-to-Sky Highway by Thursday morning [2].

Perspectives

Village of Pemberton: The village warned that its evacuation alert for all properties in its jurisdiction could become an order with little notice if conditions changed quickly [2].

Industry Impact

  • Electricity restoration: B.C. Hydro said fallen trees, debris and soft ground were slowing repairs, while remote Zeballos required specialized equipment transported by helicopter [2].
  • Road preparation: Ministry contractors were clearing debris from roadways and drainage infrastructure and monitoring corridors during the storm [7].

Humanitarian Impact

B.C. Hydro warned that some drenched areas could remain without power for days [2].

Travel Advisory

  • Road safety: The provincial transportation ministry warned of pooling water, fallen debris and possible short-notice closures on Highway 99, Highway 1 and the Malahat [7]. Check DriveBC before travelling, give road crews space and avoid closed or flooded routes.
  • Flood safety: The River Forecast Centre warned that fast river flows can destabilize banks [1]. Stay away from riverbanks and flowing or flooded areas.
