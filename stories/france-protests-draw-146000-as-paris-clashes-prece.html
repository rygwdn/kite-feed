France protests draw 146,000 as Paris clashes precede wage talks

Category: World

Protests continued in France for a fourth consecutive weekend on Saturday, with 146,000 people demonstrating nationwide, including 46,000 in Paris, according to the Interior Ministry. Unions estimated turnout at 250,000. The authorized march in Paris was largely peaceful before clashes near Place de la Nation, where police fired tear gas after a group attacked officers, police said. Police also used tear gas to disperse an unauthorized gathering in Lyon [15][13][12]. The protests began with high-school students opposing conditions in schools and widened to include anger over living costs, wages and public services. France’s public debt, reported at 119% of gross domestic product, limits the government’s room to meet demands. The next presidential election is about five months away, and polls cited by French media put far-right candidate Marine Le Pen ahead [12][16][15][9]. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu is due to meet union and employer representatives on Monday, November 9, for talks on wages and purchasing power. Student and teacher groups have called for further strike action on Tuesday, November 10, seeking more education spending [8][15][28].

Key Points

  • Thursday turnout: The Nov. 5 nationwide union protest drew 575,000 people according to authorities, while unions put turnout at 1.6 million [15][13].
  • Union demands: Unions are seeking minimum-wage and broader pay increases, tax reforms and better learning conditions; the minimum wage has had no discretionary increase since 2012 [16][2].
  • Banned gathering: Police surrounded about 100 Yellow Vests supporters at the banned rally at Place de la Concorde; coordinator Benjamin Ball and spokeswoman Azelma Sigaux were among those arrested [8][15][27].
  • Arrest total: The Interior Ministry reported about 350 arrests nationwide by Saturday evening in connection with the demonstrations [12].
  • Police scrutiny: Dozens of internal investigations have been opened into allegations of brutality against demonstrators [15][8].

Perspectives

Student Union: Spokeswoman Sabine Kharbach said young people were mobilizing because the government’s response remained unsatisfactory and described Saturday as a chance to maintain pressure.
