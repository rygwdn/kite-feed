SpaceX Crew-12 Dragon docks at International Space Station

Category: Science

Four astronauts on SpaceX’s Crew-12 mission arrived at the International Space Station on Saturday, Feb. 14, 2026, docking their Dragon capsule to the orbiting lab [1][2]. Space.com framed the docking as a timely boost for a short-staffed station crew after recent disruptions [1], while The Guardian and The Hindu noted it followed an earlier crew’s return to Earth ahead of schedule because of a medical issue [4][5]. NPR said the crew will spend about eight months in orbit, running experiments meant to help pave the way for human exploration beyond Earth orbit [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Valentine’s Day framing: Space.com dubbed the Feb. 14 docking a “Valentine’s Day” arrival [1].
  • International crew makeup: The Guardian said the incoming team includes U.S., French and Russian astronauts [4].
  • Transit time: The Guardian reported SpaceX got the crew to the ISS about a day after launching from Cape Canaveral [4].
  • Medical-evacuation context: The Hindu described the earlier off-nominal return as NASA’s first medical evacuation in 65 years of human spaceflight [5].

Historical Background

The ISS has relied on regular crew-rotation missions to maintain a continuous human presence in orbit since 2000 [common]. NASA began flying astronauts to the ISS aboard SpaceX’s Crew Dragon in 2020, restoring U.S. crew-launch capability to orbit after the Space Shuttle era ended in 2011 [common].
