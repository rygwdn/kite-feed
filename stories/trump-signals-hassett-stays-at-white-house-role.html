Trump signals Hassett stays at White House role

Category: Business

President Trump said he would prefer National Economic Council director Kevin Hassett to remain in his White House job rather than become the next Federal Reserve chair, injecting new uncertainty into the succession plan for Jerome Powell [2][1][7]. Markets interpreted the shift as improving the odds for Kevin Warsh, a former Fed governor also seen as a leading contender, as investors recalibrated expectations for the Fed’s future policy direction [4][5]. The comments land as Powell’s chair term is set to end May 15, and after heightened tensions around Fed independence following a Justice Department criminal investigation into Powell that Powell publicly criticized [4][6].

Did you know: The Federal Reserve chair position is often described as one of the most influential jobs in global finance because US interest-rate policy shapes borrowing costs worldwide [common].

Key Points

  • Leadership timeline: Powell’s chair term is scheduled to end May 15, and the articles note he could still remain on the Fed’s board as a governor afterward, an unusual but possible step [4].
  • Warsh viewed hawkish: Investors and commentators highlighted Kevin Warsh as a more hawkish option than Hassett, a perception that helped push bond yields higher after Trump’s remarks [4][5].
  • Fed subpoena fallout: Powell said Justice Department subpoenas of the Federal Reserve amounted to punishment for not cutting rates as sharply as President Trump wanted, and coverage says the episode has disrupted expectations around the selection process [4][6].
  • Trump-Powell history: Trump appointed Powell in 2018 and later criticized him over rate increases that year, underscoring the long-running frictions that frame today’s succession speculation [4].
  • Television praise: At the White House event, Trump praised Hassett’s recent TV performance while signaling he wanted him to stay in his current role, a detail that sharpened market attention to personnel signals as policy inputs [4].

Industry Impact

  • Rate-sensitive sectors: Expect heightened attention in housing, banks, and other rate-sensitive industries as investors infer future policy stance from chair contenders described as more hawkish or less hawkish [4][5].
  • Policy credibility: The reporting says the Justice Department investigation and subpoenas involving the Fed chair have become part of the backdrop for the succession decision, raising the stakes for perceptions of central-bank independence that can affect risk premiums across markets [4][6].
  • White House economics: Keeping Hassett at the National Economic Council suggests the White House may prioritize continuity in its internal economic coordination even as it weighs a separate path for Fed leadership [1][2].

Historical Background

The Federal Reserve’s chair is nominated by the US president and confirmed by the Senate, and the central bank is designed to operate independently when setting monetary policy [common]. President Trump appointed Jerome Powell in 2018, but later publicly criticized him for the Fed’s 2018 rate increases, a history the coverage ties to today’s scrutiny of the next chair choice [4].

Q&A

Q: What formal steps come next in picking a Fed chair?

A: The president nominates a chair and the Senate confirms; the chair is chosen from sitting governors or a new nominee who must also be confirmed to the Board of Governors [common].

Q: Why would yields rise on a “more hawkish” Fed chair expectation?

A: Markets generally price higher expected policy rates into bond yields, especially at the short and intermediate maturities, when investors anticipate tighter monetary policy [common].

Q: If Powell stops being chair, can he still influence policy?

A: Yes—if he remains a Fed governor, he would still vote on monetary policy at FOMC meetings, though he would no longer set the committee’s agenda as chair [4][common].
