NASA's Roman telescope activates 300-megapixel infrared camera

Category: Science

NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has powered on its 300-megapixel Wide Field Instrument, an infrared camera designed to survey large areas of the sky while retaining fine detail. The spacecraft is traveling toward the second Sun-Earth Lagrange point, about one million miles from Earth, and is undergoing a commissioning process expected to last months [1]. The telescope’s Coronagraph Instrument, designed to image planets, has also completed initial digital, electronic and mechanical checks. Roman will investigate dark energy, map the distribution of matter and help discover planets beyond the solar system [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Cold testing: Before testing the camera, engineers conducted a 10-day decontamination process and cooled it to about minus 183 degrees Celsius [3].

Technical Details

  • Wide Field Instrument test hardware: After cooling, engineers tested the camera’s filters, prisms and focus mechanism in microgravity [3].
