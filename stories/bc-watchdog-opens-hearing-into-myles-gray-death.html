B.C. watchdog opens hearing into Myles Gray death

Category: Canada

A public hearing has begun in Vancouver into the 2015 death of Myles Gray after an encounter involving Vancouver police officers, a process being led by British Columbia’s Office of the Police Complaint Commissioner [1][4]. The hearing is examining police conduct connected to the death of the 33-year-old, more than a decade after the incident [4][1]. Coverage of the first day focused on emotional testimony from Gray’s mother as the public process formally got underway [2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: British Columbia has a dedicated civilian police-oversight agency, the Office of the Police Complaint Commissioner, which can convene public hearings in certain cases [4].

  • Public hearing (police oversight): A public hearing is a formal, on-the-record proceeding convened by B.C.’s police watchdog to examine evidence and conduct in a police-related incident in a transparent setting [4].
  • Police Complaint Commissioner role: The Office of the Police Complaint Commissioner in British Columbia oversees investigations and can initiate public hearings into police conduct, as in the review of Myles Gray’s death [4][1].
