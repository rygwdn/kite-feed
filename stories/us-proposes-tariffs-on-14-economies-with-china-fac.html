US proposes tariffs on 14 economies, with China facing up to 40%

Category: World

The Trump administration proposed new import tariffs on 14 economies Wednesday, citing a months-long U.S. investigation that alleged their industrial policies support manufacturing capacity beyond market demand. The proposal is Washington’s latest effort to restore tariffs after the Supreme Court struck down earlier duties. The targeted economies include China, India, the European Union, Japan and South Korea. The U.S. Trade Representative’s office proposed a 20% rate for China, which could rise to 40% if the U.S.-China trade truce breaks down. India faces a proposed rate of 25%, with a lower 17% rate tied to a trade agreement, while the EU’s rate is expected to remain within its agreed 15% ceiling [6][16][1][20]. China, India and the European Union have rejected the overcapacity allegations; the EU says the proposal must respect its existing tariff agreement [5][9][29]. The proposed duties are not final: public comments and hearings must take place before implementation, which could begin as early as January 2027. Separately, Washington proposed a 10% tariff on Brazilian aircraft and new duties on some sugar imports, prompting Brazil to warn it could retaliate [32][53][55].

Key Points

  • Public process: The U.S. Trade Representative will accept comments through Jan. 5, 2027, and begin hearings on Jan. 25 [32].
  • Price concerns: Economists warn importers could pass higher costs on to consumers, while small-business owners say they are squeezed by input costs and customers’ resistance to further price increases [6][9].
  • Brazil measures: The separate proposal would end Brazil’s exemption for finished aircraft by imposing a 10% duty and revise the treatment of out-of-quota sugar imports [45][54].
  • Targeted sectors: The U.S. Trade Representative singled out pharmaceuticals and medical devices, steel, solar products, and information and communications technology in its concerns about Indian manufacturing policies [76][12].
  • Tariff mechanics: Most proposed duties would not be added to existing tariffs; China is an exception, while Washington says roughly $30 billion of Chinese imports could be excluded [20][36][50].

Technical Details

  • Rate calculation: The proposal sets a 17% rate for India if a reciprocal trade agreement addresses the investigation’s findings. Otherwise, duties could reach 25%. The U.S. Trade Representative says it may adjust the levies to account for existing Section 301 and Section 232 duties [4][78].
  • Section 301 process: Section 301 allows the U.S. Trade Representative to investigate foreign trade practices and propose action. The process includes public comments and hearings before the proposed duties become final [66][76].

Industry Impact

  • Indian exports: The proposed duties could affect labor-intensive Indian sectors including textiles, gems and jewelry, and marine products; analysts say exporters may accelerate shipments or diversify markets [8].
  • Brazilian aerospace and sugar: The separate proposal would remove the exemption for Brazilian finished aircraft and raise duties on sugar imports above quotas; Embraer is among the companies directly exposed [45][66].
  • Brazilian aerospace: A 10% U.S. duty on finished Brazilian aircraft would affect Embraer and could raise costs for U.S. airlines that lack a domestic alternative for regional routes [54].

Historical Background

The administration is relying on Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, which lets the U.S. Trade Representative’s office (USTR) investigate foreign trade practices and propose duties in response. This differs from the emergency-powers authority used for the invalidated tariffs [6][33].

Business Angle

  • Under the proposal, India would face a 17% tariff if a trade agreement addresses the U.S. findings, compared with duties of up to 25% without a deal [64].

Q&A

A: The proposal includes exemptions for energy products, some foods the U.S. cannot readily grow, certain semiconductors and pharmaceuticals [77][44].
