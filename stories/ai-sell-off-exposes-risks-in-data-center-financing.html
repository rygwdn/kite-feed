AI sell-off exposes risks in data-center financing

Category: AI

The AI-stock sell-off has intensified scrutiny of the debt and corporate guarantees financing data-center expansion. Nvidia’s guarantees totaled $118 billion as of Oct. 25, while its commitments to smaller cloud providers were estimated at $32 billion over the next several years. On a Nov. 17 earnings call, interim chief executive Colette Kress said Nvidia would scrutinize AI cloud companies’ financial strength and prioritize sites with visible, durable demand [16]. Those commitments have helped reassure lenders financing data centers and computing clusters. But analysts warn that banks may now be less willing to extend bridge loans to smaller providers whose debt they expect to resell. Nvidia’s tighter scrutiny could affect companies that lack established revenue or strong backers [16][57]. Jon von Tetzchner, CEO and co-founder of San Francisco-based Vivaldi, questioned whether many AI applications are useful. An unnamed worker at a major San Francisco AI company told the BBC he expected a downturn and feared it would affect him [14][12].

Key Points

  • Policy watch: Investors were awaiting U.S. October personal consumption expenditures inflation data, scheduled for Wednesday during a shortened holiday week [80].
  • IPO signal: Anthropic’s record $107 billion initial public offering raised much more than expected but failed to lift shares of backers Google and Amazon or offering partners Broadcom, AMD and Nvidia [11].
  • City spillover: San Francisco’s AI boom has brought business to services such as Paw Perk, a dog-walking service for startup offices. Rents and house prices have risen, while office vacancies have fallen [12].
  • Additional financing: Nvidia, Google and Microsoft participated in a concurrent $26 billion private placement, bringing combined gross proceeds from the IPO and placement to more than $107 billion [92].
  • AI labels: Vivaldi co-founder Jon von Tetzchner likened companies’ rush to adopt AI labels to the earlier blockchain and crypto boom. He said some use popular technology labels to attract investors despite a questionable connection to their existing businesses [14].
  • Long contracts: The disclosed commitments include multiyear purchases and equipment leases extending as far as 2045; $186.2 billion in fixed commitments include termination rights or conditions [93].
  • Market losses: Taiwan’s Taiex lost more than $500 billion in market value over the week, while South Korea’s Kospi shed more than $1.8 trillion from its June peak, according to Financial Times estimates [22].
  • AI spending: Gartner projected global AI-related investment of $2.7 trillion in 2026 and $3.6 trillion in 2027 [52].
  • Investor flows: Goldman Sachs data showed hedge funds had sold global equities for a third consecutive week, with short sales exceeding long purchases by 1.5 to 1 [61].
  • Regional demand: Santa Lucia Asset Management CEO Florian Weidinger warned that China’s shift toward domestic AI hardware could restrict Nvidia’s access to Asia, while competition from more chipmakers and weaker demand could squeeze margins and prompt order revisions [58].
  • Enterprise demand: Anthropic had 11,900 customers, with more than 100 generating at least $100,000 in annual revenue, according to figures reported around the listing [92].
  • China’s buying measures: Chinese exchanges also announced transaction-fee cuts, and Shenzhen proposed a market-making mechanism for its ChiNext board [15].
  • Investor rationale: Notable Capital said its research found developers chose Claude over ChatGPT 70% of the time, reversing the prior year’s pattern [111].

Technical Details

  • Nvidia guarantees: Nvidia’s guarantees totaled $118 billion as of Oct. 25, while its commitments to smaller cloud providers were estimated at $32 billion over the next several years. Those arrangements helped reassure lenders that borrowers could obtain GPUs and generate data-center revenue [16].
  • GPU-backed bridge loans: Banks have financed data centers and computing clusters with bridge loans backed by graphics processing units, expecting to syndicate the debt to institutional investors. Lenders may now require stronger evidence of contracted revenue and project completion [16].
  • South Korea’s stabilization fund: Financial firms and other institutions created the 10.76 trillion-won fund, valued at nearly $8 billion, to support markets during sharp downturns. The government said it was preparing the fund for immediate activation [15].
  • China margin financing: The exchanges’ reduction of the minimum margin ratio from 100% to 80% means an investor putting up 1 million yuan could theoretically borrow enough to buy 1.25 million yuan in shares, increasing both leverage and buying capacity [3].

Industry Impact

  • Semiconductors: South Korea’s Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are major memory-chip suppliers for AI applications, leaving the Kospi particularly sensitive to changes in expectations for AI demand [27][78].
  • Power equipment supply chain: AI data-center demand is drawing on turbine suppliers that also serve aviation and energy infrastructure. Reported disruptions include delays to container ships and a planned supersonic airliner, as well as construction problems at power stations on two continents [4].
  • Adjacent infrastructure: Selling also hit optical-products maker Corning and power provider Bloom Energy, which supply data-centre connectivity and electricity [79].
  • Venture capital: GIC, Baillie Gifford and Notable Capital invested more than $4.5 billion across Anthropic’s later funding rounds, and GIC held about 1% of the company after the IPO, according to a person familiar with its finances [102][111].
  • AI infrastructure financing: An investment-grade credit rating could give Anthropic access to the $8.6 trillion U.S. investment-grade corporate bond market; suppliers including Broadcom and Nvidia have guaranteed loans to AI compute providers [43].

Historical Background

South Korea’s securities-market stabilization fund was established in 2020 during the Covid-19 pandemic, according to CNBC [15]. Local media cited by Yonhap said it was last used during the 2008 global financial crisis [3]. China had raised its minimum margin ratio for credit-financed stock purchases from 80% to 100% in January 2026; the exchanges’ new reduction reverses that increase after 10 months [85].

Business Angle

  • One investor cited Samsung and SK Hynix trading at three to four times forward earnings, compared with Anthropic at 20 times projected revenue, as a reason to distinguish chip suppliers from AI developers [57].
  • UBS’s Eva Lee said investors seeking value in the AI sell-off could look for companies with strong balance sheets, sound fundamentals and the capacity to buy back shares [2].
  • Portfolio manager Owen Lamont argued that companies may sell shares either because they are overvalued or to fund AI investment, so equity issuance alone does not rule out an investment opportunity [22].
