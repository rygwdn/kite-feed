NDP seeks greenwashing probe into Carney energy ads

Category: Canada

NDP parliamentary leader Don Davies asked the Competition Bureau to investigate whether federal advertising on clean energy infrastructure amounts to illegal greenwashing, challenging Prime Minister Mark Carney's energy policy messaging in television and social media ads [1][2][3][4]. Davies cited an Environment and Climate Change Canada advertisement and a June 30 video in which Carney said Canada's emissions "will be higher in the next few years" under his government's policy changes [4]. Davies argues ads saying Canada is building a "cleaner future" could violate the law because Carney has acknowledged near-term emissions will rise [3][4]. The Competition Bureau said it received Davies' letter Tuesday and that any investigative work is confidential; a spokesperson said Competition Act provisions generally apply to commercials promoting a product or business, not Government of Canada communications [4].

To put it most bluntly, we think the Carney government is using taxpayer dollars to mislead Canadians and greenwashing. And not only is that unethical and inappropriate, but it's also illegal

— Don Davies (SooToday)

Key Points

  • Legal definition: The Competition Bureau defines greenwashing as false, misleading or unsubstantiated environmental claims about a product, service or practice [4].
  • Carney rationale: In the June 30 video titled "Forward Guidance: Canada's Energy Future," Carney said Canada's old climate plan was too expensive and divisive. He said his new plan includes significant renewable-energy investment and would lower emissions over the long term [4].
