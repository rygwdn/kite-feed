Japanese memory-chip maker Kioxia slides as AI investors reassess returns

Category: AI

Japanese memory-chip maker Kioxia’s shares fell as much as 17% on Monday after its December-quarter outlook disappointed investors, despite continued demand for chips used in AI infrastructure [9]. The company said delays in data-center storage shipments would push some sales into the following quarter, while a larger share of lower-priced smartphone and PC products would weigh on average selling prices [9]. Investors are scrutinizing how quickly heavy spending on data centers and chips can generate returns [14][31]. October factory surveys showed growth slowing in Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, key chip-exporting economies, as output and new orders lost momentum [14][21]. China’s STAR50 index, which includes AI and semiconductor companies, had fallen more than 40% from its summer peak, while Korean chipmakers were managing their exposure to a potential slowdown in customer investment [3][31]. Kioxia said its planned 2027 capacity was already sold out and long-term agreements cover more than half of its expected 2028 shipments [9].

Technical Details

  • Long-term memory agreements: These contracts can set supply volumes, pricing methods and deposits in advance, helping chipmakers plan production and investment while including provisions intended to ensure customers follow through on orders [31].
  • Phased capacity additions: Samsung plans to secure factory space before installing equipment as needed, while SK hynix is advancing construction but will bring clean-room capacity online in stages, separating construction from increases in supply [31].

Industry Impact

  • Handset market: Global smartphone shipments fell 20% year over year in the third quarter of 2026, reaching their lowest third-quarter level since 2013 amid rising memory-chip prices [53].
  • Tax revenues: A sustained downturn in memory demand could affect South Korea’s broader economy, which relies heavily on corporate tax revenue from Samsung Electronics and SK hynix [53].

Q&A

Q: Could a pullback in AI spending end the current memory shortage?

A: Samsung and SK hynix still expect memory shortages to last as long as 2030, but financing problems among major technology customers could delay investment or reduce orders, putting demand at risk [31].

Q: Why did investors react so sharply to Kioxia’s outlook?

A: Kioxia’s shares had risen more than 750% in the first half of the year on expectations of growing data-center storage demand. Its December-quarter forecast fell short of analysts’ expectations [9].
