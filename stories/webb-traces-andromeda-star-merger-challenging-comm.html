Webb traces Andromeda star merger, challenging common-envelope models

Category: Science

Astronomers say a smaller star spiraled into a red giant and merged with it in the Andromeda galaxy, marking the first closely tracked observation of this stage of binary-star evolution. The system, named AT 2025abao, brightened over several years before erupting in October 2025. NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope observed it 47 days after the outburst peaked, helping researchers reconstruct the event [3][1][12][5]. The team estimates that the giant had seven times the Sun’s mass and the companion about 0.7 times the Sun’s mass. Their interaction ended after about seven orbits, or seven years. The findings, published in Nature on Nov. 18, indicate that energy transport during the shared-envelope phase may be more complex than standard models describe. Researchers say continued observations are needed to determine the remnant’s state and confirm the merger [2][5][6][4].

Primary Source: View original article

This is probably the most uncertain and critical phase in the evolution of a star

— Jason Nordhaus (New Scientist)

Key Points

  • Ejected material: The eruption expelled an estimated 0.1 to 0.2 solar masses of material, while analysis suggests only about a tenth of the giant’s outer gas escaped [1][5].
  • Early evolution: The giant swelled to more than twice its original size while its surface temperature barely changed [5].
  • More candidates: One research team has identified about 30 possible similar mergers, while the Vera C. Rubin Observatory and Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope are expected to search for more [8][12].

Perspectives

Jason Nordhaus of the Rochester Institute of Technology said nearly one-third of stars may go through the common-envelope phase. That share could be higher if engulfments of planets and other nonstellar objects are included, he said [6].

Sources: New Scientist

Timeline

  • 2018: Archival observations show AT 2025abao began gradually brightening [4][12]
  • February 2023: Researchers noticed unusual variability in data from the retired NEOWISE mission [3][2]
  • September 2024: WINTER observations found increased brightness and newly shed dust [2][13]
  • 17 October 2025: Zwicky Transient Facility detected the system’s rapid eruption [2][9]
  • 24 December 2025: Webb observed AT 2025abao 47 days after the outburst peaked [3][9]
  • 18 November 2026: The research team published its findings in Nature [1][3]

Technical Details

  • Common-envelope inspiral: As the smaller star moved through the giant’s shared gas envelope, drag removed orbital energy and drove it inward. Observations of this phase can help determine how binary systems lose orbital energy [3][2].
  • Infrared reconstruction: Webb’s mid-infrared measurements traced dust shed by the giant over centuries. Researchers matched the measurements to updated simulations to estimate the stars’ pre-eruption properties [3][2].

Scientific Significance

  • Stellar energy transport: The observed evolution suggests energy from the inspiraling companion was redistributed through convection inside the expanded giant, rather than immediately inflating its atmosphere as some earlier models assumed [6].
  • Evidence and uncertainty: Webb’s data and observations across ultraviolet, visible and infrared wavelengths support the merger reconstruction, but dust still obscures the remnant and researchers say follow-up observations are needed to settle its final state [3][4][5].
  • Relevance to compact binaries: Common-envelope evolution is thought to help produce tightly bound pairs that can later become neutron stars or black holes; observing a system during the process supplies data for testing those pathways [3][2].
