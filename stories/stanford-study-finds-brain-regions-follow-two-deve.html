Stanford study finds brain regions follow two developmental lineages

Category: Science

A Stanford-led study published in Nature Neuroscience finds that the forebrain and midbrain develop from one embryonic cell lineage, while the hindbrain develops from another. The researchers propose that these distinct developmental programs may reflect the joining of ancient neural systems during evolution rather than a shared origin for all major brain regions [1][2][3][4]. The work also identifies a different route for producing hindbrain motor neurons in laboratory cultures, which researchers have struggled to produce. Such cells could help scientists study disorders including spinal muscular atrophy and ALS [2].
