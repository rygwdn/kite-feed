White House accuses China-based entities of industrial-scale AI model distillation

Category: Technology

The White House said Thursday that foreign entities, mainly based in China, have been running "deliberate, industrial-scale campaigns" to distill leading U.S. AI systems. It also signaled a stronger federal response to protect American AI models and intellectual property [4][7][8]. In a memo to federal agencies, the Office of Science and Technology Policy said these campaigns rely on heavy volumes of prompts to extract model behavior and build cheaper rival systems that can perform similarly on selected benchmarks without matching the original models in full [4][6]. The administration said it will step up engagement with major U.S. AI companies while exploring accountability measures for foreign actors tied to these efforts [6][8]. The accusations come as competition in advanced AI intensifies and after U.S. labs publicly alleged suspicious activity earlier this year. China rejected the claims as "slander," underscoring how AI security is becoming a central front in the broader U.S.-China technology rivalry [10][7].

Primary Source: View original article

The US government has information indicating that foreign entities, principally based in China, are engaged in deliberate, industrial-scale campaigns to distill US frontier AI systems.

— Michael Kratsios (White House memo reported by Ars Technica)

Did you know: Anthropic said the suspicious Claude activity it linked to three Chinese AI firms came through roughly 24,000 fraudulent accounts, a scale that shows how distributed modern model-harvesting campaigns can be [4][6].

Key Points

  • Named allegations: Anthropic said in February that DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and MiniMax used about 24,000 fraudulent accounts to generate more than 16 million exchanges with Claude, while OpenAI told lawmakers it had seen indicators of ongoing distillation attempts by DeepSeek [4][6][10].
  • Broader pattern: Ars Technica reported that Google said in January that commercially motivated actors, not limited to China, tried to clone Gemini by prompting it more than 100,000 times to train cheaper copycat systems [10].
  • Policy signal: NPR said the administration framed the issue as part of China narrowing the gap with the United States in AI, with a promised crackdown meant to protect domestic technological advantages [8][9].

Technical Details

  • Model distillation abuse: The memo describes an attack pattern in which a target model receives very large numbers of queries so an imitator can learn its input-output behavior and train a lower-cost system that matches selected benchmark performance without reproducing the full original model [4].

Industry Impact

  • Frontier AI labs: The White House said it will increase engagement with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, pointing to tighter coordination between the government and leading U.S. model developers [6].
  • Chinese AI firms: Anthropic publicly named DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and MiniMax in its February allegations, adding pressure on Chinese developers already under scrutiny in the U.S. policy debate [4][6].

Historical Background

Concerns about AI model distillation grew after Chinese startup DeepSeek drew intense attention in early 2026 over whether high-performing systems could be built more cheaply using outputs from frontier U.S. models [10][8]. In February 2026, Anthropic publicly accused DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and MiniMax of large-scale abusive access to Claude, and OpenAI separately told congressional lawmakers it had seen signs of ongoing distillation attempts by DeepSeek [4][6]. The current White House memo builds on those earlier company claims by turning them into a government-wide security and industrial policy issue [4][7].

Action Items

  • Review AI vendor safeguards: If your organization uses third-party AI APIs, ask vendors how they detect abusive high-volume querying and other attempts to extract model behavior, the same general risk highlighted in the White House memo [4].
  • Limit sensitive output exposure: For teams building internal AI tools, log unusual query spikes and apply rate limits so proprietary workflows are harder to replicate through mass prompting [4][common].
