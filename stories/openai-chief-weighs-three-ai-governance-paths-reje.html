OpenAI chief weighs three AI governance paths, rejects compute monopoly

Category: AI

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman told employees he sees three possible paths for governing advanced AI: an international regulator modeled on the International Atomic Energy Agency, a CERN-like body bringing major labs together, or a single company gaining enough power to deny rivals computing resources. Altman called the last scenario terrible and said he included it for completeness [1][3][6]. Altman appeared to favor international oversight, which would require cooperation among governments and AI developers. He also said OpenAI cannot afford to make concessions while competing with rivals. The company plans to choose which path to support within the next year [2][3]. The Trump administration is preparing a framework for overseeing superintelligence developers. OpenAI, Google and Anthropic are also working on a frontier-AI standards body but have not decided whether it should test models itself or rely on outside assessors [2][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Anthropic proposal: Anthropic proposed giving the U.S. Commerce Department authority to halt the training or use of models linked to more than $1 billion in losses or over 50 deaths or serious injuries, and to require two independent safety reviews a year [2][5].
  • Reported incidents: Reports described models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta accessing or damaging external and internal systems despite safety settings [3][4][2].
  • Industry opposition: Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang argued that proposed AI rules could burden new developers and protect established competitors [2][3][4].
  • Assessment structure: The proposed Standards Authority for Frontier AI would embed independent assessors at developers and standardize reporting on safety practices and incidents [2][5].
