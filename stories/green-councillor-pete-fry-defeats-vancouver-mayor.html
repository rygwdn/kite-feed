Green councillor Pete Fry defeats Vancouver Mayor Ken Sim in B.C. vote

Category: Canada

Pete Fry, a two-term Green Party city councillor, defeated Vancouver Mayor Ken Sim in British Columbia’s Oct. 17 municipal elections, becoming the city’s first Green mayor. With 145 of 149 polls reporting, Fry had 88,218 votes to Sim’s 62,603, and Sim’s ABC Vancouver lost its council majority [3][4]. Fry campaigned on housing, transportation and the economy, while Sim’s platform emphasized affordability and public safety [4]. Voters also chose new mayors in several other B.C. cities: Surrey elected former mayor Doug McCallum, and both Langley municipalities elected new mayors [15][25]. Vancouver voters also cast ballots on two plebiscite questions about banning open-air hard-drug use and asking the province to create a secure treatment facility; those results are expected Oct. 21 [3][4]. Fry said preparing the city budget would be his first order of business [4].

Key Points

  • Treatment proposal: One Vancouver ballot question asked whether the city should urge B.C. to turn part of the former St. Paul’s Hospital, or a similar site, into a secure facility with acute voluntary care and 100-plus involuntary care beds [3][4].
  • Housing pressures: Vancouver’s March 2025 homeless count identified 2,715 unhoused people, while the B.C. Coroners Service recorded 237 suspected overdose deaths in the city from January through August [3].
  • Langley turnover: In Langley Township, Jay Lundgren won 54% of the vote and his slate took six of eight council seats; in Langley City, Delaney Mack won 61% and her slate secured five of six [25].

Perspectives

Pete Fry, Vancouver mayor-elect: Fry said he wanted city hall to prioritize values-based policy and participatory democracy. He said he would seek federal housing investment and provincial help addressing street disorder [4].

Historical Background

Fry first won a Vancouver council seat in 2018 [3][4]. In 2022, Ken Sim and ABC Vancouver defeated then-mayor Kennedy Stewart [3].
