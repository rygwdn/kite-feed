SpaceX signs $6.3 billion AI compute deal

Category: AI

SpaceX signed a computing-power agreement with Reflection AI that could be worth up to $6.3 billion, giving the open-source AI startup immediate access to Nvidia GB300 chips and supporting hardware at SpaceX’s Colossus 2 data center near Memphis, Tennessee [1][6][7][9]. Reflection will pay $150 million a month starting July 1, 2026, under a contract scheduled to run through 2029 [1][6][8][9]. The contract allows either company to cancel with 90 days’ notice after the first three months [1][8][9]. Reflection has positioned its open-weight model strategy as an alternative to closed frontier AI labs such as Anthropic and OpenAI [1][8]. A Reflection spokesperson told TechCrunch the agreement gives the company “more compute” to build open models at scale [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Smaller contract: SpaceX’s Reflection agreement is smaller than its Anthropic and Google compute contracts, which TechCrunch said cost $1.25 billion and $920 million per month, respectively [1][8].
  • Open models: Reflection cited the U.S. government’s ban of Anthropic’s closed models, Fable and Mythos, as evidence that dependence on closed AI systems carries risks for nations and enterprises [1].
  • Customer base: Reports identified Reflection as another major SpaceX compute customer after Anthropic and Google [5][9].
  • DOE work: Tech in Asia said Reflection supports the U.S. Department of Energy’s Genesis Mission with foundational AI technology [10].

Perspectives

Elon Musk: Musk has publicly disputed descriptions of SpaceX’s Anthropic and Google compute contracts as three-year deals, saying those agreements can be canceled at any time.

Sources: TechCrunch

Gizmodo: Gizmodo described the Reflection agreement as part of SpaceX’s growing effort to monetize data-center capacity in addition to its rocket and space operations.

Sources: Gizmodo

Timeline

  • 2024: Reflection AI founded [1][8]
  • February 2026: SpaceX merged with Musk’s AI startup xAI [5]
  • 22 June 2026: TechCrunch reported the Reflection compute agreement [1]
  • 1 July 2026: Reflection’s $150 million monthly payments begin [1][6]
  • 2029: Reflection contract runs through 2029 if maintained [1][8]

Industry Impact

  • AI compute market: The contract ranks among the largest announced open-AI infrastructure commitments, according to Reflection’s description of the deal to TechCrunch [1].
  • AI startups: Reflection’s first compute deal is a multibillion-dollar infrastructure commitment for frontier-model development [1][7].

Historical Background

Reflection was founded in 2024, before announcing its first compute agreement with SpaceX [1][8]. SpaceX’s Colossus infrastructure grew out of xAI’s AI-compute buildout, and Gizmodo said SpaceX began marketing that capacity externally after its merger with Musk’s AI startup xAI [1][5].

Business Angle

  • SpaceX has attracted several large AI customers, but customer concentration becomes a risk if major tenants renegotiate or cancel compute contracts [1][5][9].
  • Reflection gains access to scarce frontier-training hardware without building its own facility, which may reduce upfront capital needs but creates dependence on SpaceX infrastructure [1][common].
  • If SpaceX leases unused compute profitably, its data-center assets could become a separate earnings engine from launch and satellite services [5][common].
