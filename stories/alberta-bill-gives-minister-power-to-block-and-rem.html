Alberta bill gives minister power to block and remove bike lanes

Category: Canada

Alberta introduced Bill 43 on Nov. 19, which would bar municipalities from building bike lanes that take space from motor vehicles without provincial approval. It would also allow the transportation minister to order some existing lanes removed, with cities paying the costs. Transportation Minister Devin Dreeshen says the changes are meant to preserve road capacity and address concerns about congestion, parking and emergency access. The bill does not set out criteria for approving exemptions or selecting lanes for removal [1][2][4]. If passed, the law is expected to take effect in April 2027, with regulations due in the spring to spell out implementation details [1][4]. The bill also proposes new rules for personal e-scooters and expanded provincial oversight of municipal light-rail projects [1].

Key Points

  • Removal costs: Edmonton Mayor Andrew Knack said taking out the city’s existing bike lanes could cost at least $150 million; the bill assigns removal costs to municipalities [2][3].
  • Scooter rules: Municipalities could authorize privately owned and rental e-scooters under provincial minimum safety standards; Albertans currently need a provincial exemption to ride privately owned devices [1].
  • Rail oversight: The proposed Urban Rail Transit Act would cover current and future urban rail lines and require municipalities to notify the provincial cabinet minister about planned changes [1].

Perspectives

Historical Background

Ontario’s bike-lane dispute arose after Premier Doug Ford’s government targeted Toronto routes for removal, arguing they worsened congestion. The lower court found the plan posed risks to cyclist safety, and cycling groups have asked Canada’s Supreme Court to hear the case [2][4][1].
