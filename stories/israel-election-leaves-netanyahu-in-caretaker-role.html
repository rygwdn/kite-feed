Israel election leaves Netanyahu in caretaker role as coalition talks loom

Category: World

With about 90% of ballots counted after Tuesday’s parliamentary election, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud-led bloc was projected to win 53 seats in Israel’s 120-seat Knesset, or parliament, against 48 for the opposition alliance led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot and 19 for Arab-led parties. Neither main bloc reaches the 61 seats needed for a majority. The election was Israel’s first since the Hamas-led Oct. 7, 2023, attack and the wars that followed [3][23][2]. Netanyahu will remain caretaker prime minister while parties try to form a government. Arab-led parties could give an opposition coalition the numbers to govern, but Netanyahu has rejected relying on their support, and Eisenkot has also ruled out a minority government backed by them [3][35]. President Isaac Herzog has urged parties to avoid another election. He is expected to consult party leaders after the release of official results, which are due by Nov. 4 [2][60]. Netanyahu called for a right-wing government and raised the possibility of renewed joint military action with the United States against Iran. Analysts say limits on caretaker governments do not remove their authority on security matters, while Eisenkot warned officials against using security issues for political purposes [10][37][9].

Key Points

  • Special ballots: The remaining double-envelope ballots include votes from soldiers, diplomats and people unable to vote at their assigned polling stations. The batch is estimated at 430,000 to 480,000 ballots, and counting began early Thursday [35][23][2].
  • High turnout: Turnout reached 77.7%, its highest level in more than two decades, with especially strong participation reported in Arab communities [35][24].
  • Market reaction: After the inconclusive result, the shekel weakened about 1.6% to 3.10 per U.S. dollar and Tel Aviv’s benchmark stock indexes fell more than 3% [9][12].
  • Coalition arithmetic: Whether smaller parties clear Israel’s 3.25% electoral threshold could still affect the official seat allocation [60].

Historical Background

The Netanyahu-led coalition won a 64-seat majority in the 2022 election [30][26]. In 2021, the Arab party Ra’am joined a governing coalition led by Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid [14][67].

  • Hezbollah: Hezbollah Secretary-General Naim Qassem said the group was not concerned by the election result and vowed to continue its “resistance” against Israel [2].
