Ontario measles case prompts airport warning after national outbreak declared over

Category: Canada

A new measles case in Ontario has prompted another exposure warning at Pearson airport, weeks after Canada’s two-year national outbreak involving thousands of cases was declared over. Durham Region says the case involves an unimmunized child who recently returned from international travel. Potential exposure sites on Nov. 3 included Ethiopian Airlines Flight ET552, Pearson Terminal 1 and the Lakeridge Health Ajax-Pickering emergency department [5][3][1]. People who may have been exposed should watch for symptoms through Nov. 24. Symptoms typically begin seven to 21 days after infection. Health officials advise checking that family members have received two doses of measles vaccine and calling ahead before seeking medical care if symptoms develop [5][3].

Key Points

  • Immunity: People born before 1970 are generally considered protected because they likely had measles as children [4][5].
  • Higher-risk groups: Infants under 1, unimmunized pregnant people and people with weakened immune systems face greater risk of measles complications [4][5].
  • Virus persistence: The measles virus can remain infectious in the air or on surfaces for up to two hours after an infected person leaves [4][5].
