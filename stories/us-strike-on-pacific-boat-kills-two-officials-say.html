US strike on Pacific boat kills two, officials say

Category: World

The US military says it carried out an airstrike on a vessel in the eastern Pacific Ocean that it assessed was involved in drug trafficking, killing two people [2][3][10][11][1][5][6][9]. US Southern Command said intelligence indicated the boat was traveling along known narco-trafficking routes [2]. Officials described the action as part of a wider campaign targeting suspected drug-smuggling boats since early September 2025, a campaign that critics say raises human-rights concerns [2][3][8]. The Coast Guard was notified to conduct search-and-rescue, and reporting across outlets notes at least one person survived, with accounts differing on whether the survivor was being searched for or already in custody [4][10][11][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Intelligence confirmed the vessel was transiting along known narco-trafficking routes in the eastern Pacific and was engaged in narco-trafficking operations

— US Southern Command spokesperson (statement) (The Guardian)

Did you know: US Southern Command’s area of responsibility includes the Caribbean and parts of Central and South America [common].

Key Points

  • Strike count: CBS News reports this was the 35th US strike on alleged drug-smuggling boats since early September 2025 [3].
  • Deaths tally: Al Jazeera says the broader boat-strike campaign has killed 125 people since September [8].
  • Post-raid context: Several outlets frame the strike as the first known boat strike since a US operation that captured Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro on January 3 [1][3][5][10][6][7].
  • Rescue effort: US Southern Command said it alerted the Coast Guard to launch search-and-rescue operations after the strike [10][6].
  • Congress angle: The Globe and Mail notes the US House failed to pass a resolution aimed at reining in President Trump’s Venezuela war powers [7].

Historical Background

The reporting situates the strike within a surge of US actions in late 2025 aimed at suspected maritime drug-trafficking, with Southern Command saying it has conducted dozens of strikes against boats it alleges smuggle drugs [2][10]. Separately, multiple outlets connect this episode to the US raid that captured Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro earlier in January 2026, which has increased focus on US operations tied to Venezuela [1][3][5].
