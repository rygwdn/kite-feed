Bangladesh’s first woman prime minister Khaleda Zia dies at 80

Category: World

Khaleda Zia, Bangladesh’s first female prime minister and longtime leader of the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP), died on Tuesday after a prolonged illness, her party said [1][3][16][34]. She was 80, according to multiple outlets, though some reports listed her age as 79 [1][3][16][15][18]. Her death closes a defining chapter in Bangladeshi politics, which for decades revolved around her rivalry with former prime minister Sheikh Hasina, who was removed from power after a mass uprising last year [16][20][1]. Bangladesh’s interim leader Muhammad Yunus and Hasina both issued condolence statements, as tributes also came from abroad [1][24][13].

Bangladesh "has lost a great guardian".

Did you know: Some reporting noted plans for a special air-ambulance flight to London for treatment, but it was not pursued because her condition was deemed too unstable [1].

Key Points

  • Health details: Doctors cited advanced liver cirrhosis alongside arthritis, diabetes, and heart and chest problems during her long illness [1].
  • State mourning: Bangladesh announced three days of state mourning, with her funeral scheduled for December 31 [22][29][36].
  • Election stakes: Zia had said in November she intended to campaign for the February 2026 election, widely expected to be the first national vote since the uprising that toppled Hasina’s government [1][4][36].
  • Legal chapter: She was imprisoned on corruption charges in 2018 under Hasina’s government, and later released after Hasina was forced from power, with reports noting restrictions that prevented her from seeking medical treatment abroad while in custody [1][18].
  • Succession question: Several reports noted expectations that her son, Tarique Rahman, could be positioned for top leadership if the BNP wins power [1][4].

Perspectives

Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP): The party announced Zia’s death and asked supporters to pray for her, framing her as a national leader [1].

Timeline

  • August 2024: Reports say Zia was released after protests led to Hasina’s resignation [18].
  • November: Zia vowed to campaign for the February 2026 election [1][4].
  • Late November: She was rushed to hospital as her condition deteriorated [1].
  • December 31: Funeral set for Wednesday; Bangladesh observes state mourning [22][36].

Technical Details

  • Cirrhosis of the liver: Cirrhosis is long-term liver scarring that can progress to organ failure; Zia’s doctors described her condition as advanced [1].
  • State mourning: A government-declared period of official remembrance typically includes ceremonial observances and public appeals for order during funerals, which Yunus emphasized in a televised address [22].

Industry Impact

  • Bangladesh electoral landscape: With Zia’s death and Hasina living abroad, several outlets highlighted that Bangladesh’s next election could proceed without either of the two leaders who dominated politics for decades, potentially reshaping party strategies and coalition-building [21][16].

Historical Background

Zia entered frontline politics after the assassination of her husband, former president Ziaur Rahman, and later became the first woman elected prime minister of Bangladesh [17][19]. She won office after Bangladesh returned to electoral politics in the early 1990s and later governed again in the early 2000s, repeatedly trading power with rival leader Sheikh Hasina [27][37]. That long-running two-party rivalry, often described as a defining feature of the country’s post-independence political era, shaped Bangladesh’s politics for decades [16][20].
