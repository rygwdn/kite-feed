Ukrainian strike brings down Crimea bridge span, disrupting Russian routes

Category: World

Ukraine’s second strike in two days brought down a central road span and its supporting arch on the Kerch Bridge, cutting vehicle traffic between Russia and occupied Crimea. Reuters verified images showing the collapsed section. The bridge’s railway arch also sustained damage, but Russian-installed Crimean governor Sergei Aksyonov said train services had partially resumed. Russia also struck two major bridges in Kyiv [12][7][11]. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the bridge attack capped a seven-stage operation over several weeks that targeted Russian air defenses, radars and electronic-warfare equipment before the final drone and missile strikes. Russia’s Investigative Committee confirmed structural damage and opened a criminal case. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said officials would draw up a repair plan, and Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin said they were assessing the damage and alternative cargo routes [12][7][1]. The bridge once served as a key military supply route. The channel beneath it also carries commercial shipping between the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea. Maritime analyst Andrey Sizov warned that debris could delay the resumption of Sea of Azov shipping. President Vladimir Putin is due to meet U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen on Nov. 19, with the Ukraine war expected to be discussed [12][25][3].

Key Points

  • Drone interceptions: Russia’s Defense Ministry said it shot down 1,441 Ukrainian drones across 22 regions, exceeding its previous reported daily record of 1,336 [12][8].
  • Kyiv disruption: Russian strikes left crater-like damage on Kyiv’s Northern Bridge. Road traffic on the Southern Bridge partially resumed, and its metro line resumed service by mid-afternoon [15][2].
  • Drone strikes inside Russia: Ukraine said it hit a refinery and petrochemical complex in Salavat. Moscow’s mayor said about 800 drones flew toward the Moscow region, with 321 intercepted en route [3][21].
  • Repair outlook: Structural engineer David MacKenzie told the BBC that restoring full road traffic could take more than a year [9].
  • Weapons used: Zelenskyy said Palianytsia drones struck the road section, while Neptune missiles and Ruta drones were among the weapons aimed at the railway section [38].

Technical Details

  • Load-bearing arch: The steel arch is structural rather than decorative: it transfers loads and supports the bridge span, so its collapse compromises the road crossing [9][6].
  • Scale of collapse: The road arch measured about 227 metres, and daylight images showed roughly 200 metres of roadway had fallen into the strait [9].

Industry Impact

  • Maritime trade: Ambrey said merchant vessels could not use the Kerch-Yenikale Canal after the arch fell into it, putting grain, fuel and fertilizer shipments at risk [32].
  • Transport and logistics: A regional official said deliveries of goods and fuel to Kherson continued steadily and its supply chain had not been disrupted by the bridge attacks [1].

Historical Background

Russia built the Kerch Bridge after its 2014 annexation of Crimea, and President Vladimir Putin opened it in 2018 [11][37]. Ukraine attacked it with a truck bomb in October 2022 and maritime drones in July 2023. Both attacks damaged the crossing, but it remained in service [12][37].

Humanitarian Impact

The Russian-installed governor of Crimea reported two people killed in the attacks. Russian strikes on Kyiv disrupted water and heating in some districts, and damaged crossings forced residents to use detours or walk across damaged infrastructure [12][15][2].

Business Angle

  • A truck bomb damaged the bridge in 2022, limiting its capacity for several months [12].
  • Officials advised motorists seeking to cross the Kerch Strait to use car ferries while bridge traffic was suspended [21].

  • 🇩🇪 Germany: Defense Minister Boris Pistorius rejected criticism of Ukrainian strikes on Russian infrastructure and said attention should instead focus on Russian attacks on Ukraine’s energy, water and heating systems [12].
