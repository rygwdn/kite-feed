Apple Fitness+ launches multiweek programs and Strava challenge

Category: Technology

Apple is starting 2026 with updates to Fitness+ and Apple Watch activity features designed to help people build steadier exercise routines, including new multiweek workout programs for strength, HIIT, and yoga that begin on January 5 [1][2][4][5][6][7][8]. The company is also adding a January Strava challenge that awards an Apple Watch badge for logging 12 workouts during the month, plus a limited-edition Apple Watch award for closing all three Activity Rings for seven consecutive days in January [1][7]. Apple framed the rollout around the common drop-off in New Year’s resolutions (often called “Quitter’s Day”) and said Apple Watch users in its Apple Heart and Movement Study were more likely to keep activity levels up through January and beyond [1][8].

Primary Source: View original article

Many people give up their New Year's fitness resolutions by the second Friday in January, also known as Quitter's Day, but Apple Watch users appear far more likely to stay on track.

— Apple (MacRumors)

Did you know: Apple says the second Friday of January is often nicknamed “Quitter’s Day” because many people abandon New Year’s fitness resolutions around then [1].

Key Points

  • Music spotlights: Fitness+ is bringing back its Artist Spotlight workouts with sessions featuring music from KAROL G, and it plans to add Bad Bunny workouts starting February 5 [1][3].
  • Walking episodes: New “Time to Walk” episodes are scheduled to start January 19 and feature Penn Badgley, Mel B, and Michelle Monaghan [1][3].
  • Workout structure: Apple describes the new “Make Your Fitness Comeback” format as multiweek plans that build week-over-week to help users progress and stay consistent [1][3][4][5].
  • Study details: Apple said its conclusions about sticking with exercise came from a four-year analysis of activity data shared by about 100,000 Apple Heart and Movement Study participants who consistently wore an Apple Watch [1][8].
  • Motivation design: Several outlets highlighted the gamified approach—badges, awards, and challenges—as Apple’s way to make habit-building feel more rewarding and social [1][7][6].

Perspectives

Apple: Apple positions the updates as practical habit-building support and says its Apple Heart and Movement Study analysis suggests Apple Watch wearers are more likely to sustain higher activity through January and into early spring.

Timeline

  • January: Limited-edition award offered for closing all rings seven straight days [1].
  • Throughout January: Strava challenge awards an Apple Watch badge for 12 logged workouts [1].
  • From January 5: “Make Your Fitness Comeback” multiweek programs launch for strength, HIIT, yoga [1].
  • January 19: New “Time to Walk” episodes begin with Penn Badgley, Mel B, Michelle Monaghan [1].
  • From February 5: Artist Spotlight adds new workouts featuring Bad Bunny music [1].

Technical Details

  • Activity Rings: Apple Watch’s Activity Rings track three daily goals—Move, Exercise, and Stand—and Apple is tying a January limited-edition award to closing all three rings for seven consecutive days [1].
  • Multiweek programs: Fitness+ is packaging workouts into multiweek plans where each week builds on the previous one, aiming to make progression and consistency easier to follow [1][3].
  • Strava integration: Apple is using a Strava-hosted challenge to award an Apple Watch badge inside Strava when users log 12 workouts in January, linking Apple’s wearable ecosystem with a popular third-party fitness community [1].

Industry Impact

  • Digital health apps: The Strava challenge shows how major platforms can collaborate across ecosystems, potentially encouraging more partnerships between wearable makers and community fitness apps [1][common].
  • Wearable engagement: By tying limited-time awards and structured programs to Apple Watch metrics, Apple is reinforcing the role of wearables as daily habit trackers rather than occasional workout gadgets [1][4][common].
  • Creator and music tie-ins: Artist Spotlight workouts built around specific musicians highlight how fitness subscriptions can differentiate with entertainment licensing and culturally relevant programming [1][3][common].

Scientific Significance

  • Scientific meaning: Apple’s analysis links wearable-supported routines with improved persistence in exercise after the New Year, using Apple Watch Activity data from participants in the Apple Heart and Movement Study [1][8]. While it’s not presented as a clinical trial, it reflects how large-scale wearable datasets can help researchers observe seasonal behavior changes in real-world settings [1].
  • Uncertainty and confidence: Apple described the analysis as covering about 100,000 participants over four years, but the reporting does not include error margins, statistical methods, or peer-reviewed publication details, which limits how precisely readers can judge the strength of the conclusions [1][8].
  • Limitations and future work: Because the findings come from people who consistently wore an Apple Watch and opted into sharing Activity data, the results may not generalize to all populations; additional studies would need broader sampling or experimental designs to isolate which features (awards, programs, coaching, or social challenges) drive adherence [1][common].

Historical Background

Fitness+ is Apple’s subscription workout service that works closely with Apple Watch metrics and Activity Rings, which Apple uses as a behavioral design tool to encourage daily movement [common]. Apple also runs the Apple Heart and Movement Study with major U.S. health institutions and uses participant-shared wearable data to analyze activity trends over time [1]. The 2026 rollout builds on Apple’s pattern of adding themed programming and challenges around New Year motivation [8].

Action Items

  • Try the multiweek “comeback” plan: In the Fitness app, open Fitness+ and look for the “Make Your Fitness Comeback” program (available from January 5) to follow the week-by-week structure in strength, HIIT, or yoga [1][2].
  • Join the January Strava challenge: In Strava, enroll in the “Quit Quitting” challenge and log 12 workouts at any point in January to earn the Apple Watch badge in the app [1].
  • Earn the January limited award: Use Apple Watch Activity to close Move, Exercise, and Stand rings every day for seven straight days in January to unlock the limited-edition award [1].
