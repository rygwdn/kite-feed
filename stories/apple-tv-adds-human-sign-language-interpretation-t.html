Apple TV adds human sign language interpretation to original films

Category: Technology

Apple will add optional, on-screen sign-language interpretation to selected Apple TV films starting Nov. 13 with Being Heumann. F1: The Movie will offer American Sign Language (ASL) and British Sign Language (BSL); Apple says Greyhound and Way of the Warrior Kid will follow later this year, with more Apple Original titles planned [1][3]. The interpreter appears over the picture as the original film plays, rather than in a separately listed version. Apple says Deaf and native signers helped design and test the performances, which are tailored to each production. The company is not using AI-generated interpreters [3][6]. The feature requires the Apple TV app on supported devices, including Apple devices running version 27.2 or later. Viewers can enable it in settings, then select it during playback. Apple will label eligible titles and provide search and collection tools to help viewers find them [1][15].

Bringing this experience to life required a lot of care to ensure every translation captured the emotion and meaning of the story.

— Daniel Durant, actor in Being Heumann and director of its sign-language interpretation (Cult of Mac)

Key Points

  • Collection details: Apple’s “Movies & Shows with American Sign Language” collection will include titles in which sign language is part of the story, such as CODA [7].
  • Language gap: Apple has not announced support for German Sign Language, according to a German-language report on the rollout [18].
  • Other services: Netflix offers sign-language interpretation for dozens of children’s and family titles through SignUp Media, which is also available for selected Disney+ and Peacock content [7].

Perspectives

Apple: Sarah Herrlinger said accessibility is a core value and that Apple wants more people to enjoy the content they love [1].

Sources: Apple Newsroom

Nakia Smith: The Deaf storyteller and interpreter said ASL is the Deaf community’s first language, making sign-language interpretation an option distinct from captions [7].

Technical Details

  • Screen position: The interpreter appears in the lower-right corner during playback [3][11].
