July Buck Moon precedes August eclipse, Perseids

Category: Science

The July full moon, known as the Buck Moon, will reach peak fullness at 15:36 BST on July 29, BBC said, though the maximum will occur while the Moon is below the UK horizon [4]. The Moon should still appear full to the naked eye overnight Tuesday and Wednesday, and forecast models give much of the UK a good chance of seeing it on the night of July 29 [4]. Greek coverage also identified July 29 as the date of peak brightness and said the best viewing window would be just after sunset, as the Moon rises low in the southeastern sky [9]. The full moon comes before several August skywatching events: a solar eclipse on Wednesday, August 12, will appear as a partial eclipse in parts of the UK and Germany, including Wales, while the Perseid meteor shower will also be active around that date [5][7][8]. In south Wales, the eclipse is expected to begin around 18:15 and end shortly after 20:00 [5]. In Kiel, Germany, the partial eclipse is expected to start at about 19:12, peak at 20:06 with roughly 85% of the Sun covered, and last until sunset at about 20:57 [8]. EarthSky said the Delta Aquariid and Perseid meteor showers are both active in late July, and that the Moon will determine the best viewing opportunities over the next few weeks [6]. Astronomy coverage indexed by Google cited August 12 as a date combining a total solar eclipse and strong Perseid viewing conditions, with other coverage describing 2026 as a favorable year for the Perseids [2][3].

Timeline

  • 25 July 2026: EarthSky said Delta Aquariids and Perseids were already active [6].
  • 29 July 2026: Buck Moon reaches maximum fullness at 15:36 BST [4].
  • 12 August 2026: Partial eclipse visible in Wales during early evening [5].
  • 12 August 2026: Kiel eclipse peaks with about 85% solar coverage [8].

Technical Details

  • Low-altitude eclipse viewing: In Kiel, the eclipse maximum occurs when the remaining solar crescent is only about 6.2 degrees above the horizon, so buildings, trees or low cloud can block the view even from raised sites [8].
  • Eclipse duration: Kiel’s partial eclipse window runs from about 19:12 until sunset at 20:57, giving nearly 105 minutes of possible viewing if the horizon stays clear [8].
  • Radiant-point timing: For Delta Aquariids, EarthSky places the radiant near Skat in Aquarius; it is highest around 2 a.m. and low by dawn, which affects where observers should look during late-night sessions [6].

Action Items

  • Scout an eclipse site: Before August 12, test whether your chosen spot has a clear west-to-west-northwest horizon, especially in cities or tree-lined areas [8].
  • Avoid unfiltered solar photos: Use a proper solar filter on cameras or phones when photographing the Sun, and do not rely on ordinary sunglasses or screens for eye protection [common].
