Moonshot AI releases 2.8 trillion-parameter Kimi K3 weights

Category: AI

Beijing-based Moonshot AI released the full weights for Kimi K3 on July 27 after introducing Kimi 3 in mid-July. Fast Company described the roughly 2.8 trillion-parameter open-weight model as likely the largest of its kind ever released [5][2]. The model can process text, images and audio and supports a 1-million-token context window [5]. Moonshot published a technical report for Kimi K3 on arXiv on July 27, listing the Kimi Team as authors under the title "Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence" [4]. On Hugging Face, CEO Clem Delangue said Kimi K3 reached No. 1 on the platform’s trending chart within 30 minutes and drew more than 4,000 likes, the fastest growth the site had recorded [5]. By the next day, the repository had about 7,700 likes and tens of thousands of downloads [5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • License limits: Kimi K3’s license requires large Model-as-a-Service businesses with more than $20 million in aggregate revenue over any consecutive 12 months to sign a separate agreement with Moonshot before commercial use [2].
  • Hosting options: OpenRouter listed Kimi K3 from seven providers; most priced it at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, matching Moonshot AI’s own rates [2].
  • Release size: The Kimi K3 weights occupy 1.56TB on Hugging Face, creating a storage burden before developers can run the model [2].
  • Engineering stack: Moonshot released MoonEP, FlashKDA and AgentEnv alongside the model, making infrastructure and training tools part of the Kimi K3 rollout [3].

Perspectives

Moonshot AI: Moonshot described Kimi K3 as an open-weight release and reported strong results across coding, agentic, reasoning and knowledge benchmarks. It said the model ranked behind two leading closed models in overall evaluations.

Sources: Fast Company

Simon Willison: Willison said Moonshot avoided calling the Kimi K3 license open source, unlike its earlier Kimi K2 license language, and instead consistently used the term open weight.

Technical Details

  • Sparse MoE activation: Smol.ai described Kimi K3 as a 2.8 trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with roughly 104 billion active parameters per token, meaning inference activates only a subset of the total model for each token [3].
  • Hybrid long-context attention: The architecture discussion cited Kimi Delta Attention, Gated MLA, NoPE, AttnRes over depth and a sparse LatentMoE as components used to scale Kimi K3 across context length, depth and width [3].
  • Post-training recipe: Smol.ai summarized the report as using multiple specialist reinforcement-learning teachers followed by multi-teacher on-policy distillation, a training pattern aimed at fusing specialist behaviors into one deployed model [3].

Industry Impact

  • Model hosting: OpenRouter’s early listing of multiple Kimi K3 providers shows third-party inference platforms moved quickly to package the model for paid access rather than leaving deployment to individual developers [2].
  • Open-source debate: Fast Company reported that Kimi K3 sharpened debate over whether the most capable models should remain closely held by individual companies or be distributed through more transparent open-weight releases [5].

Historical Background

Moonshot’s prior Kimi K2 license, released in July 2025, used a modified MIT-style approach requiring prominent attribution for commercial products with more than 100 million monthly active users or more than $20 million in monthly revenue [2]. Kimi K3 changed that approach by dropping the modified-MIT label and adding a separate-agreement requirement for large Model-as-a-Service operators [2].

Business Angle

  • Open-weight releases can affect pricing for commodity inference, while hosting, uptime and enterprise tooling remain paid-provider services [2][common].
  • Moonshot’s Model-as-a-Service license threshold preserves a path to commercial revenue from companies most likely to monetize Kimi K3 at scale [2].
  • Infrastructure requirements may limit who can run Kimi K3 efficiently, giving specialized inference platforms an opening despite open-weight availability [2][3].

Action Items

  • Review the Kimi K3 license terms before commercial deployment: Check whether your organization operates a Model-as-a-Service business and crosses the $20 million 12-month revenue threshold that triggers a separate Moonshot agreement [2].
  • Estimate infrastructure needs before self-hosting: Budget for downloading, storing and serving the model weights, then compare that cost with hosted OpenRouter providers priced around $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens [2].
