Quebec voters head to the polls with separatist Parti Québécois leading

Category: Canada

Quebecers vote Monday, Oct. 5, in a provincial election with the separatist Parti Québécois, led by Paul St-Pierre Plamondon, ahead of a crowded field, according to pre-election polls [27][24][1]. The result could end eight years of Coalition Avenir Québec government; the PQ won only three seats in 2022 [36][24]. St-Pierre Plamondon has promised a sovereignty referendum during a PQ government’s first term, but said he would wait until U.S. President Donald Trump leaves office in 2029 before holding one [36][16]. CAQ Leader Christine Fréchette and Liberal Leader Charles Milliard urged voters to weigh the economic uncertainty surrounding a referendum and Quebec’s place in Canada, while Conservative Leader Éric Duhaime said he would try to block a referendum if his party gained leverage [22][24]. The five main parties made their final appeals Sunday, ahead of Monday’s vote [22][5].

Key Points

  • PQ priorities: For its first 100 days, the PQ has proposed a special homelessness budget, gas tax cuts, the elimination of taxes on used goods, and funding for community groups and food banks [22][5].
  • Five-party race: The campaign featured five parties with a chance to win seats in the legislature, a level of competition Global News said had not occurred in 50 years [24].
  • Conservative target: Duhaime said he hoped the Conservatives would win at least 12 seats and gain official party status, after winning no seats in 2022 [5][7].
  • CAQ concerns: Fréchette acknowledged her campaign had sometimes reacted defensively during leaders’ debates [36].

Perspectives

Parti Québécois: St-Pierre Plamondon said a PQ government would have a “moral obligation” to serve all Quebecers [24][16].

Historical Background

The Parti Québécois was founded in 1968, and Quebec held sovereignty referendums in 1980 and 1995 [16][9]. A 2007 report led by Judge Bernard Grenier detailed about $500,000 in irregularities by the No side in 1995; associated documents remain sealed under confidentiality agreements and because they contain sensitive information [16]. St-Pierre Plamondon has said a PQ government would seek a way to disclose the records while protecting personal information and sources [16].
