Qatar presses Iran to reopen Strait of Hormuz

Category: World

Qatar’s Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani visited Tehran on Thursday to pursue mediation with Iran as talks with the United States remained stalled and the Strait of Hormuz remained at the center of the conflict [1][5][12][32]. He met Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and other senior officials to discuss de-escalation, conditions for renewed dialogue and a proposed interim framework for the waterway [21][20][42]. Qatar’s Foreign Ministry said the Hormuz proposal includes a temporary Iranian-Omani shipping corridor and a joint project to clear mines from the strait, while Sheikh Mohammed called for respect for neighbouring states’ sovereignty and freedom of navigation [5][14][32][23]. Iran’s Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei said mediators had asked Tehran to list its terms for reopening the strait, adding that ending the regional war was among the conditions and that Washington must take “practical steps” before normal passage resumes [6][31][17][20]. Washington said no negotiations were underway with Tehran, with White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt saying the US economic campaign would continue until Iran negotiated “in a meaningful way” [5][54][52]. President Trump told reporters the United States was not seeking talks with Iran, while US officials continued to emphasise sanctions and economic pressure [26][31][11].

Key Points

  • Mediator circuit: Qatar’s visit followed recent mediation activity involving Oman and Pakistan, with Al Jazeera describing Qatar as the third mediating state to visit Iran in a week [6][30][34].
  • Maritime incident: The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that an unidentified projectile hit a tanker in the strait, causing a fire that was extinguished while all crew were safe [16][43][12].
  • June framework: Qatar and Pakistan helped broker a June memorandum of understanding that produced a temporary ceasefire and set shipping terms, but it later broke down partly over disputes involving Hormuz [19][36][34].

Industry Impact

  • Energy shipping: Hormuz carried about one-fifth of global oil supplies before the war, making the drop in transits a direct constraint on Gulf energy exports [19][29][34].
  • Sanctions compliance: The Trump administration sanctioned nearly 60 entities, individuals and vessels and expanded restrictions linked to Iran’s defence sector, according to Hürriyet Daily News [52].
  • Iran fuel market: Reporting from Tehran described a US naval blockade squeezing oil imports and contributing to domestic petrol shortages, with residents queuing at gas stations [35][49].

Historical Background

Qatar hosts the US military’s Al Udeid Air Base and shares the world’s largest gas field with Iran [1]. Tehran has lived under various forms of US sanctions since Iran’s 1979 Islamic revolution, and Washington has launched a new pressure campaign [54][common].

Business Angle
