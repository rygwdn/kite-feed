Ontario school support workers vote 94% for strike mandate across four board systems

Category: Canada

Ontario school support workers represented by the Canadian Union of Public Employees (CUPE) have voted in favour of a strike mandate, making their union the last of Ontario’s five major education unions to approve one [2][1]. The Ontario School Board Council of Unions (OSBCU) reported 94.2% support, with 77.5% of members participating; its members work in public, Catholic, English- and French-language school boards across the province [6][2]. Education workers began the 2026-27 school year without new agreements after their previous contracts expired Aug. 31 [6][5]. OSBCU says it is seeking increased staffing and funding, safer schools and better working conditions in contract negotiations. Education Minister Paul Calandra said he was confident a strike could be avoided, and the union said its first bargaining day is Nov. 30 [6][2].

Q&A

Q: What steps would have to happen before a strike?

A: The parties must enter conciliation; if it fails, a no-board report can be issued. The union can give strike notice 16 days after that report, and must provide at least five days' written notice before a walkout [5].
