MeerKAT detects radio bursts from exoplanet Beta Pictoris b

Category: Science

Astronomers using South Africa’s MeerKAT radio telescope detected radio bursts traced directly to Beta Pictoris b, a gas giant about 63 light-years from Earth. Earlier radio signals from exoplanet systems could not be confidently separated from emissions from their host stars; the team used precise position measurements to identify the planet as the source [4][5][8]. The bursts are attributed to auroras generated by the planet’s magnetic field, not an artificial signal. The study reports a direct measurement of the field, but the findings are in a preprint that has not yet undergone peer review [6][8].

Primary Source: View original article

There have been tentative, indirect detections before, but this is the first truly convincing direct detection, and it hopefully paves the way for many more.

— Suzanne Aigrain (Live Science)

Key Points

  • Magnetic strength: The researchers estimated a magnetic field of at least 1,250 gauss, compared with roughly 0.5 gauss for Earth [8].
  • Observing bands: MeerKAT observed frequencies from 0.85 to 3.5 gigahertz during four sessions across 2025 and 2026 [6][8].
  • Host star: The star’s magnetically quiet state helped researchers isolate the planet’s signal [10].

Timeline

  • 2008: Beta Pictoris b was discovered, according to Deutsche Welle [11].
  • 2025-2026: MeerKAT observations were conducted [8][6].
  • 23 September 2026: News coverage of the findings appeared [4][8].

Historical Background

The Beta Pictoris system is about 20 million years old and is surrounded by a massive dust disk [12].

Q&A

Q: How massive is Beta Pictoris b?

A: Reports describe the gas giant as roughly 10 to 12 times Jupiter’s mass [4].
