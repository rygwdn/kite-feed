Sudanese rights group says suspected Egyptian strike killed 72 miners

Category: World

A Sudanese rights group said airstrikes hit an artisanal gold-mining site near the disputed Sudan-Egypt border on Saturday, killing at least 72 people. It accused Egypt’s air force, but the allegation and death toll have not been independently verified [3][10][5]. Egypt has not commented on the reported attack. The remote site lies in a border region claimed by both countries, and survivors described people fleeing after repeated strikes [4][5]. The attack was the second reported strike on the Jabal al-Uqaydat mining area this year. A similar attack in June reportedly killed 23 workers; Egypt’s military said at the time that it had conducted a large-scale operation against illegal mining within Egyptian territory [10][5]. Artisanal gold mining has expanded during Sudan’s war, which began in April 2023 and has killed more than 200,000 people, according to reports cited by the rights group and news organizations [5][10]. Gold also finances armed groups. Chatham House estimates that as much as 60% of Sudan’s unofficial gold exports go to Egypt [4]. Sudan Doctors Network warned that the casualty toll could rise because seriously injured people struggle to reach medical care [4].

Key Points

  • June arrests: After the June operation, Egypt reported detaining 223 people, including 136 foreign nationals, in a campaign against illegal mining and arms smuggling [5][10].
  • Egypt’s security concerns: Despite backing Sudan’s army chief, Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, Egypt considers the war along its 1,200-kilometer border a major threat to its security [5].
  • Casualty estimates: Sudan Doctors Network initially reported 13 deaths, while Darfur Victims Advocacy later reported 200 dead and about 120 injured; those higher figures were not independently confirmed [23][25][33].
  • Survivor account: Miner Sharaf Ali said thousands of people were at the site and scattered in different directions after the strike, complicating efforts to count casualties [5].

Perspectives

Emergency Lawyers: The Sudanese rights organization accused Egypt’s air force of striking civilian miners inside Sudan and described the attack as a violation of Sudanese sovereignty and the right to life [10][25].

Humanitarian Impact

Rights groups said more than 50 people were injured, Al Jazeera reported [8]. Both survivors saw bodies as they fled, Dawn reported, and more people were feared trapped beneath rubble at a site hours from the nearest urban centre [5].
