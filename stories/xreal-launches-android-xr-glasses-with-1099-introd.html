Xreal launches Android XR glasses with $1,099 introductory price

Category: Technology

Xreal, a smart-glasses maker, opened orders on Oct. 28 for Aura, the first wired glasses running Google’s Android XR platform, at introductory prices of $1,099 and $1,299 for two memory and storage configurations. Orders are open in the United States, Canada, Japan and South Korea; shipments are expected to start in mid-November, and the discounts run through December 2026 [11][44][5]. Aura’s transparent lenses let wearers see their surroundings while viewing digital content. The glasses weigh less than 95 grams, with the battery and Qualcomm’s Snapdragon Reality Elite computing platform housed in a separate, tethered puck. Sony Micro-OLED displays provide 1200p resolution at up to 120Hz and a 70-degree field of view; USB-C mirroring lets users view content from a compatible laptop or handheld device on a virtual screen [11][12][20]. Android XR brings Google Play apps and more than 150 XR experiences to the glasses, while Gemini can respond to what users see on-screen and around them [37][21]. Xreal is a hardware partner for the platform, which Google also supplies to Samsung; Google Photos is scheduled to add a spatial gallery for Aura users later in 2026 [11][27].

Key Points

  • Launch content: The XR catalog includes an NBA app with up to three simultaneous games for League Pass subscribers, a NASCAR app with a live 3D track map, and the game Fallout: Factions [52][47].
  • Spatial controls: Aura supports six-degrees-of-freedom tracking and hand tracking, and users can arrange as many as five apps at once [20][9].
  • Accessories: Xreal and GameSir announced a controller with a dedicated Gemini button, alongside a wireless portable keyboard for multi-window work [20][5].
  • Private display: Google says a mirrored laptop screen is visible only to the wearer, a feature it positions for viewing confidential work in public [44][37].

Technical Details

  • Tracking and latency: Aura uses two cameras for inside-out six-degrees-of-freedom tracking; its reported motion-to-photon latency is 3 milliseconds, a measure of the delay between movement and the displayed response [20][43].
  • Optical specification: The Sony Micro-OLED displays are listed at 1,920-by-1,200 pixels per eye, 32 pixels per degree and up to 1,050 nits; Xreal says the panels receive factory color calibration to 100% sRGB with ΔE below 3 [20][43].
  • Camera indicator: A separate high-resolution AI camera supports spatial features, and a privacy LED indicates when the camera is active [43].

Historical Background

Google’s Android XR platform first reached consumers on Samsung’s Galaxy XR headset. Aura brings the software to glasses [7][51].

Q&A

A: Xreal supports prescription lenses, and the frame comes in two sizes for different interpupillary-distance ranges [40][20].
