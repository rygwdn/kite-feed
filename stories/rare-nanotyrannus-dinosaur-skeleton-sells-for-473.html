Rare Nanotyrannus dinosaur skeleton sells for $47.3 million in New York

Category: Science

A rare Nanotyrannus skeleton known as Jodi sold for $47.3 million at Sotheby’s in New York on Tuesday, far above its estimate of $6 million to $9 million. It became the second-most expensive dinosaur fossil sold at auction [1][3][6]. Discovered in Montana in 2019, Jodi is one of only three substantial Nanotyrannus skeletons known and belonged to an adult animal that lived about 67 million years ago [1][3]. Thomas R. Holtz Jr., a vertebrate paleontology lecturer at the University of Maryland, warned that privately owned fossils can become inaccessible to researchers and the public [1][3]. Researchers confirmed last year that Nanotyrannus was distinct from Tyrannosaurus rex, rather than a juvenile T. rex, ending a decades-long debate [3][4]. The winning bidder has not been publicly identified [1][3].

So a person really interested in paleontology could do vastly more good by supporting the ongoing operations of multiple museums than the sale of one specimen.

— Thomas R. Holtz Jr. (The Guardian)

Key Points

  • Skeletal evidence: Sotheby’s says Nanotyrannus has more than 150 morphological differences from T. rex; paleontologist Megan Jacobs described it as more slender, with blade-like teeth [1][3].
  • Preparation costs: Holtz estimated that excavating, preparing and mounting a skeleton like Jodi would cost no more than about $1 million [1][7].
  • Earlier display: Jodi was presented as a juvenile T. rex at London’s Frieze Masters art fair in October 2023, before Nanotyrannus was classified as a distinct genus [3][4].

Perspectives

Thomas R. Holtz Jr., vertebrate paleontology lecturer at the University of Maryland: Holtz warned that fossils held in private collections can become inaccessible to researchers and the public.

Sources: The Guardian

Megan Jacobs, paleontologist at the University of Portsmouth: Jacobs said she did not begrudge those selling Jodi, citing the time, effort and money needed to excavate specimens. She also said many such fossils eventually go on public display.

Sources: BBC News

Industry Impact

  • Natural history museums: Holtz contrasted Jodi’s $47.3 million sale with U.S. natural-history museums closing or facing closure over cost overruns he said were about one-tenth of the sale price [1][8].

Historical Background

A fossil showing a Nanotyrannus and a Triceratops was found in Montana in 2006, eight miles from where Jodi was discovered. Known as “Dueling Dinosaurs,” the specimen was central to Lindsay Zanno and James Napoli’s classification research [3][4].
