Trump threatens Iran, Houthis after Saudi tanker strikes

Category: World

President Trump threatened “major military punishment” against Iran and Yemen’s Houthis if the Houthis attack ships again, after the group said it struck two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea with missiles and drones to enforce a declared blockade on Saudi Arabia [5][25][40][60]. Saudi authorities confirmed a strike on the Encelia, which caught fire, while the status of the Layla was less clear in the available accounts [8][60][43]. The Red Sea attacks added pressure to oil markets while fighting between the United States and Iran continued around the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude rose above $100 a barrel, with reports putting the increase at more than 6% or more than 8% [3][5][19][24]. Trump said the United States would hold Iran responsible because the Houthis are, in his words, a “Surrogate and/or Proxy of Iran,” and he separately told Axios he was considering a “massive attack” on Iran but had not made a final decision [13][11][22][69]. U.S. forces launched a 13th consecutive night of strikes on Iranian military targets on July 24, DW reported; earlier accounts described a 12th night of attacks across Iran [6][5][13]. The UK temporarily withdrew remaining diplomatic staff from Iran, and its Foreign Office advised against all travel to the country, citing security conditions and the risk of arrest, questioning or detention for British and dual British-Iranian nationals [18].

Key Points

  • Shipping chokepoint: The Bab al-Mandab, next to Houthi-controlled territory, carries about 12% of world trade and serves as a gateway to the Red Sea [4][66].
  • Saudi route: The Houthis said Saudi Arabia had diverted millions of barrels of oil a day by pipeline to the Red Sea to bypass Iran’s blockade of Gulf oil through Hormuz [20][31][24].
  • U.S. posture: Axios reported that two U.S. officials said Trump had issued no new military orders, despite his statement that he was near a decision on a larger Iran operation [22][69][42].
  • Regional strikes: Iran fired at neighboring Arab countries hosting U.S. bases after another U.S. round of airstrikes on Iran [19][24][31].
  • Diplomatic link: Trump said a U.S.-Saudi civilian nuclear deal would be “totally subject” to Riyadh joining the Abraham Accords recognizing Israel [27][6].

Perspectives

A Houthi military spokesman said the attack used ballistic missiles, cruise missiles and drones, and claimed the strikes hit both tankers and caused a large fire on board. He added that the group forced about 10 ships to retreat and vowed to continue naval operations under what he called an “equation of blockade for blockade.”

Industry Impact

  • Gulf exports: The Strait of Hormuz handled about one-fifth of the world’s oil and gas in peacetime, while Saudi Red Sea routing had become an alternative during the Hormuz blockade [5][20].

Historical Background

The Houthis attacked commercial shipping during the Gaza war, targeting vessels they linked to Israel, and the United States struck Houthi targets in Yemen in response to attacks on shipping in 2025 [58][73]. The Abraham Accords were brokered during Trump’s first term and established diplomatic, economic and security ties between Israel and the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Morocco [27].

Humanitarian Impact

The cited reports did not provide confirmed casualty figures from the tanker attacks; Saudi-linked reporting cited by The Guardian said all Encelia crew members were safe after the fire [60].
