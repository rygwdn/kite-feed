Trump renews bid to remove Fed governor Lisa Cook

Category: Business

President Trump has renewed his effort to remove Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook. White House deputy chief of staff Dan Scavino sent an Aug. 5 letter saying Trump was “considering” removing Cook and giving her 21 days, until Aug. 26, to respond to mortgage-fraud allegations she denies [12][17][10][15]. The letter cites allegations that Cook made false statements on mortgage applications and says the alleged conduct could call into question her competence and trustworthiness as a financial regulator [3][9][51]. The renewed effort follows a June Supreme Court decision that let Cook remain in her post while her lawsuit challenging Trump’s earlier attempt proceeds; the court ruled 5-4 that she had not received the notice and opportunity to respond required by law [1][12][17]. Chief Justice John Roberts wrote that nothing barred Trump from “trying again” if Cook received proper notice and a chance to contest the move, and the Trump administration is appealing a lower-court ruling in Cook’s favor [1][17]. The allegations began with an August 2025 criminal referral by Federal Housing Finance Agency Director Bill Pulte, who accused Cook of listing homes in Ann Arbor, Michigan, and Atlanta as “primary residence” properties, a designation that can qualify borrowers for lower rates or smaller down payments than second homes [1][17]. Cook’s lawyer, Abbe Lowell, called the allegations baseless and said her legal team would challenge the latest effort [3][10][22].

Key Points

  • Legal exposure: Scavino’s letter alleged conduct that could be punishable by up to 30 years in prison, while CNN reported that Cook has not been charged with a crime [10][37][36].
  • Fed tenure: Cook joined the Fed Board in 2022 after President Joe Biden nominated her, and several sources report her term runs until 2038 [32][38][51].
  • Rate pressure: President Trump has pressed the Fed for lower interest rates, and several reports link the personnel fight to his effort to install officials more aligned with that preference [6][37][45][51].
  • Fed response: The Federal Reserve declined to comment on the White House letter in reports citing Reuters, AFP and other accounts [43][48][56].

Perspectives

White House: Dan Scavino wrote that even if Cook’s alleged conduct did not rise to a felony, it appeared to show “gross negligence in financial transactions” and raised questions about her competence and trustworthiness as a financial regulator.

Historical Background

Congress created the Federal Reserve in 1913, and the central bank’s governors serve staggered long terms meant to insulate monetary policy from short-term political pressure [common]. Several reports say Trump’s 2025 effort to remove Cook was the first presidential attempt to dismiss a Fed governor in the institution’s 111-year history [15][30][59]. Cook is also reported to be the first Black woman to serve on the Fed Board of Governors [37][29][48].

Business Angle

  • A successful removal could make investors demand a higher risk premium on U.S. rates if they view Fed decisions as politically constrained [common].
  • Banks and rate-sensitive lenders could benefit from lower policy rates, but only if inflation expectations remain anchored and credit quality does not deteriorate [common].
  • Companies with heavy refinancing needs would gain from lower borrowing costs, while long-duration bondholders could lose if policy credibility weakens and yields rise [common].

Action Items

  • Check rate exposure: Review bond duration, floating-rate debt and refinancing dates because shifts in perceived Fed independence can affect yields and borrowing costs [common].
  • Monitor the Aug. 26 deadline: Watch for Cook’s written response or a new White House action after the deadline set in Scavino’s letter [12][51].
