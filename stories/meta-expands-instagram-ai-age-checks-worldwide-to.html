Meta expands Instagram AI age checks worldwide to identify teen accounts

Category: Technology

Meta is expanding its AI age-estimation system to Instagram users worldwide to identify teenagers who entered birth dates indicating they were adults and automatically apply Teen Account protections [1][31]. Those settings limit who can message teens, restrict some content and manage time on the app. Meta says the global expansion will extend the safeguards to millions more users. Its Facebook rollout is continuing, and a similar global launch on Threads is planned before the end of 2026 [1][4]. The system examines profile text and activity for clues such as birthday posts and school-grade references, and analyzes visual cues in photos and videos [1][25]. Meta says it is strengthening separate technology to find accounts belonging to children under 13, who are not allowed on its platforms [1]. In Australia, it has added Apple’s age-range signal as another input when a family chooses to share it [17][6]. The rollout follows Meta’s August agreement with 52 bipartisan state and territorial attorneys general to improve how it identifies teens and children under 13. The settlement could cost the company up to $18 billion [1][23]. Meta also faces other youth-safety lawsuits, including a New Mexico case in which a jury found the company liable for misleading consumers about risks to children [2].

Key Points

  • Account safeguards: Teen Accounts are private by default, and users under 16 need parental approval to loosen some settings [4][16].
  • System limits: Meta has not published accuracy or error-rate figures for the age-estimation system [4].
  • Apple signal: Apple can share a teen’s age bracket with app developers when a parent enables the signal during device setup [17][7].
  • Appeal to competitors: Meta urged TikTok and YouTube to adopt similar protections, saying teenagers encounter different safeguards across social apps [15][9].
  • Settlement terms: Meta’s agreement gives it one year to establish an age-assurance framework that separates adults, teens and children under 13, with accuracy thresholds and a way to appeal misclassification, according to legal analyses [4].

Perspectives

Meta: The company says relying on app stores to verify age and obtain parental consent could reduce repeated identity checks by individual platforms; it argues that a shared age signal could be more privacy-preserving [17].

Francis Fong, honorary president of the Hong Kong Information Technology Chamber: Fong said the measures could close a common loophole but would not stop every attempt to evade detection, and proposed legally requiring default protections and an appeals process in Hong Kong [33].

Timeline

  • September 2024: Meta introduced Teen Accounts on Instagram with built-in safety settings [15].
  • April 2025: Meta announced AI age estimation to detect teen accounts [4].
  • May 2026: Meta expanded the system to Brazil, EU countries and Facebook in the U.S. [4].
  • August 2026: Meta agreed to settlements with 52 attorneys general over youth-safety claims [1].
  • 9 November 2026: Meta announced the worldwide expansion of Instagram’s age-estimation system [1].
  • Later in 2026: Meta plans to begin a global rollout on Threads later in 2026 [1].

Historical Background

Meta launched Teen Accounts on Instagram, then extended them to Facebook and Messenger [4]. Meta first used the age-estimation system in Canada, Australia, the U.K. and the U.S. before expanding it elsewhere [4].
