Trump says US will end $3.8 billion in annual military aid to Israel

Category: World

President Donald Trump said on Nov. 3 that the United States would stop providing Israel with about $3.8 billion in annual military assistance after this year but would continue selling it weapons. Trump said Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had agreed to the change months ago and argued that Israel no longer needed the money. He also said the aid had become a political target for Democrats during midterm-election campaigning [3][4][11]. The timing and mechanism of the cutoff remain unclear. The existing 10-year U.S.-Israel memorandum of understanding provides $3.8 billion a year through September 2028, and Congress must appropriate funds annually. Trump did not explain how his proposed year-end cutoff would address those commitments. Netanyahu’s earlier public comments described phasing out aid over a longer period [12][3][16]. Israeli opposition figures said their country’s defense establishment had received no advance notice, while Trump said Israel could still buy U.S. arms and receive help if needed. Neither the White House nor Netanyahu’s office had publicly detailed an implementation plan, according to the reports [4][5][14].

Key Points

  • Poll shift: A June Quinnipiac poll found that 48% of U.S. voters and 66% of Democrats said the United States was too supportive of Israel, compared with 16% and 20%, respectively, in 2017 [3][5].
  • Implementation question: It was unclear whether the president could unilaterally halt funding already appropriated by Congress; any attempt to cancel those appropriations could face restrictions [12].
  • Defense cooperation: A provision in the House-passed 2027 defense bill would deepen U.S.-Israel cooperation on weapons and technology, while dozens of nongovernmental organizations urged lawmakers to drop it [14][3].

Perspectives

Gadi Eisenkot, Israeli opposition leader: Eisenkot said Netanyahu could no longer be trusted and called for him to be replaced [4].

Timeline

  • 2016: Obama administration signed a 10-year, $38 billion security assistance memorandum [1][3]
  • 2019: The current 10-year U.S.-Israel assistance arrangement began [5][19]
  • 7 October 2023: Hamas-led attack in Israel preceded the Gaza war and intensified aid debate [5][20]
  • 27 October 2026: Israeli election left Netanyahu without a governing majority [3][14]
  • 3 November 2026: Trump announced his proposed year-end cutoff during U.S. midterm voting [3][16]
  • September 2028: Existing memorandum is scheduled to expire [3]

Technical Details

  • Foreign military financing: Most of the annual assistance is foreign military financing that Israel uses to buy U.S.-made weapons; under the existing arrangement, purchases of Israeli-made equipment were being phased out [17][1].
  • Joint missile-defense funding: The agreement earmarks about $500 million annually for joint air-defense programs, including Iron Dome and David’s Sling, separate from the roughly $3.3 billion for weapons purchases [12].

Historical Background

U.S. aid arrangements predate the current memorandum: an earlier decade-long program ran during the Clinton administration [6]. Israel has received more than $300 billion in inflation-adjusted U.S. economic and military assistance since its founding in 1948 [19].
