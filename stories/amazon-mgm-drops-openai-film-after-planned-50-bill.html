Amazon MGM drops OpenAI film after planned $50 billion investment

Category: AI

Amazon MGM Studios has dropped “Artificial,” a film about OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and the company’s 2023 leadership crisis, and will let the filmmaking team seek another studio to release it [2][3][5][8]. The project is directed by Luca Guadagnino and stars Andrew Garfield as Altman, with an early 2027 release previously planned [3][8]. Amazon’s decision followed its announcement earlier this year of plans to invest $50 billion in OpenAI [2][3][5]. An Amazon spokesperson said the film would be better served at another studio.

Primary Source: View original article

Will be better served if it were released by a different studio

— Amazon spokesperson (PCMag)

Key Points

  • Story focus: The film centers on Altman’s brief firing and rehiring at OpenAI in 2023, after the board cited concerns that he had not been “consistently candid in his communications with the board” [3][8].
  • Employee pressure: A petition to reinstate Altman later drew 500 signatures from OpenAI staff, according to PCMag [3].

Perspectives

Amazon MGM Studios: The studio said it respected Guadagnino and was working with the production team to find the film a new home rather than release it through Amazon MGM.

Sources: PCMag

Amazon management: Puck reported that Prime Video and Amazon MGM Studios head Mike Hopkins made the decision after viewing a cut that differed in tone from the original script.

Sources: PCMag

Nederlands Dagblad: ND described the move as an uncomfortable decision because the nearly finished film promised an unflattering portrayal of Altman while Amazon maintained close business ties with OpenAI.

Sources: Nederlands Dagblad

Timeline

  • 2023: OpenAI’s board fired and later rehired Altman [3][8].
  • 2026: Amazon announced a planned $50 billion OpenAI investment [3].
  • 19 June 2026: Amazon said another studio should release “Artificial” [2][3].
  • 2027: The film was previously pegged for an early 2027 release [3].

Industry Impact

  • Film distribution: The nearly completed film will be offered to other studios, shifting its release prospects away from the originally planned early 2027 window. [2][3][8][4]
  • Streaming studios: The decision involves Prime Video and Amazon MGM Studios chief Mike Hopkins, putting the matter under Amazon’s combined streaming and studio leadership [3].
  • AI partnerships: Amazon’s planned $50 billion investment in OpenAI ties the studio decision to one of Amazon’s largest stated AI commitments this year [2][3][5].

Business Angle

  • The OpenAI investment dwarfs any single film project, so investors should focus on whether the AI partnership strengthens Amazon’s long-term competitive position [2][3][common].
  • Amazon’s ownership of both a studio and large AI investments can create conflicts between creative output and strategic relationships [2][7].
  • Letting the film team seek another studio for the release may limit direct reputational risk while preserving some value in a nearly completed film asset [2][4][8].
