Pangram study flags 41% of LinkedIn long-form posts

Category: AI

Pangram, a company that makes an AI text detector, released an analysis saying 41% of long-form LinkedIn posts it examined were flagged as fully AI-generated, along with 30% of short-form LinkedIn content [1][4][6]. Tweakers described the sample as posts encountered by users of Pangram’s browser extension, and Gizmodo said Pangram’s role as a detector vendor is a reason to scrutinize the findings [1][4]. The analysis placed LinkedIn ahead of several other named platforms for fully AI-generated long-form content: Pangram reported 31% for Medium, 29% for long-form X posts, 13% for Reddit long-form writing and 10% for Substack long-form content [1]. The same report said 9% of regular X posts and 3% of short-form Reddit posts were fully AI-generated [1]. A separate Atlantic article examined one stylistic pattern often treated as an AI tell: sentences that contrast what something is not with what it is, such as “it’s not X; it’s Y” [5]. The article noted that the structure also appears in Shakespeare’s Julius Caesar and in modern corporate and political writing, complicating attempts to infer AI authorship from style alone [5].

Perspectives

The Atlantic: The magazine examined negative parallelism — the “it’s not X, it’s Y” construction — as a suspected AI-writing tell, but noted its extensive use in Julius Caesar and contemporary business language, complicating efforts to treat it as a reliable marker of chatbot writing [5].

Sources: The Atlantic

Business Angle

  • Growth in AI-generated content could expand the market for detection tools, but enterprise buyers will need independent audits of detection claims before adding such tools to compliance workflows [1][common].
  • LinkedIn’s content strategy could face pressure if users begin to distrust the authenticity of posts, including pressure to introduce AI-content labeling similar to other social media sites [1].
  • Companies also face reputational risk from false accusations that employees or competitors used AI based on stylistic quirks, which can lead to legal and PR challenges [5].

Action Items

  • Review detector methodology: Treat AI-detector scores as one signal, and check sampling, false-positive handling and whether the tool distinguishes fully generated text from hybrid text before acting on a result [1][4].
  • Avoid style-only accusations: Do not accuse an author based only on phrases such as “it’s not X; it’s Y,” because The Atlantic identified the construction in pre-AI writing as well as recent digital text [5].
