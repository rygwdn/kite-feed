Rubio seeks Gulf backing for Iran accord

Category: World

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio traveled through the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait and Bahrain to reassure Gulf Arab partners about President Trump’s preliminary agreement with Iran, the first visit by a senior U.S. official to the region since the memorandum of understanding was signed last week [1][16][31]. Rubio met UAE President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan in Abu Dhabi, held talks with Kuwaiti leaders in Kuwait City and arrived in Bahrain for meetings with officials and the Gulf Cooperation Council [1][2][18]. The accord aims to end a four-month U.S.-Israeli war with Iran and includes a proposed $300 billion fund and waivers of some sanctions for Tehran, terms that have drawn concern from Gulf governments hit by Iranian missiles and drones during the conflict [14][16][19]. Rubio said in Kuwait that Washington would coordinate closely with Gulf partners and would not jeopardize their security as negotiations continue [14][26][12]. Rubio also said no country would be allowed to impose tolls or fees on the Strait of Hormuz, after Oman and Iran said they were considering “costs” for ships using the waterway [1][21][23]. He told RFE/RL that U.S.-Iran technical negotiations would resume in Switzerland around June 29 or June 30 [5][59][13].

Key Points

  • Unresolved files: Gulf concerns include Iran’s missile programme and proxy network, which accounts of Rubio’s agenda said the memorandum does not resolve [1][48][26].
  • Iran's targeting claim: Iran contends that it targeted U.S. bases and assets in Gulf countries during the conflict and has not accepted responsibility for all attacks [1].
  • Congressional move: The U.S. Senate on Wednesday rejected a resolution that sought to rein in President Trump's handling of the conflict [36].
  • Presidential warning: President Trump told reporters that negotiations were 'doing great' and warned Iran that it would face action if it failed to honor the agreement [7][26].
  • Lebanon issue: Israel’s Defence Minister Israel Katz said Israel would not withdraw from southern Lebanon, even under U.S. pressure [59][6].

Technical Details

  • Expert-level working groups: Rubio said the next technical phase would use staff-level groups involving State Department and Energy Department experts on nuclear energy, sanctions and other implementation subjects [13][5].
  • Sanctions snapback window: The National reported Rubio warned sanctions relief for Iran could be reversed within 60 days if Tehran fails to honor commitments made in Switzerland [26].

Industry Impact

  • Oil and LNG shipping: Hormuz disruption affected a route used for about one-fifth of global oil and LNG supplies, while the strait’s reopening coincided with benchmark oil prices falling more than $3 [6].
  • Gulf financial centres: The UAE faced economic strain because the war prompted some expatriates central to its financial-sector workforce to leave, according to Reuters reporting carried by Business Recorder [19].
  • U.S. defence spending: The White House requested $87.6 billion in supplemental funding that included tens of billions for the Pentagon, along with money for farmers, Ebola response and infrastructure [59].

Historical Background

The 1979 Islamic Revolution reshaped U.S.-Iran relations and led to decades without a presidential-level agreement between Washington and Tehran [common]. Several accounts said last week’s U.S.-Iran accord was the first signed by an American president and an Iranian president since that revolution [14][16][24].

Business Angle

  • Energy-price relief depends on uninterrupted Hormuz transit, not simply on the signing of a preliminary accord [6][common].
  • Companies with high fuel exposure remain vulnerable if disputes over fees or shipping corridors return [1][55][common].
  • The proposed $300 billion fund and sanctions waivers could alter Iran’s access to capital, but implementation details remain subject to technical talks [14][5].

International Reactions

  • 🇮🇷 Iran: President Masoud Pezeshkian said Iran’s missile capabilities were not included in the memorandum and “never will be,” while Tehran rejected nuclear inspections at bombed sites [48].
  • 🇺🇳 International Atomic Energy Agency: Director General Rafael Grossi said inspections would take place in cooperation with Iran despite conflicting statements over access to enrichment sites [4].
  • 🇴🇲 Oman: Oman and Iran, which border the Strait of Hormuz, said they were considering charging “costs” for ships navigating the waterway [1][21].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Iran’s Revolutionary Guard warned ships against using unapproved routes through the Strait of Hormuz, saying vessels outside Tehran-approved corridors could face enforcement action [36]. Check maritime and government advisories before Gulf shipping or offshore travel.
  • Security: Rubio’s tour included high-level meetings in all three countries and a GCC gathering in Bahrain, which can trigger road closures and security restrictions [1][18]. Plan extra time around official venues in Bahrain, Kuwait and Abu Dhabi.
