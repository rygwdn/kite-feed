Google makes Gemini 3 default for AI Overviews

Category: Technology

Google is rolling out updates that make Gemini 3 the default AI model behind AI Overviews in Google Search worldwide, aiming for more helpful and credible on-page summaries [7][6][2][3][4][9][1][5][10]. On mobile, Search can now flow from an AI Overview into a more chatbot-like “AI Mode” conversation so people can ask follow-up questions while keeping context from the original results [7][6][2][10][1][9]. Google frames the change as a way to make Search feel more “one fluid experience,” offering quick snapshots for simple queries and deeper exploration for complex tasks [7][10].

Primary Source: View original article

In our testing, we’ve found that people prefer an experience that flows naturally into a conversation — and that asking follow-up questions while keeping the context from AI Overviews makes Search more helpful

— Robby Stein (Engadget)

Did you know: Google says people often come to Search for simple answers like sports scores or the weather, but also for complex tasks that benefit from deeper exploration [10].

Key Points

  • Model routing: Ars Technica reports Google may route simpler queries to lighter Gemini 3 variants (like Flash) and more complex “long tail” queries to heavier reasoning or Gemini 3 Pro for paying subscribers [3].
  • AI Mode entry: Users can tap from an AI Overview into AI Mode on mobile, turning a summary into an ongoing conversation rather than starting a new prompt from scratch [7][6][9].
  • How it appears: Mashable describes a “See more” area that reveals a text input bar, letting people continue the same topic in AI Mode from within the Overview interface [10].
  • Publisher tension: Mashable notes ongoing concern that AI Overviews can reduce clicks to websites whose content informs summaries, while also highlighting that Overviews have made basic factual mistakes in the past [10].
  • Why Google says: Google Search VP Robby Stein said internal testing found people prefer an experience that naturally flows into conversation and keeps context for follow-ups [7].

Perspectives

Google: Google says it wants Search to offer a quick snapshot when you need it and a deeper conversational experience when you want it, with prominent links to keep exploring [7].

Sources: Engadget

Tech press critics: Some coverage emphasizes that AI Overviews has a track record of confident errors and raises concerns about reduced traffic to the wider web as more answers stay on Google’s results page [10][3].

Sources: Mashable

Product observers: Other outlets focus on usability gains: more natural language search, follow-up questions, and faster, more helpful AI answers directly on the results page [2][5][1].

Sources: CNET

Timeline

  • Last November: Google debuted the Gemini 3 family of AI models [7][5].
  • Late last year: Google previewed jumping from AI Overviews into AI Mode [7].
  • Last month: Google said it was testing AI Mode entry [10].
  • Starting today: Google begins rolling out Gemini 3 as the default [7].

Technical Details

  • AI Overviews: AI Overviews are AI-generated summaries placed at the top of some Google Search results pages [10][3].
  • AI Mode: AI Mode is Google’s conversational search experience that users can enter from an AI Overview on mobile to ask follow-up questions while preserving context [7][6][2].
  • Model router: Google can route queries to different Gemini 3 variants depending on complexity, according to Ars Technica’s description of how Overviews may select models [3].

Industry Impact

  • Publishers and SEO: More prominent AI-generated answers and follow-up conversations on the results page could further pressure referral traffic to sites that historically depended on clicks from Search [10].
  • Mobile search UX: The new in-Overview jump into AI Mode suggests Google is prioritizing chat-like interactions on phones, where quick clarification questions are common [7][2][6].
  • AI competition: By making Gemini 3 the default for Overviews globally, Google signals it wants generative AI to be a standard part of Search rather than an opt-in experiment [7][1][4].

Historical Background

Google introduced AI Overviews as AI-generated summaries at the top of many Search results pages, and outlets note the feature has faced criticism for occasional factual mistakes while becoming more visible across queries [3][10]. Engadget reports Gemini 3 first appeared in AI Overviews through routing that sent the hardest questions to the newer system before becoming the default for everyone [7].

Q&A

Q: Will AI Mode from AI Overviews be available on desktop too?

A: The rollout described in these reports focuses on mobile entry into AI Mode from AI Overviews; the sources do not confirm a desktop launch timing [7][6][10].

Q: Which Gemini 3 version powers my specific query?

A: Google does not specify a single fixed model for every query; it says AI Overviews chooses the right model for the job, and Ars Technica reports this could range from lighter “Flash” variants to Gemini 3 Pro for subscribers [3].

Q: Can users turn off AI Overviews entirely?

A: None of the provided articles describes a new global switch to disable AI Overviews; coverage instead emphasizes Google is expanding and upgrading the feature [4][3].

Action Items

  • Use follow-ups to verify details: On mobile Search, open an AI Overview and use the follow-up prompt/AI Mode entry to ask for sources, dates, or alternative explanations before acting on the information [7][2][10].
  • Cross-check important queries: When an Overview appears, open the linked sources to confirm key facts—especially for health, finance, or legal topics where errors matter most [10][3].
