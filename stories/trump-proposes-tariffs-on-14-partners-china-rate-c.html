Trump proposes tariffs on 14 partners; China rate could reach 40%

Category: Business

The Trump administration proposed new tariffs on imports from 14 major trading partners, citing excess manufacturing capacity after a U.S. Supreme Court ruling in February struck down much of President Donald Trump’s earlier broad-based tariff program. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the countries’ manufacturing policies burden or restrict U.S. commerce; the proposed duties would rely on a different legal authority, Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 [3][4]. A separate proposal targets Brazilian sugar and finished aircraft, including a 10% duty on aircraft [4]. China faces a proposed 20% rate that Washington says could rise to 40% if Beijing does not adequately engage. Vietnam could face up to 35%, while several other proposed rates are subject to caps or reductions tied to trade agreements [11][3]. The proposals drew objections from China, Japan, Singapore, the European Union and Brazil, among others [3][4]. The duties are not final: affected parties can submit comments, and a hearing is scheduled for Jan. 25. Final tariffs are expected as early as February or March. Economist Stephen Brown of Capital Economics said the new measures would largely replace earlier tariffs but could still keep core goods inflation firm into next year [6][15][12].

Key Points

  • China exemptions: The U.S. proposal exempts about $30 billion of Chinese goods covered by reciprocal tariff cuts [15][16].
  • Singapore trade: Singapore says the U.S. has run a trade surplus with the city-state for more than 20 years; under their free-trade agreement, Singapore imposes no tariffs on U.S. goods [11].
  • Investigation scope: The U.S. probe covered sectors including steel, aluminum, automobiles, batteries, chemicals, electronics, semiconductors, solar modules and shipbuilding [8].
  • Brazilian exports: Brazil said it had negotiated in good faith with Washington on the Section 301 matter for more than a year before objecting to the new proposal [4].
  • Market response: After the announcement, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62% and the S&P 500 slipped 0.25%; WisdomTree research director Aneeka Gupta cited renewed uncertainty over technology investment plans [27].

Perspectives

Trump administration: Greer said the measures are intended to level the playing field for U.S. workers and respond to market-distorting practices that he said weaken U.S. manufacturing [4].

Technical Details

  • Section 301 authority: The administration is using Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, which allows the U.S. Trade Representative to investigate foreign trade practices and propose responsive duties; the proposals still face public consultation and hearings [4][10].
  • Tariff stacking: Some Chinese imports may face the proposed duties in addition to existing Section 301 tariffs from 2018, while other tariff exclusions differ by product and country; this can make the total duty higher than the headline rate [15].

Industry Impact

  • Singapore exporters: If the maximum rate is imposed, about one-third of Singapore’s direct exports—S$9.4 billion—would be affected, and the effective tariff on those exports could reach 10.3% from 7.8% [11].
  • Aircraft manufacturing: Brazil’s largest aircraft maker, Embraer, could be affected by the proposed additional 10% U.S. tariff on finished aircraft from Brazil [8][6].

Historical Background

In October 2025, President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed to a trade ceasefire, which was later extended and is due to expire on Jan. 10, 2027 [25][3]. During an earlier escalation, U.S. and Chinese tariffs briefly reached 145%, before the two sides negotiated a truce [25].

Business Angle

  • Trade agreements could change competitive positions: proposed rates for India and Vietnam could fall from 25% to 17% and from as much as 35% to 20%, respectively, if negotiations produce deals [11].
  • Retaliation remains a material risk for exporters; the American Soybean Association warned that renewed counter-tariffs could burden farmers already near break-even after prior losses [19].

International Reactions

  • 🇨🇳 China: Beijing rejected the U.S. overcapacity rationale and reserved the right to respond, while urging Washington to resume dialogue [15].
  • 🇪🇺 European Union: The European Commission rejected the claim that Europe is a source of structural overcapacity and said it expected Washington to honor the existing trade deal [6].
  • 🇯🇵 Japan: Tokyo protested the proposal; Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara denied that Japan has structural excess manufacturing capacity [3].

Q&A

Q: Why does the proposal scrutinize Southeast Asian economies as well as China?

A: A European Council on Foreign Relations policy fellow said Washington is concerned that Chinese goods pass through regional economies before reaching the U.S. market, and that the tariffs are intended to make it harder for places such as Vietnam to serve as intermediaries [4].
