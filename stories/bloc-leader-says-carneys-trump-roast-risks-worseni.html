Bloc leader says Carney’s Trump roast risks worsening tariff tensions

Category: Canada

Bloc Québécois Leader Yves-François Blanchet criticized Prime Minister Mark Carney’s decision to bring an impersonator of U.S. President Donald Trump onstage at Ottawa’s annual Parliamentary Press Gallery dinner, calling the satire provocative and warning that U.S. tariffs are hurting Quebec’s aluminum sector [17]. The dinner is a political roast where politicians and journalists trade jokes; comedian Mark Critch portrayed Trump and Mark McKinney played Carney [11][7]. Canada and the United States continue trade negotiations. The impersonator joked that Trump himself was holding up a deal and mocked his handling of trade talks [13][7]. Blanchet said Quebec was paying a particularly high price for tariffs and argued the jokes should not inflame tensions [17]. Ottawa is seeking tariff relief in key sectors, including autos, but has warned that removing all U.S. tariffs is unlikely and that Canada may need to adjust to a changed trading relationship [17]. The dinner took place Saturday, Nov. 21, in Ottawa [21].

Primary Source: View original article

I think provoking President Trump is arrogant, stupid and not funny

— Yves-François Blanchet, leader of the Bloc Québécois (The Straits Times)

Key Points

  • Press secretary jokes: The Trump impersonator joked about the White House’s search for a press secretary after Katie Zacharia left the role five days after taking it [7][17].
  • Poilievre’s jabs: Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre pretended to hand out cash to journalists to criticize Liberal government funding for journalism [1][3].
  • Arbour’s remark: Gov. Gen. Louise Arbour joked that Carney was everywhere except Ottawa, poking fun at the prime minister’s frequent international travel [1][7].
  • Trump’s handshake: The impersonator recreated Trump’s forceful handshake with Carney and the Carney impersonator onstage [11].

Perspectives

Bloc Québécois: Leader Yves-François Blanchet said that without independent journalism, there is no democracy [1].

Sources: The Globe and Mail

Conservative Party of Canada: Pierre Poilievre mocked reports that Carney micromanages staff and is abrasive to journalists, quipping that Carney was stealing his ideas [1].
