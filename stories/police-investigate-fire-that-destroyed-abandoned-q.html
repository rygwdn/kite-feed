Police investigate fire that destroyed abandoned Quebec hotel

Category: Canada

Fire destroyed the abandoned La Sapinière hotel in Val-David, Quebec, overnight Nov. 8-9. Police are treating the blaze as suspicious; authorities have not said whether anyone was inside [1][2][3]. Firefighters avoided spraying water directly on the building because it contained asbestos and runoff could contaminate a nearby lake. Environmental and public-health teams are checking air and water, while officials say smoke poses the more immediate health concern [1][2][3]. The town had planned to demolish the building as part of a proposed school project. Val-David and former owner Diane Beaudry are due in court in April 2027 to settle the final expropriation payment for the property. The fire’s effect on the school plan remains unclear [2][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: La Sapinière hosted an encounter involving NATO, as well as training camps for the Montreal Canadiens hockey team [2].

Key Points

  • Smoke precautions: Public-health officials advised schools to keep children indoors while smoke is visible or noticeable, close windows and limit outside-air intake. Residents should avoid the site [3][2].
  • School costs: The proposed school is estimated to cost about $29.3 million, while demolition of the main hotel building had been budgeted at nearly $3 million [1].
  • Fire response: About 35 firefighters from three fire services responded, and officials planned a retention ditch to contain potentially contaminated water used to extinguish the remaining embers [2][3].

Perspectives

Diane Beaudry, businesswoman and former owner: Beaudry described the destruction as a loss of heritage and argued that lengthy legal proceedings left the buildings in poor condition and prevented their preservation [2].

Sources: Radio-Canada

Derek Bernard, Val-David acting mayor: Bernard said the town knew the derelict building posed a fire risk and was already seeking bids to demolish other buildings on the property [2].
