Researchers detail skull scan identifying Europe’s first megaraptoran

Category: Science

Researchers have published details of how they identified a century-old skull fragment from England’s Isle of Wight as Invictibellatrix amberina, the first known megaraptoran found in Europe [1][4][5]. The predatory dinosaur lived about 120 million years ago and is estimated to have measured three to four metres long. Researchers believe it hunted with its strong forelimbs and curved claws [2][4]. The identification relied on a computed tomography scan and a three-dimensional model of the rear of the skull, which researchers compared with remains of other predatory dinosaurs [2][4][5]. Study co-author Charlie Scherer said the fossil supports a proposed migration route from Asia through Europe to the southern landmass Gondwana, rather than an island-hopping route [1][2]. Researchers say the animal may have had feathers, but no skin or feathers are preserved in this specimen [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Migration evidence: The fossil supports a proposed migration route from Asia through Europe to Gondwana, but does not by itself document every stage of that migration [1][2].

Historical Background

Megaraptorans were thought to have originated in Asia and later spread across Gondwana, a landmass that included present-day Africa, South America and Antarctica. Before the Isle of Wight study, fossils had not provided direct evidence of their presence in Europe [2][1].

Q: Why was the fossil identified only recently?

A: After entering the Natural History Museum’s collection in 1924, the fossil remained largely unnoticed until researchers examined it and compared its skull features with those of other predatory dinosaurs [4][2].
