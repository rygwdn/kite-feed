Pope Leo arrives in Uruguay, urges unity and challenges euthanasia law

Category: World

Pope Leo XIV arrived in Montevideo on Friday, opening a 12-day visit to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru with an appeal for cooperation and peace three weeks after surgery for lung cancer [3][13][1]. In his first address, he urged Uruguayans to resolve differences through law, negotiation and patient agreement, and said people should receive care through the natural end of life—a challenge to Uruguay’s abortion and euthanasia laws [7][16][12]. Uruguay, the pope’s first stop, formally separates church and state, and only 36% of its people identify as Catholic, according to a poll cited by Mercopress [12]. Leo also said his experience as a patient strengthened his conviction that health care should be a right for everyone [32]. On Saturday, Leo is scheduled to celebrate Mass in Paysandú, visit Montevideo’s Casavalle neighborhood, which is affected by violence and poverty, and lead a prayer vigil at Centenario Stadium. He plans to celebrate Mass in Florida on Sunday before traveling to Argentina; the tour’s final leg begins in Peru on Wednesday [11][12][19].

Key Points

  • Mercosur praise: Leo praised Mercosur, the regional trade bloc, and Uruguay’s shift toward renewable energy [12].
  • Technology warning: In Montevideo, Leo warned that rapid AI development must not reduce workers to “a mere expendable cog in the production process” [13].
  • Secular identity: Christmas is officially called “Family Day,” and Easter week “Tourism Week” in Uruguay [3][11].
  • Crowd interest: The Vatican estimated that about 30,000 people lined the route or attended the pope’s first events in Montevideo, while tickets for his prayer vigil sold out in two days [7][21].
  • Casavalle work: The parish in Casavalle works with national and municipal authorities on housing construction and addiction-recovery initiatives [11].

Perspectives

Uruguayan President Yamandú Orsi: Orsi presented Uruguay as a secular country with strong democratic institutions and a diverse society that respects different beliefs and convictions [30].

  • 11 November 2026: Leo is scheduled to begin his visit to Peru [39][36].
  • 17 November 2026: Leo is scheduled to return to Rome after the South America tour [19][36].

Historical Background

Pope John Paul II last visited Uruguay in 1988, making Leo’s visit the first papal trip there in nearly four decades [7][8]. Uruguay’s 1919 Constitution established the separation of church and state, a distinction Leo addressed in his opening remarks [12].
