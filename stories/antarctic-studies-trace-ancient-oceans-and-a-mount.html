Antarctic studies trace ancient oceans and a mountain range linked to complex life

Category: Science

Researchers have reported evidence of two ancient Antarctic settings: a colossal mountain range that formed in Gondwana about 600 million years ago and has been linked to the evolution of complex life, and an open, sunlit ocean beneath what is now West Antarctic ice [3][2][1]. The mountain evidence comes from analyses of tiny ancient rocks, while the ocean evidence comes from a drilling project at Crary Ice Rise near the inner margin of the Ross Ice Shelf [3][2]. The drilling team completed the project in January 2026, recovering a sediment core after passing through the overlying ice. The core contained shell fragments and remains of marine organisms that require sunlight [2]. The evidence indicates that open ocean once occupied an area now covered by ice, but it does not establish that an ice-sheet collapse is occurring now or predict future sea-level rise [2].
