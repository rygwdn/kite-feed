Trump expected to name intelligence chief Jay Clayton as AI adviser

Category: AI

President Donald Trump is expected to name Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton as the White House’s lead artificial intelligence adviser, a role Trump has called the “AI czar,” according to people familiar with the discussions. Clayton is expected to retain his intelligence post, which he took after Senate confirmation in July. He oversees 18 U.S. intelligence agencies [8][7][15][26]. Clayton has described “super intelligence” as a national-security issue. Trump has argued that the United States must maintain its AI lead over China and has resisted broad new federal rules, saying the industry should address risks itself [26][17][19]. Trump said last month he would appoint an AI adviser and create an “AI Force,” but gave no details on how either would work. David Sacks, the previous adviser, left the role in March. The White House said the president would announce personnel decisions and described reports ahead of an announcement as speculation. No formal appointment had been confirmed in reporting through Oct. 3 [2][26][15][18].

  • Voter concerns: A Quinnipiac University poll found 72% of respondents opposed a new data center in their community, up from 65% in March [17][19].
  • Company pledges: After the White House meeting, executives agreed to use outside auditors for model-safety assessments and strengthen internal controls [19].
  • Senate proposal: Republican Sen. Josh Hawley and Democratic Sen. Chris Murphy were working on a bill to establish civil and criminal liability for some AI-related safety incidents [17].
  • Role authority: The AI adviser position does not require Senate confirmation, unlike Clayton’s current intelligence post [26][23].
