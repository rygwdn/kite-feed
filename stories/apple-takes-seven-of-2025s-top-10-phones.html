Apple takes seven of 2025’s top 10 phones

Category: Technology

A Counterpoint Research roundup of 2025’s best-selling smartphones shows Apple in a strong position: the iPhone 16 led global sales, and Apple devices made up seven of the year’s top 10 models, while Samsung held the remaining three spots [2][5][6][7][1][8][4][3][9]. The list underscores how concentrated the mainstream market has become at the top end, with Apple and Samsung again occupying every slot in the top 10 [5][6][3]. One upbeat takeaway for everyday buyers: the standard (non-Pro) iPhone models did especially well, and Counterpoint highlighted the base iPhone 17 as a fast mover thanks to practical upgrades like a 120Hz refresh rate and a jump to 256GB base storage [1][5]. Meanwhile, Samsung’s presence leaned heavily on affordable devices—its Galaxy A16 5G ranked as 2025’s best-selling Android phone—showing that price-focused options can still win big volume in a premium-leaning market [5][6][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Counterpoint said the iPhone 16e—positioned as a more budget-friendly iPhone option—still placed within the global top-10 best-sellers of 2025 [5][6].

Key Points

  • Top 10 share: Counterpoint said the top 10 models from Apple and Samsung represented 19% of all smartphones sold worldwide in 2025, extending a four-year streak of top-10 exclusivity between the two brands [5][6][3].
  • IPhone 17 lift: Counterpoint reported the iPhone 17 lineup posted 16% higher sales than the prior generation in its first full quarter, with demand cited in the U.S., China, and Western Europe [5].
  • Budget Androids: Samsung’s Galaxy A06 4G joined the Galaxy A16 5G in the top 10, signaling that Samsung’s volume in 2025’s ranking came largely from lower-priced models [7].
  • Japan momentum: Counterpoint said the Galaxy S25 Ultra’s sales grew more than threefold year over year in Japan, making it the only high-end Android phone mentioned as a top-10 seller [5][9].
  • Pricing pressure: Counterpoint forecast that rising memory prices could shift 2026’s mix toward premium flagships because entry-to-mid-tier devices feel component-cost increases more acutely [5].

Technical Details

  • 120Hz refresh rate: A 120Hz display refreshes the screen up to 120 times per second, which can make scrolling and animations look smoother; Counterpoint cited the iPhone 17’s move to 120Hz (from 60Hz) as one upgrade tied to its improved performance [5][1].
  • Base storage tier: Base storage is the default built-in capacity a phone ships with; Counterpoint pointed to the iPhone 17 increasing base storage to 256GB (from 128GB) as part of the value proposition that helped its growth [5][1].
  • Memory price cycle: NAND and DRAM prices can swing with supply and demand, and Counterpoint warned that rising memory prices could squeeze entry-to-midrange phone economics more than premium models, potentially reshaping 2026’s best-seller mix [5].

Industry Impact

  • Component suppliers: Counterpoint’s expectation of rising memory prices implies that suppliers of storage and memory components could gain pricing leverage, while budget phone makers may face tighter bill-of-materials constraints [5].
  • Android competition: With Samsung’s top-10 presence led by the Galaxy A16 5G and another budget model, the ranking suggests room for Android OEM differentiation may be strongest in value segments rather than premium flagships—outside rare standouts like the S25 Ultra [5][7][6].
  • Carrier retail: Strong U.S. demand cited for the iPhone 17 series points to continued carrier-driven upgrade activity in key Western markets, which can influence trade-in values and financing promotions [5].
  • App ecosystems: A larger installed base of best-selling iPhones can reinforce developer focus on iOS optimization and monetization, while Samsung’s volume in budget tiers keeps Android reach broad for ad-supported and emerging-market apps [common].

Historical Background

Modern smartphone competition has typically split between Apple’s iPhone and Android devices from many makers, but Apple and Samsung have increasingly dominated global premium and mass-market segments over the last decade [common]. Counterpoint’s report says 2025 marked the fourth consecutive year in which Apple and Samsung alone filled the global top-10 best-selling model list, reinforcing that consolidation at the very top [5][6][3].

Q&A

Q: Which specific iPhone models (besides the iPhone 16) appeared in the top 10?

A: Reports list several Apple entries alongside the iPhone 16, including the iPhone 16 Pro Max, iPhone 16 Pro, iPhone 17 Pro Max, the base iPhone 17, the iPhone 15, and the iPhone 16e [2][7][5][8].

Q: Did Counterpoint publish exact unit sales for each model in the top 10?

A: Android Headlines noted that Counterpoint did not share exact sales numbers, focusing instead on rankings and comparisons to 2024 [7].

Q: Why did the iPhone 16e matter to the ranking despite being a lower-priced phone?

A: Counterpoint linked the iPhone 16e’s contribution to its lower entry price point into Apple’s ecosystem, and Slashdot also described it as a budget-friendly option with demand in markets like Japan and the U.S. [5][6].

Action Items

  • Compare base-model specs before upgrading: If you’re shopping this year, use the iPhone 17 base model’s 120Hz display and 256GB default storage as benchmarks when comparing similarly priced phones [5][1].
  • Price-check budget options: Consider cross-shopping Samsung’s Galaxy A16 5G and Galaxy A06 4G if you want a lower-cost Android approach that still proved popular in 2025’s global rankings [7].
