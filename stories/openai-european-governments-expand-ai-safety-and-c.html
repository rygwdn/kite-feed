OpenAI, European governments expand AI safety and cyber defense work

Category: AI

OpenAI and Poland’s NASK National Research Institute agreed to explore methods for evaluating advanced AI systems and identifying cybersecurity weaknesses. Separately, leaders from Singapore, Norway and Finland discussed international reporting of frontier-AI incidents, while a European Union cybersecurity body launched three generative-AI projects backed by about €42 million [1][4][8]. The projects bring together more than 70 partners in 14 EU member states and the United Kingdom, with trials planned in sectors including healthcare, transport and telecommunications. AI can help detect vulnerabilities and respond to attacks, but attackers can also use it to automate operations [8]. Anthropic said about 950 AI agents identified a previously uncharacterized enzyme system, which researchers then investigated experimentally. The findings remain a preprint requiring further evaluation. Tremont AI and AstraZeneca separately agreed to develop and assess models for preclinical drug-safety screening [3][6].

Primary Source: View original article

AI safety cannot be achieved in isolation. It is a collaborative effort, and only through active cooperation between public AI Safety Institutes and frontier AI developers can we ensure that AI becomes truly reliable and safe to use.

— Radosław Nielek, director of NASK (EdTech Innovation Hub)

Key Points

  • Prior security work: CERT Polska previously used OpenAI models to identify six MikroTik RouterOS vulnerabilities; some could reportedly be combined to take control of devices without authentication. MikroTik issued fixes [1].
  • EU project design: The EU-backed AIGENTIC project is developing auditable generative-AI agents with guardrails for threat intelligence and incident response, with nine planned use cases spanning automotive, rail, finance, maritime and smart cities [8].
  • Agent testing: Mace AI’s Fisher product tests whether AI agents can exceed their granted permissions, recommends system changes and retests the agents; the company is also funding an AI-safety doctoral fellowship at the University of Virginia [2].
  • Drug screening: Tremont AI and AstraZeneca will evaluate foundation models for toxicologic pathology, where reviewers must distinguish rare treatment-related tissue changes from normal biological variation [6].

Perspectives

Singapore government: Prime Minister Lawrence Wong proposed beginning with a pilot to test secure information-sharing methods for AI incident reports, including serious failures and near misses [4].

Technical Details

  • Accelerator access controls: The proposed integration layer makes process-variable access read-only by default, with narrowly defined exceptions. It adds audit logs and a watchdog that can return control to operators if an AI service stops responding [5].
  • Approval workflow: The design requires an AI to propose actions that operate accelerator equipment for an operator to approve or reject. It distinguishes these operational writes from display-only updates [5].

Industry Impact

  • Cybersecurity: GENESIS-AI is developing a regulation-aware system to defend complex infrastructure, combining multi-agent systems, Red/Blue team co-evolution and federated privacy-preserving learning. It will be tested in a secure cyber range, with industrial partners including Siemens, Airbus and Bosch [8].
  • Preclinical drug safety: Tremont AI says foundation models could make pathology review more measurable and reproducible, helping pathologists spend less time on repetitive screening and more on interpreting findings [6].

Historical Background

OpenAI also works with public AI safety research organizations including the UK’s AI Security Institute and the US Center for Advancing Standards and Innovation for Super Intelligence. The company has advocated shared technical standards for evaluating model capabilities, assessing risks, testing safeguards and reporting security incidents [1].
