US blacklists Singapore-based Megaspeed over suspected Nvidia chip diversions

Category: World

The U.S. Commerce Department added Singapore-based cloud and data-center operator Megaspeed and six related companies in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand to its Entity List, requiring government licenses for shipments of U.S.-regulated goods and technology [2][15][11]. The department cited a significant risk that the companies could divert advanced computing products to unauthorized users or uses; license applications face a presumption of denial [2][4]. The listing also includes Apex Enterprise Solutions, a Singapore subsidiary of Chinese technology company Alibaba [3][15]. U.S. authorities have investigated Megaspeed since 2024 over allegations that it helped route Nvidia AI chips to China in breach of export controls. The Justice Department is conducting a criminal investigation. Beijing has denied separate U.S. intelligence allegations that former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang acted for China; Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing [10][11][1]. Commerce announced the action on the same day Nvidia’s board reinstated CEO Jensen Huang, who had resigned less than two weeks earlier [11][6]. The decision followed public pressure from U.S. officials and lawmakers over why Commerce had not acted sooner. Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said his government is investigating possible breaches of Singapore law and coordinating with U.S. authorities; Malaysia has told data-center operators not to host or provide services to the listed firms [1][9][4].

We do not want Singapore to be used and abused as a conduit by companies to circumvent America’s export controls.

— Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong (Financial Times)

Key Points

  • Shipments continued: Trade data cited by Firstpost showed Megaspeed’s Malaysian subsidiary received more than $800 million in Nvidia-chip shipments from July 2025 to July 2026, after Nvidia reportedly put Megaspeed on its internal no-sell list in June 2025 [7][10].
  • Apex loan: Apex lent money to Megaspeed in 2024 through Megaspeed’s Malaysian subsidiary, Speedmatrix, which pledged its entire business as collateral [3].
  • Transit allowance: Goods already en route to the designated companies may proceed under a 30-day grace period. The new licensing requirement applies to items covered by U.S. export regulations [11][2].
  • Singapore invoicing hub: Nvidia said very few chips sold through its Singapore subsidiary arrive in the city-state. The company uses the subsidiary to centralize invoicing for international purchases; it accounted for nearly half of Nvidia’s global revenues last year [8].
  • Congressional scrutiny: Democratic lawmakers opened a probe into Nvidia’s possible influence on Commerce’s investigation [3][19].

Perspectives

Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong: Wong said Singapore is not part of the U.S. investigation into Jensen Huang’s role and that Singapore previously investigated Alice Huang but found no basis to prosecute her [1][26].

Industry Impact

  • Singapore semiconductor economy: The semiconductor industry accounts for nearly 7% of Singapore’s GDP, and the country produces about one-tenth of the world’s semiconductors [8].

Historical Background

U.S. restrictions have barred China from buying advanced Nvidia chips since 2022, according to the BBC [6]. During a June 2025 on-site check of how Megaspeed used the chips, U.S. and Singaporean law enforcement interviewed then-CEO Alice Huang [15].
