Japanese island leopard cats show unexplained short-tail trait

Category: Science

Scientists identified an unusual short-tail trait in three Tsushima leopard cats observed on Japan’s Tsushima Island in 2024 and 2025. The cats’ tails measured about half the usual average length, and researchers said the feature had not previously been reported in this population or any other leopard-cat population. About 100 Tsushima leopard cats remain on the island, where the population is considered critically endangered [2]. Because short tails are common among domestic cats on Tsushima, researchers examined whether the trait could result from hybridization. They sequenced the whole genome of one short-tailed leopard cat; Nikkei reported that Anicom’s analysis found the trait was not derived from domestic cats [2][3]. The researchers said the repeated occurrence makes separate traumatic injuries less likely and raises the possibility of a shared hereditary or developmental cause [2].
