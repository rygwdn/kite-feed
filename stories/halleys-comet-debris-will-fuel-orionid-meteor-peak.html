Halley’s Comet debris will fuel Orionid meteor peak October 21-22

Category: Science

The Orionid meteor shower is expected to peak overnight Oct. 21-22 as Earth crosses debris left by Halley’s Comet [1][4][6]. Under ideal dark-sky conditions, observers could see up to 20 meteors an hour. The fragments burn in Earth’s atmosphere, appearing as streaks of light [5][3]. A bright waxing gibbous moon will wash out some of the fainter meteors, but viewing may improve after moonset. The source lists moonset at around 3 to 3:30 a.m. for its observing location, leaving a few darker hours before dawn [5]. Observers need no binoculars or telescope and should find a dark, open area and give their eyes 20 to 30 minutes to adjust [4][8].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Fast-moving fragments: Orionid meteors enter the atmosphere at about 66 kilometers per second, or 148,000 mph, among the faster meteors visible from Earth [6][4].
  • Look widely: Although the meteors appear to radiate from Orion, they can cross any part of the sky, so observers should take in a broad view rather than stare at the constellation [6][5].
  • Occasional fireballs: Particularly bright Orionids can appear as fireballs, briefly illuminating a larger area of the sky [6].

Timeline

  • 2 October 2026: Orionid activity begins, continuing into early November [4][8]
  • 21 October 2026: Peak viewing begins overnight, continuing into October 22 [1][12]
  • 7 November 2026: The Orionid activity period ends [8][4]

Scientific Significance

  • Earth strikes the incoming debris nearly head-on, helping explain the Orionids’ exceptional speed [5].

Historical Background

Halley’s Comet returns to the inner solar system about every 75 to 76 years; its last close passage was in 1986 and its next is expected in 2061 [6][4].

Action Items

  • Bring a folding chair and warm layers for the overnight wait; a blanket or sleeping bag can help you stay comfortable [5].
