Quebec’s sovereigntist Parti Québécois seeks majority ahead of Oct. 5 election

Category: Canada

The Parti Québécois is asking Quebec voters for a majority government in the Oct. 5 provincial election. The party leads in polls, and leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon says he is confident of winning [1][3][14]. The PQ has pledged to hold a referendum on Quebec independence during its first mandate, but only after President Trump leaves the White House [6]. Rival parties have challenged the PQ’s bid for a majority, while final campaign pitches have focused on Indigenous issues, immigration and Quebec’s relationship with the monarchy. St-Pierre Plamondon defended his proposal to abolish the lieutenant-governor’s office; the Liberals have sought support from undecided voters and appealed to anglophones [4][5]. More than 1.6 million Quebecers had voted in advance, according to a campaign update [4].

Key Points

  • Polling: A campaign analysis said the PQ had not polled above 30% during the race, despite its lead [6].
  • Party landscape: The Parti conservateur du Québec, led by Éric Duhaime, has campaigned on health-care reform and tax cuts [6].

Perspectives

Quebec Liberals: The party is trying to reverse its fortunes in a province described as being in a conservative era, with the PQ poised to win Monday’s vote [2].
