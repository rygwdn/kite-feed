Vancouver mourns soldier killed in Alberta training fire

Category: Canada

Pte. Aidan Goulding, a 23-year-old member of the Canadian Army’s 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry, was laid to rest in Vancouver on Sunday, Nov. 8, weeks after a grass fire overtook soldiers during a live-fire exercise at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta. Four soldiers died in the Oct. 17 incident and 39 were injured [6][2][3]. Hundreds of relatives, friends and military personnel attended the service at Seaforth Armoury. Goulding’s mother, Sonia Monk, said military service had given him a sense of purpose and belonging. He had been preparing for possible deployment overseas, including peacekeeping in Latvia [4][2][3]. An investigation into the incident, including the grass fire, is ongoing. Alberta experienced unusually strong winds that day, Global News reported [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Aidan was never still. He loved life and wanted to experience all of it. He was fearless, sometimes more fearless than I would have preferred.

— Sonia Monk, mother of Pte. Aidan Goulding (Global News)

Key Points

  • Other victims: The other soldiers killed were Pte. Shaun Farrell, 23, of Prince Albert, Sask.; Pte. Hayden Lykkemark, 19, of Langley, B.C.; and Sgt. Morgan Wong, 26, of Edmonton [3][6].
  • Hospital care: Many of the 39 injured soldiers remained in hospitals in Edmonton and Calgary after the fire [7][1].
  • Military training: Goulding completed infantry training in July 2026 before joining the 3rd Battalion; his commander said he often went for runs after work and pushed himself [2].
  • Family memories: His family recalled that he repaired and resold used cars and travelled to countries including Mexico, Germany, Italy and Spain [6][7].

Perspectives

Sonia Monk, Goulding’s mother: Monk said Goulding left a high-paying job to join the army because he wanted to do something important, a choice she viewed as unselfish [6][7].

Sources: The Globe and Mail

Capt. Jack O’Connell, Goulding’s commander: O’Connell described Goulding as a confident, charismatic soldier who encouraged those around him and kept his sense of humour when training became difficult [2].
