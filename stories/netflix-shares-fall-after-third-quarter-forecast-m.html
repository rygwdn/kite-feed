Netflix shares fall after third-quarter forecast misses estimates

Category: Business

Netflix shares fell in after-hours trading Thursday after the company issued a third-quarter outlook below analyst expectations, even as it reported higher second-quarter profit and revenue. The company forecast July-September revenue of $12.86 billion and diluted earnings per share of 82 cents, below analyst estimates of $13 billion and 84 cents, according to LSEG and FactSet figures cited by several reports [12][26][27][5][18]. In the March-June quarter, Netflix reported net income of $3.4 billion, or 80 cents a share, up from $3.13 billion, or 72 cents a share, a year earlier; revenue rose 13% to $12.56 billion from $11.08 billion, slightly below analyst revenue expectations of about $12.58 billion [5][18][30][48]. Netflix said growth reflected membership gains, price increases and higher advertising revenue, and said price changes performed in line with its expectations [5][15][46][48]. Netflix also said it would publish its “What We Watched” viewing-hours report once a year rather than twice a year, starting after the first quarter of 2027, saying it wanted investors to focus on revenue and operating profit [10][11][7][6][26]. The company reported about 97 billion viewing hours in the first half of 2026, up roughly 2% from a year earlier, and said advertising remains a priority, with about $3 billion in expected ad revenue this year [11][14][1][5][22].

Key Points

  • Share reaction: Netflix shares fell roughly 8% to 9% after hours, with Reuters-cited reports putting the decline at nearly 8.6% to $67.99 [12][27][28][44].
  • Full-year outlook: Netflix narrowed its 2026 revenue forecast to $51 billion to $51.4 billion from a prior range of $50.7 billion to $51.7 billion [23][22][33][46].
  • Regional growth: Netflix reported double-digit revenue growth across regions, with quarterly revenue above $4 billion in Europe, the Middle East and Africa and above $1.5 billion in both Latin America and Asia-Pacific [15][23].
  • Live strategy: Netflix said live events are drawing advertiser interest, while one report said six of its 10 biggest membership-signup days over the past five years were tied to live events [5][22].

Technical Details

  • AI search features: Netflix said it is using large language models to improve content discovery and is adding voice search and AI-powered natural-language search for subscribers [18].
  • Live-content economics: Netflix said live content represented more than 5% of content spending but about 1% of viewing hours, and that events such as the Women's World Cup, NFL, MLB and WWE support advertising demand [22].

Industry Impact

  • Streaming competition: Netflix faces competition from YouTube, TikTok and other short-form video providers as it expands into shorter-form video, live events, podcasts and gaming [41][32].
  • Advertising market: Netflix said it expects ad revenue to roughly double year over year, and that U.S. upfront talks were in late stages, with live sports and WWE supporting advertiser demand [22][5].
  • Content spending: Huxiu reported Netflix spent about $5.1 billion on content in the second quarter and nearly $10 billion in the first half, with full-year content investment on track around $20 billion [36].

Business Angle

  • Netflix expects about $3 billion in ad revenue this year, but the ad tier still trails the standard ad-free plan in revenue per member [5][22][44].
