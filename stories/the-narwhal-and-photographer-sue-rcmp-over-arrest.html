The Narwhal and photographer sue RCMP over arrest

Category: Canada

A five-week civil trial is getting underway in Vancouver over the 2021 arrest of photojournalist Amber Bracken while she covered Wet’suwet’en resistance to the Coastal GasLink pipeline in northwestern B.C. [4][1][3]. Bracken and her outlet, The Narwhal, are asking the court to declare that her arrest and detention violated Charter protections for freedom of the press, and they allege wrongful arrest and wrongful detention by the RCMP [4][1][3]. The case is being closely watched because it could shape whether journalists can report from inside police-controlled “exclusion zones” during protests and enforcement operations in Canada [2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: The Narwhal is a B.C.-based news outlet and is a co-plaintiff alongside Bracken in the lawsuit [1].

Key Points

  • Opening arguments: Government lawyers argued the lawsuit should be treated as a narrow dispute about Bracken’s specific arrest rather than a broad test of how police treat the media as a whole [1].
  • Access at issue: The Narwhal says a key question is whether police can bar journalists from reporting inside an exclusion zone set up during enforcement, with the judge’s decision potentially reinforcing—or limiting—newsgathering access [2].
  • Media-wide stakes: The plaintiffs say the case has implications for journalists across Canada who cover protests and police actions, not just those reporting on pipeline conflicts [3].
  • Turning point: An analysis piece frames the lawsuit as a “historic fight for press freedom,” describing it as an effort to set clearer boundaries on police powers when media are doing on-the-ground reporting [5].

Technical Details

  • Charter press freedom: Bracken and The Narwhal argue their claim engages constitutional protections for freedom of the press, asking for a court declaration that those rights were violated in the arrest and detention [4][1].
  • Exclusion zones: The dispute includes whether police-created “exclusion zones” can lawfully keep journalists from entering areas where enforcement actions occur, which could affect newsgathering access at future protests [2].
  • Civil trial remedies: The plaintiffs are pursuing a civil case seeking declarations and findings tied to wrongful arrest and detention, rather than a criminal proceeding about the underlying protest events [4][3].

Industry Impact

  • Journalism access: A decision on whether journalists can report inside police “exclusion zones” could influence how outlets plan protest coverage, including staffing, legal support, and risk management for field reporters [2][3].
  • Policing practices: Because the lawsuit asks for Charter findings about press freedom, the case could prompt clearer operational guidance on how officers handle credentialed media during enforcement actions [4][1].

Historical Background

The case stems from Bracken’s arrest in 2021 while she was covering Wet’suwet’en resistance to construction of the Coastal GasLink pipeline in northwestern British Columbia [4][1]. The current lawsuit seeks Charter findings specifically tied to press freedom, using that 2021 policing operation and the arrest as the factual foundation for the court’s review [4][3].
