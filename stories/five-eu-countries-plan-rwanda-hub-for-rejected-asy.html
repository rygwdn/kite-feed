Five EU countries plan Rwanda hub for rejected asylum seekers

Category: World

Germany, Austria, Denmark, Greece and the Netherlands announced plans on Nov. 16 to create a return hub in Rwanda for people whose asylum claims have been rejected and who have exhausted legal routes to remain in Europe. The governments say transfers could begin in 2027, making it the first return hub outside the bloc established by EU member states [1][6][12][31]. The plan is intended for people subject to enforceable removal decisions who cannot be returned to their home countries. In Rwanda, they could settle, return voluntarily to their country of origin or move to another country where they have a right to reside. The participating states say the centre would be open, not a detention facility [8][39][14][1]. The announcement follows a new EU Return Regulation that enables hubs outside the bloc, though negotiations with Rwanda on implementation continue [1][8]. The five governments argue that the scheme will increase removals and deter irregular migration. Rights groups and some European leaders warn of risks to migrants’ rights and question whether deterrence will work. The pilot is expected to accommodate about 100 people at a time, while safeguards, costs and operational arrangements remain under discussion [7][12][8][18][39].

Key Points

  • Initial eligibility: The Netherlands says only single adult men will be transferred in the first phase, with a focus on people from countries in Rwanda’s region. The scheme excludes minors [6][8].
  • Cost and oversight: The five European governments will cover transfer and hosting costs but have not disclosed a budget. Officials say an independent monitoring mechanism is planned but have not provided details [8][39].
  • Return rates: Less than 30% of people ordered to leave the EU are currently returned to their countries of origin, a shortfall supporters cite in arguing for new mechanisms [21][22].
  • UN involvement: The U.N. refugee agency says it supports return hubs only when they meet human-rights standards and follow a full, fair assessment of asylum claims [7].
  • Potential expansion: The pilot could expand once it is operational and the risk of legal challenges has diminished [8].

Perspectives

Participating European governments: They maintain the arrangement will comply with EU and international law [6][8].

Historical Background

Rwanda and the United Kingdom agreed in 2022 on a separate plan to send asylum seekers there while their claims were still being processed. The UK Supreme Court ruled that plan unlawful in 2023, and the British government abandoned it in 2024 [1][6][25].

International Reactions

  • 🇪🇸 Spain: Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez rejected the proposal, saying it had no “future or humanity” and was not a solution [6][12].
  • 🇫🇷 France: President Emmanuel Macron has opposed external return centres, saying they are neither effective nor consistent with EU values [36][41].
  • 🇬🇧 United Kingdom: The UK government said it would watch the EU initiative “with interest” and has not ruled out joining a return-hub approach [6][19].

Q&A

Q: Who will decide whether a person can be transferred to Rwanda?

A: The five European countries will submit names, but Rwanda says it will vet each person proposed for transfer and retain the right to refuse individuals [11].

Q: How long could someone stay at the centre?

A: The plan calls for stays of a few months, but the exact duration has not been determined [39][8].
