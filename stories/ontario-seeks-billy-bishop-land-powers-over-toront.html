Ontario seeks Billy Bishop land powers over Toronto Islands

Category: Canada

Ontario has introduced legislation that would let the province replace the City of Toronto in the tripartite agreement that governs Billy Bishop Toronto City Airport and expropriate land tied to airport-related works, including part of Little Norway Park and broader areas identified in the bill around the airport lands [1][2][4]. The Ford government said it does not plan to seize all of that land and would use the new powers only for property it says is needed for Billy Bishop expansion, but the breadth of the proposed authority has already set off a clash with Toronto City Hall [1][2]. Mayor Olivia Chow said the proposal amounts to a provincial takeover of city land and urged residents to oppose it, while provincial officials cast the bill as a way to secure space for infrastructure connected to Billy Bishop Airport [2][4][1]. For Toronto readers, the dispute matters because it extends beyond the airport itself to public waterfront space and the balance of power between Queen’s Park and the city over planning on the harbourfront [1][2].

  • Province pledge: Provincial officials said that, despite the bill’s broad wording, they would take only the land they say is necessary for Billy Bishop expansion [1][2].

Historical Background

Billy Bishop Airport has long operated under a tripartite agreement involving the City of Toronto, PortsToronto and the federal government, a governance structure that gave the city a direct role in airport land decisions [1][2]. This latest bill matters because it would remove Toronto from part of that established framework and hand the province authority over key airport-related lands [2].
