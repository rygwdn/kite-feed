Leaders mark Treaty 6 anniversary at Fort Carlton

Category: Canada

Political leaders, Crown representatives, Indigenous chiefs and elders marked the 150th anniversary of Treaty 6 on Sunday at Fort Carlton, about 100 kilometres north of Saskatoon, where the treaty was first signed [1][2][5]. Speakers said the treaty was intended as an agreement for coexistence and collaboration between First Nations and the Crown, but that its first 150 years included broken promises, unrealized commitments, residential school trauma and disputes over the medicine chest clause [1]. Treaty 6 was the sixth of 11 Numbered Treaties signed after Confederation and covers much of what is now central Alberta and Saskatchewan, although it predates both provinces [1][5]. The Government of Saskatchewan said the province has 29 Treaty 6 First Nations and that it joined Treaty 6 First Nations, the Office of the Treaty Commissioner, Pê mîska Tourism and community members for a four-day gathering from August 20 to 23 at Fort Carlton Provincial Historic Park [2].

Key Points

  • Treaty interpretation: The treaty text says signatory First Nations ceded claims to certain lands, while Indigenous leaders have long said the agreements were about sharing land, not relinquishing title [5].
  • Regional events: Anniversary activities included celebratory powwows in Edmonton and viewings of the original treaty parchment at Fort Carlton Provincial Historic Park [5].
  • Gathering program: The Fort Carlton gathering included ceremonies, Treaty forums, cultural programming and opportunities to learn about Treaty 6 history and continuing significance [2].

Historical Background

Canada was established through Confederation in 1867. Treaty 6 was one of 11 Numbered Treaties signed in the years that followed [1][5]. The treaties cover most of what is now Ontario, all of Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Alberta, northeast British Columbia, most of the Northwest Territories and the southeast corner of Yukon [5]. They enabled widespread non-Indigenous settlement while creating reserves for First Nations [5].
