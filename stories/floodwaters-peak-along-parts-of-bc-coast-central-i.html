Floodwaters peak along parts of B.C. coast; central Island remains under warning

Category: Canada

Floodwaters have peaked across much of Vancouver Island and British Columbia’s south coast, but river levels remain a serious concern on central Vancouver Island after days of rain, high tides and storm surge. The provincial River Forecast Centre issued a flood warning for central Island waterways, where rivers had reached their banks or were expected to overflow soon. Officials said eastern Vancouver Island remained under a flood watch and coastal flood warnings continued in many areas [1][3][5]. Courtenay declared a local emergency and ordered evacuations from several low-lying properties, including the Maple Pool campground, while the Regional District of Nanaimo ordered some Cedar residents to leave homes along the Nanaimo River [1][5]. Rain and wind warnings for the South Coast were lifted Thursday, but officials said rivers would need time to recede before advisories could be withdrawn. The Effingham Island weather station recorded 469 millimetres of rain in just over two days [1][3].

Key Points

  • Rapid snowmelt: The River Forecast Centre said large amounts of mountain snow were melting quickly at lower elevations and feeding already swollen waterways [1][2].
  • Evacuation support: Nanaimo Search and Rescue went door to door in Cedar, while emergency support services opened a reception area at Cedar Community Centre [5].
  • Farm flooding: Jenny James said flooding at her family’s farm has worsened in recent years; the family has owned the land since 1981 [5].
  • Ferry disruption: BC Ferries cancelled two Thursday morning sailings between Tsawwassen and Swartz Bay because of storm conditions [2].

Perspectives

Nanaimo Search and Rescue: Search manager Dave Stewart said some residents under the Cedar evacuation order chose not to leave [5].

Industry Impact

  • Agriculture: Water inundated parts of Waterloo Farms and damaged a barn. The family is building a concrete wall around the barn to prevent future floods, a project Jenny James said will take years to complete [5].

Historical Background

Environment Canada meteorologist Jennifer Kowal said a similar combination of heavy rain, storm surge and king tides caused flooding in coastal Metro Vancouver and on Vancouver Island in 2022 [5]. Nanaimo Search and Rescue manager Dave Stewart said the Cedar area was last under an evacuation order in 2022 [5].

Humanitarian Impact

Cedar residents were asked to take pets and anyone else needing transportation with them, lock windows and doors, and bring government ID and medicine if immediately available [5].

Travel Advisory

  • Road safety: The B.C. Transportation Ministry advised drivers to check conditions before travelling through coastal areas because of flood risk [1][3].
