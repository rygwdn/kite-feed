Tiny chicken-sized dinosaur fossil rewrites evolutionary history of alvarezsaurs

Category: Science

Scientists have described a nearly complete fossil skeleton of Alnashetri cerropoliciensis, a chicken-sized dinosaur that lived about 95 million years ago in Patagonia, Argentina [7][8][2]. Estimated to have weighed under 1 kilogram (less than 2 pounds), it ranks among the smallest known non-avian dinosaurs from South America [3][7]. Alnashetri belongs to the alvarezsauroids, a theropod lineage related to larger meat-eaters but best known for evolving unusually modified bodies [2]. Because this specimen preserves far more of the animal than earlier finds, researchers can see that this early, tiny alvarezsauroid had not yet developed some of the most extreme specializations seen in later members of the group [7][8]. The study was published in Nature [1][8][2].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Only two specimens of Alnashetri cerropoliciensis are known worldwide, making this newly studied complete skeleton an extraordinarily rare scientific treasure [2]

Key Points

  • Physical features: Alnashetri had a beak-like snout, short forelimbs, and long hindlimbs with claws [7]
  • Comparative material: Researchers also analyzed two additional alvarezsauroid specimens from the northern hemisphere, using them to compare anatomy across regions [8]
  • Adult miniaturization: The Patagonian fossil appears to be a fully grown individual despite weighing less than 2 pounds, suggesting the tiny size wasn’t just a juvenile stage [3]
  • Sequencing of specializations: Alnashetri’s forelimbs were less extremely modified than those of later alvarezsaurs with powerful digging claws, hinting that some hallmark specializations evolved after tiny body size appeared in parts of the lineage [7][4]

Timeline

  • 2012: First Alnashetri specimen discovered, consisting only of leg bones [2]
  • about 95 million years ago: Alnashetri cerropoliciensis lived in the Patagonian region [7][8]
  • 25 February 2026: Complete fossil study published in Nature journal [2]

Scientific Significance

  • Evolutionary paradigm shift: The new specimen supports the idea that miniaturization occurred more than once within alvarezsauroids, rather than as a single uniform event across the entire group [7].
  • Biogeographic implications: Pairing the complete Alnashetri fossil with northern-hemisphere material discussed by the authors helps narrow down when alvarezsauroids dispersed between landmasses—potentially before major continental separation—clarifying the group’s geographic history [8][1].
  • Morphological sequence: The fossil’s exceptional preservation shows that some specialized features (such as forelimb digit reduction and dental changes) were not all present in Alnashetri, supporting a mosaic pattern of evolution in which anatomical traits changed at different times [7]
