Commons sends assisted-dying bill to committee before March deadline

Category: Canada

Canada’s House of Commons voted 225-89 on Nov. 17 to send Liberal Bill C-41, which would allow advance requests for medical assistance in dying (MAID) and exclude people whose sole underlying condition is a mental illness, to committee [2][3]. The bill must pass both the Commons and Senate before March 17, when the current exclusion for people whose sole underlying condition is a mental illness expires [3][4]. The vote followed the government’s decision to limit second-reading debate, prompting Conservative and Bloc Québécois MPs to call for more scrutiny; Justice Minister Sean Fraser said debate would continue in committee [3][2]. The committee will examine the bill, while provinces would decide whether to implement advance requests through their health systems [3].

Key Points

  • Provincial choices: Quebec is the only province with an advance-request regime; Alberta has passed legislation barring advance requests for MAID [3].
  • Consent safeguards: The bill requires agreement from two independent medical or nurse practitioners and prohibits administering MAID if a person expresses refusal or resistance through words, sounds or gestures [3].
  • Advance requests: The bill would allow someone with a degenerative condition such as dementia or ALS to request MAID in advance, to be carried out after they lose the capacity to consent [3].

Perspectives

Conservative Party of Canada: The party supports excluding people whose sole condition is mental illness but urged the government to split the bill so MPs could scrutinize advance requests separately; Conservative MP Tamara Jansen said MPs still lacked the government’s analysis of possible Charter impacts [3][2].

Historical Background

Parliament has repeatedly postponed the date when mental illness could qualify for MAID and referred the question to a special committee [3]. Under existing law, MAID is available to adults with a serious and incurable physical illness, disease or disability who are in an advanced or irreversible decline and can make a voluntary request and give informed consent, including at the time MAID is administered [3].
