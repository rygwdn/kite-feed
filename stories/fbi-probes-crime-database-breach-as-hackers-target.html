FBI probes crime-database breach as hackers target USDA payment system

Category: Technology

The FBI is investigating an apparent intrusion into its National Crime Information Center (NCIC), a database used by federal, state and local law enforcement, while the U.S. Department of Agriculture separately told employees that hackers targeted a system used to pay farmers [4][14][2]. Officials have not established whether the incidents are connected or who was responsible [2][8]. The scope of the FBI incident remains unclear: it is not known whether attackers accessed sensitive NCIC records or stole information. The FBI has not publicly confirmed the incident or responded to requests for comment [3][4]. A former FBI official warned that inaccurate NCIC records could affect officers’ decisions and public safety [14]. Former USDA officials told NBC News that access to the payment system could provide a route to more sensitive food-safety data and research on animal-borne illnesses [2][5]. President Donald Trump called the reported FBI breach “not good,” then suggested it might reveal information about voter fraud [4][6]. ABC News reported that NCIC would not hold information on the Russia and Ukraine investigations Trump also invoked [24]. The FBI and USDA incidents are being described as cyberattacks, distinct from recent service outages that officials attributed to routine system upgrades and vulnerability fixes [2][4].

Officers need to know that when they run somebody through NCIC, the information coming back is accurate.

Key Points

  • ShinyHunters arrests: The FBI investigation into the September jobs-portal breach led to arrests of suspected members of the ShinyHunters group, including a Canadian cybersecurity executive reportedly linked to the attacks [3].
  • Disrupted services: A nationwide aviation halt disrupted more than 18,000 flights, while a global Microsoft outage affected hundreds of millions of users. Officials said the outages were not evidence of cyberattacks [16][2][4].
  • Cyber director: The White House fired National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross during the week of the reported breaches, as criticism mounted over outages affecting key services [2][17].

Perspectives

Retired FBI Supervisory Special Agent Jason Pack: Pack said NCIC’s nationwide links are important to understanding how an attacker might have entered the system [24].

Historical Background

In March, the FBI said it was investigating suspicious activity on an internal system containing sensitive information about surveillance operations and investigations [4]. In September, the ShinyHunters group said it had obtained data on FBI employees and applicants through the bureau’s jobs portal [4][3]. A separate 2023 incident involved a computer system used by the FBI’s New York field office in child sexual exploitation investigations, CNN previously reported [14].
