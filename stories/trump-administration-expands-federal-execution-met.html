Trump administration expands federal execution methods beyond injection

Category: World

The US Justice Department said it will again allow firing squads, electrocution, and gas asphyxiation in federal death penalty cases, while also restoring the lethal injection protocol used during President Trump's first term [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8]. The department also said it will streamline internal processes to speed death penalty cases after appeals are exhausted, casting the policy as part of the Trump administration's broader push to pursue capital punishment more aggressively in federal cases [1][4][2]. The change reverses Biden-era limits on federal executions [1][2][7]. Before leaving office in January 2025, Joe Biden commuted 37 of 40 federal death row sentences to life without parole, leaving three federal inmates still facing execution [1][2]. The move lands as capital punishment remains deeply debated in the United States, with critics continuing to warn about fairness and the risk of wrongful convictions [3].

Technical Details

  • State-law linkage: AP, as cited by RT, said a 2020 federal rule already allowed any execution method authorized in the state where a prisoner was sentenced, but the new policy now explicitly adds firing squads at the federal level [1].
  • Sentence commutation: A commutation reduces a sentence without overturning the conviction; Biden used that power to convert 37 federal death sentences into life without parole, reducing the number of inmates eligible for federal execution [1][2].

Historical Background

The federal government resumed executions in 2020 after a 17-year pause during President Trump's first term, carrying out 13 executions in six months [2][6]. In 2021, the Biden administration paused federal executions, and in late 2024 Biden commuted 37 of 40 federal death row sentences to life without parole [1][2][7]. Federal capital punishment remains available only for a limited set of crimes, even though most executions in the United States happen at the state level [2].
