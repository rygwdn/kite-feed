Saudi-led coalition intercepts Houthi missile, strikes launchers after airport attack claim

Category: World

Yemen’s Houthi movement said Monday it had struck Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport with a ballistic missile and Saudi oil-pumping stations with drones. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a missile headed for the capital and struck Houthi weapons sites in Yemen [10][3][11]. Saudi authorities did not confirm the Houthis’ claims of hitting the airport or oil facilities [7][11]. The claims followed the airport’s partial reopening on Sunday after a two-week shutdown prompted by an earlier Houthi-claimed attack. Terminals 1 and 2 resumed domestic and some international services, but flight-tracking data showed little traffic. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree warned airlines, staff and passengers that the airport remained a military target [12][22]. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said the operation destroyed four ballistic-missile launchers, a missile depot and tunnels, and targeted Houthi supply lines [11][18]. The airport closure disrupted travel and delayed Saudi Arabia’s flagship investment conference until 2027. Organizers also said the 2027 Dakar Rally would move out of the kingdom, with a new location to be announced in the coming days [10][16].

Key Points

  • Earlier attacks: The Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis launched 20 attacks on King Khalid International Airport between Oct. 1 and 11 [12][22].
  • Southern damage: Saudi Civil Defense said debris from interceptions injured two people and damaged homes and vehicles in the south [10][11].
  • Other targets: The Houthis also claimed attacks on the East-West oil pipeline, Aramco facilities in Yanbu and the Rabigh refinery; Saudi authorities did not confirm those claims [9][7].

Perspectives

Houthi movement: Military spokesman Yahya Saree said the attacks were retaliation for what the group calls Saudi aggression and would continue until the coalition ends its campaign and restrictions on goods and people entering Yemen [9].

Sources: RT

Timeline

  • 10 October 2026: Airport operations halted after a Houthi-claimed attack killed 13 [12][22].
  • 25 October 2026: Explosions rattled Riyadh, and residents received alerts about a hostile aerial threat [12][10].
  • 26 October 2026: Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed operations against Saudi troop concentrations and weapons depots in Yemen’s Aden and Lahj governorates, saying they caused dozens of casualties [3][10].

Historical Background

A United Nations-brokered truce had largely held since 2022 until fighting resumed in 2026 [7][12]. Saudi Arabia intervened in Yemen’s civil war in 2015, a year after the Houthis seized the capital, Sanaa [7]. The latest escalation followed Houthi advances along Yemen’s Red Sea coast toward the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a key shipping passage [7][22].

Humanitarian Impact

More than 250,000 people have been displaced by the renewed fighting, according to the U.N. refugee agency [27]. Pope Leo XIV also highlighted shortages of food, water and medical assistance in Taiz [16].

International Reactions

  • 🇬🇷 Greece: Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis responded favorably to Saudi Arabia’s request to keep Greece’s Patriot air-defense battery in the kingdom. The battery operates in Yanbu, a Red Sea oil-export hub [10][11].
  • The Vatican: Pope Leo XIV appealed for an end to hostilities in Yemen [16].
