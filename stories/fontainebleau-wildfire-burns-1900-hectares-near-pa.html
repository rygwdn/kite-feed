Fontainebleau wildfire burns 1,900 hectares near Paris

Category: World

A wildfire in the Fontainebleau forest southeast of Paris has burned more than 1,900 hectares, prompting evacuations around nearby communities and disrupting the A6 highway and rail traffic during a heatwave in the Paris region [8][1][4][6]. The fire began late Sunday afternoon in the former royal hunting estate, about 60 kilometers from the French capital, and officials described it as “very virulent” and of “exceptional scale” [3][6][17][20]. France sent hundreds of firefighters and water-bombing aircraft to the area, including Canadair planes, Dash aircraft and helicopters, while President Emmanuel Macron said France was mobilizing all necessary resources [1][4][37][25]. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said two people had been arrested in connection with the Fontainebleau fire and that investigators were examining possible arson after crews identified about 10 ignition points within a 1,000-meter perimeter [6][10][63][54]. The blaze is one of several fires in western Europe during France’s third heatwave of the summer, with the Paris region under the highest heat alert and Spain still searching for 10 people missing after a separate fire that killed 13 people last week [1][18][15]. Pierre Ory, the Seine-et-Marne prefect, said another fire had broken out in a different section of the forest and that shifting winds were complicating firefighting work [1][42].

Key Points

  • Air response: Officials said four Canadair aircraft, two Dash planes and three water-bombing helicopters were deployed, with 187 water drops carried out by Monday evening [37][68][54].
  • Regional first: Eric Brocardi of France’s national federation of firefighters said it was the first time firefighting planes had been sent from southern France to tackle fires in the Paris region [6][19][25].
  • National arrests: Nuñez said authorities had arrested 59 people nationwide in connection with deliberate or accidental fire-setting, including two linked to Fontainebleau [6][34][41][54].
  • Wider heat: Météo-France placed 37 departments under red heat alert Monday, while authorities said wildfires had already burned about 25,000 to 32,000 hectares in France this year [46][66][54][34].

  • Energy: Heat in France also forced three nuclear plants to pause operations to avoid releasing warm cooling water into overheated rivers, according to RTVI [36].

Historical Background

The forest is home to the Château de Fontainebleau, a residence associated with French monarchs and Napoleon [1][25][common]. French archives cited by Digi24 identify the current fire as worse than major Fontainebleau blazes in 1921, when 762 hectares burned, and 1945, when 825 hectares burned [41].

Humanitarian Impact

Around 1,000 people in and around Fontainebleau were evacuated, and sources reported no injuries or only limited material damage as of Monday evening [37][54][34][45]. Urgent needs centered on evacuation shelter, smoke protection and keeping roads clear for emergency vehicles [17][40][32].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Officials evacuated communities near the forest and emergency services urged residents to avoid smoke exposure [32][17][40]. Avoid the Fontainebleau forest and follow evacuation instructions.
  • Transport: The A6 highway and rail services around Gare de Lyon and TGV routes experienced closures or disruption because of the fires [1][15][66]. Check road and rail status before travel south or southeast of Paris.
  • Heat: France placed dozens of departments under red or orange heat alerts as temperatures exceeded 40 C in western and central areas and reached about 37 C in Paris [21][66][46]. Limit outdoor activity during red heat alerts.

Action Items

  • Check transport changes before traveling: Consult SNCF and highway updates for routes using Gare de Lyon, TGV Sud-Est or the A6 corridor, which were affected by fire-related disruption [1][66].
  • Avoid smoke exposure near affected villages: Stay indoors, close windows and leave if authorities order evacuation; local reporting described smoke as difficult to breathe near forest-edge communities [32][40].
