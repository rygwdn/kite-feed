Physics survey and supernova data challenge cosmology model

Category: Science

A global survey described as the largest ever of physicists found little consensus on major questions in fundamental physics, including black holes, dark matter and efforts to reconcile Einstein’s gravity with quantum mechanics [1][4]. The standard ΛCDM cosmological model did not receive majority support in the survey, according to the SciTechDaily account of work led by Niayesh Afshordi, Phil Harper and the American Physical Society’s Physics Magazine [1]. Separately, a University of Queensland-led effort compiled information on 2,884 Type Ia supernovae used to measure cosmic distances, creating what Phys.org called the most comprehensive catalog of such exploding white dwarf stars assembled to date [5][2][3]. Ryan Camilleri and colleagues combined the supernova data with cosmic microwave background measurements and galaxy-mapping data, and Camilleri said the results add evidence that dark energy may change over time rather than stay fixed as assumed in the standard cosmological constant model [2].

Key Points

  • Model pressure: SciTechDaily linked the ΛCDM survey result in part to 2025 Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument findings that suggested dark energy could vary over time [1].
  • Catalog scope: The supernova dataset combines historic measurements with Dark Energy Survey data published in 2024 and reanalyzes older observations under a single framework [2].
  • Statistical caution: The team found the data slightly favor a changing dark energy model, but the statistical significance is not yet high enough to claim a discovery [2].
