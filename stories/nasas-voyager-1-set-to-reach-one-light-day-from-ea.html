NASA's Voyager 1 set to reach one light-day from Earth

Category: Science

NASA’s Voyager 1 is expected to become the first human-made object to reach a distance of one light-day from Earth on Nov. 18, when its radio signals will take 24 hours to reach the planet from more than 26 billion kilometers away [2][4][9]. Launched in 1977, the probe still sends science and engineering data from interstellar space. Controllers completed a power-saving measure in September, then briefly lost contact with Voyager 1 in October. NASA is still investigating the communication disruption [2][7]. The twin Voyagers measure magnetic fields, surrounding pressure, dust and galactic cosmic rays, with data arriving almost daily [2][4]. NASA estimates Voyager 1 will need another 18,000 years to travel a light-year and more than twice as long to approach another star [2]. To mark the milestone, NASA has invited people to flash “VGR” in Morse code after sunset on Nov. 17 [2][6].

It's a national treasure.

Key Points

  • Voyager 2: Voyager 2, launched two weeks after its twin, is not expected to reach the same one-light-day distance until 2035 [2][4].
  • Mission operations: NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory flight-control team sends commands about once a week and sometimes brings in retired project staff when problems arise [2][9].
  • Golden Record: Both spacecraft carry a 30-centimeter gold-plated record with greetings in 55 languages, images and diagrams [2][4].
  • Planetary flybys: Voyager 1’s Jupiter and Saturn encounters revealed two previously unknown Jovian moons and five new moons around Saturn [8].

Perspectives

Alan Cummings, an astrophysicist involved with the Voyager program, described the distance record as a technical achievement, citing Voyager 1’s longevity and the continued ability to communicate with it across an immense distance.

Sources: BNR

Bill Nye of the Planetary Society said everyone on Earth has been touched by the Voyager missions.

Timeline

  • 5 September 1977: Voyager 1 launched from Cape Canaveral aboard a Titan IIE-Centaur rocket [8]
  • 1979-1980: Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter and Saturn, returning images and discoveries [2][4]
  • 2012: Voyager 1 entered interstellar space, as NASA defines the region [2][4]
  • 2018: Voyager 2 entered interstellar space after exploring Uranus and Neptune [2][4]
  • 18 November 2026: Voyager 1 is due to reach one light-day from Earth [2][4]

Scientific Significance

  • Observation limits: Voyager 1’s cameras were shut down decades ago after it captured the “Pale Blue Dot” image [3][2].
