Alberta cuts new oilsands royalties to attract Pacific Link investment

Category: Canada

Alberta will offer qualifying new oilsands projects and expansions a 10-year royalty schedule starting at 2% to attract investment to support the proposed Pacific Link pipeline to the West Coast. The rate stays at 2% for five years, then rises by one percentage point annually to 6% in years nine and 10, before projects move to the province’s standard royalty system [3]. The Oil Sands Growth Program is scheduled to launch in spring 2027. The government says the incentive supports a goal of doubling production to 8 million barrels a day and could generate about $205 billion in additional cumulative revenue by 2040. It has not estimated the royalties it would forgo [3]. Finance Minister Jason Nixon said Alberta expects the changes to attract new investment [4].

Primary Source: View original article

Since the program’s deadline is set for the end of 2029, it will incentivize companies to make meaningful multi-billion-dollar investments in the oilsands and begin construction before that date

— Alberta Premier Danielle Smith (The Hub)

Key Points

  • Revenue exposure: Resource royalties have recently generated about $20 billion a year, roughly a quarter of Alberta’s revenue, while a $1 change in oil prices or the Canadian heavy-crude discount shifts provincial revenue by about $750 million [3].
  • Existing rates: Under the current system, pre-payout oilsands royalties range from 1% to 9% based partly on oil prices, then move to a higher tier after companies recover capital costs [3].

Perspectives

Pembina Institute: Oil and gas program director Janetta McKenzie said Alberta has not shown that the existing royalty system deters investment and argued that the province is asking taxpayers to subsidize production for a publicly backed pipeline [3][1].

Sources: The Hub

Richard Masson, former Alberta oilsands policy director: Masson said lower royalties in the early years make it easier for companies to recover cash after major capital spending and that projects brought forward by the incentive could increase the overall economic return [3].
