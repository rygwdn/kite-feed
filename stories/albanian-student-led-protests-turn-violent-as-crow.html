Albanian student-led protests turn violent as crowds demand Rama’s resignation

Category: World

Clashes between protesters and police entered a fifth day in Tirana on Saturday, Oct. 17, as students joined a widening movement demanding Prime Minister Edi Rama’s resignation. Police used tear gas, water cannon and, for the first time during the protests, rubber bullets. Demonstrators threw rocks and petrol bombs, set fires and attacked public buildings. Police Chief Skender Hita said 167 people had been arrested, 388 faced criminal proceedings and 132 officers had been injured over five days [3][8][7]. The demonstrations began with student complaints about university conditions and high tuition, then broadened to anger over corruption, living costs and job prospects. Months of protests against a planned luxury resort on Albania’s southern coast also fed the movement. The development is linked to an investment firm founded by President Donald Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner; Reuters said it found no evidence of wrongdoing by Kushner [11][15]. Rama has rejected calls to resign, accused opposition figures of inciting violence and said students’ right to protest must be protected. Seven officers were suspended pending investigations into alleged excessive force. Protests also spread to other Albanian cities [13][12].

Key Points

  • Police scrutiny: Albanian law permits rubber bullets at protests only in extreme cases [8][25].
  • Student demands: Journalist Osman Stafa proposed a neutral, one-year caretaker government to organize elections [7].
  • Opposition role: Democratic Party leader Sali Berisha backed the protests [6].
  • Political pressure: Rama acknowledged knowing businessman Ergys Agasi but denied knowledge of illegal transactions [7].
  • Outside Tirana: Police made arrests in Lezha and Durrës; in Durrës, protesters damaged a post office and blocked roads [22][12].

Perspectives

Prime Minister Edi Rama: Rama warned that masked individuals vandalizing businesses were committing crimes and would be held accountable [12][13].

Historical Background

Villagers told Reuters their land had been wrongfully sold for the coastal resort development [15]. A special prosecution service established in 2019 under European Union and U.S. pressure has pursued separate cases involving senior officials. It recently arrested secret service chief Vlora Hyseni for allegedly assisting businessman Ergys Agasi, who denies the allegations [7].

Humanitarian Impact

Albania’s Health Ministry said nine protesters and four police officers received medical treatment after clashes on Saturday; a separate police tally said 132 officers had been injured during the five days of unrest [8][3].
