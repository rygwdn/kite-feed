Musk denies SpaceX AI handset prototype report

Category: Technology

The Wall Street Journal reported that SpaceX showed selected investors an early prototype of a slim, handset-like AI device before the company's initial public offering. Accounts citing the Journal described it as thinner than an iPhone and built around a proprietary operating system, xAI technology and Qualcomm Snapdragon silicon [1][2][4][5][7][9][10][16][20][18]. Elon Musk denied the report on X, calling it "Utterly false," wording cited by several publications [2][4][9][10][20]. Sources citing the Journal said the prototype remained at an early stage, its design could change and no launch was guaranteed; articles also said no public details were available on pricing, features or a release timeline [4][7][10][20].

Primary Source: View original article

Utterly false

— Elon Musk (Tom's Hardware)

Key Points

  • Prior denials: Musk also denied earlier reports of a Starlink-based phone in February and said publicly in October that "the idea of making a phone makes me want to die" [4][9][10].
  • Analyst speculation: An industry analyst said SpaceX could acquire T-Mobile or AT&T to strengthen its wireless push, though such a deal would be expensive [1].
  • Investor sentiment: Futurism said SpaceX showed the AI prototype to investors ahead of the company's IPO, which some described as underwhelming, potentially to sustain investor interest [5].

Perspectives

Elon Musk: Musk rejected the reported prototype in a brief X post rather than providing a detailed explanation of what SpaceX is or is not developing.

Sources: Tom's Hardware

TechRepublic: TechRepublic framed the reported SpaceX-xAI link as a platform-risk issue for IT teams that depend on Starlink or may need to monitor vendor lock-in across Musk-controlled services.

Sources: TechRepublic

Bug.hr: Bug.hr emphasized that the Wall Street Journal was relying on unnamed sources and explained that such reporting typically depends on corroboration by at least two independent sources before publication.

Sources: Bug.hr

Historical Background

Musk bought Twitter in 2022 and later renamed it X, a platform he has linked to his interest in an "everything app" model that combines communications, payments and services [20][21]. In 2022, he said he would develop a phone if X were removed from Apple's App Store, and later said he would consider one if Apple or Google took actions he viewed as censorship [17].
