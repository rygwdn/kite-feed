South Korea’s Kospi jumps a record 18% on chip rally

Category: Business

South Korea’s benchmark Kospi index closed 17.91% higher at 6,595.45 on July 31, adding 1,001.89 points for the largest one-day gain in its history and reclaiming the 6,500-point level [10][13][1]. The rebound followed a three-session drop of more than 17% tied largely to concerns about heavy artificial intelligence infrastructure spending and weakness in chipmakers and other AI-related stocks [4][13]. Semiconductor shares led the rally: SK Hynix gained nearly 30% in Seoul, while Samsung Electronics rose 28% [1][15]. The advance followed Microsoft’s stronger-than-expected quarterly results, including sales and cloud-computing growth that beat forecasts, while Amazon and Microsoft earnings updates helped revive demand for AI-linked shares [4][1][13]. South Korea’s regulator announced measures aimed at curbing the week’s sell-off, and trading remained volatile after the Kospi had more than doubled earlier in 2026 before a series of sharp declines since a mid-June record high [1]. Yonhap reported that the local currency rose sharply against the U.S. dollar during Friday’s session [10][13].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: LSEG data cited by Cnyes showed that three of the Kospi’s four biggest single-day drops of the past decade occurred within the most recent five weeks [12][20].

Key Points

  • Buyer mix: Foreign investors bought a net 7.22 trillion won of Korean shares and institutions bought a net 1.15 trillion won, according to Yonhap [10][13].
  • Wall Street lead: Before Seoul’s rally, the S&P 500 rose 1.66%, the Nasdaq gained 2.78% and the PHLX semiconductor index jumped 8.2% [19].
  • ADR divergence: SK Hynix’s Seoul-listed shares rose 30% to 1.718 million won, while its U.S. ADR fell 3.6% to $143.69 [20].

Perspectives

Barron's: Barron's said weakness in SK Hynix’s ADR did not necessarily mean investors should avoid the stock, while noting the ADR’s premium and short-term chasing risk.

Sources: Cnyes

Bankier.pl: Bankier.pl reported that retail investors were not among the main buyers during the rebound and used the session to sell shares.

Sources: Bankier.pl

Timeline

  • June 2026: The Kospi hit a record high before later sharp declines [1].
  • 2026-07-28 to 2026-07-30: The Kospi shed more than 17% over three sessions [13].
  • 30 July 2026: Microsoft reported earnings, with sales and cloud growth beating forecasts [13].
  • 31 July 2026: The Kospi closed up 17.91% at 6,595.45 [13].

Industry Impact

  • Semiconductors: SK Hynix is a major supplier to Nvidia, and Samsung and SK Hynix sit at the center of the global memory market tied to AI infrastructure demand [1][9].
  • Asian equities: The semiconductor rebound spread beyond South Korea, with Japan’s Nikkei 225 up 4.03% and Taiwan’s TAIEX up 7.98% [15].
  • Retail trading: Cnyes reported that South Korean retail investors have increasingly used leveraged ETFs tied to SK Hynix and other technology shares, increasing exposure to amplified daily losses and rebalancing costs [12][20].

Historical Background

The July 31 advance topped the Kospi’s previous record one-day gain of 11.95%, set on Oct. 30, 2008 [10][13]. South Korea’s tech-heavy benchmark triggered circuit breakers multiple times in 2026; the mechanism pauses trading during extreme market moves [1]. Even after several sharp falls since mid-June, the index remained more than 50% above its level at the end of 2025 [1].

Business Angle

  • The rally improved sentiment toward AI suppliers, but valuations depend on sustained profits from AI infrastructure demand rather than one-day index moves [4][1][common].
  • Extreme volatility and leveraged retail products can force buying or selling that disconnects prices from business fundamentals [12][20][common].
  • ADR investors should compare the U.S. price with the Seoul ordinary share because conversion friction and premiums can affect the margin of safety [20][common].

Action Items

  • Check SK Hynix ADR pricing before trading: Compare the ADR price with the Seoul ordinary share using the reported 10-ADR-to-one-share ratio and the current dollar-won exchange rate before placing an order [20].
  • Review leveraged ETF exposure: Look for funds that use derivatives to amplify daily moves, because Cnyes reported that daily rebalancing and larger recovery hurdles can erode returns after losses [12][20].
