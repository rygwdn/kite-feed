Scientists identify 210-million-year-old dinosaur in Zimbabwe

Category: Science

An international team of paleontologists identified a new Late Triassic dinosaur species, Musango matusadonaensis, from fossils excavated in 2018 near Musango Island on the shore of Lake Kariba in northern Zimbabwe [1][3][4][5][8]. The animal lived about 210 million years ago, measured about 4.5 meters, weighed an estimated 222 kilograms and is the fifth dinosaur species identified from Zimbabwe [1][3][4][5][6][8]. Researchers said the find challenges earlier assumptions that Late Triassic dinosaurs across southern Africa were broadly similar and points instead to several distinct ecosystems with different dinosaur species [1][3][4][5][8]. The species name uses the Shona word “musango,” meaning “living in the bush,” and the team’s findings were published in the Journal of Systematic Palaeontology [3][4][5][6][8].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Fossil material: The partial skeleton includes vertebrae, limb bones and pieces of the hip and shoulder girdles from the Pebbly Arkose Formation [1].
  • Evolutionary placement: The team classified Musango matusadonaensis as an early sauropodomorph in Unaysauridae, close to Musankwa sanyatiensis from the same broad region [1].
  • Specimen condition: Researchers estimated the individual died at about eight years old, was nearly fully grown and had recovered from a serious injury or infection [8].
  • Diet inference: Although no skull has been found, researchers inferred it was likely herbivorous or omnivorous and fed on plants along rivers and streams [8].

Perspectives

Jonah Choiniere, Evolutionary Studies Institute: Choiniere said the discovery shows the value of paleontological fieldwork in remote, hard-to-access places.

Sources: Sci.News

Research team: Researchers said Zimbabwe’s fossil record remains largely unexplored, making the new species part of a still-developing picture of the country’s prehistoric fauna.

Sources: The Eastleigh Voice

Scientific Significance

  • Evidence quality: Researchers described the specimen as among the most complete dinosaur skeletons of its age found in Zimbabwe, but the preserved material is partial and does not include a skull [3][4][8].
  • Future work: Researchers said Zimbabwe’s fossil record remains largely unexplored; further fieldwork could test whether the apparent ecosystem separation reflects real prehistoric diversity or current sampling gaps [6].

Historical Background

The Late Triassic, about 237 million to 201 million years ago, was when dinosaurs spread but had not yet become the dominant land animals [common][3][4][5][8].
