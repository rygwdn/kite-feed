Google launches official YouTube app for Vision Pro

Category: Technology

Google has released an official YouTube app for Apple’s Vision Pro headset, giving visionOS users a dedicated way to watch YouTube without relying on Safari’s web player or third-party clients [1][4][8][12][5]. The app supports YouTube’s immersive formats (including 180°/360° and 3D-style playback) and offers a full signed-in experience with access to subscriptions, playlists, and watch history [1][4][3][8]. The launch fills a long-standing gap in Vision Pro’s app lineup since the headset debuted in 2024—an especially noticeable omission for a device built around video and spatial experiences [4][3][8].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: MacRumors says the YouTube app can play videos in 8K on Apple Vision Pro models with the M5 chip [1].

Key Points

  • App quality: Early hands-on coverage calls the app simple and familiar, broadly resembling YouTube on iPad, with a few spatial touches like a floating sidebar and controls [2].
  • Where it shines: AppleInsider says the native app is at its best with user-uploaded VR180 and 3D/360 content, while noting some people may still prefer Safari or other apps for standard 2D viewing [2].
  • Shorts interface: Ars Technica points out that YouTube’s listing and screenshots show a dedicated Shorts interface alongside support for other formats [4].
  • Platform dynamics: Engadget frames the release as a sign Google is taking Vision Pro more seriously as it pushes into Android XR, and notes that other big streaming apps—like Netflix—still don’t offer a dedicated Vision Pro app [8].

Perspectives

AppleInsider (hands-on): The reviewer calls it a “bare-minimum” native app, but still a clear step up from having no official option at all—and says immersive formats benefit the most compared with 2D viewing [2].

Sources: AppleInsider

Ars Technica: The outlet highlights the two-year wait since Vision Pro’s debut and says this is more than a simple iPad port, pointing to spatial panels and support for 3D plus 360/180 videos [4].

Sources: Ars Technica

Engadget: Engadget argues a dedicated YouTube app makes visionOS feel more complete than using YouTube through Safari, and points to Google’s broader XR push as context for the timing [8].

Sources: Engadget

MacRumors: MacRumors focuses on features and compatibility, saying the app includes a full signed-in experience and supports multiple video formats, including 8K playback on M5-based models [1].

Sources: MacRumors

Timeline

  • February 2024: Vision Pro launches without a dedicated YouTube app [4][3].
  • 2024: Third-party "Juno" YouTube client removed from visionOS App Store [1][3].
  • February 12, 2026: Google releases an official YouTube app for Apple Vision Pro [1][8][12][5].

Historical Background

Apple launched Vision Pro in February 2024, and the lack of a dedicated YouTube app quickly became a frequent complaint, given YouTube’s library of immersive VR and 360 videos [4][3]. Ars Technica notes the gripe continued even after Apple released a second-generation Vision Pro last year [4]. Multiple outlets also point to the third-party “Juno” YouTube client, which appeared on visionOS in 2024 before Apple removed it from the App Store after it was deemed to violate YouTube’s Terms of Service [1][3][4].

Action Items

  • Install the official YouTube app on Vision Pro: Open the visionOS App Store on Apple Vision Pro, search for YouTube, and download the free official app [1][3].
  • Start with immersive formats: Seek out 180°/360°/3D or VR180 uploads to better match the headset’s strengths [1][2][8].
  • Compare 2D playback options: If you mostly watch standard videos, try the same video in the YouTube app and in Safari to see which layout and controls you prefer [2][1].
