Amazon replaces Fire tablets with Android Alexa lineup starting at $230

Category: Technology

Amazon introduced three Alexa-branded tablets on Oct. 8, replacing its Fire tablet line with Android devices that include Google Play access and Alexa+ [5][3]. Prices start at $229.99 for the 8.7-inch Tablet 8, $329.99 for the 11-inch Tablet 11 and $499.99 for the 12-inch Tablet 12 Pro. The tablets are scheduled to begin shipping Oct. 14 in the United States, Canada and Mexico [10][5]. The flagship has a 120Hz, 2.8K display, a MediaTek Dimensity 8400 processor, Wi-Fi 6E and up to 16GB of RAM. Amazon also offers an anti-glare Nanomatte version, with a keyboard and stylus sold separately [14][5]. The smaller models have 90Hz and 60Hz displays, respectively, and use a MediaTek 8189 processor [6]. Alexa+ is integrated across the line, with an optional On-Screen Intelligence feature that can interpret displayed content and help with tasks. Amazon says screen access is off by default and an indicator appears when it is active [10][5]. The tablets ship with Android 16 and are due to receive Android 17 in early 2027 [12].

Key Points

  • Reading mode: Kindle mode silences notifications, reduces blue light and applies battery optimizations; Amazon quotes up to 20 hours of reading on the 11-inch and Pro models [24][5].
  • Kids models: The 8.7- and 11-inch Kids Tablets include protective cases and one year of Amazon Kids+; parents must approve Google Play downloads and can manage screen time remotely [29][25].
  • Alexa access: Tablet-specific Alexa+ is included, but using the full assistant across compatible Alexa devices requires Prime membership or a separate subscription [5].
  • Accessory costs: The Pro Keyboard costs $149.99 and the Pro Pen $89.99, bringing a Nanomatte 12 Pro with both accessories to nearly $790 [10].
  • Ad removal: The Tablet 8 and Tablet 11 listings offer a $20 option to remove lock-screen ads [20].

Technical Details

  • Nanomatte display: Amazon and Corning developed a precision-etched glass surface intended to reduce glare while retaining detail and color accuracy; the option is available on the Pro model [2][5].
  • Pro stylus: The Pro Pen supports 4,096 pressure levels, attaches magnetically and charges wirelessly [14][5].

Industry Impact

  • The new entry model costs more than twice as much as the previous-generation Fire HD 8, which sold for around $100 [4].

Historical Background

Earlier Fire tablets ran Fire OS, an Android fork, and relied on Amazon’s Appstore rather than Google Play, restricting direct access to Google services and apps [15][22]. Amazon built those devices around low prices and its own Kindle, Prime Video and Amazon Music services [4].

Business Angle

  • Amazon’s tablets retain services such as Kindle, Prime Video and Amazon Music alongside third-party apps [4].
