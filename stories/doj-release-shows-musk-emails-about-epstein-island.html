DOJ release shows Musk emails about Epstein island

Category: Technology

A new Department of Justice release of Epstein-related documents includes email exchanges from 2012–2014 that multiple outlets say show Elon Musk seeking to arrange visits to Jeffrey Epstein’s private island, including a message asking, “What day/night will be the wildest party on your island?” [1][2][3][7][5][6]. Several reports emphasize that the emails appear to contradict Musk’s past public statements that he declined Epstein’s island invitations [3][6][7]. Beyond the personal scandal, the story matters to the technology world because Musk leads and influences major tech platforms and companies; the reporting frames the disclosure as a reputational and trust issue for a high-profile tech executive whose public claims are frequently scrutinized [3][7][4]. The documents also include references to potential business-related contact, with Epstein asking about electrifying properties via SolarCity, according to 404 Media [7].

Key Points

  • Holiday travel context: One email thread describes Musk saying he would be in the “BVI/St Bart’s area over the holidays” and asking Epstein about timing for a visit, with Epstein replying “Anytime” and offering to “send heli” [6][7].
  • Prior denials: Reports note Musk previously told Vanity Fair in 2019 that he visited Epstein’s Manhattan home once for about half an hour and said he declined repeated island invitations, a claim the newly released emails are described as contradicting [6][3].
  • 2014 references: Outlets link the emails to broader reporting that Musk was listed on Epstein’s calendar as being slated to visit the island in 2014, and they describe additional 2014-era planning messages [7][5].
  • SolarCity mention: According to 404 Media, Epstein asked whether SolarCity—Musk’s solar company later folded into Tesla—could electrify Epstein’s island or his New Mexico ranch [7].
  • Media framing: Gizmodo argues the emails’ plain meaning is already damaging and warns against attempts to “misinterpret” them into something more favorable for Musk [4].
