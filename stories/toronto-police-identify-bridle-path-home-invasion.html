Toronto police identify Bridle Path home invasion suspect, issue Canada-wide warrant

Category: Canada

Toronto police have identified Daijohn Richards, 23, as one of three suspects in a violent Bridle Path home invasion and issued a Canada-wide warrant for his arrest. Police say 119 home invasions have been reported in Toronto so far this year, more than 32% above the same period in 2025 [10][1]. Investigators believe an accomplice accidentally shot Richards in the right shoulder during the break-in. Police spoke with him after he went to a hospital on Thursday but say they had not yet established grounds to arrest him. Investigators connected him to the case on Friday evening. Richards remains at large, and police are searching for two other suspects. Police consider all three armed and dangerous [4][2][6].

Primary Source: View original article

What we need to make sure is that we ironclad a conviction and we don’t just go off of suspicion

— Inspector Kristy Smith, Toronto police (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Charges sought: Richards is wanted on four counts of discharging a firearm with intent to wound, four counts of robbery with a firearm, three counts of aggravated assault and additional charges [4][6].
  • Motive assessment: Police said they have found no evidence that the family was specifically targeted and believe the suspects were seeking valuables in an affluent neighbourhood [2][9].
  • Public warning: Police describe the two unidentified suspects as men believed to be in their 20s who were wearing dark clothing. They urge the public not to approach any of the suspects [2][1].
  • Home protection: Inspector Kristy Smith said the law permits people to use force to protect a home in dangerous situations, but police advise calling officers instead when possible [2].

Perspectives

David Morton and family: The family said it was deeply concerned about violence connected with Toronto home invasions and urged anyone with information to help identify those responsible; the family also asked for privacy while recovering.

Sources: CP24

Toronto Police Chief Myron Demkiw: Demkiw said he was assigning additional resources to address the increase in home invasions and that residents should expect an increased police presence in the neighbourhood.
