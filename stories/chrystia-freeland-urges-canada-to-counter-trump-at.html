Chrystia Freeland urges Canada to counter Trump attacks

Category: Canada

Chrystia Freeland, Canada’s former deputy prime minister under Justin Trudeau, sharply criticized President Trump’s rhetoric toward Canada in a New York Times guest essay published Tuesday and urged Ottawa to respond. National Post said her rebuke cast Trump as the “bully in chief” and said “Canada must fight back” [1][5]. Freeland resigned her seat in Parliament in January and this month became warden of Rhodes House and chief executive officer of the Rhodes Trust, while continuing to write and comment on economics and geopolitics [1]. Freeland linked Trump’s attacks to the old “Blame Canada” joke from the 1999 South Park movie and argued that the idea had moved from satire into U.S. politics [1][5]. Alternet said Freeland called Trump’s trade-war approach toward Canada “irrational” and “truly absurd,” pointing to the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement negotiated during Trump’s first administration and praised by him as “the best trade deal ever” [6]. Canada’s National Observer reported that Trump has repeatedly called Canada the “51st state,” referred to Prime Minister Mark Carney as a “governor,” and threatened tariffs connected to wildfire smoke drifting into the United States [3]. The article cited University of Kansas rhetoric professor Robert C. Rowland saying Trump has used Canada as a scapegoat while his poll numbers fell under controversial policies and an unpopular war with Iran [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Freeland’s New York Times essay title echoed a line from a Leonard Cohen poem written in 2003 and published after his death in 2018 [1].

Key Points

  • Republican rhetoric: National Observer reported that anti-Canada messaging has spread among rank-and-file Republicans in recent weeks, including Michigan state senator Aric Nesbitt’s July 16 post telling Canada to “learn to manage your forests” [3].
  • Wildfire dispute: Michigan Rep. John James posted a “FINAL WARNING TO CANADA” on July 16 after wildfire smoke moved south, saying “sovereignty comes with responsibility” [3].
  • Media work: Freeland has continued public commentary through Bloomberg News and Wall Street Week after leaving Parliament, focusing on “the intersection of economics and geopolitics” [1].
