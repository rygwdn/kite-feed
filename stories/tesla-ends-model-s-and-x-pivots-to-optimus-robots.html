Tesla ends Model S and X, pivots to Optimus robots

Category: Technology

Tesla CEO Elon Musk said the company will wind down and “basically stop” production of the Model S and Model X, ending its two longest-running premium EV lines as Tesla shifts its emphasis toward autonomy and humanoid robotics [10][7][4][3][16][18]. Musk said Tesla will continue supporting existing owners “for as long as people have the vehicles,” while encouraging would-be buyers to order soon while inventory lasts [3][10][18]. A major operational change underpins the pivot: Tesla plans to convert production space at its Fremont, California factory from building these vehicles to manufacturing its Optimus humanoid robots [1][10]. The shift comes as Tesla reported weaker financial performance and slowing vehicle momentum, with several outlets noting declines in revenue, profits, and deliveries alongside the strategic reframing of Tesla as a more AI- and robotics-driven company [6][13][16][5][15][14].

Primary Source: View original article

It’s time to bring the S and X programs to an end with an honorable discharge

— Elon Musk (Engadget)

Did you know: Tesla reports Model S and Model X sales together in an “other models” category, and Mashable said that bucket totaled 11,642 deliveries in the last quarter of 2025 [18].

Key Points

  • Optimus scale: Musk told investors Tesla’s long-term goal is to build about 1 million Optimus robots using the production space being freed up [10].
  • Public sales: Musk said at the World Economic Forum in Davos that Tesla expects to start selling Optimus to the public by the end of next year [10].
  • China headwind: Engadget noted Tesla stopped selling the Model S and X in China in mid-2025 because they were imported from the US and subject to tariffs imposed in response to US President Donald Trump’s tariffs on imported goods [10].
  • XAI investment: The New York Times reported Tesla also disclosed plans to invest $2 billion in xAI, the AI company controlled by Musk [6].
  • Delivery mix: Tesla’s Model 3 and Model Y dominated recent sales, with 1,585,279 deliveries in 2025 versus 418,227 for Model S/X, Engadget reported [10].

Perspectives

Elon Musk / Tesla leadership: Tesla framed the move as part of a shift to an autonomy-first future and repurposing Fremont capacity toward Optimus robot manufacturing, while pledging continued support for existing Model S/X owners.

Sources: The New York Times

Sources: Electrek

Tech press observers: Ars Technica emphasized that Tesla’s financial results were weak and portrayed the discontinuation as shrinking an already aging lineup as Musk prioritizes autonomy and humanoid robots.

Mainstream business media: BBC reported Tesla’s annual revenue fell for the first time and linked the model cuts to a broader shift toward robots and AI.

Timeline

  • Mid-2025: Tesla stopped selling Model S/X in China amid tariffs [10].
  • Wednesday: Musk announced S/X production wind-down on earnings call [7][10].
  • Next quarter: Tesla expects to wind down Model S/X production [1][18].
  • Second quarter of 2026: Tesla plans to stop making Model S and X [7].
  • End of next year: Musk said Optimus could go on sale publicly [10].

Technical Details

  • Optimus humanoid robot: Optimus is Tesla’s humanoid robot program, and Musk said Tesla will repurpose Fremont’s S/X capacity toward building it at scale [10][1].
  • Factory retooling: Tesla plans to convert production space at its Fremont plant from Model S/X vehicle manufacturing to Optimus robot manufacturing, signaling a shift in capital and engineering resources [1][10].
  • Autonomy-first strategy: Musk positioned the discontinuation as part of a broader move toward autonomy and robotaxi-style services rather than expanding Tesla’s traditional vehicle lineup [10][8][9].

Industry Impact

  • Premium EV segment: By ending the Model S and X, Tesla cedes more of the high-end EV sedan/SUV battlefield to rivals, while concentrating its remaining vehicle volume in the Model 3/Y lines [10][17].
  • Robotics supply chain: Shifting Fremont capacity to Optimus could redirect procurement toward actuators, sensors, and AI compute components typically associated with robotics rather than traditional vehicle trim and powertrain parts [1][13].
  • Autonomy competition: Tesla’s renewed focus on autonomy and robotaxi-style services positions it more directly against dedicated autonomous-ride firms, a dynamic Ars Technica highlighted when describing Musk’s long-standing robotaxi narrative [12].
  • Policy sensitivity: The China sales halt for imported S/X models amid retaliatory tariffs underscores how trade policy decisions under President Trump can affect cross-border product strategy and model availability [10].

Historical Background

The Model S launched in 2012 and the Model X in 2015, helping establish Tesla in the premium EV market before the lower-priced Model 3 and Model Y became the company’s volume sellers [17][10][9]. Multiple outlets describe Tesla’s latest move as a continuation of Musk’s longer-running shift in focus from traditional car manufacturing toward autonomy, robotaxis, and humanoid robotics [12][15][13].

Q&A

Q: What happens to Model S and X service once production ends?

A: Musk said Tesla will continue supporting existing owners “for as long as people have the vehicles” [3][10].

Q: Why did Tesla lump the Model S and X into a single reporting bucket?

A: Tesla reports them in an “other models” category in its sales reporting, and Mashable noted the small recent volume in that bucket [18].

Q: What is “transportation as a service” in Tesla’s framing?

A: Electrek described Tesla’s stated pivot toward “transportation as a service” as part of its autonomy-centered strategy rather than prioritizing new consumer car models [8].
