Trump threatens tax on overseas AI data centers as chip curbs tighten

Category: Business

President Donald Trump threatened to impose a “tremendous tax” on U.S. companies that build artificial intelligence data centers abroad, saying he wants computing capacity concentrated in the United States to keep pace with China. He offered no details on how the tax would work and said he had not yet told his advisers about the proposal [12]. Separately, the Commerce Department issued new licensing requirements targeting Chinese access to advanced U.S. chips, including remote access through overseas servers [3][8]. U.S.-based infrastructure also delivers Chinese AI models to global customers. Some overseas data centers help companies comply with local data-storage rules and reduce transmission delays [3][12]. Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping are due to meet at the G20 summit in December; the countries’ trade truce is set to expire Jan. 10, 2027 [8].

China can hit back, and they can hit back hard.

— Han Lin, China country director for The Asia Group (CNN)

Key Points

  • U.S. infrastructure concentration: Nvidia, Google, AMD and Amazon design nearly all deployed data-center AI chips, while Amazon, Microsoft and Google together control more than half of cloud services [3].
  • China’s alternatives: Huawei is developing an Atlas 950 SuperPoD designed to link as many as 8,192 Ascend chips; it demonstrated a 1,024-chip network in July [11].
  • Local opposition: U.S. communities have raised concerns about data centers’ electricity demand, utility bills and water use. A bill passed by the Senate would require large facilities to cover their own power costs; it still needs House approval [12].
  • Industrial policy: China’s 2026-2030 Five-Year Plan prioritizes technological self-reliance, advanced manufacturing and wider AI adoption [5].

Perspectives

President Donald Trump: He argued that U.S. companies should build data centers at home rather than overseas and suggested firms could negotiate with local communities over electricity costs, tax breaks and other benefits [12].

Sources: eToday

Kyle Chan, Brookings Institution: Chan said Chinese firms can draw on shared industrial expertise and supply networks, but government priorities for expanding capacity may conflict with companies’ commercial returns [5].

  • 20 November 2026: Trump threatened a large tax on firms building data centers abroad [12]

Historical Background

The United States introduced restrictions on exports of advanced semiconductors and chipmaking equipment to China in 2023, citing national security concerns [7]. In October 2025, President Trump and Xi Jinping agreed to reduce tariffs and roll back some export controls [1][8].

Business Angle

  • Moonshot AI reportedly requires companies with more than $20 million in annual sales that serve its Kimi K3 model to negotiate a license. It has sought 30% of Kimi K3 revenue from Microsoft, Amazon and Google [3].
  • Cloud hosting can offer enterprise buyers privacy, uptime and technical-support guarantees, while self-hosting makes a company’s own IT staff responsible for cybersecurity and maintenance [6].

International Reactions

  • 🇨🇳 China: China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs called the U.S. chip restrictions economic coercion, while the Ministry of Commerce warned it would respond if Washington persisted [8].
