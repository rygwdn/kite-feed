US completes Iraq troop withdrawal, ending 12-year ISIS mission

Category: World

The U.S. military said on Sept. 30 that all American forces and equipment had left Erbil Air Base, ending the U.S.-led Operation Inherent Resolve mission in Iraq. The withdrawal fulfilled a 2024 agreement between Baghdad and Washington and ended the 12-year campaign against Islamic State. It also ended a U.S. military presence that began with the 2003 invasion, except for a gap from 2011 to 2014 [12][5][14][50]. Iraqi leaders presented the withdrawal as a sovereignty milestone, while some residents and officials in the Kurdish region voiced concern about threats from Islamic State remnants and Iran-backed armed groups. President Trump praised the withdrawal, and the Pentagon said the Iraqi government now bears primary responsibility for remaining security challenges [11][50][47].

Key Points

  • British departure: About 100 remaining British military personnel also left Iraq, completing the UK’s participation in the coalition mission [46].
  • Militia deadline: Iraq’s deadline for disarming non-state armed groups was pushed to June 2027 [37][38].
  • Regional operations: The U.S.-led task force shifted its Iraq headquarters to Jordan, while American counter-Islamic State operations continue in Syria [23][42].

Islamic Resistance in Iraq commander Abu Mojtaba al-Yasiri: He described the departure as a “crushing defeat for the American project” [9].

Timeline

  • 2003: U.S.-led forces invaded Iraq and toppled Saddam Hussein’s government [16].
  • 2011: U.S. forces withdrew after the initial occupation and military campaign [13].
  • 2014: U.S. forces returned to fight Islamic State after the group seized territory [13][5].
  • 2017: Islamic State lost control of its territory in Iraq [5].
  • September 2024: Baghdad and Washington agreed to wind down the coalition mission [14][5].
  • 30 September 2026: Final U.S. forces and equipment departed Erbil Air Base [47][50].

Historical Background

The U.S. troop presence reached more than 170,000 in Iraq in 2007, during the war’s peak [16]. The 2003 invasion was justified by claims that Saddam Hussein possessed weapons of mass destruction, allegations Washington never substantiated [8].
