Jordan strikes southern Syria after church attack kills nine in Karak

Category: World

Jordan’s air force struck targets in southern Syria on Monday, Oct. 26, saying the sites belonged to militant groups linked to the gunmen who killed nine people at a church in Jordan three days earlier. The Jordanian military said its strikes destroyed positions and vehicles and killed or wounded members of the groups; it did not identify the groups or give precise locations [3][5][11]. Syrian authorities said the strikes killed between five and eight civilians, including children, and that Jordan had not coordinated the operation in advance. Jordan said it had coordinated with Damascus. Syria’s government opened an investigation and said it was communicating with Jordan about the incident [4][6][10][7]. The strikes followed a shooting during a funeral at St. George’s Greek Orthodox Church in Hammoud, in Karak governorate, on Oct. 23. Jordanian security forces arrested three Jordanian suspects after a shootout on Monday; all three suspects were wounded. Syria closed the Nassib crossing on Tuesday, suspending passenger and commercial traffic in both directions [13][11][5][4][3].

Historical Background

In 2016, an attack on a security centre and police patrols in Karak killed 10 people [4][15]. Jordan has also carried out airstrikes in southern Syria against suspected smuggling networks, including those involved in Captagon trafficking. Those operations have strained relations with Damascus over Syrian sovereignty [7].

A: No group had claimed responsibility in reports published after the arrests, and the attackers’ motive remained under investigation [11][13].
