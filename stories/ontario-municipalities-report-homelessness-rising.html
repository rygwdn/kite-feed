Ontario municipalities report homelessness rising, hitting north hardest

Category: Canada

A new report from Ontario’s municipal sector estimates that roughly 85,000 people experienced homelessness in the province in 2025, and that the total grew over the prior year [3][2][4]. The report also points to a rise in encampments across Ontario, underscoring how visible the crisis has become in many communities [3][2]. It highlights a particularly sharp increase in rural and Northern Ontario, where communities often have fewer local supports to respond [4]. While the findings are sobering, the shared data gives municipalities and the province a clearer baseline to plan services and track what’s working over time [3][4].

Key Points

  • Northern strain: The report estimates about 12,800 people were homeless in rural and northern areas such as Timmins, Sault Ste. Marie and Thunder Bay [4].
  • Indigenous overrepresentation: It says at least 13% of homeless Ontarians are Indigenous, compared with about 3% of Ontario’s overall population [4].
  • Long-term homelessness: More than half of people counted are described as experiencing prolonged homelessness, according to the municipalities’ report [2].
  • Encampment growth: The report cites nearly 2,000 encampments across the province, signalling rising pressure on local outreach, public health and bylaw services [3][2].

Technical Details

  • Encampments: Encampments are informal outdoor living sites (often tents or temporary shelters) that can expand when people lack access to affordable housing or shelter spaces; the report tracks them as a visible indicator of unmet need [3][2].
  • Prolonged homelessness: The report describes many people as experiencing prolonged homelessness, meaning homelessness lasting a long time rather than brief episodes, which typically requires sustained supports such as housing with services rather than short-term fixes [2].

Industry Impact

  • Municipal services: With encampments rising, municipalities may face higher demand for outreach, emergency shelter coordination and related local services, as reflected in the report’s provincewide counts [3][2].
  • Health and social supports: A larger share of prolonged homelessness implies greater need for integrated support systems (housing, mental health, addictions and income supports) rather than only short stays in shelters [2].
  • Indigenous-led programs: The report’s finding of Indigenous overrepresentation suggests demand for culturally safe, Indigenous-led housing and support approaches alongside mainstream services [4].
