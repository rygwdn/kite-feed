Guinea landfill collapse kills 30 in Conakry

Category: World

At least 30 people were killed when a mound of garbage collapsed at the Dar Es Salam landfill in Guinea’s capital, Conakry, early Sunday after heavy overnight rain, the government said [1][2][3][9][6][8][35]. Another 22 people were injured, six seriously, after waste engulfed nearby homes, tents and shacks in the Dar-es-Salam area of the Gbessia commune [1][9][40][36]. Authorities said heavy rain beginning around 2 a.m. washed over refuse at Dar Es Salam, Conakry’s largest open-air waste dump, triggering a landslide that destroyed several homes [2][8][16][17]. The site was scheduled to close Sunday, and residents living nearby had received eviction notices, though authorities said many people remained in the area when the collapse happened [2][8][35][45]. Search-and-rescue teams used excavators to search the debris, and Prime Minister Amadou Oury Bah visited the site to assess the situation and oversee the response [1][15][24][40]. The government said it deployed personnel and equipment to search for people who may still be buried, rescue survivors and recover bodies [1][6][24][49].

Key Points

  • Rescue network: State agencies coordinated operations with the Red Cross, defense and security forces, medical teams, administrative and local authorities, and nearby residents [40][53][49].
  • Collapse scale: Witnesses said the slide was large enough to bury an apartment building as well as more precarious dwellings [9][12][46][48].
  • Rainy season: The government had cited the site’s danger during the rainy season, when landslides and fatal flash floods occur each year in crowded, underdeveloped cities across parts of West Africa [5][9].
  • Poverty context: Guinea is rich in bauxite, iron ore, gold, diamonds and other minerals, but the World Bank says 43.7% of its population lives below the national poverty line [16][17].
