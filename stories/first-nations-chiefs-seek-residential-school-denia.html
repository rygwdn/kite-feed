First Nations chiefs seek residential school denialism offence

Category: Canada

The Assembly of First Nations passed an emergency resolution Tuesday at its annual general assembly in Ottawa calling on the federal government to criminalize residential school denialism by treating it as hate speech [5][7][4]. First Nations chiefs said Ottawa has allowed denialism to persist by not making it a criminal offence [7], and several leaders addressed the resolution at a Wednesday news conference [5]. The call came after Parliament passed Bill C-9, the Combatting Hate Act, and the Senate defeated an amendment that would have added residential school denialism to the bill [5][4][7]. A spokesperson for Justice Minister Sean Fraser said after senators rejected the amendment that denying the harms of residential schools is a “serious and distinct issue” but did not fit within the scope of the act [4].

It is a shame to live in a country that will not protect our people from hate crimes.

Key Points

  • Proposed offence: Nunavut Sen. Nancy Karetak-Lindell’s defeated amendment would have made “condoning, denying or downplaying” residential schools, outside private conversation, an indictable offence punishable by up to two years in prison [4].
  • Earlier bill: NDP MP Leah Gazan introduced a private member’s bill in 2024 that would have made residential school denialism a criminal offence, but it had not yet been put to a vote [7].
  • Repeated resolutions: First Nations chiefs have adopted similar resolutions over several years, while the federal government has not amended the Criminal Code to add such an offence [7].
  • Senate lobbying: Anishinabek Nation Grand Council Chief Linda Debassige and AFN B.C. Regional Chief Terry Teegee said they spoke to the Senate about adding residential school denialism to Bill C-9 [5].

Historical Background

More than 150,000 Indigenous children were forced to attend residential schools in Canada, and the last school closed in 1996 [7]. About 6,000 children are estimated to have died in the institutions, though experts cited by La Presse said the true number could be higher [7][2].
