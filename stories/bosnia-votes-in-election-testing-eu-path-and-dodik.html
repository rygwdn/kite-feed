Bosnia votes in election testing EU path and Dodik’s influence

Category: World

Bosnians went to the polls Sunday to elect the country’s three-member presidency, national and entity lawmakers, and Republika Srpska’s president, with about 3.4 million people eligible to vote. Milorad Dodik, the separatist former Republika Srpska president, is barred from public office but remains influential. His party is expected to perform strongly in the Serb-majority entity. Problems with new digital equipment delayed voting at some polling stations [5][4][13]. The EU opened accession negotiations with Bosnia in 2024, but political blockades have slowed progress. The campaign featured competing calls for a stronger central state, greater Serb autonomy and more Croat influence in choosing the Croat member of the presidency [11][13]. Authorities introduced biometric voter identification and ballot scanning to address concerns about past irregularities, while voters continue to mark paper ballots. Preliminary results were expected on election night, with the Central Election Commission due to update the count. Coalition talks are likely to determine how the many elected bodies are governed [19][18][5][11].

Key Points

  • Scale of ballot: The vote covers 518 offices across state, entity and cantonal institutions, with 75 parties, 29 coalitions and four independent candidates registered [19][12].
  • Counting safeguards: About 30,000 observers will monitor the vote, most of them at the municipal level [12].
  • Everyday concerns: Voters and activists expressed frustration over low living standards, young people leaving and politicians’ emphasis on ethnic divisions rather than practical problems [5][13].
  • Election mechanics: Voters in the Federation elect the presidency’s Bosniak and Croat members, while voters in Republika Srpska choose the Serb member [11][12].

  • 2 October 2022: Denis Becirovic, Zeljko Komsic and Zeljka Cvijanovic won presidency seats [24]
  • December 2022: European Union granted Bosnia candidate status [11]
  • 2024: EU opened accession negotiations as political blockages slowed reforms [11]
  • 2 October 2026: Dodik told his party’s final rally Bosnia was an impossible country [5][13]
  • 4 October 2026: Polling began nationwide, with delays reported at some stations [4][13]

International Reactions

  • 🇷🇺 Russia: Russian President Vladimir Putin met Dodik shortly before the election. Moscow continues to back Republika Srpska [4][1].
  • 🇪🇺 European Union: The EU has criticized Bosnian Serb officials’ attendance at Ratko Mladic’s funeral in Belgrade, which angered Bosniak politicians [5][4].

Q&A

Q: Why is the Republika Srpska presidency race seen as pivotal for the entity’s government?

A: Analyst Dimitrije Ceranic said a win by Dodik-backed Savo Minic would leave the SNSD and its coalition partners in power, while a Blanusa victory could break up the governing coalition [23].

Q: How does Bosnia’s collective presidency make decisions?

A: Its three members represent Bosniaks, Serbs and Croats; decisions require consensus, each member has veto power, and the chairmanship rotates every eight months [12].
