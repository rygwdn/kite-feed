France pledges 3,000 substitute teachers as student protests persist

Category: World

Student-led demonstrations across France drew 71,500 people on Thursday, Oct. 8, and police detained more than 500, the Interior Ministry said. In Paris, police used tear gas and water cannon and charged protesters near the Bastille after clashes. Demonstrations elsewhere were largely peaceful, though some turned violent [4][15][37]. Education Minister Édouard Geffray said the government would send 3,000 substitute teachers to schools with acute shortages between Friday and Tuesday. He also said several hundred teachers could be recruited, while the government reviews long school days and the university admissions platform Parcoursup [14][37]. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu has promised proposals by the end of October but has not detailed them [14][9]. Students cite teacher shortages, deteriorating school buildings and demanding schedules. Student representatives said Thursday’s meeting with the minister had not produced enough concrete commitments, and unions called for another national day of action on Tuesday, Oct. 13 [23][52][14].

Key Points

  • Funding demand: Student association representative Sabrine Kharbach said the movement would continue until €10 billion was committed to schools [14][35].
  • School disruption: Education Ministry figures cited by Reuters showed 224 schools were closed Thursday morning. That number fell to 157 by lunchtime, with 96% open [37][46].
  • Public opinion: An Elabe poll for BFM TV found 72% considered the students’ demands legitimate, while 55% thought the protests should stop [14][37].
  • Wider support: University students, teachers and school staff have joined the demonstrations, which began as a high-school movement [51][41][7].
