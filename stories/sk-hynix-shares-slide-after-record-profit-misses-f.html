SK Hynix shares slide after record profit misses forecasts

Category: Business

SK Hynix reported record second-quarter results on Wednesday, posting sales of about 79.31 trillion won, operating profit of 60.54 trillion won and net profit of 93.92 trillion won, helped by demand for high-bandwidth memory and other chips used in AI servers [4][5][13][1][3]. Revenue rose about 257% from a year earlier and operating profit rose about 557%, but both missed consensus forecasts of roughly 84 trillion won in revenue and 64 trillion won in operating profit [4][5][13][14][3]. The earnings miss prompted a selloff in Seoul. SK Hynix shares fell as much as 13% to 16% in Wednesday trading, and Samsung Electronics also dropped as investors cut exposure to AI-linked chip stocks [4][13][24][2][3]. South Korea's KOSPI fell for a second straight session, dropping as much as 12.6% on Wednesday after a nearly 11% decline on Tuesday [24][2]. SK Hynix said demand remained strong. President Song Hyun-jong said major customers continued to request more memory supply, and the company said it was seeking more long-term supply agreements to manage memory-price volatility [13][43][3]. Analysts cited concerns about a possible pause in AI infrastructure spending, limited detail on shareholder returns and the risk that long-term contracts could cap upside from spot memory price increases [13][42].

Key Points

  • Half-year milestone: The company's first-half revenue exceeded 100 trillion won for the first time in its history [14][22][3][36].
  • Shipment constraint: SK Hynix said delays in shipments of some advanced products limited price gains for its main DRAM chips [13].
  • Market concentration: SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics represent more than half of the KOSPI's market capitalization, giving their declines outsized influence on the benchmark [24].
  • Regional pressure: The selloff spread to Asian chip names including Japan's Kioxia, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., Tokyo Electron and SoftBank Group [4][27][2].

Perspectives

SK Hynix: The company said AI memory demand would remain strong through 2030 and that it was securing long-term supply agreements to stabilize revenue and protect against memory-price volatility.

Technical Details

  • Five-year supply agreements: SK Hynix said long-term agreements typically run for five years and include financial safeguards such as deposits to support contract implementation [13].
  • HBM4 ramp: SK Hynix said HBM4 shipments began in the second quarter and that it planned to expand production in the second half of the year [30][27].
  • Net income exceeded revenue: Asset sales boosted profit beyond operating earnings, an unusual outcome for a manufacturer [43][28].

Industry Impact

  • Retail leverage: Small investors using borrowed money helped drive the Korean rally, and forced position closures worsened the selloff as prices fell [24][34].
  • Capital spending: SK Hynix earmarked about $31 billion for investments, with 2026 capital expenditure expected near the upper end of the 40 trillion won range [12][14][45].

Historical Background

SK Hynix moved early into high-bandwidth memory and became a key supplier to Nvidia as AI accelerator demand surged [40][28]. The latest decline followed an AI-fueled rally in South Korean shares, with the KOSPI having more than tripled from the start of 2025 before the July decline [4].

Business Angle

  • Record margins point to strong near-term pricing power, but investors are watching whether those margins hold through the next supply expansion [1][common].
  • Long-term supply contracts can stabilize revenue but may reduce gains from future memory-price spikes [13][43].
  • Analysts cited the lack of detailed shareholder-return plans as a concern for how today's earnings windfall will flow to owners [13][common].
  • A concentrated KOSPI and leveraged retail positioning can amplify share-price moves without changing SK Hynix's underlying production economics [24][common].

Action Items

  • Check AI-chip concentration in portfolios: Review whether funds or direct holdings are heavily exposed to SK Hynix, Samsung, Nvidia, Micron, TSMC or leveraged semiconductor ETFs before adding risk [24][47].
  • Read SK Hynix contract disclosures: Look for updates on five-year supply agreements, deposits, HBM4 production and capital spending in the company's earnings materials and subsequent filings [13][43].
