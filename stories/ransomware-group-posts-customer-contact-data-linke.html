Ransomware group posts customer contact data linked to Chase, Visa, Mastercard

Category: Technology

Cyber-extortion group n0n published files on Nov. 19 that it said came from attacks on outside providers linked to JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard; the companies said their payment systems and card numbers were not affected [2][18][34]. Service providers held records used for marketing and customer-benefit programs outside the financial firms’ own networks [30][21][23]. JPMorgan said marketing data held by its vendors may include customer names and email addresses. Mastercard reported a limited exposure of email addresses for cardholders in a benefits program; Visa linked its incident to a provider in the Asia-Pacific region. The companies have not named the providers or said whether the incidents involved a shared supplier [2][21][30]. The authenticity and completeness of the published files remain uncertain: cybersecurity firm SOCRadar said it identified n0n’s claims and sample data but could not independently verify that the group carried out the intrusions [21][18]. The firms have not disclosed how attackers entered provider systems. Mastercard said it was working with its provider to support affected cardholders [23][2].

Primary Source: View original article

The most immediate risk for consumers is targeted phishing that impersonates their bank or card provider.

— Ensar Seker, chief information security officer at SOCRadar (Business Insider)

Key Points

  • Leaked records: A review of files linked to JPMorgan found more than 16,000 customer email addresses, some phone numbers and postal codes, and 124 employee email addresses; the 502-kilobyte file may be only a sample [30][1].
  • Reserve card link: Cybernews said the records appeared to relate to Chase’s Reserve card, an invitation-only Visa card for clients with at least $10 million in managed assets [1].
  • Group profile: Cybersecurity firm ZeroFox said n0n averaged two claimed attacks a day, ranking it among the five most active threat actors it tracked [8].
  • Exposure footprint: Reuters’ review, as recounted by Business Standard, found purported Visa records linked to Asia-Pacific markets and Mastercard marketing records associated with customers in North America, West Asia and Africa [30].

Perspectives

Carissa Robb, a consultant at SolomonEdwards, said financial firms should face greater scrutiny over how often they review customer data shared with vendors and whether vendors need all of it [16].

Timeline

  • 16 November 2026: n0n posted claims involving the financial firms [30][34]
  • 19 November 2026: Companies disclosed vendor incidents as n0n published files [2][30]

Q&A

Q: What support might affected Chase customers receive?

A: Chase said it would contact affected customers directly where appropriate and offer support, including complimentary credit monitoring [21].

Action Items

  • Verify unexpected bank or card-provider messages before responding: Open the institution’s app or call the number printed on your card instead of following links in an unexpected breach or account message [23].
