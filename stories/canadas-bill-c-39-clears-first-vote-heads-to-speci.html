Canada's Bill C-39 clears first vote, heads to special committee

Category: Canada

Canada’s 243-page Bill C-39 cleared its first vote in the House of Commons on Oct. 8, with Conservative MPs joining the Liberals to send it to a special committee for study [1][2]. The bill is part of Prime Minister Mark Carney’s economic agenda [2]. It would give the federal government more authority to fast-track projects deemed in the “national interest” and amend the Canada Labour Code to reduce labour disruptions in federally regulated sectors [1][3]. The committee will examine the legislation; Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre said his party plans to propose amendments [1].

This bill should be scrapped because it threatens the right to strike

— Avi Lewis (The Canadian Press)

Key Points

  • Conservative amendments: Poilievre wants project decisions made within six months to a year and has called for adding worker-rights protections [1].
  • NDP objection: Lewis said the bill fails to meet the government’s constitutional duty to consult Indigenous communities and obtain full, prior and informed consent for projects in their territories [1].
  • Bill’s scope: The legislation would let the jobs minister intervene in labour disputes, subject to proposed guardrails [1].

Perspectives

Government of Canada: The government says it will uphold its treaty obligations [1].
