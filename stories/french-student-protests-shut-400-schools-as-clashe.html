French student protests shut 400 schools as clashes spread nationwide

Category: World

French high school protests over teacher shortages, overcrowded classrooms and deteriorating facilities spread nationwide on Friday, Oct. 2, as students clashed with police and set fires. Education Minister Édouard Geffray said 735 schools were affected and about 400 were closed; Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez reported 1,747 arrests on Friday. The education ministry said 170 students and 65 staff members had been injured since the unrest began, and more than 100 schools had suffered severe damage [3][4][17]. Students have also cited long school days, overheated classrooms and inadequate learning materials. A draft 2027 budget would raise education spending by 1.7% to €65.53 billion, excluding teacher pensions, an increase below inflation [20][17]. The government says the right to protest is legitimate but blames violent groups and has accused the left-wing La France Insoumise party of helping drive the unrest; the party denies the accusation [9][30]. After meeting student and union representatives on Friday, Geffray announced a plan to replace absent teachers more quickly, while students said the talks had not produced concrete measures. Student groups and unions have called for another nationwide mobilization on Tuesday, Oct. 6; a government consultation platform is due to open Monday [4][28][32].

Key Points

  • Repair costs: Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes officials estimated protest-related damage at €25 million to €30 million in their region, while Île-de-France put its losses at about €7 million [9].
  • Police scrutiny: French authorities opened investigations into allegations of police violence against demonstrators, including minors; students say tear gas and other crowd-control tactics have worsened tensions [19][4].
  • Admissions concern: Some protesters have also criticized Parcoursup, France’s online university application system, as a source of stress and mistrust [20].
  • Classroom shortages: A 15-year-old in Pantin said there were 35 pupils in her class and too few chairs, while students elsewhere cited inadequate toilets and water leaks [20].
  • Fee reversal: The government has abandoned planned registration fees for some higher-education programmes, one measure announced during the protests [28].

Perspectives

La France Insoumise: LFI lawmaker Louis Boyard rejected claims that the party initiated the protests, saying the government was politicizing them and that frustration would not be resolved with batons [30].

  • 6 October 2026: Student groups and unions scheduled a nationwide mobilization and strike action [9][28].

Historical Background

During the 2024-2025 school year, nearly 10% of teaching hours in French public secondary schools went untaught, with about 7.5% of lost hours attributed to the failure to find substitute teachers [20]. The teacher shortage predates the demonstrations; students are demanding reliable staffing.

Humanitarian Impact

A youth wounded in Tours lost the use of his right eye, according to the city's prosecutor; in La Ciotat, a teacher was sprayed with petrol before being assaulted [4].

Q&A

Q: What is the government offering students beyond its staffing plan?

A: A national online consultation platform is scheduled to open Monday, asking students about their difficulties and expectations; Geffray said submissions would inform a national report [32][20].
