Apple schedules October 28 event as touchscreen MacBook rumors build

Category: Technology

Apple has invited journalists and creators to an October 28 product event in Los Angeles called “Pro and Tell,” but has not disclosed which devices it will announce [48][14][16]. The event is expected to feature the first touchscreen MacBook Pro, alongside other Mac and iPad updates; Apple has not confirmed those product details [13][6][26]. Reports describe planned 14- and 16-inch high-end MacBook Pro models with OLED touchscreens, a lighter design and an iPhone-style Dynamic Island, powered by M5 Pro and M5 Max chips rather than a new M6 Pro or Max generation [17][9][16]. A separate entry-level 14-inch MacBook Pro and an iMac are expected to move to the M6 chip, while an updated iPad mini with an OLED display is also among the rumored products [26][6]. A touchscreen laptop would reverse Apple’s longstanding position that Mac laptops should rely on keyboards and trackpads. Apple has not confirmed prices, availability or whether it will livestream the event; reports have suggested a possible November 6 retail launch [43][36][14].

Key Points

  • Touch interface: MacRumors reports that Apple is adapting macOS 27 for touch gestures, while a sturdier hinge is intended to keep the display steady when tapped [17].
  • Display shift: OLED panels would replace the mini-LED displays used in current high-end MacBook Pro models [3][17].
  • Tablet changes: The expected iPad mini update could move its front camera to the landscape edge and include a revised speaker system, but Apple has not confirmed the hardware [26][16].
  • Pricing pressure: Mashable reported a rumored price of $799 for the OLED iPad mini, $200 above its predecessor, while PCMag noted that rising memory costs could affect laptop pricing; Apple has confirmed neither the iPad mini price nor any effect on laptop prices [15][11].

Perspectives

Ben Reitzes, an analyst at Melius Research, said the recent launch schedule marks a “meaningful shift” from Apple’s pattern of holding two events a year, as CEO John Ternus oversees more frequent product launches [43].
