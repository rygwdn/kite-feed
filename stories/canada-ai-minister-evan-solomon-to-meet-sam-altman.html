Canada AI minister Evan Solomon to meet Sam Altman

Category: Canada

Canada’s federal Minister of Artificial Intelligence, Evan Solomon, says he will meet OpenAI CEO Sam Altman next week to push for clearer, concrete next steps after OpenAI outlined safety changes following the mass killing in Tumbler Ridge, B.C. [3][5][7][4]. Solomon said Canada supports innovation, but “not at the expense of safety,” and added that OpenAI’s public commitments still don’t spell out a detailed plan for how the changes will be put into practice [3][7]. OpenAI told Solomon it had previously banned the alleged shooter’s ChatGPT account for misuse tied to “violent activities,” but the person later got around that ban by creating a second account, according to reporting on a letter OpenAI sent to the minister [2][3]. In the same letter, OpenAI laid out “immediate steps,” including stronger detection for warning signs of serious violence, better safeguards against evasion, and changes to how it contacts authorities—adding that, in hindsight, it would have referred the account to police under the tighter approach it says it uses today [2][3][5].

Key Points

  • Implementation detail sought: Solomon said OpenAI’s public commitments still don’t include “a detailed plan for how these commitments will be implemented in practice,” and he plans to press Altman for concrete follow-through when they meet next week [7][3].
  • How the letter came about: OpenAI sent the letter to Solomon after OpenAI executives met earlier in the week with Canadian government officials, setting up the Altman meeting as the next step in the talks [3].
  • Authority-contact updates: OpenAI said it is changing how it contacts authorities, including setting up clearer channels and direct points of contact with Canadian officials, alongside other safety and escalation updates [2][3][5].
  • B.C. political response: B.C. Premier David Eby credited OpenAI for “coming clean” about what it learned after the Tumbler Ridge killings, according to coverage referenced in the news roundup [1][6].
