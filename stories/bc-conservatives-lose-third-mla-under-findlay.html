B.C. Conservatives lose third MLA under Findlay

Category: Canada

B.C. Conservative MLA Rosalyn Bird left the party caucus Tuesday and said she will continue representing Prince George-Valemount as an Independent, making her the third party member this month to quit caucus [2][5][7]. Party members elected leader Kerry-Lynne Findlay, a lawyer and longtime Conservative, less than three months ago [2][5]. Bird cited infighting and a change in the party’s values, saying division in the legislature over the past year and a half has not served the province [2]. She said she did not tell Findlay before issuing her statement but later emailed the leader to thank her for the shadow-cabinet post and to say she respected the membership’s decision [2]. The B.C. Conservatives did not respond to the Vancouver Sun’s request for comment on Bird’s exit. Findlay later defended the party and said her caucus remains focused on affordability policies [2][6].

Division, not only amongst parties, but the division amongst various groups around the province, has been very prevalent in the last year and a half at the B.C. legislature, and I don’t think it’s helpful to the greater good of the province.

— Rosalyn Bird (Vancouver Sun)

Key Points

  • Internal strain: The Vancouver Sun reported staff have resigned or been fired, and said Findlay faces allegations that she offered MLAs financial compensation to resign so she could run in a byelection [2].
  • New party: Two MLAs who recently left the provincial Conservatives said they plan to form a new party when politicians return this fall, calling it an option for frustrated voters [4].
  • Leadership direction: Canada’s National Observer reported the party could face more defections as Findlay pulls it further to the right [3].
  • Public warning: Google News’ aggregation of coverage said Bird warned against racism and homophobia in connection with her decision to leave the B.C. Conservatives [7].
