Anthropic mathematician says Claude helped prove Riemann hypothesis

Category: AI

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says he used the company’s Claude model to develop a proof of the Riemann hypothesis. The claim remains subject to scrutiny. He says the approach also proves the broader Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. Alpöge said he chose the research direction, while Claude made rapid technical advances near the end; the model worked as a collaborator rather than solving the problem on its own [21][64][63]. The argument is available as computer-checkable Lean code rather than a conventional paper. Independent mathematicians, including Massachusetts Institute of Technology mathematician Andrew Sutherland, have compiled and rechecked the code with Lean’s kernel. Those checks verify the formalized reasoning but do not by themselves establish whether the encoded theorem matches the intended conjecture or explain the proof’s ideas to mathematicians [53][44]. Alpöge says he has not yet mastered all the mathematics involved and plans to explain the work and write it up. Mathematicians expect review and comprehension to take time [3][29][41].

Technical Details

  • Theta lifts: Alpöge says Claude proposed chaining theta lifts to adapt an approach descended from Stepanov’s method, addressing the difficulty of translating multiplicity techniques from function fields to ordinary number theory [93][42].
  • Lean formalization: Lean encodes mathematical statements and deductions so a proof assistant can check that each step follows formal rules. Researchers still need to verify that the encoded theorem matches the intended hypothesis and that the formalization does not exploit a loophole in the system [2][54].
  • Theta-lift strategy: Alpöge says the argument adapts an approach associated with Sergei Stepanov’s proof for function fields, using repeated theta lifts to recreate a needed multiplicity structure; he says Claude suggested setting the chain length near the square root of the weighted-prime-count scale [64][35].

Scientific Significance

  • Conditional results: If the generalized Riemann hypothesis is proved and the argument’s scope checks out, mathematical results that currently depend on assuming it could become unconditional theorems [22].
  • Practical limits: A proof would not itself provide a known shortcut for factoring the large numbers used in RSA encryption; the reported near-term consequences are mainly within mathematics [95].
