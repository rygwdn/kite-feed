Texas governor win puts AI data-center permits in play

Category: AI

Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor’s race in the Nov. 3 midterms, while Democrats won both chambers of Congress. Texas accounts for 27% of planned U.S. data-center capacity [15][22][25]. Hinojosa has supported a moratorium on AI data-center construction, while Republican Gov. Greg Abbott had already ordered officials to withhold new permits pending an audit of power-grid risks [25][13]. The change in Texas leadership could affect projects through state and local approvals even if federal restrictions prove harder to enact [13][5]. A Democratic-led Congress can investigate companies and hold hearings, but major legislation would face President Trump’s veto and the Senate’s 60-vote threshold for most bills [5]. The new Congress is due to convene on Jan. 3, 2027, while state officials who favor AI guardrails are expected to renew their efforts next year [5][4].

Key Points

  • Local ballots: Voters considered at least 41 data-center measures across 10 states and 38 local jurisdictions; Ohio had 21 proposals, the most of any state [11].
  • Regional pipeline: Texas accounts for 27% of planned U.S. data-center capacity, while Georgia accounts for 7% and Ohio and Pennsylvania 5% each, according to Wells Fargo estimates cited by CNBC [25].
  • Anthropic exposure: Anthropic’s data-center agreements include a $50 billion partnership with Fluidstack covering Texas and New York sites, a $35 billion pact with Lambda for a Nueces County facility and a $9.1 billion agreement with Riot Platforms for a Rockdale campus [13].
  • Uneven costs: Analysts said delays could postpone revenue for cloud operators that rent computing capacity, while highly indebted developers would continue to incur financing costs; firms with secured power and stronger cash flow may be better positioned [14].

Historical Background

In September 2026, the U.S. House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act by a vote of 417 to 3. The bill would require states to consider making large data-center operators pay more of the cost of expanding the power grid [3]. It failed to advance in the Senate, where the vote was 57 in favor and 43 against, short of the 60 votes needed to proceed [3].

Q&A

Q: Would the election put all U.S. data-center capacity under heightened scrutiny?

A: No. ICICI Bank estimated that around 15% of U.S. data-center capacity could face increased scrutiny; the 15 largest data-center states account for about 82% of capacity, and Republican-governed states hold 62% [19].
