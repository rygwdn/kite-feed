Canadian military to continue howitzer avalanche control at Rogers Pass

Category: Canada

Parks Canada and the Canadian Armed Forces have put plans to end Operation Palaci on hold and say there is no set end date for the military’s howitzer-based avalanche-control program at Rogers Pass in southeastern British Columbia [3][1][2]. The military will continue supporting the operation while the agencies develop a transition plan. The decision reverses a plan to end military involvement after the current season, when the agreement expires in August 2027 [3]. The operation triggers controlled slides above the Trans-Canada Highway to reduce the risk of larger avalanches and also protects a railway through the pass [3]. The Department of National Defence had previously cited changing military priorities and plans to retire older artillery systems as reasons not to renew the agreement [3][1].

Key Points

  • Avalanche-control network: Parks Canada says the operation uses 16 gun positions covering 135 avalanche paths and about 270 artillery targets [3].
  • Highway stretch: The operation covers a 43-kilometre corridor where high elevation and a dense concentration of avalanche slopes create particular challenges [3].
  • Only defence department program: Operation Palaci is the only avalanche-control program the defence department currently manages [3].

Perspectives

British Columbia Transportation Ministry: The ministry expressed concern earlier in 2026 after the defence department announced it would not renew the agreement when it expired in 2027 [1][2].

Sources: The Canadian Press

Technical Details

  • Howitzer system: Gun crews use 105-millimetre howitzers in the operation [3][1].

Historical Background

Military personnel have used artillery to trigger avalanches at Rogers Pass for more than 70 years, according to CBC. The Canadian Press reports that the practice has continued for more than 60 years [3][1].
