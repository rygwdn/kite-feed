Amazon and Google to own 28% of AI firm Anthropic

Category: AI

Amazon and Google are set to own a combined 28.4% of Anthropic after the Claude developer’s public offering and a separate private placement, making two of its largest cloud customers major shareholders [35][47]. Anthropic’s filing describes Amazon, Google and Microsoft as investors, customers, distribution partners and competitors [35]. Customers can access Claude through their existing cloud accounts, while providers handle billing and retain a fee before passing the remainder to Anthropic [35]. Technology analysts say public-company reporting may give enterprise buyers more visibility into Anthropic’s operations, while quarterly pressure to show growth could prompt it to seek longer contracts and firmer spending commitments [27]. Anthropic is scheduled to begin trading on Nasdaq on Nov. 19, becoming the first pure-play frontier AI lab on public markets, according to Morningstar [15]. Its filings and quarterly disclosures will detail its AI business and ties to cloud infrastructure providers [15][27].

Key Points

  • Cloud revenue: Resales through cloud providers generated about 44% of Anthropic’s revenue in the first nine months of 2026 [35].
  • Enterprise base: Anthropic reported more than 11,900 enterprise customers spending at least $100,000 annually, including about 250 spending $10 million or more [27].
  • Customer protections: Analysts say enterprise buyers can negotiate price protections, capacity guarantees, model choice and exit terms [27].
  • Software exposure: Salesforce shares fell 9% before the offering as investors worried AI agents could automate work now billed per software seat [56].
  • Index entry: Only about 6% of Anthropic’s shares will be publicly tradable initially, limiting its initial weight in float-adjusted indexes [25].

Perspectives

New Street Research analyst Pierre Ferragu: Ferragu expects AI to capture close to 25% of incremental knowledge-work spending through 2040, describing its addressable market as 10 times larger than cloud’s [10].

Sources: CNBC

Investor Steve Eisman: Eisman argued the listing had become less important than concerns over local and state limits on data-center construction, which he said could affect AI infrastructure investment [38].

Timeline

  • 2021: Researchers who left OpenAI founded Anthropic [57].
  • 10 November 2026: Anthropic amended its registration statement before the planned offering [30].
  • 17 November 2026: A filing disclosed Amazon and Google’s post-offering ownership stakes [35].
  • 19 November 2026: Anthropic is scheduled to begin trading on Nasdaq [15].

Industry Impact

  • Large-customer exposure: Many of Anthropic’s largest customers are not on long-term contracts and can reduce or stop spending at any time [27].
  • Nasdaq entry process: A newly listed company must rank among the index’s 40 largest holdings to qualify for fast entry; Nasdaq waits 15 trading days before inclusion and plans to consider Anthropic at its mid-December review [25].

Historical Background

Anthropic adopted public-benefit-corporation status, which legally requires it to balance shareholder interests with public well-being, and its prospectus discusses AI safety at length [68].
