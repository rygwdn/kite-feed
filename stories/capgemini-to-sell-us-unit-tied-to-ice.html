Capgemini to sell US unit tied to ICE

Category: Business

Capgemini says it will begin selling Capgemini Government Solutions, a US subsidiary that provides technology services to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), amid international scrutiny of immigration enforcement under President Trump [1][6][5][3][2]. The company said legal constraints governing work with US federal agencies limited its ability to exercise appropriate control over aspects of the subsidiary’s operations, making the sale the chosen path [1][2]. French officials had pushed the company to clarify what the unit was doing, increasing pressure for transparency and oversight [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

The nature and scope of this work has raised questions compared to what we typically do as a business and technology firm.

— Aiman Ezzat (Fortune)

Did you know: Capgemini said Capgemini Government Solutions makes up only about 0.4% of its estimated 2025 revenue [1].

Key Points

  • Small revenue share: Capgemini said the subsidiary represents about 0.4% of the group’s estimated 2025 revenue, suggesting limited direct financial dependence on the business being sold [1].
  • CEO awareness: Capgemini CEO Aiman Ezzat said he only recently learned about the subsidiary’s ICE contract, signaling an internal oversight issue the company now says it is addressing [1].
  • French parliament pressure: French Finance Minister Roland Lescure told parliament he wanted Capgemini to be extremely transparent about its activities and to question the nature of that work [1].
  • Tools alleged: An NGO, Multinationals Observatory, reported that Capgemini Government Solutions provided ICE technical tools used to locate targets [1].
  • Ministerial criticism: French ministers criticised Capgemini for a lack of oversight regarding skip-tracing services linked to US immigration tracking, according to the Financial Times [3].

Historical Background

US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is a federal agency within the Department of Homeland Security created in 2003 after the post‑9/11 reorganization of US security agencies [common]. In recent years, companies providing data, analytics, and tracking capabilities to immigration enforcement have faced growing reputational and governance scrutiny from governments, employees, and civil-society groups, especially when the work is linked to locating individuals for enforcement actions [1][3].
