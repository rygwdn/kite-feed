Anthropic CEO rejects political rule by AI as lawmakers seek oversight

Category: AI

Anthropic Chief Executive Dario Amodei said he opposes handing political authority to artificial intelligence, calling that interpretation of his views “absurd” and arguing that democratic processes should govern AI. He distinguished political control from automating specific tasks and warned that AI systems developing successor models without meaningful human supervision could outpace people’s ability to control or adapt to them [1][10][17]. His response followed Axios reporting that some Anthropic employees believe people may eventually need to cede certain decisions to superintelligent systems. The company said Amodei has repeatedly supported human control. Meta AI chief Alexandr Wang and OpenAI Chief Executive Sam Altman have also publicly argued against a societal “handover” to AI [5][17][7]. Separately, Reps. Josh Gottheimer, a New Jersey Democrat, and Nathaniel Moran, a Texas Republican, introduced a bill that would require qualifying AI developers to report how much research they automate and how humans review it. The proposal would create a Commerce Department office to report to Congress and recommend possible restrictions, but would not authorize the department to halt development [2].

Key Points

  • Reporting threshold: The proposed reporting rules would cover companies training models above specified computing benchmarks and spending at least $100 million on that training in a year [2].
  • Safety disclosures: Covered developers would publish summaries of safety and oversight practices and notify Commerce of major milestones, including systems able to conduct entire research cycles [2].
  • Automation at Anthropic: Anthropic’s September stock-market filing said Claude produced more than 90% of contributions to its codebase [25].
  • Industry disagreement: Former OpenAI board member Helen Toner said shutdown mechanisms cannot provide a universal fix, in part because AI may run on infrastructure outside the jurisdiction of the company or government seeking to intervene [23].
  • Release concerns: Some employees argued that companies should stop training new models entirely, while others believed control problems might be fixable [15][20].

Perspectives

Dario Amodei, Anthropic chief executive: Amodei argued that whether to automate specific tasks depends on consequences: he said deployments might need to slow or stop when end-to-end automation risks serious job disruption, giving workers more time to adapt [19].

Historical Background

In a February interview with The New York Times, Amodei said people might want to turn over some life-critical decisions to AI, while his 2024 essay “Machines of Loving Grace” imagined AI systems helping allocate resources. Those remarks helped fuel the dispute over his views [5][7]. Anthropic has also said that each new Claude model accelerates development of the next, a feedback process the company calls “closing the loop” [10].

Q&A

Q: What did Anthropic say about holding a model back internally?

A: An Anthropic spokesperson told Axios that keeping a model internal does not by itself make it safe; the company’s responsibility also covers training and its own use of models [15].
