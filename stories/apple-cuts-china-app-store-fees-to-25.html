Apple cuts China App Store fees to 25%

Category: Technology

Apple says it will lower App Store commissions in mainland China starting March 15, cutting its standard take on paid apps and in-app purchases on iPhone and iPad to 25% from 30% [1][8][10][4][5]. For developers that qualify under the App Store Small Business Program and Mini Apps Partner Program—and for auto-renewing subscription renewals after the first year—Apple says the rate will drop to 12% from 15% [1][8][10][4]. Apple linked the change to “discussions with the Chinese regulator,” as antitrust scrutiny continues to build around app-store payment rules and commissions [1][9][7][2]. For users and smaller developers, the upside is simple: a lower take rate can leave more money in the ecosystem for new ideas, updates, and localized services. Whether that turns into lower prices is still up to each developer [1][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • No opt-in: Apple says developers don’t need to accept updated terms by March 15 to get the lower commission rates starting that day [1].
  • Regulatory pattern: The China adjustment follows a recent Japan change, where Apple lowered its commission to 21% on some third-party in-app payments as part of broader regulatory-driven updates [1].
  • Apple’s messaging: Apple says it’s sticking with terms it calls “fair and transparent” for developers, and it plans to offer “competitive App Store rates” as it updates China pricing [1].

Perspectives

Apple: Apple says the new commission rates in mainland China follow “discussions with the Chinese regulator,” and it aims to keep China App Store rates “no higher than overall rates in other markets.”
