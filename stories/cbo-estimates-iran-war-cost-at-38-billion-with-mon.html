CBO estimates Iran war cost at $38 billion, with monthly costs rising

Category: World

The Congressional Budget Office estimated that U.S. combat operations against Iran cost the Defense Department approximately $38 billion through Aug. 1, with an additional $2 billion to $3 billion expected for each month the conflict continues, depending on its intensity [2][3][14]. The estimate covers replacing expended munitions and lost equipment, higher fuel costs and other operational expenses [6][46]. The CBO projected that energy disruptions linked to the conflict could leave U.S. inflation 0.5 percentage points higher than previously forecast in the first quarter of 2027 [2][7]. The report also found that the war reduced stocks of missile interceptors [29].

Did you know: The CBO estimate excludes the cost of repairing U.S. bases damaged during the conflict [2].

Key Points

  • Munitions breakdown: Replacing expended munitions accounts for $21.7 billion of the estimated costs, including $13.1 billion for interceptors, $7.3 billion for land-attack cruise missiles and $1.2 billion for other munitions [29][32].
  • Inventory depletion: CBO analysts estimated that U.S. forces probably used between one-half and two-thirds of certain interceptor inventories since June 2025. Replenishment would take at least five years even if production increases [29][32].
  • Congressional challenge: The House voted 220-204 for a war-powers resolution seeking to end the conflict, but earlier resolutions did not reach the president, and Trump was expected to veto them [5].
  • Cost estimates: The CBO assessment broadly matched Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s July Senate estimate of $37.5 billion, while a Pentagon inspector general report put costs through June 29 at $33.4 billion [6][70].

Perspectives

Congressional Budget Office: The CBO said its estimate was limited because the Defense Department did not respond to congressional requests for information about the conflict’s costs [42].

Timeline

  • 21 July 2026: Hegseth told senators the war had cost $37.5 billion [6].
  • 1 August 2026: CBO’s accounting captured Defense Department costs through this date [2][29].
  • 15 September 2026: CBO released its estimate of costs and future monthly spending [3][2].

Technical Details

  • Interceptor systems: The CBO estimate covers Patriot, THAAD, Standard Missile-3 and Standard Missile-6 interceptors. It combines reported expenditures with Pentagon procurement totals because inventory figures are not publicly disclosed [29].
  • Operational additions: Increased flying missions accounted for $10.4 billion in costs, while higher fuel prices added another $2.7 billion [70].

Industry Impact

  • Defense production: The depletion estimate is driving tens of billions of dollars in production agreements involving Raytheon, Northrop Grumman and Lockheed Martin to address interceptor and other munitions shortfalls [19].
