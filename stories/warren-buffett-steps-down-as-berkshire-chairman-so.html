Warren Buffett steps down as Berkshire chairman; son Howard succeeds him

Category: Business

Warren Buffett stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway on Friday, ending more than six decades leading the conglomerate he took control of in 1965 and chaired from 1970. The 96-year-old became chairman emeritus effective immediately and will remain on Berkshire’s board. His oldest son, Howard Buffett, 71, succeeded him as non-executive chairman; Greg Abel remains chief executive after taking over that role in January 2026 [3][9][10][12]. Under Berkshire’s planned leadership separation, Abel is responsible for operating the businesses and allocating capital, while Howard Buffett oversees the board and protects the company’s culture and values. Howard has served as a Berkshire director since 1993, and Susan Decker remains lead independent director [7][14]. Buffett said in his shareholder letter that Abel had already made important decisions without his intervention and that the timing was appropriate to complete the transition [23][25].

Did you know: Berkshire’s original textile business was shut down in 1985 [23].

Key Points

  • Operating footprint: Berkshire controls close to 40 companies and has nearly 400,000 employees across its businesses [16][23].
  • Investment portfolio: The group holds a publicly traded portfolio worth more than $267 billion, according to La República, alongside its controlled businesses [16].
  • Long-run record: Berkshire generated compounded annual returns of 19.7% for shareholders during Buffett’s era, nearly twice the performance of the S&P 500 [23].
  • Shareholder tradition: Berkshire’s annual Omaha meeting, nicknamed the “Woodstock of Capitalists,” draws thousands of shareholders and admirers [23].

Perspectives

Warren Buffett: Buffett said he sought to avoid retaining control after his death and to keep learning and improving throughout life [25].

Sources: Cnyes.com

Greg Abel: Abel said Warren Buffett’s impact on Berkshire and its owners had no parallel in the history of American business and that the culture Buffett built would remain central [23].

Sources: The Standard

Michael Withers, University of Notre Dame: Withers described the change as continuity but said Abel and Howard Buffett would need to preserve Berkshire’s legacy while adapting to a different market environment [21][27].

Sources: VnExpress

Timeline

  • 1965: Buffett took control of the struggling New England textile company Berkshire [9][10].
  • 1970: Buffett became Berkshire Hathaway’s chairman and held the position until 2026 [9].
  • January 2026: Greg Abel became Berkshire Hathaway’s chief executive officer [6][10].
  • 18 September 2026: Berkshire announced Buffett’s immediate move to chairman emeritus [3][12].

Industry Impact

  • Berkshire became the first American company outside the technology sector to exceed $1 trillion in market value [12].
  • Buffett plans to continue communicating with shareholders through annual letters despite leaving Berkshire’s executive leadership [25].

Business Angle

  • Advisory independence: With Buffett out of management, Abel will no longer have him as a regular adviser, making the CEO’s independent decision-making more consequential [27].
  • Resumed buybacks could support per-share value if management purchases stock below intrinsic value, but their effectiveness depends on Berkshire’s valuation and future cash generation [23][common].
  • Howard Buffett’s background as a farmer and former Illinois sheriff differs from his father’s investment career [23][20].
  • Berkshire shares have underperformed the S&P 500 this year [23].
