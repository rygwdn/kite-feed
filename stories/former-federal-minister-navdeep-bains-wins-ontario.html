Former federal minister Navdeep Bains wins Ontario Liberal leadership

Category: Canada

Former federal industry minister Navdeep Bains won the Ontario Liberal leadership on Saturday, beating Toronto-area member of provincial parliament Lee Fairclough by 50.3% to 49.7% on the third ballot. Bains, who has no seat in the provincial legislature, said he intends to run for one but did not say where or when. He ruled out asking a sitting Liberal MPP to resign for him [2][1][3]. Bains takes charge of a party with 14 seats, behind the governing Progressive Conservatives’ 79 and the New Democrats’ 27. The Liberals have lost three straight provincial elections since Ontario Premier Doug Ford’s party first took office in 2018, and the next election may not come until 2030. Bains campaigned on jobs, economic growth and affordability, while his critics have pointed to his ties to former Prime Minister Justin Trudeau [6][8][2][1].

This may be the end of a leadership race, ladies and gentleman, but this is just the beginning of our campaign to replace Doug Ford and the Conservative Party of Ontario

— Navdeep Bains, Ontario Liberal leader (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Member turnout: Of about 55,000 eligible members, 20,510 cast ballots; the party reported turnout of roughly 37%, up from about 22% in its 2023 leadership vote [6][2].
  • Ballot rounds: Bains led with 44.1% on the first ballot. Dylan Marando was eliminated in that round, followed by Stephanie Bowman in the second, when she received 15.7% [6][5].
  • Party outlook: A Liaison Strategies poll during the race found that all four candidates were little known to Ontario voters. A recent Abacus Data survey put the Liberals at 33%, behind the PCs at 39% and ahead of the NDP at 18% [5][4].

Perspectives

Navdeep Bains, Ontario Liberal leader: Bains said he wants to build a grassroots movement and raise more money than the party has ever raised to challenge Ford’s Conservatives [4].

Sources: The Toronto Star

Doug Ford, Ontario premier: Ford said families want parties to work collaboratively, put the province over party and focus on standing up to tariffs, protecting workers and making life more affordable [1].

Timeline

  • 2018: Ontario Liberals lost government and official party status to Ford’s PCs [9][6].
  • 2020: Steven Del Duca became leader after Kathleen Wynne resigned [1][9].
  • 2022: Del Duca resigned after the Liberals lost the provincial election [1][6].
  • 27 February 2025: Liberals won 14 seats, restoring official party status [6].
  • January 2026: Bonnie Crombie resigned, leaving John Fraser as interim leader [1][5].
  • 21 November 2026: Bains won the leadership contest at the party convention in Toronto [1][2].

Historical Background

After nearly 15 years in government, the Liberals suffered a steep decline, winning only eight seats in 2022 [9][6].

Q&A

Q: Why did the Liberals win more votes than the NDP but fewer seats in 2025?

A: The Liberals received 30% of the provincewide vote, while the NDP received 18.6%; the NDP’s support was more concentrated in ridings where its candidates won, producing more seats [6].
