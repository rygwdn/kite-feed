First Nations youth plan Dec. 8 protest over federal projects bill

Category: Canada

First Nations youth from Attawapiskat plan a national day of action in Ottawa on Dec. 8 to oppose Bill C-39, the federal Building Canada Strong Act, saying faster approvals could sideline Indigenous rights and environmental protections [1][3][4]. The date coincides with an Assembly of First Nations meeting expected to draw hundreds of chiefs to the capital [2][4]. The bill would coordinate and shorten federal reviews for major infrastructure, energy and resource projects. During the committee’s review, Liberal MPs rejected most New Democrat proposals to strengthen references to Indigenous rights and consent. Liberals say existing laws and the bill preserve the government’s duty to consult, and point to a proposed Crown Consultation Hub [4].

We have to draw a line in the sand and say, ‘No more.’

— Jeronimo Kataquapit, Here We Stand youth leader (Winnipeg Free Press)

Key Points

  • Labour powers: Bill C-39 would let a federal minister order a federally regulated business to resume operations or refer parties to binding arbitration [1][2].
  • Law changes: The omnibus bill proposes amendments to the Impact Assessment Act, Fisheries Act and Species at Risk Act, among other federal statutes [3].
  • Committee dispute: New Democrat MP Leah Gazan said points of order interrupted her questioning on environmental protections [4].

Perspectives

First Nations youth organizers: Ramon Kataquapit said the Dec. 8 demonstration had support from Idle No More and Onion Lake First Nation, and organizers planned to maintain a presence on Parliament Hill [3].

Sources: APTN News

Federal government officials: Officials told the committee they did not expect the bill to rush Indigenous consultation, saying work with affected communities would begin well before a project reached the regulator [4].

Sources: CBC News

Historical Background

Canada passed the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act in 2021, committing the federal government to align Canadian laws with the declaration [3]. The Canadian Press reported that Indigenous leaders also opposed similar fast-track major-project legislation advanced by Prime Minister Mark Carney’s government the previous year [1][2].
