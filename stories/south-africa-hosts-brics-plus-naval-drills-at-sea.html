South Africa hosts BRICS Plus naval drills at sea

Category: World

South Africa is hosting a week-long set of naval exercises in its coastal waters involving ships from China, Russia and Iran, described by the host as a “BRICS Plus” operation focused on protecting shipping and maritime economic activity [4][7][5]. The drills, branded “Will for Peace,” bring together countries that have tense relations with the United States and come as Pretoria’s already-strained ties with Washington face fresh scrutiny [1][3]. South African officials say the exercise was planned well in advance and aims at cooperation and regional maritime stability, while domestic and foreign critics warn it could deepen diplomatic friction [1][3].

Primary Source: View original article

This exercise was planned long before these tensions we are witnessing today.

— Bantu Holomisa (Dawn (via Reuters))

Did you know: The drills were referred to as “Exercise Mosi” in earlier iterations before being branded “Will for Peace” for this round [5].

Key Points

  • Who is involved: South Africa’s deputy defence minister said the United Arab Emirates was also expected to send ships, while Indonesia, Ethiopia and Brazil planned to send observers [1].
  • BRICS expansion: Sources describe “BRICS Plus” as an expanded grouping beyond the original five members, with additional countries now included in the bloc framework referenced around the drills [4][5].
  • Local politics: South Africa’s Democratic Alliance said parliament had not been properly briefed on the drills’ cost, command structure, or diplomatic consequences, and argued defence policy should remain transparent and constitutional [5].
  • G20 rescheduling: The manoeuvres were first slated for November 2025 but were postponed because they would have overlapped with the G20 summit in Johannesburg [1][5].
  • Indian Ocean signal: Analysis of the exercise frames it as a message about South Africa’s intention to project influence in a strategically important Indian Ocean region where Moscow and Beijing seek greater presence [2].

Perspectives

South African National Defence Force: The defence force says the drills are meant to share best practices, improve joint operational capability, and support safer shipping routes and regional maritime stability.

Timeline

  • November 2025: Drills were initially scheduled then, but postponed for G20 clash [1][5].
  • Wednesday: AFP journalists saw two Chinese ships in False Bay harbour [5].
  • Thursday: Iranian vessel joined the Chinese ships near Cape Town [5].
  • Friday: A Russian warship arrived near Simon’s Town ahead of exercises [1].
  • Saturday: China, Russia and Iran began a week of joint drills [4].

Technical Details

  • Corvette: One of the arriving Russian vessels is described as a corvette, a smaller warship class often used for coastal and escort roles [1].
  • Forward base vessel: Iran is reported to have sent a “forward base vessel,” a type of ship that can support operations away from home ports by providing sustainment functions [1].
  • Replenishment ship: China’s participation includes a replenishment ship, which typically provides fuel and supplies at sea to extend fleet endurance during operations [1].

Industry Impact

  • Port and logistics confidence: By emphasizing safer shipping routes and maritime security cooperation, organisers are positioning the exercise as supportive of regional shipping reliability, which matters to ports and logistics operators along major sea lanes [1][4].
  • Diplomacy and trade exposure: Coverage warns the drills risk worsening Pretoria’s ties with Washington amid earlier US actions such as expelling South Africa’s ambassador and imposing 30% trade tariffs, factors that can ripple into business planning and cross-border investment decisions [1][3].

Historical Background

BRICS began as a grouping of Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa and has since expanded to include additional members, a context the articles describe as “BRICS Plus” around the naval exercise [4][5]. South Africa has previously hosted similar multinational maritime drills, and this iteration continues that pattern while drawing extra attention because of current geopolitical rivalries involving some participants [5][2].

Q&A

Q: Which parts of South Africa’s coastline are used for the manoeuvres and why?

A: Reporting places activity around Simon’s Town and False Bay near Cape Town, suggesting the exercise uses nearby coastal waters connected to South Africa’s main naval facilities [1][5].

Q: How many BRICS Plus countries are mentioned as participating versus observing?
