US accuses six Chinese AI firms of distillation

Category: Technology

The FBI, National Security Agency and Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency accused six China-based artificial intelligence companies of using “industrial-scale” distillation campaigns to extract capabilities from U.S. frontier AI models since at least late 2024 [1][2][4][5]. The advisory named DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI, and said they extracted billions of tokens across millions of exchanges or requests from models including Anthropic’s Claude, OpenAI’s GPT, Google’s Gemini and SpaceXAI’s Grok, likely with Chinese government awareness [2][7][10][22]. U.S. agencies said distillation can be legitimate AI research, but alleged the Chinese campaigns aimed to extract restricted proprietary functions and reduce the cost and time needed to train frontier models [2][16][22]. The accusation came as President Trump’s administration prepares for Chinese President Xi Jinping’s planned visit to the United States in late September and for a mid-September U.S.-China dialogue on AI safety risks [10][17][6]. China rejected the allegations on Wednesday, with foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning saying China’s AI advances reflected scientific and technological self-reliance and urging Washington to stop making unfounded accusations [1][43][16]. China’s Ministry of Commerce called the U.S. accusations groundless, described them as double standards, and said Washington was using distillation enforcement as a pretext to seek an industrial monopoly [13][27][44].

Did you know: Anthropic alleged in a June letter that Alibaba used 25,000 fraudulent accounts to exchange 28.8 million conversations with Claude between April and June [51].

Key Points

  • Access methods: The advisory said the firms routed requests through fraudulent or shared accounts, cloud services, aggregators and transfer-station proxies to bypass geographic limits, usage caps and detection [2][11][24].
  • Enforcement threat: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent warned that sanctions and Entity List designations would be options when Chinese firms’ covert distillation crossed into intellectual-property theft [24][14].
  • Company targeting: The advisory accused DeepSeek of distilling training data and capabilities from Claude, Gemini, GPT-4, GPT-5 and Grok 4, while Moonshot allegedly extracted Claude Fable 5 data for Kimi-K3 and GPT-4o data for Kimi-K2 [4].
  • Defensive guidance: U.S. agencies urged AI providers to detect malicious prompts, use deceptive or degraded responses against suspected distillation, and share intelligence across providers [3][5].

Perspectives

U.S. security agencies: The NSA, CISA and FBI characterized the alleged campaigns as “aggressive, malicious, and targeted” and said their scale indicated distillation formed a core development strategy for the named Chinese firms.

Technical Details

  • Chain-of-thought extraction: The advisory described prompts that sought restricted reasoning traces and named chain-of-thought extraction as an advanced distillation tactic used to capture higher-value model behavior [2][7].
  • Automated failover: U.S. agencies said automated systems switched between access pathways during blocking attempts and tested whether model providers had degraded responses as a defensive countermeasure [2][7].
  • MITRE ATLAS mapping: The agencies mapped the alleged tactics to MITRE ATLAS stages including resource development, access, execution, discovery, AI attack staging, collection, exfiltration and impact [4].

Industry Impact

  • Frontier labs: U.S. officials estimate unauthorized distillation costs American AI developers billions of dollars in annual revenue, according to YNA’s account of the dispute [51].
  • Model security: Anthropic has argued that illicitly distilled models may lack necessary safeguards, creating national-security risks if safety controls are stripped from copied capabilities [28][51].

Historical Background

U.S. AI companies and officials had raised similar complaints before this week’s advisory: a senior adviser to President Trump accused Moonshot in July of copying Anthropic technology [26]. YNA reported that U.S. officials also made similar claims before President Trump’s April visit to Beijing [33].

Business Angle

  • Cloud and API providers may need higher compliance spending if cross-provider monitoring, account verification and prompt-detection systems become baseline defenses [3][2].

Action Items

  • Audit AI API access patterns: Review logs for high-volume prompt clusters, shared-account behavior, proxy routing and repeated attempts to elicit restricted reasoning, which U.S. agencies identified as distillation indicators [2][11].
