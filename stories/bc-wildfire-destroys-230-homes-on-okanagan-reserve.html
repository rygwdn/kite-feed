B.C. wildfire destroys 230 homes on Okanagan reserve

Category: Canada

The Bradley Creek wildfire destroyed about 230 homes on Okanagan Indian Band land near Vernon, B.C., Chief Dan Wilson said Tuesday, including 30 homes belonging to band members and 200 owned by non-members [1][2][7][9][10][6][12][13][14][15][16]. Wilson said the loss ranks among the most destructive wildfire events in the band’s history and among Canada’s worst recent wildfire losses [1][2][9][12][15]. The fire started late Friday and expanded Saturday as winds gusted up to 115 km/h, burning through a lakeside area on the northwest shore of Okanagan Lake and into forested reserve land [1][2][4][9][12][15]. The BC Wildfire Service said the Bradley Creek fire showed no significant growth on Tuesday [4][6]. Bradley Creek was one of six B.C. “wildfires of note” as crews fought fires that forced thousands from their homes across the province [4][6]. More than 1,300 firefighters were battling wildfires in British Columbia after Saturday’s windstorm spread fires around northern Okanagan Lake [3][5].

It was hard to recognize my community from the air with all the structures and the forest gone.

— Okanagan Indian Band Chief Dan Wilson (The Canadian Press via Lethbridge Herald)

Key Points

  • Evacuation speed: Wilson said some Okanagan Indian Band members had only five minutes to evacuate as the fire moved through the community [9][12][15].
  • Critical sites: Wilson said aerial crews helped save a daycare centre and a newly built school by repeatedly dropping water on the area [12].
  • Other fires: The Brunswick Creek and Ainslie Creek fire complex covered a combined 480 square kilometres near Boston Bar, while the Pear Lake fire measured more than 1,150 square kilometres [4][6].
  • Fire outlook: Officials expected the Brunswick Creek and Ainslie Creek fires to increase in activity on Wednesday and Thursday [4].

Perspectives

Okanagan Indian Band Chief Dan Wilson: The chief said the reserve “looked like a war zone” after he viewed the burned area from a helicopter and described the event as “surreal.”

Evacuee Atlyn Becker: Becker said she saw an orange glow climbing the hill as she drove away from her home near Okanagan Lake.

Evacuee Gord Ross: Ross said he could see propane tanks exploding and black smoke, which told him houses were burning rather than only grass or trees.

Sources: The Canadian Press via Lethbridge Herald

Resident Louis Fred: Fred said extreme winds blew through his home on the Okanagan Indian Band reserve and embers singed his son’s hair.

Timeline

  • July 2026: The Brunswick and Ainslie fires began threatening Boston Bar [4].
  • July 2026: Lightning triggered the Pear Lake fire in mid-July [4].
  • 31 July 2026: The Bradley Creek fire started late Friday [1][2].
  • 1 August 2026: A windstorm drove rapid fire spread around Okanagan Lake [3][5].
  • 4 August 2026: Wilson announced that about 230 homes had been destroyed [1][9].

Industry Impact

  • Firefighting resources: Crews assigned to the Brunswick Creek and Ainslie Creek complex included 109 firefighters, structure protection crews and 18 helicopters [4].
  • Emergency management: The fires prompted evacuation orders, including one covering the entire community of Boston Bar in the Fraser Canyon [4].

Historical Background

Recent Canadian wildfire losses cited for comparison include the 2024 wildfire in Jasper, Alberta, which destroyed about 350 homes, and the 2023 McDougall Creek wildfire in B.C.’s Okanagan region, which destroyed about 200 homes [12][15]. The Pear Lake blaze became part of the Big Bar complex, which destroyed homes near Clinton [4].

Humanitarian Impact

About 8,000 people had been evacuated across British Columbia, Emergency Management Minister Kelly Greene said, and there were 37 evacuation orders in effect [12]. Volunteers in B.C.’s North Okanagan were providing help and supplies to wildfire evacuees [11].
