YouTube tightens monetization rules for low-quality AI videos

Category: AI

YouTube has clarified how its YouTube Partner Program treats “inauthentic content,” listing more specific types of videos that cannot earn money through the platform’s monetization program [2][4][5][6]. The July 16 policy revision targets low-quality material that creators can produce quickly with AI and other tools, while YouTube said the change adds nuance to existing rules rather than creating a blanket ban on AI-generated content [2][6]. The revised guidance identifies three non-monetizable categories: generic, repetitive or template-based content; off-putting or emotionally manipulative content; and videos using AI personas to discuss sensitive subjects such as health, finance or legal issues [2][4][5][6]. Channels that publish large volumes of such content may lose eligibility for the YouTube Partner Program, which lets creators earn revenue through advertising and subscriptions [2][6]. In a Creator Insider video, YouTube trust and safety chief Matt Halprin said AI can support high-quality creative work but that the platform wants to limit automated, low-effort videos [4][5]. TechCrunch reported that YouTube has a financial incentive to keep low-quality content out of the Partner Program as the platform competes with television and streaming services for advertising budgets [2].

Key Points

  • Definition challenge: YouTube says AI slop is hard to define precisely, likening it to the 'you know it when you see it' standard for obscenity, and says its updated categories are meant to give creators clearer boundaries for permissible AI use [2].
  • Off-putting category: YouTube’s 'off-putting' label is a separate non-monetizable category that goes beyond traditional harmful-content rules, allowing the platform to demonetize videos it considers unpleasant or emotionally manipulative even if they do not break other guidelines [5][4].
  • Clickbait examples: The off-putting category includes videos that deliberately create distress, fear or emotional manipulation, such as staged animal-in-danger rescue clips designed to attract clicks [5][6].

Historical Background

YouTube’s Partner Program ties creator earnings to platform rules on advertiser suitability and originality, giving the company a way to limit revenue from content that violates monetization standards [common]. The current update follows YouTube’s 2025 effort to limit revenue from “inauthentic content,” including mass-produced and repetitive videos [2][4].

Business Angle

  • YouTube is using monetization eligibility, not an outright ban, to reduce the financial incentive for low-quality supply while leaving AI tools available to creators who add original work [common].
  • The policy links ad eligibility to brand-safe, original content rather than auto-generated videos that YouTube classifies as inauthentic [common].
  • The rules may shift revenue away from channels built on mass production and repetition and toward channels that invest in original production [common].

Action Items

  • Audit monetized YouTube content: Creators should review recent uploads for generic templates, repetitive tutorials, emotionally manipulative scenarios or AI personas giving advice on sensitive topics before relying on YPP revenue [2][5][6].
  • Add original production elements: Channels using AI should document original scripting, commentary, editing or reporting so videos do not appear to be cookie-cutter content with minimal variation [5][common].
