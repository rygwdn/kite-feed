Apple plans explicit Mac disk-access controls as AI agents expand

Category: Technology

Apple said it will tighten macOS Full Disk Access controls, requiring explicit user approval before an app receives broad access to a Mac’s data. The company warned that some developers use the permission without users fully understanding its scope: apps with access can read files, email, messages and browsing history [15][3][1]. The announcement followed claims by Inc. columnist Jason Aten that Meta’s Muse AI agent accessed private Messages content on his Mac, although he believed he had not granted that access. Meta spokesperson Andy Stone disputed the account, saying Muse requires both Full Disk Access and a separate Messages connector to read messages. Apple did not name Muse or any other app in its announcement [15][6]. Apple said the risks will rise as agents become more capable and autonomous, and warned that access to communications can expose information about people other than the Mac user. It has not detailed the new controls, announced when they will arrive or clarified whether they will affect permissions already granted [3][6].

Key Points

  • Agent adoption: OpenAI said more than 35 million people use its ChatGPT Work and Codex agent tools, up from 10 million in July [7].
  • Messages integration: OpenAI’s opt-in ChatGPT Mac feature can read, summarize, write and send texts and requires Full Disk Access to use Apple Messages [7].

Perspectives

Patrick Wardle: The Mac security expert said Full Disk Access can allow an app to read non-root files, including browser histories, cookies and chats [6].

Sources: PCMag

Timeline

  • August 2026: OpenAI added a Mac feature that uses Messages with Full Disk Access [7]
  • September 2026: Jason Aten said Meta’s Muse referenced his private Messages conversation [6][15]
  • 2 October 2026: Apple announced plans for additional Full Disk Access controls [15][3]

Action Items

  • Review which apps have Full Disk Access. On a Mac, open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Full Disk Access. Disable access for apps that do not need it [1][6].
