Israel strikes Syria as US envoy disputes warning

Category: World

Israel said it targeted a “terrorist” in southern Syria on Saturday. Syria’s Foreign Ministry said an Israeli drone hit a civilian vehicle near Beit Jinn, southwest of Damascus, injuring civilians; Syrian state television said one person was wounded [5][3][8][2][25][53]. The Israeli military said the target was in the “final stages” of preparing attacks and posed an immediate threat to Israeli troops, while Damascus called the strike a violation of Syrian sovereignty and international law [8][2][54][62]. The strike followed an Israeli attack on Tuesday on the Abu al-Duhur military airbase in northwestern Syria near the Turkish border. U.S. Ambassador to Turkiye and Special Envoy to Syria Tom Barrack called that strike an “unnecessary escalation that does not advance regional stability” [4][6][8][46][16]. Barrack said Israel did not give Washington or Ankara sufficient advance notice, suggested the airbase strike may have been intended to “bait” Turkiye, and said U.S. intelligence agencies did not confirm Israel’s claim that Turkish military assets were moving to the base [4][7][10][12][13][31][46]. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz rejected Barrack’s account as “full of inaccuracies.” Katz said Israel shared intelligence with U.S. authorities before the airbase strike and sent a warning to senior Syrian officials before Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Katz authorized the operation [4][6][11][45][51]. Netanyahu’s office said Israel and Syria had agreed to a security status quo that Syria was about to breach by allowing Turkish troops to deploy at an airbase near Aleppo [8][46][19].

Did you know: Barrack compared the risk around the Abu al-Duhur strike to Turkiye’s 2015 downing of a Russian fighter near the Syrian border, an incident that sharply worsened Ankara-Moscow relations [60][49].

Key Points

  • Turkish risk: Barrack said Israeli aircraft flew within about 80 kilometers of Turkiye’s border and that Ankara could have considered scrambling jets without knowing the aircraft’s intent [13][31][47][26].
  • Airbase damage: Syrian reports cited by regional sources said eight Israeli strikes hit Abu al-Duhur on Aug. 18, damaging runways and storage facilities, while AP reported damage but no casualties [26][32][46].
  • Israeli rationale: Israeli officials said the Abu al-Duhur operation was meant to prevent a Turkish military buildup near Israel’s border, citing Turkiye’s presence across roughly 5% of Syria and Erdogan’s calls for Israel’s destruction [24][45].
  • Post-Assad context: After Bashar al-Assad’s overthrow in December 2024, Israel carried out hundreds of strikes in Syria and deployed troops into a UN-patrolled Golan Heights buffer zone [6][16][18][28].

Historical Background

Israel captured most of the Golan Heights from Syria in the 1967 Arab-Israeli War and later annexed the territory it controlled; the United States recognized Israeli sovereignty there under President Trump, while most countries do not [common][6]. The UN-patrolled buffer zone between Israeli and Syrian forces dates to post-1973 disengagement arrangements on the Golan front [common]. After Assad’s fall in December 2024, Israel expanded operations in Syria, and Israel, Syria and Turkiye entered new security disputes [6][18].

  • 🇹🇷 Turkiye: Ankara denied Israeli allegations about Turkish troops or assets at Abu al-Duhur and accused Netanyahu’s government of using “cheap politics” for electoral gain [26][58].
  • 🇯🇴 Jordan: Jordan condemned the Beit Jinn strike and joined Arab and Islamic countries in condemning Israeli strikes on the Abu al-Duhur airbase [22][65].
