Horse whinnies combine whistle-like and voice-like sounds, study finds

Category: Science

A new study in the journal Current Biology finds that horses produce their familiar whinny by generating two distinct sound components at the same time—one that behaves like a whistle and another that resembles a voice-like tone [2][7][6][4][5]. Researchers describe it as a layered call, which helps explain why horse vocalizations can sound surprisingly high-pitched for such a large animal [6][5]. Across coverage of the work, scientists point to the horse’s larynx as the key to producing this dual signal, offering a clearer anatomical explanation for a sound many people know from stables and fields [7][5][2]. The Guardian adds that horses use whinnies in social situations, including keeping in touch with other horses and showing anticipation around feeding time [4].

Key Points

  • Where published: The research appeared February 23 in Cell Press’s Current Biology, according to coverage citing the paper’s release date and journal [2].
  • Atypical in nature: New Scientist says the horse whinny is unusual among animal sounds because two sound sources operate at the same time [5].
  • Social use: The Guardian reports that horses whinny to connect socially—finding new friends, greeting familiar horses, and showing excitement around feeding time [4].
  • Explaining pitch: The New York Times notes that this mechanism helps explain how horses can produce notably high-pitched calls despite their large body size [6].
