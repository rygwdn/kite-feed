SpaceX Falcon 9 stage to strike the moon Wednesday

Category: Science

An abandoned SpaceX Falcon 9 upper stage is expected to hit the moon on Wednesday, Aug. 5, at about 2:35 a.m. ET, or 06:35 UTC, near Einstein Crater on the sunlit western to northwestern limb, traveling at roughly 5,400 mph, or 8,700 kph [2][23][24][18][15]. The stage launched on Jan. 15, 2025, carrying Firefly Aerospace’s Blue Ghost lander and ispace’s Hakuto-R Resilience lander, then remained in a high Earth orbit that crossed the moon’s path [2][7][19][13]. SpaceX said the collision was not intentional and that solar activity and gravitational forces changed the stage’s trajectory after the company followed passivation rules for the hardware [23][28][13]. Independent tracker Bill Gray followed the object for months, identified an Aug. 5 close lunar encounter in September 2025 and later concluded it would become a direct impact [2][14]. The strike is not expected to affect Earth, while astronomers will try to observe the ejecta plume from telescopes and lunar orbiters [2][15][19][8].

Primary Source: View original article

This impact will not be a problem. But in a future where there are long-term bases on the moon, similar impacts would be an issue and we need not to leave rocket stages in chaotic orbits of this kind.

— Jonathan McDowell (Fast Company)

Key Points

  • Crater estimates: Researchers estimate the crash could produce a crater at least 17 meters to about 27 meters wide, with some estimates putting the range at 50 to 100 feet [24][13][22][19].
  • Observation plans: NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter has already imaged the predicted site and is expected to image it afterward, while South Korea’s Danuri orbiter plans to use its LUTI high-resolution camera to record the impact [19][24][8].
  • Visibility limits: Experts said the impact flash may last less than a second and be too dim to see, though ejecta could extend for several miles and remain visible to telescopes for tens of minutes [15][18].
  • Prevention work: SpaceX said it is working with NASA on ways to prevent similar upper-stage lunar collisions, and space experts said a solar-orbit disposal maneuver could have avoided this crash [23][15].

Perspectives

SpaceX: Julianna Scheiman, SpaceX’s director of NASA science and Dragon programs, said the company passivated the stage under applicable rules and is studying prevention options with NASA.

Timeline

  • 15 January 2025: Falcon 9 launched the Blue Ghost and Resilience moon landers [19][13].
  • September 2025: Gray identified an Aug. 5 lunar close approach [2][14].
  • March 2026: Refined calculations showed the stage would hit the moon [2][14].
  • April 2026: Gray first publicly reported the projected collision [23].
  • 3 August 2026: SpaceX discussed the case at a NASA briefing [23].
  • 5 August 2026: Impact is expected near Einstein Crater [23][6].

Technical Details

  • Tracking challenge: Bill Gray noted that while Earth-moon gravity is well understood, the rocket's tumbling and the constantly changing solar radiation pressure on its long cylindrical shape made precise orbit predictions difficult [14].
  • Upper-stage parameters: The impactor is described as roughly 12 meters long and about 3,900 to 4,000 kilograms, giving modelers a better-known projectile than a natural meteoroid [2][22][28].

Scientific Significance

  • Observation uncertainty: Updated amateur observations placed the site slightly farther northwest and more beyond the lunar limb than earlier estimates, which Jonathan McDowell said could make the event harder to see from Earth [18].

Historical Background

Human-made objects have struck the moon many times, including deliberate impacts by spacecraft and rocket stages during earlier exploration missions [common]. A relevant accidental precedent came in 2022, when a Chinese rocket segment hit the lunar far side and carved a pair of craters [15][29][18].

Action Items

  • Contribute telescope observations: Professional and amateur astronomers can image the predicted impact region around 06:35 UTC on Aug. 5 and share usable data, as researchers requested in a recent paper [19][18].
