Apple raises Music, One and iCloud+ subscription prices

Category: Technology

Apple raised prices for Apple Music and some Apple One bundles in several markets, including the United States, Germany, Singapore, Thailand, Croatia and Türkiye. Apple cited “rising licensing costs” for the Apple Music increase [4][5][6][14][15][16][13]. In the U.S., Apple Music Individual rose from $10.99 to $11.99 a month, Family from $16.99 to $19.99 and Student from $5.99 to $6.99; Apple One Family increased from $25.95 to $27.95 and Premier from $37.95 to $39.95, while Apple One Individual stayed at $19.95 [4][6][5]. Apple also raised iCloud+ prices in eight countries: Nigeria, Türkiye, Vietnam, Japan, Egypt, New Zealand, the Philippines and Indonesia [3][9][12][21]. The increases ranged from 11% to 55% depending on plan and country. Nigeria’s 50GB plan rose from ₦900 to ₦1,300, and Türkiye’s 50GB plan rose from 39.99 TL to 49.99 TL; U.S. iCloud+ pricing did not change [3].

Key Points

  • Subscriber timing: New Apple Music subscribers in Germany pay the higher prices immediately, while existing subscribers move to the new rates from the next billing period after agreeing to the change [11][18].
  • Bundle math: 9to5Mac calculated Apple One Individual at $19.95 against $32.96 if its included services were bought separately, while Apple One Family costs $27.95 against a $42.96 separate-services total [8].
  • Türkiye pricing: In Türkiye, Apple Music Individual rose from 59.99 TL to 89.99 TL, a nearly 50% increase, while the Family plan jumped from 99.99 TL to 149.99 TL [13].
  • Vietnam iCloud+: In Vietnam, iCloud+ 50GB rose from 19,000 VND to 29,000 VND, and the 2TB plan increased from 199,000 VND to 299,000 VND [9].

Perspectives

MacRumors: MacRumors cited currency movements as a likely reason for iCloud+ changes, noting the yen weakened over the past year and the Turkish lira depreciated against the dollar.

Sources: MacRumors

9to5Mac: 9to5Mac listed the subscription changes alongside other Apple increases, including hardware, AppleCare+ and Japan iPhone pricing.

Sources: 9to5Mac

Timeline

  • October 2022: Apple last raised Apple Music prices, with the U.S. Individual plan moving from $9.99 to $10.99 a month [5][4].
  • October 2023: Apple One bundles last increased before the 2026 adjustment [6].
  • 25 June 2026: Apple raised prices on several Mac and iPad products, including MacBook Neo to $699, the 13-inch MacBook Air to $1,299, and the base iPad to $449 [7].
  • 17 July 2026: Apple’s site showed the Apple Music and Apple One price changes [4].
  • 17 July 2026: Apple updated iCloud+ prices in eight countries [3].

Industry Impact

  • Music streaming: Apple’s U.S. Individual Apple Music plan now costs $11.99, below Spotify Premium’s $12.99 after Spotify’s February 2026 increase [6][5].
  • Music rights: WinFuture said Apple tied higher Apple Music prices to increased payments to record companies, publishers and artists [11].
  • Cloud storage: Apple’s iCloud+ paid tiers cover 50GB, 200GB, 2TB, 6TB and 12TB, and all of those tiers changed in several listed markets [3][12][21].

Business Angle

  • 9to5Mac calculated Apple One savings versus buying included services separately, listing Individual at $19.95 against $32.96 separately and Family at $27.95 against $42.96 separately [8].
  • Family plans had the largest Apple Music dollar increases in the U.S., Germany and Singapore [4][11][14].
  • Apple attributed the June hardware price increases to rising memory and storage component costs, with CEO Tim Cook citing AI data center demand for high-bandwidth memory [7].
  • Apple also raised iPhone prices in Japan by up to 11% in July [7].

Action Items

  • Check renewal pricing: Open your Apple ID subscription settings before the next billing date to see whether Apple Music, Apple One or iCloud+ now shows a higher local price [11][21].
  • Recalculate Apple One value: Compare the bundle price with only the Apple services you actually use; 9to5Mac lists Apple One tier contents and separate-service totals [8].
