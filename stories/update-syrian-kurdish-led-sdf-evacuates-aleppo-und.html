Update: Syrian Kurdish-led SDF evacuates Aleppo under ceasefire

Category: World

In the latest development in northern Syria, Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) fighters have left Aleppo after a ceasefire understanding with Syria’s transitional authorities, with buses transporting the final group out of Sheikh Maqsoud toward SDF-held areas in the northeast [1][10][5][9]. The SDF said the deal also enabled the evacuation of wounded people, bodies, and trapped civilians from the Ashrafiyeh and Sheikh Maqsoud districts [3][11][10]. The clashes erupted after talks on integrating the SDF’s administration and forces into the new Syrian state structures stalled, and the withdrawal is being framed by multiple parties as a step back from further fighting in the city [3][6][7].

Did you know: Aleppo is one of the world’s oldest continuously inhabited cities, with a long history as a regional trade hub [common].

Key Points

  • Bus evacuations: AFP reporters cited by multiple outlets described several buses moving fighters out of the Kurdish-majority Sheikh Maqsoud district under escort, with additional buses seen entering and leaving as the area calmed [3][6].
  • Operations halted: Syria’s military announced “a halt to all military operations” in Sheikh Maqsoud, according to state media carried by Dawn [6].
  • Surrender claim: Syrian state television said some Kurdish fighters who “announced their surrender” were transported by bus to Tabaqa in the northeast [3][6].
  • Diplomatic push: The US envoy, Tom Barrack, met Syria’s President Ahmed al-Sharaa and called for a “return to dialogue” in line with an integration agreement sealed last year [6][2].
  • Negotiations urged: The United States and the European Union publicly urged Syrian authorities and Kurdish leaders to return to negotiations following the violence [3].

Technical Details

  • Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF): The SDF is a Kurdish-led armed coalition that controls parts of northern and eastern Syria and has been backed by the United States, making its integration into Syrian state structures a central political and security issue [11][2][3].
  • Ceasefire evacuation corridors: The agreement described by the SDF and state media centered on moving fighters and civilians out by bus from the Ashrafiyeh and Sheikh Maqsoud areas, a common conflict-management tool intended to stop urban fighting and reduce civilian harm [3][1][10].
  • Integration negotiations: Several reports tie the violence to stalled negotiations over merging the SDF’s de facto administration and military into Syria’s new government, highlighting that governance arrangements—not only frontline control—can drive escalations [3][6][7].

Historical Background

The SDF emerged during Syria’s civil war as a Kurdish-led force that later partnered with the United States against ISIL (ISIS) [common]. In parts of northern and eastern Syria, it developed a de facto autonomous administration that has periodically clashed and negotiated with Damascus over governance and security arrangements [common]. In this round, several outlets say the immediate trigger was stalled talks over folding that administration and its military structures into Syria’s new government framework [3][7][6].
