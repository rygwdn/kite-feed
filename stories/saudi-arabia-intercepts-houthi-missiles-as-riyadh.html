Saudi Arabia intercepts Houthi missiles as Riyadh airport closure is extended

Category: World

Saudi Arabia said its forces intercepted missiles fired toward Riyadh and the southwestern city of Khamis Mushait, while Yemen’s Houthi movement claimed it had again targeted King Khalid International Airport and a military airbase. Authorities reported no immediate damage from the latest attempted strikes [6][3][10][33]. Riyadh’s airport, closed after repeated attacks, will remain shut through Saturday, Oct. 17, for safety inspections. Airlines have suspended or cancelled services to Saudi cities, and the Federal Aviation Administration advised U.S. carriers to exercise caution when flying to or over the kingdom [3][74][75]. Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman vowed a serious response, while President Donald Trump said he was considering U.S. strikes against the Houthis. Houthi official Mohammed al-Bukhaiti warned that U.S. involvement would draw a “swift and painful response.” Iran has offered to help facilitate dialogue between the sides [3][8][33].

Key Points

  • Air defense review: Saudi Arabia is investigating how missiles and drones penetrated its defenses; a separate Reuters report said the government had stopped issuing public alerts before four recent Riyadh attacks [8][73].
  • Flight disruption: Cirium put the cancellation rate across Saudi airports at 46.4%, while OPSGROUP advised airlines to avoid the southern Middle East corridor and route Europe–Asia flights over Central Asia [75][74].
  • Wider fighting: The Yemeni Network for Rights and Freedoms said a five-year-old girl was killed when shelling struck her family home in Taiz’s al-Misrakh district; it also reported more than 180 projectiles fired at residential areas and drone attacks [51].
  • Energy exposure: Aramco chief Amin Nasser said no single country can protect energy supplies alone and warned that attacks on regional infrastructure can affect economies worldwide [9].
  • Business disruption: The WPC Energy Congress continued in Riyadh, but some senior oil executives skipped the event or joined virtually as the airport remained closed [9].

Historical Background

Yemen’s civil war began in 2014, when the Houthis ousted the government from Sanaa; a Saudi-led coalition intervened the following year to support government forces [69]. An informal ceasefire lasting about four years began to unravel in July 2026, after the Houthis announced a maritime embargo against Saudi Arabia and resumed missile and drone attacks [69]. In September, Houthi forces advanced along Yemen’s southwestern Red Sea coast and took areas overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait, prompting a Saudi-backed government counteroffensive [4][69].

Humanitarian Impact

In Yemen’s Taiz province, the Yemeni Network for Rights and Freedoms reported five civilians killed and 17 injured in Houthi attacks over 24 hours; it said shelling damaged homes, vehicles, a hospital and a mosque [51].
