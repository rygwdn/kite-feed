Trump Accounts for children launch with S&P 500 default

Category: Business

Trump Accounts, a new federal savings and investment vehicle for U.S. children under 18, went live on July 4 as the United States began its 250th Independence Day celebrations [8][11][9]. The Treasury Department said more than 6 million accounts had been opened, including 1.4 million that will receive the $1,000 federal pilot contribution for newborns [6][11]. Children born from Jan. 1, 2025, through Dec. 31, 2028, who have Social Security numbers and are U.S. citizens qualify for the federal $1,000 seed money [2]. Treasury said initial contributions will be invested by default in the State Street SPDR Portfolio S&P 500 ETF, or SPYM, which it said was chosen for broad U.S. stock-market exposure and expenses below the statutory fee limit [2]. Parents, relatives and friends may contribute up to $5,000 a year in after-tax dollars before the year in which the child turns 18, while participating employers may contribute up to $2,500 [2][8]. Michael and Susan Dell pledged $6.25 billion, equal to $250 for 25 million children, and Treasury said the accounts can accept donations of public stock [2][8].

Did you know: The IRS directs parents to use Form 4547, which The Guardian described as an apparent reference to Trump being the 45th and 47th president [4].

Key Points

  • Account control: The child owns the account, but a parent, legal guardian or authorized adult serves as custodian until the beneficiary turns 18 [6][11].
  • Tax treatment: Earnings grow tax-deferred. Withdrawals generally cannot occur until the year the child turns 18, and they are taxed as ordinary income except for the after-tax contribution portion [6][11].
  • Corporate backing: Visa, Dell and Comcast were among participating companies, and Micron pledged $250 million to support Trump Accounts earlier in the launch week [10].
  • App functions: Treasury said the app lets families view balances, track contributions and investment performance, set recurring contributions, and use financial education modules [12][8].
  • Older children: Children born from 2016 through 2024 do not qualify for the federal $1,000 contribution, though some may qualify for a $250 Dell-funded payment in ZIP codes with median income of $150,000 or less [5].
