Canada marks 30th National Indigenous Peoples Day nationwide

Category: Canada

Communities across Canada held events Sunday for the 30th annual National Indigenous Peoples’ Day, which honours the cultures, histories and contributions of First Nations, Inuit and Métis people [1][2][5][10]. The day coincides with the summer solstice and closes Indigenous Peoples Month. Then-governor general Romeo LeBlanc proclaimed it in 1996 after years of advocacy from Indigenous groups [1][2][5]. Events included Prime Minister Mark Carney’s visit to a Snuneymuxw culture exhibit at the Nanaimo Museum in British Columbia, a ceremony for the National Aboriginal Veterans Monument in Ottawa, and Métis National Council president Victoria Pruden’s appearance at the Rhythms of the Homeland event in Ottawa [2][3][5]. Other gatherings featured Métis food, music and dance in Ottawa; dancing and storytelling near Saskatoon; canoe activities and a salmon dinner in Vancouver; a Batchewana First Nation powwow at the Sault Ste. Marie Canal National Historic Site; and Calgary events including a Walk for Reconciliation [2][4][9].

Key Points

  • Veterans ceremony: Aboriginal Veterans Autochtones hosted the Ottawa ceremony on the 25th anniversary of the National Aboriginal Veterans Monument’s unveiling. About 100 veterans, serving Armed Forces members and past attendees were present [1][5].
  • Monument design: The bronze sculpture by Noel Lloyd Pinay of Peepeekisis First Nation depicts four warriors with an elk, bear, buffalo and wolf beneath an eagle meant to represent the Creator [1][5].
  • Calgary focus: About 200 people joined Calgary’s annual Walk for Reconciliation from the Harry Hays Building to the Confluence Historic Site and Parkland [9].
  • Inclusion demand: The Congress of Aboriginal Peoples said federal consultations have not always accounted for the fact that most Indigenous people in Canada live off reserve, citing policy gaps affecting non-status and off-reserve communities [6].

Perspectives

Elder Clarence Wolfleg: Wolfleg urged unity during Calgary celebrations and rejected calls for Alberta separation, telling attendees that leaders should be guided by faith, honesty and kindness.
