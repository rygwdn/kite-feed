El Niño forecast points to delayed winter across much of Canada

Category: Canada

The Weather Network's three-month outlook calls for a milder-than-normal fall across much of Canada, with a potentially historic El Niño expected to extend autumn conditions and delay winter's arrival [1][2][3][4][6]. Senior meteorologist Doug Gillham said the season should trend milder and, in many regions, drier than normal as fall progresses [3]. The forecast links the pattern to El Niño, a climate phenomenon tied to warm water shifts in the Pacific Ocean [3]. Several forecasters say this event could rank among the strongest El Niños on record, while Le Devoir reported that El Niño will keep intensifying and that its effects will likely become more evident in winter [3][4][6].

Winter is probably going to wait on the sidelines for a while

— Doug Gillham (Canada's National Observer)

Key Points

  • Early swings: September and October are still expected to bring warm spells as well as bursts of chilly, stormy weather [1][3].
  • Storm pattern: Gillham said El Niño tends to push the storm track south of the border, draw milder Pacific air into Canada and limit southward plunges of Arctic air [3].
  • Seasonal effects: The pattern could mean fewer classic fall storms and a milder start to winter [3].
  • Global hazards: El Niño is expected to raise temperatures already elevated by human-caused climate change, increasing risks of serious floods, drought and extreme heat in some regions into 2027 [3].
