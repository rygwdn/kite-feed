Carney calls Alberta referendum plan a dangerous bluff

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney called Alberta’s referendum plan a “dangerous bluff” before a scheduled Calgary Stampede appearance, as Albertans prepare for an Oct. 19 vote on whether to stay in Canada or trigger a binding referendum on separation [1]. The debate has moved into public events, including Ponoka’s annual rodeo parade, where participants waved Canadian and Alberta flags while many spectators focused more on tossed candy than politicians [2][5]. A separatist campaign called Let Alberta Decide launched this week, arguing Alberta has the resources and economic strength to leave Canada and survive on its own [3]. The campaign says Alberta pays too much to Ottawa through equalization and other transfers while receiving too little back, and it cites what supporters call chronic under-representation in the House of Commons [3]. Ipsos polling cited by Darrell Bricker suggests separation remains a minority position: 18% of Albertans said they would vote to separate if a referendum were held today, support has fallen 10 points since January, and three-quarters of Albertans support remaining in Canada [4]. Bricker wrote that separation is one of 10 questions Albertans are being asked in October, with others focused on immigration and provincial authority [4].

Historical Background

Canada’s Supreme Court ruled in the 1998 Quebec Secession Reference that a clear vote on a clear question would create a duty to negotiate but would not allow unilateral secession [common]. Parliament later passed the Clarity Act in 2000 to set federal conditions for assessing any future provincial secession vote [common].
