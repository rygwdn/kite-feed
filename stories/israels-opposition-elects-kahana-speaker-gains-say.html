Israel’s opposition elects Kahana speaker, gains say over legislative agenda

Category: World

Israel’s parliament elected opposition lawmaker Matan Kahana as speaker on Tuesday, giving the bloc seeking to replace caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu greater influence over the legislative agenda. Kahana, an ally of Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot, won 66 votes in the 120-seat Knesset, while Likud’s candidate, Ofir Katz, received 52. Arab-led parties backed Kahana alongside Jewish opposition parties, helping secure his election after a debate lasting about seven hours [2][13][10][4]. The speaker helps set the timetable for debates and votes. Eisenkot’s bloc says it wants to reverse laws that weakened judicial powers, establish an inquiry into the Hamas-led Oct. 7, 2023, attack, and introduce term limits for prime ministers. The opposition has also gained leadership of key committees, while Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister [10][3][9]. President Isaac Herzog has tasked Eisenkot with forming a coalition, but his bloc lacks a majority without Arab-party support, which some Jewish opposition leaders reject. Eisenkot has until Dec. 6 to form a government, with a possible two-week extension. His bloc has agreed not to advance repeal of the judicial overhaul before Monday, according to Ynet [21][13].

Did you know: The Knesset speaker can serve as Israel’s acting head of state if the president is unable to perform official duties [5].

Key Points

  • Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee chair Avigdor Lieberman said he would summon security chiefs, all appointed by Netanyahu, for oversight hearings, including on Iran [3].
  • Legislative hurdles: Bills must pass through committee and multiple floor votes, limiting how quickly legislation can pass even with the speaker’s control over scheduling [4][2].
  • Election inquiry: Israel’s Central Elections Committee said an investigation found no evidence of voter bribes on a scale that could have affected the election outcome [5][7][25].
  • Arab-party role: The two Arab-majority slates hold 19 seats; one of their lawmakers was absent from the speaker vote [13].
  • The pledge to pause repeal was part of a reported agreement in which Netanyahu’s bloc agreed not to extend its filibuster against the speaker vote [13].

Perspectives

Likud Party: The party condemned Kahana’s election as achieved with “the votes of terror supporters,” objecting to the Arab parties’ backing [13].

Timeline

  • 10 November 2026: Newly elected Knesset convened without a government in place [10].

  • 6 December 2026: Eisenkot’s initial deadline to form a government; president may extend it [21].

Historical Background

Netanyahu’s government began advancing judicial changes shortly after he returned to office in December 2022; the proposals triggered large demonstrations, with tens of thousands taking to the streets [5][19].

A: President Isaac Herzog could give Netanyahu a chance to form a government; if neither succeeds, another election is expected in 2027 [2][12].
