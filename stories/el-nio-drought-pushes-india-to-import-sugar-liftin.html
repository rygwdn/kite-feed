El Niño drought pushes India to import sugar, lifting global prices

Category: Science

El Niño-linked drought and India's weakest monsoon in 11 years have damaged sugarcane crops, prompting the world's second-largest sugar producer to open its market to imports as prices rise in India and globally [2][3]. India is also the world's largest sugar consumer, and shortages have hit during the Hindu festival season, when demand for sweets is high [3]. Sugarcane supplies India's ethanol fuel program, which has made a 20% ethanol blend the standard at petrol stations and provides sugar mills with an additional source of revenue. Reduced cane availability complicates efforts to cut oil imports [3]. Protests have erupted over rising prices, and the government has warned traders against speculative stockpiling [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Crop reversal: A year earlier, excessive rain had inundated sugarcane plantations and pests had damaged them, before drought depleted them [3].
  • Price protections: India has long supported its sugar industry with guaranteed prices for farmers and, until this year, high import duties [3].

Perspectives

Indian government: The government says ethanol blends reduce oil demand and pollution while conserving foreign currency, and has described the program as a key pillar of India's energy transition [3].

Indian motorists: Drivers have complained that fuel containing 20% ethanol increases consumption and damages older vehicles whose engines are not compatible with the blend [3].
