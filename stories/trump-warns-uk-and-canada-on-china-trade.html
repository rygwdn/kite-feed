Trump warns UK and Canada on China trade

Category: World

President Donald Trump warned that it is “very dangerous” for close U.S. allies Britain and Canada to deepen business ties with China, commenting as U.K. Prime Minister Keir Starmer visited China to pursue improved trade terms and investment [1][3][5][12][11][9][10][8][13]. The remarks underline growing differences among allies over how to balance economic engagement with Beijing against security and geopolitical concerns [3][12]. Starmer’s trip signaled a reset in U.K.–China relations after years of strain, with London pointing to practical gains such as eased travel and targeted tariff cuts, while also facing criticism from some rights activists who say the UK should prioritize human rights concerns in its China policy [11][9][2]. More broadly, several reports frame this as part of a wider pattern: U.S. partners exploring new or revived links with China amid trade tensions and uncertainty in transatlantic and global economic relationships [4][7][12].

Primary Source: View original article

Well, it’s very dangerous for them to do that.

— Donald Trump (The Guardian)

Did you know: Starmer’s meeting with Xi included lighter cultural touchpoints—reportedly discussing football and Shakespeare—alongside trade and investment issues [11].

Key Points

  • Whisky tariffs: China agreed to halve import tariffs on British whisky to 5% from 10%, according to reporting from the UK visit [9][11].
  • Travel facilitation: The UK side highlighted that China confirmed visa-free travel for British nationals for trips under 30 days [9][11].
  • UK minister pushback: UK Trade Department Minister Chris Bryant said President Trump was wrong to describe stronger UK-China ties as dangerous, in comments to the BBC reported by The Hindu [6].
  • Canada track: Canada’s Prime Minister Mark Carney traveled to Beijing in mid-January and reached a preliminary arrangement involving capped imports of Chinese electric vehicles at a 6.1% tariff, alongside expectations that China will reduce canola-related tariffs by March [9].
  • April China trip: Downing Street noted that the White House knew the aims of Starmer’s trip in advance and pointed out that President Trump is due to visit China in April [10].

Technical Details

  • Import tariff: An import tariff is a tax on goods entering a country; China’s reported whisky cut reduces the tax from 10% to 5%, lowering the border cost for that product category [9][11].
  • Visa-free travel: Visa-free travel allows entry without obtaining a visa in advance; China’s reported policy would permit UK visitors to enter for stays under 30 days without a visa [9].
  • Tariff-rate cap (EVs): RT reported Canada’s preliminary deal included “capped imports” of Chinese electric vehicles at a 6.1% tariff, a design that can limit volume while setting a defined import tax rate [9].

Industry Impact

  • Spirits exporters: UK whisky producers could benefit from the reported tariff cut in China, which would reduce the tax applied at import and potentially improve price competitiveness [9][11].
  • Pharmaceutical investment: AstraZeneca outlined plans to invest $15 billion in China through 2030 during the UK trip, highlighting how major multinationals may keep prioritizing the China market even amid geopolitical tension [9].
  • Autos and EV trade: Canada’s reported preliminary framework on Chinese EV imports suggests governments may experiment with managed trade tools—like caps paired with set tariffs—rather than choosing between open access and outright restriction [9].
  • Diplomatic services: China’s reported visa-free access for short UK visits would likely increase business and tourism travel volumes, affecting airlines, hotels, and conference organizers serving UK-China routes [9].

Historical Background

UK–China ties have swung between closer economic engagement and periods of political strain in recent years, with Starmer describing the relationship as moving “from a golden age to an ice age” before his current push for a reset [9]. Several reports frame the current outreach as part of allies’ broader efforts to diversify trade and investment links amid disputes with Washington over tariffs and broader strategic demands [7][12].

Q&A

Q: What did President Trump say this at, and why did it draw attention?

A: Several outlets reported he made the comments while speaking to reporters at the premiere of a documentary about First Lady Melania Trump at the Kennedy Center [11][9][13].

Q: Why did some UK-based activists object to Starmer’s Beijing visit?

A: The Guardian reported that prominent Hong Kong and Uyghur activists criticized the trip and cited concerns including Jimmy Lai’s imprisonment and abuses against Uyghurs [2].

Q: How do these moves fit into broader Asia-Europe diplomacy right now?

A: NPR reported that Trump’s reshaping of the global order has been one factor encouraging closer Asia-Europe coordination, even when his name is not explicitly invoked [4].

  • Assess tariff exposure for exporters: If you run a UK spirits business, map which of your SKUs face China import duties and model how a shift from 10% to 5% could change landed prices and distributor margins [9][11].
