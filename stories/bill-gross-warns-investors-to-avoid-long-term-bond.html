Bill Gross warns investors to avoid long-term bonds as volatility rises

Category: Business

Bill Gross, the veteran bond investor and PIMCO co-founder, advised investors to avoid bonds other than one-year Treasury bills, which he said yield 4.55%, and urged caution on stocks at record levels [1]. He cited about $84 trillion in government, mortgage and corporate debt, including U.S. federal debt equal to 100% of GDP, and warned that rising debt could fuel inflation and slow future growth [1]. Gross also pointed to changes in Treasury trading: hedge funds now hold 8.5% of Treasuries, nearly twice their share in 2023, while 10-year yields have risen more than 100 basis points since the Iran war began and recently reached a 24-year high [1]. He warned that funds could unwind leveraged positions during market stress, reducing liquidity in government bonds [1].

Key Points

  • AI valuations: Gross called borrowing by AI hyperscalers historically unusual and said he is suspicious of the companies unless their price-to-earnings ratios are below 20 [1].
  • Telecom exposure: He said Verizon and AT&T offer decent yields but face a competitive threat to their mobile businesses from SpaceX’s Starlink [1].
  • Income funds: Gross said some income funds trading below net asset value may present opportunities, but could be hurt if short-term rates rise more than expected [1].

Business Angle

  • Gross said higher yields over time could squeeze profit margins and urged caution on stocks at record levels [1].
  • A stock-market selloff could force hedge funds to sell bond positions to cover equity losses, spreading stress across markets [1].
  • Gross described his current investment motto as “Preserve and protect” [1].
