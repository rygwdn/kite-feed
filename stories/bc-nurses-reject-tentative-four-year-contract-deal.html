B.C. nurses reject tentative four-year contract deal

Category: Canada

Members of the BC Nurses’ Union, which represents 60,000 nurses, voted 67 per cent to reject a tentative contract agreement reached with the provincial health employer on May 22 [3][1][5]. The union said the vote reflected “growing frustration” over pressures facing the nursing profession, The Canadian Press reported [2][1]. The rejected offer included a 12 per cent wage increase over four years and improved benefits, though the union had said before the vote that full details would be released after ratification [2][3]. Health Minister Josie Osborne said the province respected members’ decision and the democratic process, and that the Nurses’ Bargaining Association would engage with members to determine next steps [4].
