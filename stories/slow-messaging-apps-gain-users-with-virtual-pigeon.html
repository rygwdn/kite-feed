Slow messaging apps gain users with virtual pigeon delivery

Category: Technology

Slow messaging apps Carrier Pidge and Roost are drawing attention for sending digital messages after delays that mimic carrier-pigeon delivery, with longer distances taking longer to arrive [1][3][4][6]. Carrier Pidge has reached 75,000 users, and Roost has logged 650,000 downloads, Techmeme said in a summary of New York Times reporting [4]. The apps appeal to users who say constant notifications and instant messaging have made communication feel rushed [3][6]. The concept is not entirely new, Gizmodo noted: Slowly, a pen-pal app dating to the 2010s, already used geography-based delivery delays before newer apps added virtual animals and pigeon-themed interfaces [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Built-in delay: The apps are designed for low-urgency messages, and The New York Times said users should not rely on them for plans such as arranging dinner [3].
  • Virtual risk: Carrier Pidge includes a feature in which a user's virtual pigeon can die, requiring an in-app purchase for a replacement pigeon [1][2].
  • Design hook: Carrier Pidge uses homing pigeons, while Roost uses a broader set of simulated animals that includes pigeons [1].
  • Aggregator interest: Google News grouped coverage from Gizmodo, The New York Times, Creative Bloq, Gadget Review and USA Today around the same virtual-pigeon messaging trend [5].

Perspectives

Gizmodo: Gizmodo argued that real carrier pigeons should not be treated as inferior technology, calling them the original wireless carriers.

Sources: Gizmodo

Business Angle

  • Download and user counts show consumer curiosity, but long-term value depends on repeat use rather than viral attention [4][common].
  • In-app pigeon replacement gives Carrier Pidge a revenue mechanism, but user tolerance for forced replacement purchases remains a retention risk [1][common].
