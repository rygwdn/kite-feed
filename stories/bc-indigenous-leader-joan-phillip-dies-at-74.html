B.C. Indigenous leader Joan Phillip dies at 74

Category: Canada

The Union of British Columbia Indian Chiefs said British Columbia NDP MLA and Indigenous leader Joan Phillip died peacefully Friday afternoon at 74, surrounded by her husband, Grand Chief Stewart Phillip, their children, grandchildren and family [1][3][4][6][7][8]. Phillip represented Vancouver-Strathcona after first winning a 2023 Vancouver-Mount Pleasant byelection and then a seat in B.C.'s 2024 general election [1][3][4][7][8]. Phillip had been ill for months. The Union of British Columbia Indian Chiefs said Thursday she was in hospital “courageously fighting kidney infection and septic shock” [1][3][8]. Premier David Eby said B.C. had lost “a powerful and respected leader.”

Joan was an epic fighter who dedicated her life to making the world a better and fairer place. We’ll miss her so much.

— David Eby (SooToday)

Key Points

  • Byelection result: Phillip won the June 24, 2023, Vancouver-Mount Pleasant byelection with just under 68 per cent of the vote after Melanie Mark resigned [7].
  • Rights law: The Vancouver Sun reported that, as MLA, Phillip opposed a planned suspension of the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act. Eby backed off the proposal at the beginning of April [7].

Perspectives

Union of British Columbia Indian Chiefs: The organization called Phillip “a fearless warrior and inspirational leader” and asked people to donate to the B.C. Cancer Society instead of sending flowers.
