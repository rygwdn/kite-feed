Marsh links AI-enabled hacking to rising business cyber incidents

Category: AI

Insurance broker Marsh says artificial intelligence is accelerating cyberattacks as businesses report more incidents, creating new challenges for companies and their cyber insurers. Meredith Schnur, Marsh Risk’s U.S. and Canada cyber practice leader, told Reuters that attackers are using AI-enabled tools to find vulnerabilities and that a significant number of incidents could be attributed to those tools [8]. Google’s latest threat report describes how attackers are moving from using AI as an assistant to linking tasks in autonomous workflows. A framework Google calls exploit_agent compared software code before and after patches, generated exploit code and attempted to run it without a human directing each step. Telemetry suggested interest in French media, entertainment and education websites [13][26][12]. Google says attackers still target known flaws that organizations have not fixed, but automation can shorten the time available to respond. The company also said current tools can leave recognizable traces and AI-generated exploit attempts may fail. Security researchers say defenders can use those traces to detect and investigate activity as attack volume and speed increase [14][37][10].

Key Points

  • Business concerns: In an F5 survey of Asia-Pacific executives, 63% cited unpredictable AI and token costs as a barrier to wider use, 50% said current security tools were insufficient for AI, and 35% worried about data leaks from autonomous agents [28].
  • Faster access: The FBI said attacks using the ARTEX framework had in some cases moved from target identification to elevated system access in under three hours [5].
  • Developer tools: Google reported that a suspected North Korean group altered settings in the Claude Code coding assistant to trigger a backdoor download when it started [10][14].
  • LLMJacking: Google flagged criminals stealing credentials and cloud resources for AI models, then reselling access through underground proxy services [14].

Timeline

  • August 2026: Google counted 22 exploited zero-days during August [4].
  • September 2026: Google said it had not observed autonomous attack pipelines against targets [4].
  • 13 November 2026: The FBI warned that attackers were using the ARTEX framework [5].
  • 16 November 2026: Google published its latest threat report on autonomous AI-enabled attacks [13][37].

Technical Details

  • N-day vulnerabilities: These are known flaws that remain unpatched on some systems, even after disclosure or the release of a patch. Comparing pre- and post-patch code can reveal the flaw and help attackers exploit it [12][4].
  • Agent frameworks: Tools such as CAIRN and PentAGI coordinate specialized agents across reconnaissance, vulnerability scanning and other intrusion tasks. Google observed CAIRN in activity against U.S. aerospace organizations and PentAGI scanning organizations in Asia [13][10].

Industry Impact

  • Cyber insurance: Marsh says AI is accelerating attacks and creating new challenges for companies and insurers as businesses report more cyber incidents [8].
  • Enterprise security teams: The FBI said ARTEX-linked attacks targeted financial services, government agencies, technology companies and health-care organizations [5].
  • AI deployment: F5’s Asia-Pacific executive survey found that 53% of respondents would not allow sensitive data to be transferred overseas [28].

Business Angle

  • The FBI said ARTEX makes sophisticated attacks accessible to people with less technical experience, potentially widening the pool of capable attackers [5].
  • F5 found that 93% of surveyed companies operated hybrid multicloud environments across an average of 19 locations [28].

Q&A

Q: Does “without a human in the loop” mean no person was involved at all?

A: It refers to the framework automating the vulnerability-analysis and exploitation steps; the operator configured the system, and Google’s report described API credentials in its setup [20].

Q: Did Google identify the organizations affected?

A: Google did not identify the victims or disclose how many organizations were compromised or the extent of the damage [26].

Action Items

  • Prioritize security updates for internet-facing systems: Track exposed systems and install relevant vendor patches promptly; the FBI also recommends limiting sensitive-system access and monitoring for unusual activity [5].
  • Strengthen account access controls: Use multifactor authentication and unique passwords, and review accounts for suspicious activity [5].
