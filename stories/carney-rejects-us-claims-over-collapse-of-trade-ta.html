Carney rejects U.S. claims over collapse of trade talks

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada remains ready to negotiate a trade agreement with the United States, but only one that benefits both countries and gives businesses stability and credibility [6][10][11]. Speaking Thursday in Thunder Bay, Ontario, he rejected assertions from U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent that Canada abandoned the talks for domestic political reasons [6][4][3]. Carney said several late U.S. conditions made a deal untenable, including auto-tariff language, no reduction in tariffs on heavy-duty trucks made in southern Ontario, and provisions he said would restrict Canada’s protections for language, culture and sovereignty [4][8]. He said U.S. negotiators had pushed terms that could leave core Canadian industries, including automotive, operating as effective subsidiaries of U.S. industries or gradually wound down in Canada [1]. The talks collapsed on Aug. 21, one day before a U.S.-imposed deadline, after Carney said Washington had “asked too much and offered too little” [1][8]. President Trump then imposed 50% tariffs on about $20 billion in certain Canadian imports, and Canada prepared dollar-for-dollar retaliatory duties on U.S. goods set to take effect Sept. 8 [7][8].

The deal that's possible, that's in the interests of Canadian workers, families and businesses, is also the deal that is in the interests of American families, American businesses, American consumers, and... we're ready to sit down and strike that deal when the Americans are ready

— Prime Minister Mark Carney (Reuters via The Express Tribune)

Key Points

  • Changed positions: Carney said some issues Washington had treated as red lines before the breakdown were no longer being described that way after talks halted [4][6][8].
  • U.S. criticism: Lutnick said Canada made last-minute demands, including a request to relax tariffs on medium and heavy trucks, and said the trade dispute would change after mid-October elections [4][8].
  • Industry conditions: Carney said any agreement must recognize Canadian-made content and the highly integrated Canada-U.S. auto market. He described Canada as the largest foreign customer for U.S. automobiles [8][9].
  • Trade shift: Statistics Canada data released Thursday showed exports to the U.S. in July fell to their lowest share of total Canadian exports since at least 1997, excluding the pandemic period [8].

Perspectives

Prime Minister Mark Carney: Carney said appointed U.S. cabinet officials were not experts on Canadian politics and tied Canada’s refusal to accept the deal to concerns over industry, language, culture and sovereignty.

Industry Impact

  • Automotive manufacturing: Carney said U.S. proposals on auto tariffs and heavy-duty trucks would have affected plants in southern Ontario, and he said any deal must account for the integrated Canada-U.S. auto market [4][8].
  • Steel and aluminum: Carney said Canada would not sign an agreement that seriously weakened domestic steel and aluminum industries, according to accounts of his Thunder Bay remarks [8][9].
  • Passenger rail procurement: Carney made the trade comments while announcing a $4.7-billion contract in Thunder Bay to build more than 300 new Via Rail passenger rail cars [4].

Historical Background

Canada and the United States trade under the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement, which replaced NAFTA in 2020 [common]. The current dispute followed talks aimed at resolving trade irritants before a U.S.-imposed Aug. 22 deadline [1].
