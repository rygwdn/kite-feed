Sophie Adenot sets ESA orbital-duration record after nearly eight months

Category: Science

European Space Agency astronaut Sophie Adenot set a record for the longest continuous orbital stay by an ESA astronaut on her first spaceflight. Reports differ on the exact duration: RTS gives 235 days, while VnExpress reports 237 [11][12]. During the mission, she completed three spacewalks in 15 days, totaling 19 hours and 42 minutes [11]. The Crew-12 mission also conducted research and tested equipment relevant to long-duration flights, including on-demand production of intravenous fluids and studies of how pneumonia-causing bacteria may contribute to lasting heart damage [4]. NASA has scheduled a crew news conference for Oct. 15 at Johnson Space Center in Houston, where astronauts are expected to discuss their mission and research [1][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Adenot became the first French woman to venture outside the International Space Station [4].

Key Points

  • Previous record: Adenot’s stay surpassed the previous ESA continuous-flight record of 201 days, set by Luca Parmitano in 2019-20 [12].
  • Crew-13 transit: The replacement crew reached the ISS in less than eight hours, which VnExpress described as the fastest trip to the station by a U.S. spacecraft [12].
  • Spacewalk total: Jessica Meir completed four spacewalks on the mission, bringing her career total to seven and placing her third among NASA women [1][4].
  • Cargo mission: SpaceX was preparing a station resupply flight carrying more than 6,000 pounds of cargo, including the final planned solar-panel additions, a toilet pump, a spacesuit and fresh food [7].

Perspectives

NASA Low Earth Orbit Program: Program manager Dana Weigel said ISS work supports science, engineering and risk reduction needed for future missions across the solar system [1].

Sophie Adenot: Adenot described completing three spacewalks in 15 days as an extraordinary experience and said she had not expected to do three in such a short span [11].

Timeline

  • 12 September 2026: Crew-13 was originally scheduled to launch on this date, but a spacecraft propulsion issue delayed the launch [12].
  • 1 October 2026: Crew-13 docked at the station and began its rotation [14].
  • 7 October 2026: Crew-12 undocked and began its return journey to Earth [14].
  • 8 October 2026: Crew-12 splashed down in the Pacific off California [4][14].

Historical Background

Crew-12 launched in February to replace four station occupants whom NASA had sent home early after astronaut Mike Fincke fell ill. The episode was NASA’s first medical evacuation in 65 years of human spaceflight [7].

Q&A

Q: How long is the crew’s planned post-flight rehabilitation?

A: The astronauts were set to begin a 45-day post-flight rehabilitation period to help their bodies readjust to gravity, according to VnExpress [12].
