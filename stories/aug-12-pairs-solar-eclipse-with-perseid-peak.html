Aug. 12 pairs solar eclipse with Perseid peak

Category: Science

August’s skywatching calendar centers on Aug. 12, when the new Moon will cast its shadow on Earth during a total solar eclipse, according to NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day [4]. The path of totality will cross Greenland, Iceland, Spain, parts of the North Atlantic and northern Russia; partial phases will be visible across parts of North America, Europe and North Africa [5][2][23]. The Perseid meteor shower also peaks around that date. The Taipei Astronomical Museum places the best viewing period from about 8 p.m. Aug. 12 until dawn Aug. 13 and estimates up to 70 visible meteors per hour under favorable conditions [8]. Other August events include Mercury reaching greatest apparent separation from the Sun on Aug. 2, Venus reaching greatest apparent separation on Aug. 15, and a partial lunar eclipse on Aug. 28 [8][9][22].

Did you know: Switzerland’s next total solar eclipse is not expected until Sept. 3, 2081 [1].

Key Points

  • European partials: Switzerland is forecast to see up to 91% of the Sun covered at about 20:17 on Aug. 12, shortly before sunset [1].
  • North Africa: Some areas of Algeria could see up to 96% solar coverage, while Rabat, Morocco, is forecast to see up to 88% [2].
  • Lunar timing: Turkey's sky-events almanac says the Aug. 28 partial lunar eclipse will be visible in the country [22].
  • Venus detail: Astronomy reported that Venus opens August about 56% lit, with a 21-arcsecond disk [9].

Perspectives

Jackie Faherty, American Museum of Natural History: Faherty described August as the strongest summer month for observing and pointed readers to seasonal constellations such as Scorpius.

Sources: Gothamist

Taipei Astronomical Museum: The museum said the Perseids are known for fast-moving, bright meteors and frequent fireballs during the shower.

Sources: Focus Taiwan

Pavol Rapavý, Slovak Union of Astronomers: Rapavý said the Aug. 12 solar eclipse will be the most significant in Slovakia since 1999, with the Moon covering up to 85% of the Sun in western parts of the country.

Sources: STVR

Georges Meynet, University of Geneva: Meynet said Switzerland’s partial eclipse will not produce the daytime-night effect of totality, but observers should notice a clear drop in brightness.

Sources: 20 Minuten

Timeline

  • 17 July 2026: Perseid activity period began before the August peak [11][16]
  • 2 August 2026: Mercury reaches greatest apparent separation from the Sun [8]
  • 12 August 2026: Total solar eclipse crosses Greenland, Iceland and Spain [5]
  • 13 August 2026: Taipei museum expects Perseid peak after overnight viewing [8]
  • 15 August 2026: Venus reaches greatest apparent separation from the Sun [8]
  • 28 August 2026: Partial lunar eclipse occurs late in the month [9][22]

Historical Background

Several European reports compare the Aug. 12 solar eclipse with the Aug. 11, 1999, solar eclipse, the last event to bring similarly high solar coverage to parts of the continent [1][11]. In Switzerland, the 1999 eclipse covered 92% to 98% of the Sun, depending on location, compared with a forecast maximum of up to 91% in 2026 [1].
