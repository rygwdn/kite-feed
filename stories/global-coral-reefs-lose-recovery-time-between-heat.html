Global coral reefs lose recovery time between heatwaves

Category: Science

The Global Coral Reef Monitoring Network said Monday that coral reefs are bleaching so often that many no longer have enough time to recover between marine heatwaves, putting global hard coral cover at risk of irreversible decline [3][1][4][5][10][13]. The group’s “Status of Coral Reefs of the World: 2025” report drew on more than 40 years of data, including 21.1 million observations and 87,299 surveys from 36,886 reef sites across 124 countries, territories and economies [12][8][9][6]. The report found a 9.5% relative decline in global hard coral cover compared with the 1980-2009 average, driven primarily by human-induced climate change and climate-driven heat stress [12][8][9][11]. EUobserver said hard coral cover fell from just over 30% during 2009-2020 to 25.8% in 2024 [8]. The assessment says each of the four major global bleaching events since 1998 caused losses in hard coral and that recovery windows have shortened as bleaching events become more frequent [9][8][11].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Bleaching losses: The four global bleaching events cut hard coral cover by about 6.5% in 1998-99, 9.9% in 2010-11, 6.6% in 2016-17 and 8.9% in 2023-24 [9].
  • 2017-19 recovery: Global hard coral cover increased by about 6% between 2017 and 2019, which researchers cited as evidence that reefs can recover when disturbances ease long enough [12][9].
  • Local pressures: Dr Manuel González Rivero said poor water quality, overfishing and coastal development compound the damage from more frequent and severe marine heatwaves [12].
  • Ecological role: Coral reefs cover less than 0.2% of the seafloor but provide habitat for at least a quarter of marine species [6].

Perspectives

Global Coral Reef Monitoring Network: The network released the assessment as the most comprehensive global coral reef status report yet, using reef observations across 124 countries, territories and economies.

Australian Institute of Marine Science: EUobserver identified the Australian Institute of Marine Science’s long-term monitoring programme as the source for data showing live hard coral cover at 25.8% in 2024.

Sources: EUobserver

Timeline

  • 1998: First global bleaching event caused about 6.5% hard-coral loss [9].
  • 2010: Second global bleaching event caused about 9.9% hard-coral loss [9].
  • 2016: Third global bleaching event caused about 6.6% hard-coral loss [9].
  • 2017: Global hard coral cover began a recovery that reached about 6% by 2019 [12][9].
  • 2023: Fourth global bleaching event caused about 8.9% hard-coral loss [9].
  • 31 August 2026: GCRMN released its 2025 global coral reef status report [12][9].

Scientific Significance

  • Limitations: The available summaries do not include confidence intervals, regional uncertainty ranges or site-level sampling gaps, so the cited global percentages should be read as aggregate estimates from the GCRMN compilation [12][8].

Historical Background

Manuel González Rivero said coral cover remained relatively unchanged until 2010, before the series of bleaching events began to cause significant declines [3].
