Pentagon fires senior Army planner as leak inquiry reaches Justice Department

Category: World

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth removed Army Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia from his post overseeing strategy, plans and policy for the Joint Chiefs of Staff on Wednesday. According to an internal investigation report described by Axios, Sylvia acknowledged on video that he had shared classified information about the wars in Iran and Ukraine with journalists over roughly a year [18][10]. A Pentagon official said Sylvia was accused of disclosing classified information and had failed three polygraph tests; the report Axios reviewed says he failed four. Two other Pentagon sources told CBS News the accusations were not true. A colleague told The Washington Post that Sylvia had denied leaking classified material but acknowledged contact with reporters [4][18][10][7]. Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said the case had been referred to the Justice Department for possible legal action. The Justice Department said it was investigating possible leaks of classified information. The Pentagon said U.S. Central Command, not Sylvia’s Joint Staff directorate, is responsible for operational planning for the Iran war. No replacement for Sylvia has been announced [7][10][13].

Classified information is not personal property. Leaking it for personal, political or professional gain is a betrayal of the trust placed in those responsible for keeping it.

— Sean Parnell, chief Pentagon spokesman (CBS News)

Key Points

  • Diner meeting: The investigation report says Sylvia met a journalist at a Silver Diner in Arlington after the reporter lost Pentagon access and used notes from a classified report to discuss U.S. assistance to Ukraine [18][9].
  • Signal messages: Investigators suspected Sylvia deliberately deleted a Signal conversation with a journalist; according to the report, Sylvia said he thought he had accidentally erased the entire thread [18][12].
  • J5 turnover: Sylvia was the second general under Hegseth to be removed from the Joint Staff’s strategy, plans and policy directorate, known as J5. His predecessor, Lt. Gen. Joseph McGee, was forced to retire in October 2025 [10][13].

Perspectives

Pentagon leadership: Chief spokesman Sean Parnell said the personnel action followed an extensive Air Force Office of Special Investigations inquiry [10].

Sources: CBS News

Army official: An Army official rejected claims that Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle’s office sought a statement supporting Sylvia, saying Telle and his staff had not advocated for one [18].

Timeline

  • February 2025: President Trump fired Joint Chiefs Chairman Charles Brown without explanation [5][19]
  • June 2025: Investigation report says Sylvia’s alleged disclosures began as early as June [18]
  • July 2026: Pentagon and Justice Department announced a joint task force on leaks [7]
  • 28 October 2026: Hegseth removed Sylvia from his Joint Staff planning post [10][4]
  • 3 November 2026: U.S. midterm elections are scheduled; Trump has weighed renewed Iran strikes afterward [5][10]

Industry Impact

  • Pentagon press access: The department’s escort requirement and access restrictions prompted dozens of journalists to surrender press badges; three Stars and Stripes journalists sued the Pentagon in August, arguing their dismissals violated their First Amendment rights [7][4].

Historical Background

Since President Donald Trump returned to office in 2025, the administration has pushed out or sidelined senior military leaders, including the heads of the Army, Navy and Coast Guard, the general who headed the National Security Agency, the Air Force vice chief of staff, a Navy admiral assigned to NATO and three senior military lawyers [5]. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the Pentagon had reduced the number of generals and admirals by 20% as part of an accountability drive [5].
