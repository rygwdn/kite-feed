Researchers trace vertebrate eyes to a one-eyed ancestor

Category: Science

Researchers at Lund University and the University of Sussex proposed that the earliest ancestor of vertebrates passed through a one-eyed, cyclops-like stage nearly 600 million years ago, in work described by Lund University and summarized by ScienceDaily [1][2]. The researchers said the ancestor’s central, or median, eye may have contributed to the evolution of the paired eyes that vertebrates have today [1]. They also linked that ancient visual system to the modern pineal gland, an organ in the brain that helps synchronize sleep with daylight [1].

The results are a surprise. They turn our understanding of the evolution of the eye and the brain upside down

— Dan-E Nilsson (ScienceDaily)
