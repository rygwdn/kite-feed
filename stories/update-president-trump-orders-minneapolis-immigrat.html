Update: President Trump orders Minneapolis immigration operation de-escalation

Category: World

In the latest development in Minneapolis, President Trump said the federal immigration operation in Minnesota will “de-escalate” after nationwide backlash over two fatal shootings by federal agents, including the weekend killing of 37-year-old nurse Alex Pretti [5][4][10]. Trump said he wants an “honorable” investigation into Pretti’s death, while the White House reshuffled on-the-ground leadership by putting border czar Tom Homan in charge and moving Border Patrol official Gregory Bovino out of the city [8][7][9]. The episode has widened political pressure on the administration from some Republicans, local Minnesota leaders, and parts of the business community, even as Trump reiterated confidence in Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and promoted his economic message ahead of midterm politics [1][20][2].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Gregory Bovino previously led Border Patrol-led operations in Los Angeles, Chicago and New Orleans before being sent to Minnesota in December [9].

Key Points

  • Tactics reset: People briefed on the White House discussions said officials considered narrowing the mission toward deportations rather than broad sweeps, reducing the federal footprint, improving coordination with state and local officials, and even requiring body-worn cameras for immigration officers [10].
  • Local outreach: Tom Homan met with Minnesota Governor Tim Walz, and reporting described it as an effort at detente that included plans for additional talks with Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey [4][10].
  • Protocol concerns: White House aide Stephen Miller said agents may have breached “protocol,” after earlier claims by administration figures that had labeled Pretti an “assassin” were walked back in later reporting [11][17][18].
  • Gun-rights split: Trump’s remark that Pretti should not have been carrying a gun—even though he was legally armed under Minnesota law—put him at odds with some Republicans and gun-rights advocates who criticized blaming a lawful gun owner in a fatal encounter [22][23][21][24].
  • Corporate dissent: Some prominent tech leaders, including OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Apple CEO Tim Cook, joined calls for restraint and accountability, while other top Silicon Valley CEOs remained publicly quiet, reflecting a divided response in the sector [14][15][13].

Perspectives

President Trump: Trump said the Minnesota operation will “de-escalate a little bit,” described Pretti’s killing as “very sad,” and said he still has confidence in Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem [7][8][1].

Technical Details

  • Body-worn cameras: The White House considered requiring immigration officers to use body-worn cameras, similar to many police departments, as part of a tactics review after the shootings [10].
  • Operational command: Trump replaced Gregory Bovino with border czar Tom Homan to “recalibrate tactics” and improve cooperation with Minnesota and Minneapolis officials [10][4].
  • Protocol compliance: Stephen Miller said agents may have breached “protocol,” which would make internal rules and use-of-force procedures central to the investigations [11][18].

  • Policy messaging: Trump’s effort to refocus on economic “bread-and-butter” themes while immigration backlash dominates headlines shows how political risk can spill into economic narratives that businesses track closely ahead of elections [2].
  • Public safety tools: Renewed discussion of body-worn cameras for immigration officers could expand demand for camera hardware, data storage, and compliance systems if adopted more broadly [10][common].

Historical Background

Tom Homan became a prominent immigration-enforcement figure during Trump’s earlier presidency and has remained a leading public defender of stricter immigration policies, a background that helps explain why Trump deployed him as a high-level troubleshooter in Minneapolis [6]. In late 2025, the Department of Homeland Security described the Minnesota operation as its largest-ever immigration enforcement effort, with Gregory Bovino as a key public face of the administration’s city-by-city sweeps [9].

Q&A

Q: What role did Gregory Bovino play before being moved off the Minneapolis operation?

A: Reporting describes Bovino as the public face and architect of large-scale city-by-city immigration crackdowns, and says he previously led operations in Los Angeles, Chicago and New Orleans before heading to Minnesota [9].

Q: Why did some Republicans react strongly to Trump’s comments about Pretti carrying a gun?

A: Several reports say Pretti was legally armed under Minnesota law, and critics argued the White House’s framing effectively blamed a lawful gun owner, clashing with long-standing pro-carry messaging in conservative politics [22][23][24].

Q: What accountability steps have been publicly discussed after the shootings?

A: Coverage cites calls for impartial investigations from Minnesota’s governor and mentions White House consideration of measures like body-worn cameras and operational changes to reduce friction with local authorities [10][8].
