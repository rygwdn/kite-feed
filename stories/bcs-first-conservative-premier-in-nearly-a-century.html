B.C.'s first Conservative premier in nearly a century takes office

Category: Canada

Lorne Doerkson was sworn in Friday as British Columbia’s 38th premier, leading the province’s first Conservative government in almost a century after the party won 63 seats. His cabinet has 18 ministers and six ministers of state. He said it will focus on affordability, the economy, health care and public safety [2][3][6]. The new government plans to repeal the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act (DRIPA), with a bill expected in spring 2027, and has promised to raise the personal income tax exemption from $13,216 to $32,000. Doerkson will not recall the legislature for a fall session. His cabinet is due to meet next week, when it may consider orders-in-council, including action on school resources on sexual orientation and gender identity [4][3][9].

I promise that personally, I will work with every one of our nations

— Lorne Doerkson, British Columbia premier (The Narwhal)

Key Points

  • Provincial finances: Finance Minister Peter Milobar is due to launch an independent review of provincial finances. The deficit is forecast at nearly $14 billion, and the proposed tax cut could add as much as $4 billion to it [9][11].
  • Major projects: The province has 41 major projects, including 16 in critical minerals and mining. The party has proposed fixed mine-permitting timelines and a “one project, one review” approach [4].
  • Court challenges: Attorney General Caroline Elliott will oversee the province’s appeal of the Cowichan Tribes Aboriginal-title ruling, among other legal cases [3].

Perspectives

Grand Chief Stewart Phillip described the proposed DRIPA repeal as a “declaration of war” on First Nations [11].

Sources: Vancouver Sun

The British Columbia Teachers’ Federation said sexual orientation and gender identity (SOGI) is not part of the curriculum and argued that teachers need resources to help students feel safe and accepted. The union said the government had not explained what it planned to repeal or replace [3].

Sources: The Globe and Mail

Greg Lyle, president of Innovative Research Group, said naming Caroline Elliott attorney general sent “a clear message” about the government’s approach to Indigenous issues [3].

  • 20 November 2026: Doerkson and his cabinet were sworn in at Government House [3][2]

Industry Impact

  • Steelmaking coal: The party says it will support the sector and seek strategic-mineral status for B.C. steelmaking coal. Elk Valley Resources operates four mines and has three new proposals and an expansion in the early stages of review [4].

Historical Background

British Columbia’s legislature unanimously passed the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act in 2019. Section 7 allows joint decision-making agreements with First Nations on projects in their traditional territories. The Narwhal reported that such agreements helped advance projects including Eskay Creek, Red Chris and Galore Creek [4][11].
