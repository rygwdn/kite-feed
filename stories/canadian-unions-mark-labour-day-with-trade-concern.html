Canadian unions mark Labour Day with trade concerns

Category: Canada

Canadian unions used Labour Day events on Monday, Sept. 7, to press for worker protections, as organizers and labour leaders cited U.S. trade tensions, layoffs and bargaining pressure in several communities [3][7][8]. In Sault Ste. Marie, the local labour council held its annual parade and celebration at the Roberta Bondar Pavilion after a year that included layoffs at Algoma Steel, layoff notices for WSIB employees and pressure on education workers [7][8]. In Greater Victoria, the annual Labour Day picnic in Esquimalt highlighted wage gains after last fall’s eight-week provincial government workers’ strike, which involved more than 25,000 BCGEU and Professional Employees’ Association workers and ended with a four-year agreement providing three per cent annual wage increases [3]. In British Columbia, Premier David Eby marked the holiday by saying workers and unionized labour would be central to building critical infrastructure with the federal government and First Nations [6].

Key Points

  • Steel pressure: United Steelworkers Local 2251 member Richard Beall said USW representatives were lobbying Ottawa to curb steel dumping as Algoma Steel workers faced layoff notices after the company moved to electric arc furnace production [7].
  • Holiday closures: Labour Day fell on Monday, Sept. 7, in 2026, giving many Canadians a three-day weekend while many Ontario businesses closed for the statutory holiday [2].
  • Transit support: Union supporters gathered outside B.C. Transit’s head office on Gorge Road to back bus drivers and mechanics [3].
  • Training pledge: British Columbia said it had doubled skilled-trades training funding with a $241-million investment aimed at increasing apprentice seats, reducing industrial-trades wait lists and advancing skilled-trades certification [6].

Perspectives

Sault Ste. Marie and District Labour Council: The council urged residents to support good jobs and workers’ rights and said trade deals should not come at the expense of local workers or communities.

Victoria Labour Council: President Kirk Mercer said many public-sector unions had to move toward strike votes or prepare for job action before reaching acceptable agreements.

Sources: Times Colonist

Government of British Columbia: Premier David Eby said Labour Day is a time to honour and celebrate the working people who help shape the province, and to acknowledge those who care for, protect and teach families and build communities.

Sources: Government of British Columbia

Timeline

  • 1872: Toronto printers struck for a nine-hour workday [1]
  • 2 September 2025: BCGEU and PEA workers began strike action [3]
  • 26 October 2025: Mediators produced a four-year wage agreement [3]
  • 7 September 2026: Labour Day events took place across Canadian communities [2][7]

Industry Impact

  • Public sector bargaining: The BCGEU and Professional Employees’ Association agreement set a wage pattern that other public-sector unions sought to match in later bargaining [3].

Historical Background

In 1872, Toronto printers struck for a nine-hour workday at a time when workers commonly laboured 10 or more hours a day and authorities could still prosecute trade union activity as criminal conspiracy [1]. Canada’s Parliament passed the Trade Unions Act in 1872, legalizing union activity [common]. Canada made Labour Day a national holiday in 1894, observed on the first Monday in September [common].
