Trump meets Putin and Xi ahead of summit on wars, nuclear arms

Category: World

President Donald Trump held separate meetings with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen on Thursday before the first formal trilateral talks among the three leaders. The Trump-Putin meeting lasted about 35 to 40 minutes, while Trump’s meeting with Xi lasted roughly an hour. The three-way session was scheduled on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit [43][99][12]. Trump said he wanted to discuss the wars in Ukraine and Iran and nuclear arms. Beijing described the trilateral agenda more generally as safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability. Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov said relations among major powers would be central and that the leaders were unlikely to avoid discussing Ukraine [2][11][65]. The last U.S.-Russia nuclear treaty expired in February, and Beijing says Washington and Moscow should reduce their much larger arsenals before China considers limits on its own [25][69]. Ushakov said a joint communique remained under discussion and that the leaders would decide how long the trilateral session would last [47][49].

Key Points

  • Farm purchases: Trump told Xi he would press China to buy more U.S. agricultural goods, saying he would ask Beijing to “do some more for our farmers” [99][2].
  • Arsenal gap: China’s stockpile exceeded 600 warheads, compared with more than 5,000 each for the United States and Russia [25].
  • APEC funding: Xi pledged $20 million for APEC cooperation and announced a Shenzhen research center focused on Asia-Pacific community policy and exchanges [77].
  • Trade message: Xi urged Asia-Pacific economies to favor openness and integration and criticized protectionism in remarks at the forum [6][4].
  • Limited access: Officials allowed journalists to record only the opening exchange of the Trump-Putin meeting, citing room size and parity between delegations [29].

Perspectives

Historical Background

Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Trump and Putin previously met in person in Anchorage in August 2025, but those talks failed to produce a deal to ease the war [14]. Their Shenzhen encounter was their first meeting since that Alaska summit [43].

Q&A

Q: Why is China reluctant to join nuclear arms talks now?

A: Beijing says the United States and Russia should first cut their substantially larger arsenals. Estimates put China’s stockpile at more than 600 warheads, compared with more than 5,000 each for the United States and Russia [25].

Q: What outcome is the Kremlin considering after the summit?

A: Russian presidential aide Yury Ushakov said a joint communique might follow the meeting, but work on it was continuing [47].
