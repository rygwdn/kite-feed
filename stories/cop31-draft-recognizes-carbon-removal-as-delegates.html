COP31 draft recognizes carbon removal as delegates debate power target

Category: Science

Negotiators at the United Nations COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, are considering draft language recognizing carbon dioxide removal’s role in meeting the long-term temperature goal, while debating a proposed global target to raise electricity’s share of final energy use from 23% to 35% [2][5]. The draft also calls for expanding carbon capture and storage, which prevents new emissions rather than removing carbon dioxide already in the atmosphere [2]. If adopted, it would be the first COP decision to explicitly refer to carbon dioxide removal, apart from a brief mention in 2023 [2]. The United Nations said earlier this year that crossing the Paris Agreement’s 1.5C warming threshold can no longer be prevented; returning below it by the end of the century would require deep emissions cuts alongside large-scale carbon removal [2][7]. Critics say removal methods are unproven at the scale required, while the draft electricity target does not specify that power should come from clean sources [2][5]. COP31 was scheduled to end Friday, Nov. 20, but difficult negotiations raised the prospect of an extension [5].

Primary Source: View original article

Is carbon dioxide removal feasible technically? Yes. Is it likely to happen at the billion-ton scale needed to make a difference? No.

— Rob Jackson, Stanford University climate scientist and emissions tracker (Newsday)

Key Points

  • Scale challenge: An industry assessment cited by POLITICO put current carbon removals at 2.2 gigatons of CO2 annually, with newer technological methods accounting for just 0.002 gigatons [2].
  • Land concerns: India raised concerns that planting forests for carbon removal could displace agricultural land, while Fiji’s climate minister Lynda Tabuya warned of possible ecological effects from ocean-based approaches [2].
  • Emissions first: Climate Analytics chief executive Bill Hare said deep emissions reductions must come before reliance on removal, arguing that current policy paths make it impossible to remove enough carbon to compensate for continued warming [7].
  • SF6 potency: Sulfur hexafluoride (SF6), used in grid infrastructure, traps about 24,000 times as much heat as CO2 over its first 100 years in the atmosphere [1].

Perspectives

Mary Church, geoengineering campaign manager at the Center for International Environmental Law, said relying on future carbon-removal technology distracts from immediate emissions cuts and warned that large-scale deployment could harm ecosystems [2].

Scientific Significance

  • Permanence risk: Trees store carbon only while they remain standing; losing them risks releasing that carbon again. Some technologies can theoretically lock away CO2 for millennia [2].
  • Climate-policy dependency: Carbon removal complements emissions cuts rather than replacing them. Climate Analytics chief executive Bill Hare said removal cannot compensate for a pathway that continues to drive warming toward at least 2.7C by 2100 [7].

Q&A

Q: How much carbon dioxide would need to be removed to return below 1.5C?

A: The United Nations says 320 billion metric tons would need to be permanently sequestered over this century, alongside sharp emissions cuts [2][7].
