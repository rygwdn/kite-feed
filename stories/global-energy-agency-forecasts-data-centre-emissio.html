Global energy agency forecasts data-centre emissions to rise 70% by 2030

Category: Science

The International Energy Agency (IEA), a global energy watchdog, forecasts that carbon emissions from data centres will rise 70% by 2030 as electricity use doubles, driven largely by the expansion of artificial intelligence [1][4][8]. AI facilities are expected to account for three-quarters of the growth in data-centre power demand, adding 350 terawatt-hours of annual consumption by the decade’s end—more than Great Britain uses in a year [1][2]. The IEA expects fossil fuels to supply much of that additional power in the United States and China: gas is projected to provide about 60% of US data-centre electricity, while coal is projected to supply about half of China’s AI data-centre demand. The European Union is forecast to get about 70% of its data-centre electricity from renewables [1][6]. The agency used AI-server shipment data and satellite imagery to assess the fast-growing, opaque sector. Its forecasts appear in the World Energy Outlook 2026 [1][4].

  • Conservation use: The Jane Goodall Institute uses AI to process handwritten chimpanzee records, a task estimated to take about two years rather than 13 to 15 years [3].

Perspectives

Greenpeace International: The environmental group has questioned the need for large-scale data-centre expansion even as it uses software with AI features [3].

Environmental and development organizations: All 15 organizations interviewed by Reuters said they used AI in some capacity; examples include wildlife-survey analysis and processing long-held research records [3].

Timeline

  • 2020: Global shipments of AI servers have increased eighteenfold since 2020 [1][4].
  • 2025: Data-centre electricity consumption rose 17%, compared with 3% growth in overall electricity demand [3].
  • August 2026: Plans linked to US data centres included about 180 gigawatts of new gas-fired power plants [1][2].
  • 2030: The IEA forecasts that data-centre emissions will be 70% above current levels by the decade’s end [1][8].

Technical Details

  • Forecast inputs: The IEA based its projections on annual global shipments of AI servers and satellite imagery tracking construction at 93 large AI facilities, addressing limited visibility into the sector [1][4].
  • Capacity estimates: Satellite analysis indicated that global data-centre capacity reached 17 gigawatts after increasing tenfold in 24 months. The 93 tracked AI facilities could have a combined capacity of about 50 gigawatts when completed [1][4].

Industry Impact

  • Electricity grids: The IEA projects grid investment to rise 75% over the next decade under its Current Policies Scenario, with supply shortages and limited network capacity already delaying or complicating data-centre projects [3].
  • Nuclear power agreements: Conditional electricity purchase agreements linked to small modular reactor projects reached 45 gigawatts, up from 25 gigawatts at the end of 2024 [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Capacity outlook: The IEA’s central scenario projects an additional 120 gigawatts of data-centre capacity by 2030, with three-quarters of it in AI facilities. China, the United States and the EU account for 80% of the existing 120 gigawatts of capacity [1][4].
  • Energy-system implications: The IEA expects 26 million kilometres of new electricity lines worldwide by 2035, along with the replacement or refurbishment of another 16 million kilometres [3].

Historical Background

Five major technology companies spent more than $400 billion on capital projects in 2025, with spending projected to increase by another 75% in 2026 [3].
