Former B.C. Conservative MLAs plan new centre-right party

Category: Canada

Delta South MLA Ian Paton left the B.C. Conservative caucus Monday to sit as an Independent, becoming the second MLA this month to leave the party after Kamloops Centre MLA Peter Milobar left earlier, citing a lack of “ideological alignment” with new leader Kerry-Lynne Findlay [1][3][4]. Paton and Milobar said they plan to register a new party with Elections BC before the fall legislative session, casting it as an option for voters dissatisfied with the governing New Democrats but unwilling to support the Conservatives under Findlay [1][3]. The two MLAs said the new group could begin as a two-person party and did not provide a launch date, membership list or name [1][3]. Paton cited disputes over staff changes, an AI-generated video portraying Premier David Eby as a military dictator, and limits on his ability to speak to media as reasons he left [3]. Findlay said she appreciated Paton’s contributions and described the party’s mission as defeating the NDP [3].

Key Points

  • More defections: Milobar said he was hearing from Conservative caucus members and that some could still join the new party, though he said the choice remained theirs [1].
  • Leadership tensions: Paton said he had little contact with Findlay after she became leader and sent her a text message Monday morning saying he was leaving [3].
  • Caucus role: Milobar had served as the B.C. Conservative Opposition finance critic and was described as one of the party’s leading moderate voices before leaving [1].
