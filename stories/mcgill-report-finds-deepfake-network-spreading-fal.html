McGill report finds deepfake network spreading false Canada-U.S. trade claims

Category: Canada

A McGill University research centre says a network of nearly 300 TikTok and Instagram accounts has circulated more than 3,201 deepfake videos that drew 22.1 million views over three months. Many focused on Canada’s trade dispute with the United States [2][5]. One widely viewed false story described a nonexistent 48-hour ultimatum from President Donald Trump and claimed Prime Minister Mark Carney responded by cancelling $8 billion in U.S. defence contracts. It appeared in 161 videos on 129 accounts and drew 5.1 million views [2][5]. The report says the accounts promote misleading claims from both sides of the trade debate, while the network’s operators and purpose remain unknown [2][3]. Researchers said posting patterns and some accounts’ Chinese-language settings or registrations in China point to activity outside North America, but they could not firmly attribute the network to anyone [2]. The report says the accounts have not been monetized and could be building an audience for later use, testing a tool or operating on behalf of someone seeking to influence Canadians [2][5].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Few spotted fakes: Only 6.3% of commenters identified the videos as fake or propaganda, while one in 10 addressed the narrator as if they were a real person [2][5].
  • Few AI labels: The report says TikTok had labelled just 4% of the videos as AI-generated, and the use of real people’s footage may help the fakes evade detection [2][3].
  • Shifting identities: Narrators present themselves as Canadian in some clips and American in others [2][5].
