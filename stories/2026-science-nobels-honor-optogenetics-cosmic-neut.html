2026 science Nobels honor optogenetics, cosmic neutrinos and chiral chemistry

Category: Science

The 2026 Nobel prizes in physiology or medicine, physics and chemistry recognized work on controlling neurons with light, detecting high-energy neutrinos from space and understanding mirror-image molecules [1][7]. The medicine prize went to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for optogenetics. Physicist Francis Halzen received the physics prize for his work on the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of astrophysical neutrinos. Chemists Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai received the chemistry prize for research on how molecular asymmetries can grow during chemical reactions [1][7]. Optogenetics allows researchers to activate selected neurons and observe the resulting behavior, while the chemistry research investigates why living systems favor one molecular handedness [1][7]. IceCube uses sensors embedded in Antarctic ice to detect light from rare neutrino collisions, while optogenetics uses light-sensitive proteins to activate selected nerve cells [1][4].

Key Points

  • Early demonstration: Researchers inserted the channelrhodopsin gene into nerve cells in living mice and delivered blue light through optical fibers to control the rodents’ whisker movements [1]
  • IceCube scale: The detector spans about one cubic kilometer of ice; its sensors can help determine neutrinos’ direction, energy and type [4]
  • Potential applications: The chemistry research may inform pharmaceutical synthesis, asymmetric catalysis and investigations into the origins of life [7]
  • International project: IceCube involves about 450 physicists from 58 institutions in 14 countries, according to project figures cited by The Conversation [4]

Timeline

  • early 2000s: Hegemann and Nagel identified channelrhodopsin in a green alga [1]
  • 2015: Two physicists received the physics Nobel for demonstrating that neutrinos have mass [1]
  • 2018: IceCube data linked a detected neutrino to a distant blazar [1]
  • 2023: A decade of IceCube observations produced a neutrino map of the Milky Way [1]

Scientific Significance

  • Experimental control: Optogenetics allows researchers to activate selected neurons with light and observe the resulting behavior to test causal links between brain circuits and actions [1][7].
  • Clinical report: Researchers described a light therapy for retinitis pigmentosa that improved light sensitivity in seven of 10 treated people [1].
  • New astronomical observations: Neutrinos rarely interact with matter, and magnetic fields do not deflect them, so their paths can help researchers trace high-energy events to distant sources [1].

Historical Background

Francis Halzen proposed the Antarctic Muon and Neutrino Detector Array, or AMANDA, in 1987. Installation of its 677 optical sensors on 19 strings in the ice began in 1993 [4]. After AMANDA demonstrated that Antarctic ice could detect neutrinos, Halzen and colleagues proposed expanding the detector in 2001. IceCube was deployed from 2004 to 2011 [4].
