Intel earnings lift chip stocks to fresh records

Category: Business

Intel shares jumped about 23.6% on April 24 after the chipmaker reported first-quarter results and a second-quarter outlook that beat Wall Street expectations, helping lift the Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 to record closes [1][2][9][11]. Reports pointed to stronger demand tied to artificial intelligence, with Intel saying the next wave of AI is increasing demand for its chips and other products [1][9][8]. The rally quickly spread across the semiconductor sector. Nvidia returned to record territory and topped a $5 trillion market capitalization, while AMD and other chip stocks also rose as investors took Intel’s results as a broader sign that AI infrastructure spending remains strong [3][10]. The move also marked a symbolic milestone for Intel: its stock climbed above its dot-com-era peak from 2000, even as some market watchers cautioned that one strong quarter does not yet prove a lasting turnaround [1][8][11].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Nvidia recorded its first record close in six months during the same chip-sector rally that followed Intel’s results [10].

Key Points

  • Revenue beat: Intel reported first-quarter revenue of $13.58 billion, above the $12.42 billion analysts expected, while adjusted earnings per share came in at $0.29 versus forecasts of $0.01 [9].
  • Strong guidance: For the second quarter, Intel forecast revenue of $13.8 billion to $14.8 billion, ahead of the roughly $13 billion analysts expected, with projected earnings per share of $0.09 [9].
  • Best day: The stock’s surge gave Intel its best trading day since 1987 and pushed it past its previous 2000 high from the dot-com boom [1][4][6].

Perspectives

Intel management: Chief executive Lip-Bu Tan said the company made “fundamental” changes during a year-long turnaround.

Sources: Financial Times

Wall Street analysts: Some market participants welcomed the earnings surprise but still debated whether Intel’s financial prospects have materially changed after a long stretch of lagging peers in AI hardware.

Sources: MarketWatch

Market commentators: Jim Cramer argued investors should “own it, don't trade it” as Nvidia, Intel, AMD, and other chip stocks rode AI-driven momentum across the sector.

Sources: Benzinga

Timeline

  • 2000: Intel set its previous dot-com-era stock peak [1][8]
  • 24 April 2026: Intel reported Q1 results and issued a stronger spring outlook [1][9]
  • 24 April 2026: Shares surged about 23.6%, marking the stock’s best day since 1987 [1][11]
  • 24 April 2026: The Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 closed at records [1][2]

Industry Impact

  • Semiconductor stocks: Intel’s earnings sparked a broader rally in chip shares, lifting Nvidia, AMD, and the wider semiconductor group as investors read the report as a positive signal for AI demand [3][10].
  • US equities: The chip rally helped drive the Nasdaq Composite up 1.6% and pushed the S&P 500 to another all-time high, even as the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.2% [1].

Historical Background

Intel was one of the defining companies of the dot-com era, and its previous stock peak came in 2000 during that boom [1][8]. This rally matters in part because it suggests Intel may be regaining relevance after years of struggling to match the market’s enthusiasm for AI-focused chipmakers [1][8][11].

Action Items

  • Review semiconductor concentration in your portfolio: Check whether recent gains have made chip stocks an outsized share of your holdings, especially after sharp moves in Intel and Nvidia [1][3].
  • Read the guidance, not just the headline: Compare Intel’s second-quarter revenue forecast of $13.8 billion to $14.8 billion with the roughly $13 billion analyst consensus to judge how much of the move came from forward expectations [9].
