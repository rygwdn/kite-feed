Zimbabwe ferry disaster kills 72 on Lake Kariba

Category: World

Zimbabwe police said the death toll from Tuesday’s ferry capsizing on Lake Kariba rose to 72 after recovery teams retrieved more bodies Saturday, as the search for victims continued [2][4][6][7][11][12][17][19][20][23][24][27][30][31][37][29]. The passenger ferry, operated by Zimbabwe’s Rural Infrastructure Development Agency, overturned in choppy waters on the lake along the Zambia border, and police investigations suggested it was overloaded when strong winds hit [11][9][1][25][22][36][39]. Officials said the vessel was authorized to carry 90 passengers, but estimates put the number aboard as high as 153; Zimbabwe’s disaster management agency said 77 people were rescued [4][7][10][26][30][31]. Police and state broadcaster ZBC said 18 children were among the dead, including 16 aged 10 or younger, and local reports identified some of the youngest victims as one-year-old Wendy Ndandarika and 14-month-olds McDonald Nyasha Gumpo and Hailly Tauro [4][7][10][17][3][31]. Authorities said police divers, military personnel and local fishermen remained involved in recovery operations [4][31].

Did you know: Lake Kariba is the world’s largest human-made lake by volume [7][1][27][24].

Key Points

  • Recovery details: SABC reported that fishermen found two bodies early Saturday and that other bodies were recovered floating on the lake, while no bodies had been found beneath the wreckage, which had been towed to a nearby island [30].

Perspectives

Zimbabwe Republic Police: Police said late Saturday the death toll had reached 72 after three more bodies were recovered and that identification of bodies recovered that day was still under way.

Technical Details

  • Capacity calculation: The licensed capacity was 90 passengers, while recorded adult passengers and crew alone totaled 119 before unticketed children were counted, putting the minimum known load about 32% above the authorized passenger limit [5][9][32].
  • Passenger manifest gap: Authorities said children below ticketing age were not included in initial passenger estimates, so the manifest could not immediately establish how many people were aboard or missing [7][10][41].

Historical Background

Lake Kariba was created by damming the Zambezi River in the late 1950s and early 1960s, and Zimbabwe and Zambia share the lake with their border running through its middle [7][26][38]. It stretches more than 200 kilometres and reaches up to 40 kilometres wide in places, making it a major transport corridor for settlements along its shores [7][26][24].
