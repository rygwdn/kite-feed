Apple says revamped Siri remains on track for 2026

Category: Technology

Apple says its long-promised Siri revamp, powered by Apple Intelligence, is still set for 2026. The company reiterated that timeline in a statement to CNBC after reports pointed to hiccups in internal testing [1][2][7]. Those reports—sparked by Bloomberg and echoed widely—say Apple has run into reliability and speed issues: Siri sometimes misfires on queries or takes too long to respond. That, in turn, has pushed Apple to consider shipping the new capabilities across multiple iOS updates instead of delivering everything at once [1][11][5][6][12][4][13][3]. First previewed in 2024, the upgrade aims to make Siri more context-aware and more useful across apps. Apple has described features like onscreen awareness, personal context, and deeper in-app and cross-app actions [1][11]. Some coverage frames the latest reporting as a miss against Apple’s internal targets, but Apple’s public promise has stayed at “2026.” That still leaves room for a staged rollout that lands within the year [1][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Still on track to launch in 2026

— Apple spokesperson (statement to CNBC) (CNBC (via AppleInsider/9to5Mac/MacRumors))

Key Points

  • Rollout approach: Bloomberg’s reporting (as cited by multiple outlets) says Apple may roll out the revamped Siri in stages across future iOS updates, rather than as one big launch [11][5][6][13][3].
  • What Apple says you’ll be able to do: In Apple’s 2024 preview, Siri is shown handling practical, task-heavy requests—like finding a specific photo, tracking details, adding info to a contact card, editing photos, and turning note summaries into draft emails [11].
  • What testing found (reported): Outlets citing Bloomberg say Siri can be sluggish, may mishandle some queries, and can stumble if requests are spoken too quickly [11][13].
  • Market sensitivity: One outlet says the delay chatter spooked investors, with Apple shares dipping after the Bloomberg report before Apple restated its 2026 plan [7].

Perspectives

Apple (company statement): Apple told CNBC it is “on track to launch in 2026,” sticking to the broad window it has publicly committed to—without tying the revamp to a specific iOS point release [1].

Sources: MacRumors

Bloomberg reporting as relayed by multiple outlets: Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman is cited as saying testing turned up speed and reliability problems. As a result, Apple is weighing a piecemeal rollout across later iOS updates instead of shipping everything at once [11].

Sources: Engadget

AppleInsider (media analysis): AppleInsider says the initial delay reporting had “oddities,” while also noting Apple’s response doesn’t fully rebut the claims—it largely restates the 2026 target [2].

Sources: AppleInsider

Timeline

  • June 2024: Apple previews new Siri capabilities at WWDC [1].
  • Spring 2025: Apple had aimed to ship the upgrade, then delayed it [1].
  • March 2026: iOS 26.4 had been the targeted internal release window [1][11].
  • May 2026: Apple shifts internal testing focus to iOS 26.5 for some new Siri capabilities [5][11].
  • September 2026: Reporting points to a more chatbot-like, next-step Siri effort being associated with iOS 27, potentially including a Gemini-related approach [5][6].

Technical Details

  • Preview toggle for personal data: Internal iOS 26.5 builds reportedly include a “preview” settings toggle tied to personal-data features, suggesting Apple may keep sensitive functionality behind an explicit opt-in during rollout [5].
  • Model fallback behavior: Testing reports say Siri sometimes hands requests off to the existing ChatGPT integration unexpectedly, which may point to unfinished orchestration between Apple’s models and external assistants [5][13].

Historical Background

Apple introduced Siri in 2011 as a built-in iPhone assistant and later expanded it across its platforms [common]. At WWDC in June 2024, Apple previewed a more capable Siri powered by Apple Intelligence, pitching it as a more context-aware assistant that can work across apps [1][11].

Action Items

  • Watch for staged/opt-in features: As Apple rolls out new Siri capabilities, look for anything labeled “preview” or otherwise opt-in before enabling it on a primary device [5].
  • Review assistant privacy settings: When new “personal context” features arrive, revisit Siri and Apple Intelligence permissions to make sure you’re comfortable with what data is used [1][11].
