Federal Court pauses Ottawa order winding down TikTok Canada

Category: Canada

A Federal Court decision has set aside Ottawa’s order that would have required TikTok Canada to wind down its business operations, meaning the company can keep operating in Canada while the file is reconsidered [2][3][5]. The matter is being sent back for another review by Industry Minister Mélanie Joly, after the government itself asked the court to set the earlier order aside [2][4]. The original wind-down order was tied to national security concerns, while TikTok and some observers have pointed to uncertainty about how the business shutdown addressed risks without restricting the app’s availability to Canadian users [2][4]. For Canadians who create, watch, or work around the platform, the near-term effect is continuity while the policy process resets [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Keeping TikTok’s Canadian team in place will enable a path forward that continues to support millions of dollars of investment in Canada and hundreds of local jobs.

— TikTok Canada spokesperson (BetaKit)

Did you know: TikTok has more than 14 million active monthly users in Canada, according to reporting on the case [2].

Key Points

  • What changed: Justice Russel Zinn set aside the Nov. 5, 2024 wind-down order and directed a fresh review that “allows for a decision,” though the short judgment did not provide reasons [2].
  • Users unaffected: The earlier wind-down order targeted TikTok’s Canadian business operations, and sources note it did not stop Canadians from using the app or posting content [2].
  • Security rationale: The government has framed its action around national security, and reporting highlights privacy and safety concerns tied to ByteDance and Chinese laws that can compel organizations to assist with intelligence gathering [4].
  • Jobs and investment: TikTok said keeping its Canadian team in place supports “millions of dollars” of investment in Canada and “hundreds of local jobs” while it works toward a resolution [2].
  • Spillover effects: TikTok began winding down its Canadian arts funding as it prepared for the possibility of a shutdown of its business operations in Canada [2].

Historical Background

Canada’s federal government issued a wind-down order for TikTok Canada on Nov. 5, 2024, citing national security risks, while reporting noted the order focused on the company’s Canadian business rather than blocking Canadians’ access to the app [2]. As the legal challenge proceeded, TikTok prepared for a possible shutdown by scaling back Canadian arts funding [2]. The latest court ruling pauses that wind-down direction and restarts the ministerial review process [2].
