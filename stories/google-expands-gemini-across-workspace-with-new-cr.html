Google expands Gemini across Workspace with new creation tools

Category: AI

Google announced a new wave of Gemini features for Google Workspace—especially Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Drive—designed to help people draft, organize, and edit work faster without leaving the apps [2][4][6]. Google frames the update as making Workspace feel more “personal,” with Gemini helping across longer creation workflows rather than only quick, one-off edits [4][2]. In Sheets, Google says users can describe what they want and have Gemini help create, organize, and edit entire spreadsheets, including more complex data-analysis workflows [1]. Separately, Google appears to be testing an enterprise-focused “multi-agent planning” mode for Gemini Business/Enterprise. TestingCatalog says it could suggest task specialists, map out the work, and produce a delegation plan before a human decides what to run [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Docs workflow: Docs now opens with a more chat-like creation and editing flow, with a Gemini prompt box in a new document. The idea is to generate a formatted first draft from a single request, and it can also match the tone and format of an existing doc so teams don’t have to start by copying old files [10][12][8].
  • Sheets automation: In Sheets, you can ask Gemini in plain language to create an entire spreadsheet, then use “Fill with Gemini” to populate tables or close gaps. Google is pitching it as a way to move from an idea—like a tracker or plan—to a workable structure without building it cell by cell [12][20].
  • Drive discovery: Drive search is getting AI Overviews that summarize relevant results, plus an “Ask Gemini” mode that lets you query what’s inside your stored files—for example, to get an answer without opening a pile of documents [12][7].
  • Access and cost: The beta is tied to paid consumer tiers—Google AI Pro at $19.99/month and AI Ultra at $124.99/month. Businesses will need Gemini Alpha access, and an admin may have to enable the features [16][17].
  • Sheets benchmarks and rollout: Google says Gemini in Sheets now hits state-of-the-art performance on spreadsheet tasks and is rolling out new beta features that can build and edit whole sheets from a description, from basic setup to complex analysis [1].
  • Slides assistance: Slides can generate a new slide that follows an existing deck’s visual theme, and it can draw on your Workspace context to draft slide content that fits the presentation you’re building [12][19].
  • Help me create: Wired’s hands-on describes a Gemini-powered “Help Me Create” experience in Docs that’s aimed at quickly generating polished, corporate-style drafts from inside Workspace [5].
  • Broader Workspace footprint: TestingCatalog says Gemini has been pushing deeper into apps like Gmail and Chat, and that these enterprise tests fit Google’s broader effort to make AI a default layer in everyday work tools [3].
  • Persistent projects: TestingCatalog reports Google is moving toward more structured, persistent work with “Gemini Projects” for enterprise users, including file uploads, Drive attachments, and instruction setup via a drag-and-drop-style menu [3].

Historical Background

Google began bringing Gemini into Workspace in 2024, starting with more limited assistive features inside apps like Docs, Sheets, and Slides [16][14]. The March 2026 update leans harder into “create from a single prompt” workflows—like a chat-style prompt box in new Docs and tools that can reuse an existing document’s style and format—while expanding Drive with AI Overviews and file Q&A. The aim is for Gemini to start from what’s already in your Workspace, rather than asking you to re-enter everything by hand [10][12][7].

Action Items

  • Assess enterprise readiness for multi-agent workflows: If you use Gemini Business/Enterprise, list the “specialist” agents or automations your teams already rely on, and consider how a delegation-plan layer could fit into your governance and approval process [3].
  • Pilot Gemini in Sheets for analysis tasks: If your account is included in the beta, try describing the spreadsheet structure or analysis you want, then compare the results with your existing templates before rolling it out more broadly [1].
  • Set a safe trial workflow: Let Gemini handle structure (outlines, slide layouts, spreadsheet templates), then double-check any details it pulled in from other sources before you share or publish [10][12].
  • Try the new Gemini creation flows in Workspace: If you have access to Gemini in Workspace, open Docs, Sheets, Slides, or Drive and look for the new Gemini-assisted creation and editing options described in Google’s March update post [2].
  • Check whether you can access the beta: In Docs, look for the new Gemini entry point (like “Help me create”). In Drive, look for AI Overviews in search and the “Ask Gemini” option for questions about files. If you don’t see them yet, your plan or admin settings may be the reason [12][7].
