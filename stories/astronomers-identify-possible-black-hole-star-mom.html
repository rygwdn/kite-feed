Astronomers identify possible black hole star MoM-BH*-1

Category: Science

Astronomers using data from NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope have identified MoM-BH*-1, an extremely bright red object in the early universe that researchers describe as a possible new class of astrophysical object: a “black hole star” [2][3][4][7][8]. The object appears roughly the size of the solar system, emits about 100 billion times more energy than any known star can produce through nuclear fusion, and has an energy output closer to that of a black hole than an ordinary star [2][4][5][7][8]. Reports describe MoM-BH*-1 as a black hole likely wrapped in dense hydrogen and helium gas, with an exterior that resembles a giant star although its power source is not stellar fusion [4][6][5]. Reports citing the research place the object in the constellation Cetus and estimate that it formed about 660 million years after the Big Bang [7][8]. MDR reported that the finding appears in Nature, while Wired said the observation supports one explanation for mysterious red spots seen in the early universe [3][6].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Central mass: Bug cited measurements suggesting a central black hole with a mass of about 100,000 Suns inside the gas envelope [4].
  • Chance find: The discovery emerged during the “Mirage or Miracle” project, which was designed to examine unusually bright early galaxies rather than search for a new object type [4].
  • Spectral clue: RBC reported that MoM-BH*-1 shows a Balmer break and a spectrum pointing to a large ionized-gas cloud rather than dust as the cause of its red color [5].
  • Scale contrast: MDR said the surrounding hydrogen cloud reaches roughly Pluto’s distance from the Sun, while the event horizon is estimated at six million to 60 million kilometers across [6].

Perspectives

MDR described the object as appearing no larger than a speck of dust on a window pane and surrounded by thousands of more striking galaxies, yet said it made it into Nature and sparked debate.

Sources: MDR

Scientific Significance

  • Researchers cited by RBC said objects like MoM-BH*-1 may represent an early stage in the formation of supermassive black holes, a longstanding problem because very massive black holes appear early in cosmic history [5][common].
  • MDR reported that specialists are debating whether the red source explains an entire class of puzzling objects, so its classification as a new type of object remains tied to interpretation of Webb data rather than direct imaging of the central black hole [6].
