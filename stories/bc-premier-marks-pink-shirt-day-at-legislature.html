B.C. premier marks Pink Shirt Day at legislature

Category: Canada

British Columbia Premier David Eby marked Pink Shirt Day by urging people to speak up against bullying and reinforce a message of inclusion and kindness as part of the annual anti-bullying initiative observed across Canada [1]. At a ceremony at the B.C. legislature, Eby also acknowledged the Tumbler Ridge shooting and the people affected by the tragedy [3][2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Who attended: Politicians, invited guests and a school choir joined the Pink Shirt Day event at the B.C. legislature building [3].
  • Moment of silence: Attendees paused during the ceremony to honour those killed in the Tumbler Ridge shooting [3].
  • Eby’s tribute: The premier paid tribute to what he called the heroism shown by children caught up in the Tumbler Ridge shooting [3][2].
  • Minister’s call: In a separate statement, B.C.’s education minister urged people to challenge bullying and help build safer, more welcoming schools and communities [1].

Perspectives

Premier David Eby: Eby used his Pink Shirt Day remarks to link anti-bullying efforts with a tribute to the courage he said Tumbler Ridge children showed during the shooting [3].

Sources: Toronto Star

B.C. government (education minister): In an official Pink Shirt Day statement, the education minister encouraged British Columbians to take part and to speak up when they see bullying [1].

Sources: Government of British Columbia

Historical Background

Pink Shirt Day began in Nova Scotia in 2007, after students organized a show of support for a peer who faced bullying for wearing a pink shirt. It has since become an annual anti-bullying campaign observed across Canada [common]. Schools and governments often use the day to promote safe, inclusive learning environments [common].
