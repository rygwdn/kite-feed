Tesla ends standard Autopilot, shifts lane-centering to $99 FSD

Category: Technology

Tesla has discontinued its standard “Basic Autopilot” bundle for new Model 3 and Model Y purchases in the US and Canada, removing the Autosteer (lane-centering) feature that previously shipped with those cars [3][4][8]. Traffic-Aware Cruise Control (TACC) remains included, but buyers who want lane-centering now need the Full Self-Driving (Supervised) subscription priced at $99 per month [3][4]. The shift comes as Tesla pushes FSD toward a subscription-only model and as the company faces regulatory and legal scrutiny over how it marketed Autopilot/FSD capabilities, including a California DMV-related license suspension that was stayed for 60 days [4][9]. For customers, the clear upside is simpler packaging and clearer expectations about what the software does and doesn’t do—while the immediate tradeoff is a higher ongoing cost for a feature many drivers consider a baseline driver-assist tool [3][2].

I should also mention that the $99/month for supervised FSD will rise as FSD's capabilities improve

— Elon Musk (Engadget)

Did you know: Ars Technica noted Tesla helped popularize over-the-air updates and touchscreen-heavy vehicle interfaces that other automakers later copied [4].

Key Points

  • Upfront option ends: After February 14, Tesla will no longer offer FSD as a one-time $8,000 purchase, leaving subscription as the only route to get FSD features (and, for new buyers, Autosteer) [3][7].
  • Price may rise: Elon Musk wrote on X that the $99/month supervised FSD price will increase “as FSD's capabilities improve,” without giving timing or a new price point [3][7].
  • Buyer checklist: Digital Trends reported Tesla has quietly removed Autosteer from all new Model 3 and Model Y variants, including Performance trims, prompting buyers to verify exactly which driver-assist features are included at purchase [2].
  • Legal pressure: Ars Technica said Autopilot has faced investigations and lawsuits, including wrongful-death cases and a $329 million judgment against Tesla, alongside a California administrative law judge’s finding of deceptive marketing that led to a license suspension later stayed for 60 days [4].
  • Subscription targets: Engadget linked the move to Tesla’s broader push for more active FSD subscriptions, noting Musk has tied ambitious subscription goals to compensation-related milestones [3].

Timeline

  • Thursday: Tesla announces Autosteer now requires $99/month FSD subscription [3].
  • After February 14: Tesla ends $8,000 FSD purchase, leaving subscription-only access [3][7].
  • In December: California administrative law judge finds deceptive marketing, suspends license [4].

Technical Details

  • Autosteer (lane-centering): Tesla’s Autosteer is a lane-keeping assist feature that actively centers the car within a lane; Tesla is moving it from the former Basic Autopilot bundle into the FSD subscription for new Model 3/Y buyers [3][4].
  • Traffic-Aware Cruise Control (TACC): TACC is adaptive cruise control that adjusts speed based on traffic ahead; it remains a standard feature even after Tesla removed Basic Autopilot for new Model 3/Y orders [3][4].
  • FSD (Supervised) subscription: Tesla’s “Full Self-Driving (Supervised)” is a Level 2-style driver-assistance system that still requires human attention, and it now acts as the paid gatekeeper for Autosteer on new purchases at $99/month [4][7].

Industry Impact

  • Subscription software: The move reinforces the automotive shift toward “features as a service,” where core capabilities can be enabled via monthly fees rather than included in the sticker price [3][4].
  • Competitive packaging: By removing lane-centering from standard trims, Tesla changes the baseline feature comparison shoppers make against other automakers’ driver-assistance bundles, potentially reshaping how rivals package and price similar systems [2][4].
  • Regulatory playbook: High-profile scrutiny of Autopilot/FSD marketing may encourage clearer labeling and consumer disclosures across the driver-assistance industry, especially around “supervised” use and driver responsibility [4][9].

Historical Background

Tesla historically bundled “Basic Autopilot” (adaptive cruise plus lane-keeping) with many vehicles and used over-the-air software updates to expand features over time, influencing broader industry expectations for software-defined cars [4]. More recently, regulators and courts have scrutinized Tesla’s Autopilot/FSD naming and marketing claims, and Ars Technica reported a California administrative law judge found deceptive marketing and suspended Tesla’s license to sell cars in California, with a 60-day stay [4].

Q&A

Q: Does this change affect current Tesla owners or only new purchases?

A: Engadget reported the paywall applies to new Model 3 and Model Y purchases in the US and Canada, not existing owners [3].

Q: What happens to Tesla’s ability to sell cars in California?

A: Ars Technica reported an administrative law judge suspended Tesla’s license to sell cars in California, and the California DMV stayed that decision for 60 days [4].

Q: What is a practical annual cost of the $99/month plan?

A: At $99 per month, a full year of supervised FSD totals $1,188, as calculated in Engadget’s coverage [3].

Action Items

  • Confirm driver-assist features before taking delivery: In the Tesla order flow or purchase agreement, verify whether Autosteer is included or requires the $99/month FSD subscription, especially for new Model 3/Y orders [2][3].
  • Compare ownership costs under subscription-only FSD: If you want lane-centering, estimate your expected subscription duration and compare it to the previous $8,000 purchase option that ends after February 14 [3][7].
  • Set realistic expectations for “supervised” driving: Treat FSD/Autopilot as Level 2 driver assistance that requires active driver attention, as outlets emphasized ongoing human responsibility despite Tesla’s branding [4].
