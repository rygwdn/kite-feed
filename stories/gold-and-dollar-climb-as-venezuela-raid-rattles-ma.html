Gold and dollar climb as Venezuela raid rattles markets

Category: Business

Markets opened the first trading week of 2026 digesting the U.S. military operation that captured Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro, an event that raised geopolitical uncertainty and pushed investors toward traditional “safe-haven” assets [4][1][19]. Gold jumped to around $4,403–$4,404 per ounce in spot trading, while the U.S. dollar also strengthened, reflecting demand for perceived stability during headline-driven risk [1][12]. Oil’s reaction was more mixed, with some early gains fading as traders weighed Venezuela’s reduced near-term role in global supply and an already well-supplied market [12][13][18].

Primary Source: View original article

The physical global oil market situation remains the same. Oil prices have declined due to an oversupplied global oil market.

— Rob Hummel (Fortune)

Did you know: SCMP cited Wind data showing spot gold briefly traded above $4,420 per ounce in early Monday trading before easing [1].

Key Points

  • Energy stocks: U.S. oil equities moved sharply higher in early trading, with Chevron shares up about 8% premarket as investors priced in potential longer-run shifts around Venezuela and U.S. producers [20].
  • Supply reality: Despite Venezuela’s vast reserves, its production is currently less than 1% of global daily output, which helps explain why many analysts see limited immediate impact on physical oil flows [13].
  • OPEC+ stance: OPEC+ said it would keep production steady through the first quarter, reinforcing the view that global crude supply remains ample even as geopolitics heats up [12].
  • Crypto bid: Bitcoin rose to a three-week high and traded above $93,000 as some investors also used crypto as a risk-hedge during the Venezuela headlines [24][16].
  • Viral spillover: A photo shared by President Trump showing Maduro in a Nike Tech Fleece tracksuit went viral, with multiple outlets reporting a surge in searches and social-media chatter around the outfit [8][10].

Perspectives

President Trump: President Trump said Maduro’s removal would unleash a surge of investment in Venezuela’s oil industry and revive output, though he framed it as a longer-run opportunity rather than an immediate supply shift.

Sources: Fortune

Oil market investors: Some market participants focused on the possibility that higher Venezuelan production over time could add to global oversupply and weigh on crude prices, making the price path less about geopolitics and more about barrels.

Sources: Business Insider

Macro traders: Investors rotated into gold and other havens as a straightforward hedge against geopolitical risk, a pattern that tends to appear when uncertainty rises suddenly.

Sources: BBC

Equity strategists: Some equity coverage emphasized that risk appetite stayed resilient, with technology shares helping support broader markets even as havens rose.

Sources: Bloomberg

Timeline

  • Saturday: U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro in a Caracas raid [12][13].
  • Sunday evening: U.S. stock futures and oil edged higher as markets digested implications [12].
  • Monday: Maduro set to face drug trafficking charges in New York [21].

Technical Details

  • Safe-haven assets: Assets such as gold and the U.S. dollar often attract inflows when investors want to reduce exposure to geopolitical uncertainty; this pattern appeared after the Venezuela news [4][1].
  • Dollar index: The U.S. dollar index measures the dollar versus a basket of major currencies; it rose to 98.76 from 98.41 in early trading, according to Wind data cited by SCMP [1].
  • WTI vs. Brent: WTI is the main U.S. crude benchmark and Brent is the main international benchmark; both moved only modestly in early trade despite the headlines [12].

Industry Impact

  • Apparel marketing: Nike received an unexpected surge of attention after a widely shared photo and meme cycle around the Nike Tech Fleece outfit, with reports of increased search activity and claims of sellouts in some channels [8][10].
  • Defense sector: Defense shares helped lift parts of Asia’s markets, reflecting how geopolitical events can quickly translate into sector rotations, even when broader indexes stay calm [17].
  • Precious metals: Gold and silver rose as investors sought protection from geopolitical volatility, supporting near-term demand for bullion-linked products and miners [12][5].

Historical Background

Venezuela holds the world’s largest proven oil reserves, but years of underinvestment, mismanagement, and U.S. sanctions have contributed to a long decline in production [12]. Even with huge reserves on paper, current output is small relative to the global market, which is why analysts say a meaningful recovery would take substantial capital and time [13].

Q&A

Q: Why did some reports say oil rose while others said it fell?

A: Coverage described intraday swings: oil initially edged up as traders reacted to geopolitics, then softened as the market refocused on oversupply and Venezuela’s limited near-term production role [12][18].

Q: What specific market moves showed a “mixed reaction” beyond gold and oil?

A: Reports pointed to uneven performance across assets—precious metals up, oil softer, and stock futures roughly steady—indicating investors hedged risk without fully abandoning equities [12][19].

Q: What could keep Venezuela’s oil output from rebounding quickly even with new investment interest?

A: Analysts cited aging infrastructure, years of underinvestment, and the need for very large capital commitments over a long period, implying production recovery would be gradual [12][13].

Action Items

  • Stress-test portfolio exposure to oil-price swings: If you hold energy stocks or airline/transport names, model a range of crude outcomes given continued oversupply and policy uncertainty highlighted in market coverage [12][13].
  • Rebalance hedges deliberately (not emotionally): If you use gold as a hedge, decide on a target allocation and rebalance toward it rather than chasing rapid price moves reported after the Venezuela headlines [4][1].
