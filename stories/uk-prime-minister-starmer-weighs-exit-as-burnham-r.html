UK Prime Minister Starmer weighs exit as Burnham returns

Category: World

U.K. Prime Minister Keir Starmer is weighing whether to set out a timetable for leaving office as soon as Monday after pressure from Labour colleagues intensified following Andy Burnham’s special-election victory in Makerfield last week [9][1][2][20]. Starmer has publicly said he would stay and fight any leadership challenge, while allies said he spent the weekend at Chequers considering his position [1][9][51][12]. Burnham, the former Greater Manchester mayor, is due to be sworn in as a House of Commons lawmaker on Monday, a step required for any Labour leadership bid [3][9][20]. If Starmer resigns or loses the party leadership while Labour retains its large parliamentary majority, his successor as Labour leader would be positioned to become prime minister without a general election [3][18][11].

Key Points

  • More than 100 Labour MPs have publicly called on Starmer to resign, including in a signed letter last month that warned against a leadership contest [49][36].
  • Burnham's victory: Burnham's Makerfield win nearly doubled Labour's majority in the seat and defeated a Reform UK candidate at a time when Reform had led national polls for more than a year [3][2][29].
  • Possible contest: Wes Streeting, who resigned as health secretary last month in protest at Starmer's leadership, has said he would run if Labour holds a contest [9][15].
  • Fiscal constraints: Dawn reported that any Burnham government would have limited room for additional borrowing because Britain has the highest borrowing costs in the Group of Seven amid high debt, interest payments and weak growth [2].
  • U.S. comment: President Trump wrote on Truth Social that Starmer "will resign" and criticized him on immigration and energy, while a U.K. official said the government had not communicated anything to Trump [21][12][19].

Perspectives

Peter Kyle, business secretary: Kyle told the BBC that reports of a Starmer resignation were “speculation” and said he expected any decision to reflect what Starmer judged to be the country’s interest.

Historical Background

Britain voted to leave the European Union in a June 2016 referendum, after which David Cameron announced he would resign as prime minister [27][common]. Since then, Theresa May, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss and Rishi Sunak also left office, and several sources said a Starmer departure would add to a decade of unusually rapid turnover at No. 10 [27][3][9].
