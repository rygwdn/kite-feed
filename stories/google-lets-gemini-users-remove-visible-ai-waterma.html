Google lets Gemini users remove visible AI watermarks

Category: Technology

Google said on Aug. 14 that Gemini users can switch off the visible watermark on AI-generated images, videos and music created with its tools. The mark had appeared automatically on generated media [9][4][5][10]. Josh Woodward, Google's vice president for Gemini, said the toggle applies to Nano Banana, Omni and Lyria models; TechCrunch reported it will be available in Gemini and Google's Flow video editor, with Search support planned later [9][2][12]. The setting, called Media Watermark, controls whether future creations display the visible mark. Google's invisible SynthID watermark and C2PA metadata will remain embedded [5][6][13][16]. Google said the feature is rolling out over the next few days and will not appear where law requires visible AI labels; Engadget cited the EU and South Korea as examples, and Android Police also listed China [2][8][15].

Primary Source: View original article

We’re striking a balance here between creative control and safety: while the visible watermarks are now optional, invisible SynthID watermarks and C2PA metadata are still being used for transparency. So you can still use Gemini or Search to see if an image was AI-generated

— Josh Woodward (TechCrunch)

Key Points

  • Model update: Android Headlines reported that the watermark setting arrived with Gemini 3.7 Flash, which replaced 3.6 Flash across Android, iOS, web and desktop apps [7].

Perspectives

Gizmodo: Gizmodo said removing the visible mark reduces an immediate cue for AI-generated material and noted that C2PA metadata can be stripped, including by social media services.

Sources: Gizmodo

Canaltech: Canaltech said its own tests found the option was already available and working.

Sources: Canaltech

Industry Impact

  • AI provenance: Google will keep using SynthID and C2PA metadata after making visible marks optional, while TechCrunch reported Google is also releasing Credentio for app-level validation [9][1].
  • Platform policy: Yahoo Hong Kong reported that OpenAI and Meta products do not use visible watermarks in the same way; it said OpenAI relies on SynthID and C2PA and Meta uses its Content Seal standard [11].
  • AI regulation: TechCrunch linked Google's change to Anthropic's recent move to add watermarks to Claude-generated text and files for EU compliance [9].

Historical Background

Google introduced SynthID through Google DeepMind in 2023 as an invisible watermarking system for AI-generated images [common]. C2PA, a provenance standard backed by technology and media companies, defines metadata that can record how digital content was created or edited [common]. The EU's AI Act entered into force in 2024 and includes transparency obligations for certain AI-generated or manipulated content [common].

Action Items

  • Change Gemini watermark visibility: Open Gemini settings, select Media Watermark, and choose On or Off for future generated media [5][6].
