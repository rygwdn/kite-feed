Arab and Muslim states condemn US envoy Huckabee remarks

Category: World

Arab and Muslim-majority countries, along with regional blocs, criticized US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee after comments he made in an interview with Tucker Carlson that appeared to endorse a much broader Israeli territorial claim framed in biblical terms [1][3][5][7][8][6][10][2]. In the exchange, Huckabee said he would not oppose Israel taking territory described in biblical language as stretching from the Nile to the Euphrates. He also said Israel is not seeking to expand and argued it has security rights in land it legitimately holds [1][3][5][8][2]. A joint statement cited by Pakistan’s Foreign Office and reported by Dawn said foreign ministers from Pakistan and other Arab and Muslim states — along with the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC), the League of Arab States (LAS), and the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) — rejected the remarks as a breach of international law and the UN Charter, warning they could threaten regional security and stability [3][6][10]. Several governments also issued their own denunciations, and some framed the comments as out of step with diplomatic norms and with efforts to de-escalate the Gaza war and pursue a political settlement [1][7][6].

Key Points

  • Who signed the joint condemnation: The statement cited by Dawn lists Pakistan, Egypt, Jordan, the UAE, Qatar, Indonesia, Kuwait, Oman, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Lebanon, Syria and Palestine as signatories, along with the OIC, LAS and GCC [3].
  • Arab League detail: RT says the Arab League, a coalition of 22 member states, criticized the remarks as contrary to diplomatic norms and likely to inflame religious and national emotions [1].
  • Additional national reactions: Al Jazeera reports Saudi Arabia joined Egypt and Jordan in denouncing the remarks, with Jordan calling them “absurd and provocative” [7].
  • US domestic split: The Guardian says the Carlson–Huckabee exchange underscored a divide on the US right between factions skeptical of close US-Israel ties and long-standing Christian conservative support for Israel [9].

Perspectives

Arab League: The Arab League condemned the remarks as extremist, said they clash with diplomatic norms, and warned they could inflame religious and national emotions [1][6].
