Two men arrested after suspected break-in at Prince William’s Norfolk home

Category: World

Police arrested two men on suspicion of burglary and trespass after responding to a report of suspicious activity at the Sandringham estate in Norfolk on Monday evening, Nov. 23. Norfolk Police said officers searched the area with police dogs, drones and a helicopter. The men remained in custody on Tuesday and had not been charged [28][3][5]. British media identified the property as Anmer Hall, Prince William and Princess Catherine’s 10-bedroom country residence. Police said the property was unoccupied at the time [2][8][3]. The late Queen Elizabeth II gave Anmer Hall to the couple as a wedding present in 2011. They use it for holidays and short breaks, while their main residence is Forest Lodge on the Windsor estate [13][4][5]. Norfolk Police said they are continuing to investigate. A Kensington Palace representative said the couple’s office would not comment on security matters [5][26].

  • Estate access: The Sandringham estate covers about 80 square kilometres; visitors can access its parkland year-round, while the house and gardens are currently closed [8][27].

  • Nearby resident: Val Blackmur described Anmer Hall as secluded and out of view, saying people would be shocked by an attempted break-in there [27].
  • Downing Street meeting: On the day the arrests were announced, William met Prime Minister Andy Burnham at 10 Downing Street to discuss homelessness, defence and security, among other topics [16][30].
