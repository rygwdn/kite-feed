Brazilian right-wing rivals endorse Flávio Bolsonaro ahead of runoff

Category: World

Two right-wing politicians who lost Brazil’s first-round presidential vote endorsed Flávio Bolsonaro on Monday. Former Goiás governor Ronaldo Caiado and former Minas Gerais governor Romeu Zema backed the senator ahead of his Oct. 25 runoff against President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva [19][22]. Bolsonaro finished first with 47.03% of the vote to Lula’s 45.16%, a margin of about 2.2 million votes, but neither won a majority [30][37]. Lula’s Workers’ Party chairman, Edinho Silva, said Lula would meet campaign managers to refine his message, review regional turnout and redirect resources to battleground states. Silva also identified televised debates as important to the runoff campaign [10]. Bolsonaro has been contacting political figures, party leaders and regional allies to consolidate support [10]. Eliminated candidates received about 9 million votes combined, while the two finalists were separated by roughly 2.2 million [10]. Three of the eliminated candidates have said they will endorse neither finalist [12]. The runoff is scheduled for Oct. 25 [30].

Key Points

  • Congressional shift: Bolsonaro’s Liberal Party won 19 of the 54 Senate seats contested and is set to hold 28 of 81; the Guardian reported that its combined hard-right allies would control 43 seats [3][38].
  • Market response: Brazil’s Ibovespa stock index rose as much as 8% and the real gained 1.4% against the dollar after the first-round result [25].
  • Campaign rhetoric: Bolsonaro accused Lula’s government of setting records for violence against women, taxes and business closures, claims the government rejects [20].
  • Campaign issues: Analysts cited crime, household debt and food costs as immediate voter concerns that Lula’s campaign did not address as directly as Bolsonaro’s security message [7][4].
  • Runoff endorsements: Caiado received 2.18% of the first-round vote, while Zema received 0.27%, according to TASS [22].
