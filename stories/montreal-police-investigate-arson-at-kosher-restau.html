Montreal police investigate arson at kosher restaurant Nöam

Category: Canada

Montreal police are investigating a deliberately set fire that heavily damaged Nöam, a kosher restaurant on Decarie Boulevard in the Côte-des-Neiges–Notre-Dame-de-Grâce borough, around 3 a.m. Saturday [5][7][3][1]. The Montreal fire department said nearly 80 firefighters responded to the two-alarm blaze near Plamondon Avenue and Vézina Street. No injuries were reported [7][9]. Police said they had not ruled out any hypothesis, but investigators had not determined a motive and were not treating the fire as a hate crime at this stage [7][3][5]. A video published by CBC and cited by several reports appears to show a man starting the fire before running away, but police had not confirmed the authenticity of the images [1][5]. Federal Public Safety Minister Gary Anandasangaree said the government was concerned by what appeared to be an arson attack on a gathering place for Montreal's Jewish community. B'nai Brith Canada and the Centre for Israel and Jewish Affairs condemned the incident and urged stronger government action [1][6][8]. La Presse reported that a separate fire also damaged a mosque in Côte-des-Neiges during the same night [5].

Key Points

  • Damage scope: The fire caused extensive damage to the two-storey building, and Nöam sustained the heaviest damage among businesses in the commercial property [7][9][5].
  • Camera account: A neighbouring garage owner told the Montreal Gazette that security cameras captured a young man setting the fire [6].
  • Political response: Liberal MP Anthony Housefather said Saturday that the matter must be investigated as a hate crime, and later thanked U.S. officials for expressing solidarity with the owners and community [9].
  • Owners react: Nöam's owners wrote that the restaurant meant a great deal to them and many others, calling the devastation an immense pain [5].

Perspectives

Historical Background

Jewish organizations linked the fire to a recent pattern of attacks on Canadian Jewish institutions, while also saying investigators should determine the motive before conclusions are drawn [6][8]. Côte-des-Neiges has a substantial Jewish population and Decarie Boulevard includes community institutions and businesses serving that neighbourhood [6][7].
