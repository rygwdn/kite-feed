Trump ties Canada trade talks to zero deficit as tariff deadline nears

Category: Canada

President Donald Trump said Friday the United States and Canada are working toward a “fair” trade deal, but tied negotiations to eliminating the U.S. trade deficit with Canada and said more work remains. Trump spoke after meeting Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in China. U.S. government figures cited by The Globe and Mail put the U.S. goods deficit with Canada at $48.3 billion and its services surplus at $27.7 billion; Trump has cited a deficit of about $80 billion [1][6][11]. Ottawa is seeking an interim tariff arrangement covering steel, aluminum, autos, lumber and other sectors, partly to avert a threatened Jan. 1 tariff increase on Canadian autos. Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc said the aim is to lower U.S. tariffs, not necessarily eliminate them, and that talks could continue into January or February. An interim arrangement could lead to formal talks on a review of the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement, while Canada is pursuing other trade ties to reduce its reliance on the U.S. [4][11][2]

Frankly, if you can get an interim deal, I'd take it, and then put it to us in the new majority to legislate an even better deal

Key Points

  • Democratic divisions: U.S. Sen. Chris Coons said some Democrats representing steel-producing states may advocate retaining steel and aluminum tariffs, though he supports removing Section 232 duties [4].
  • Trade diversification: Carney said Canada would not wait indefinitely for Washington. His government recently signed trade deals with the Philippines and the Association of Southeast Asian Nations [2].
  • CUSMA review: The U.S. did not renew the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement in July, triggering annual reviews that could continue for up to a decade before the pact expires without an extension [1][4].
  • Truck sticking point: Ottawa objected to a proposed auto-tariff reduction that excluded Canadian-made trucks [6][11].

Perspectives

President Donald Trump: Trump highlighted a possible Keystone pipeline, saying Carney wants to build it for the United States but that a trade deal would require more than that [1][6].
