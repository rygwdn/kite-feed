B.C. NDP MLA Joan Phillip dies at 74

Category: Canada

Joan Phillip, a British Columbia NDP legislator and Indigenous leader, died Friday afternoon at 74, the Union of British Columbia Indian Chiefs said in a family statement [1][3][4][5]. The union said she died peacefully, surrounded by family, including her husband, Grand Chief Stewart Phillip, their children and grandchildren [1][3][5]. Phillip had been ill for months, and the union asked for donations to the B.C. Cancer Society in lieu of flowers [3][5]. Premier David Eby said British Columbia had lost “a powerful and respected leader,” describing Phillip as an “epic fighter” who dedicated her life to making the world “a better and fairer place” [3][4][5].

Key Points

  • Hospital vote: Eby said on April 23 that Phillip was very ill, a day after she voted on legislative business from her hospital bed [5].
  • Policy stand: Vancouver Sun reported that in office, Phillip was best known for opposing the suspension of the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act; Eby backed off the plan at the beginning of April [5].
  • Public impact: CBC’s headline on tributes to Phillip after her death at 74 said, “Her impact was nationwide” [2].
