Update: Trump floats tariffs as Congress reassures Denmark

Category: World

In the latest development in a growing U.S.-Denmark dispute, President Trump said he may impose tariffs on countries that do not support U.S. control of Greenland, arguing the Arctic territory is needed for national security [4][26][24][6]. The remarks came as a bipartisan delegation of U.S. lawmakers traveled to Copenhagen to meet Danish and Greenlandic officials and signal that Congress does not back any move to take Greenland by force [1][9][14][8]. Denmark and Greenland have continued to reject the takeover idea, even as the sides agreed to set up a working group to keep talks going [6][20][3].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: An Inuit leader noted Inuit people make up about 90% of Greenland’s population [28].

Key Points

  • Congressional leverage: Sen. Lisa Murkowski said Congress can use appropriations as a constitutional “power of the purse” check, framing Greenland as an ally rather than an asset [1].
  • War powers push: Sen. Chris Coons said he will pursue legislation to curb unilateral presidential action, including a bipartisan bill introduced this week aimed at preventing an invasion of a fellow NATO member [1].
  • GOP warnings: Rep. Don Bacon said a military move could trigger impeachment, while Sen. Thom Tillis said Congress could pass a war powers resolution and potentially override a veto if action looked imminent [2].
  • Greenland anxieties: Greenlandic officials and residents have described mounting fear and stress, including reports that children are afraid and people are struggling to sleep amid takeover rhetoric [30][29].
  • Envoy’s message: A U.S. special envoy, Jeff Landry, said a deal to acquire Greenland “should and will be made,” and planned a visit in March [22].

Perspectives

President Donald Trump: Trump says the U.S. needs Greenland for national security and has suggested tariffs on countries that oppose his plans [4].

U.S. Congress delegation (bipartisan): Visiting lawmakers say they aim to reassure Denmark and Greenland and emphasize Congress’s role in limiting any military action [9].

Greenland’s leadership and political parties: Greenland’s Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen and party leaders have rejected annexation language and called for respect for Greenlanders as a people and a democracy [31].

Inuit leader Aqqaluk Lynge: Lynge warned that a U.S. invasion would be catastrophic for Greenland’s Inuit population and urged international support [28].

Timeline

  • Wednesday: Denmark and Greenland foreign ministers met U.S. officials; disagreement persisted [6][20].
  • Thursday: U.S., Denmark and Greenland agreed to form a working group [6][3].
  • Friday: Trump raised possible tariffs; bipartisan lawmakers visited Copenhagen to ease tensions [4][1][9].

Technical Details

  • Power of the purse: Congress can shape or block executive actions through appropriations and federal spending decisions, which Sen. Murkowski highlighted as a check on presidential policy [1].
  • War Powers resolution: Lawmakers discussed using war powers tools to require congressional signoff for military action, with some saying it could pass even over a veto in extreme circumstances [2][1].
  • Working group diplomacy: The U.S., Denmark and Greenland agreed to a continuing working group, described as meeting every two to three weeks, as a channel to manage differences [6][20].

Industry Impact

  • Arctic security policy: Trump’s case centers on Arctic security concerns involving Russia and China, while some lawmakers dispute whether Greenland faces an immediate threat, underscoring how threat assessments can drive defense procurement and basing debates [1][26].
  • Transatlantic trade ties: Tariff threats aimed at allied countries risk complicating broader U.S.-Europe economic cooperation, especially if businesses anticipate shifting costs or countermeasures [3][4].
  • Greenland governance: Heightened outside pressure has amplified attention on Greenland’s autonomy and domestic politics, as local leaders and residents weigh their future amid global interest [31][27].

Historical Background

Greenland is an autonomous territory within the Kingdom of Denmark, which controls its foreign and defense policy [6][31]. Greenland’s Self-Determination Act was enacted in 2009, building a framework for self-rule and the possibility of future decisions about sovereignty [28]. President Trump has pursued the idea of bringing Greenland under U.S. control since his first term and has renewed that push since returning to office [11].

Q&A

A: It established a self-rule system and a pathway for Greenlanders to make future choices about their status, as discussed in the context of the Self-Determination Act enacted in 2009 [28].

Q: Which U.S. officials took part in the White House talks with Denmark and Greenland’s foreign ministers?

A: The Hindu reports the meeting included Vice President J.D. Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio [6].

Q: What are Greenlanders saying on the ground about daily life amid the rhetoric?

A: Reporting describes anxiety and fear among residents, including comments from Greenlandic minister Naaja Nathanielsen about sleeplessness and children being afraid [30][27].
