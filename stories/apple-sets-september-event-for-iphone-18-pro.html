Apple sets September event for iPhone 18 Pro

Category: Technology

Apple scheduled its “Surprise and shine” hardware event for September 9 at 10 a.m. Pacific time at Apple Park, where it is expected to introduce the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, Apple Watch Series 12, Apple Watch Ultra 4 and possibly its first foldable iPhone, widely referred to in reports as the iPhone Ultra [2][4][7][6][10][11][13]. CNET and Ada Derana reported that the event would be the first major product launch under incoming CEO John Ternus, after Tim Cook announced in April that he would step down as CEO and move to executive chairman while Ternus takes over on September 1 [4][11][13]. Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman reported that AirPods 5 could arrive as early as September and that a new iPad mini with an OLED display should launch by the end of October, after Apple last updated the iPad mini in October 2024 with the A17 Pro chip, Apple Pencil Pro support and Apple Intelligence compatibility [1][2][3][5]. The next iPad mini is expected to add an OLED screen, faster performance, improved water resistance through a redesigned casing and a vibration-based speaker system [1][2]. Apple may also alter its usual preorder cadence for the iPhone 18 Pro line: Macwelt reported that preorders could open on Saturday, September 12, rather than Friday, September 11, because September 11 is a U.S. remembrance day [6][10]. The reported fall lineup follows price increases across Apple computer and home devices this summer, which CNET and Ada Derana linked to memory chip supply constraints driven by AI-related demand [4][11][13].

Key Points

  • Staggered lineup: CNET and Apfeltalk reported that Apple may delay the base iPhone 18 until 2027 while launching the foldable model and Pro phones first [4][11].
  • Foldable pricing: Apfeltalk cited expectations for a roughly $2,000 foldable iPhone, while Ada Derana said analysts view high-end foldables as a driver of higher average selling prices and margin growth [11][13].
  • Camera clue: Kien Thuc reported that Apple’s event logo has been interpreted as a hint at new iPhone 18 Pro colors and a variable-aperture camera, possibly limited to the Pro Max model [8].
  • Pencil testing: Macerkopf reported that Apple internally prototyped and tested a shorter Apple Pencil for the foldable iPhone with magnetic attachment and charging, but Gurman considered a launch unlikely [9].
  • Streaming details: HardwareZone reported that Apple will stream the event on Apple’s website, YouTube channel and Apple TV app, with Singapore viewers seeing it at 1 a.m. on September 10 [7].

Perspectives

Apple: Apple has described summer price increases for Macs and iPads as a response to memory and storage chip shortages and in July said supply-chain shortages were "very significant."

Industry Impact

  • Foldable phones: Ada Derana reported that Samsung Electronics currently dominates foldable smartphones and that device makers hope the premium category will become mainstream as Apple prepares to enter it [13].
  • Component supply: CNET and Ada Derana linked Apple’s summer hardware price increases to memory-chip shortages tied to AI demand, while Ada Derana said Apple expected the snags to dampen current-quarter sales [4][13].
  • Tablet displays: MacRumors reported that Apple’s move would bring OLED beyond the iPad Pro to the iPad mini, while the iPad Air and entry-level iPad remained on LCD panels [2].

Historical Background

Steve Jobs introduced the first iPhone in 2007 and publicly criticized smartphone styluses at the launch, a detail Macerkopf noted in connection with Apple’s internal testing of a Pencil for a foldable iPhone [9][common].

Business Angle

  • A roughly $2,000 foldable iPhone could raise average selling prices if demand holds, but the high price also narrows the addressable market [11][13].
  • Memory and storage shortages create margin risk if Apple cannot continue passing higher component costs to customers [4][13][common].
  • Delaying the base iPhone 18 would concentrate September demand around higher-margin Pro and foldable models, but it could also defer volume sales into 2027 [4][11].
  • An OLED iPad mini refresh may protect a niche product line, but upgrade demand beyond existing iPad users remains unclear [1][common].
