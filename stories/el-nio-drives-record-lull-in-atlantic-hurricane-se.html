El Niño drives record lull in Atlantic hurricane season

Category: Science

The 2026 Atlantic hurricane season had produced no hurricanes by Sept. 12, marking the latest date for the season's first hurricane in the satellite-observation era. Only five relatively weak, short-lived tropical storms had formed. The National Hurricane Center said no tropical cyclone activity was expected over the next seven days, even though September is normally near the season's peak [3][11][2]. Meteorologists attribute the unusually quiet Atlantic primarily to a powerful El Niño, which strengthens upper-level winds over the Caribbean and tropical Atlantic. The resulting vertical wind shear disrupts developing systems. The season's overall activity measure was at a post-1950 record low and stood at 7% of normal for this point in the year, according to Colorado State University hurricane expert Phil Klotzbach [3][2][20]. Experts warned that the lull did not eliminate the possibility of a damaging late-season storm; the National Hurricane Center said about half of normal seasonal activity typically occurs after Sept. 10 [18][11].

Did you know: No Atlantic season in the satellite era has ended without producing at least two hurricanes [11][18].

Key Points

  • Storm names: The five named storms so far are Arthur, Bertha, Cristobal, Dolly and Edouard [11][14].
  • Near miss: Edouard came within about 22 kph of hurricane strength before making landfall near the Texas-Louisiana border on Sept. 1 [15].
  • Season forecast: Colorado State University’s annual 2026 forecast projected nine named storms in the Atlantic basin [19].

Perspectives

Weather Prediction Center meteorologist Nick Novella said the record is based on the time elapsed without a storm reaching the 74-mph (119-kph) threshold used to classify a hurricane [3].

Sources: PBS NewsHour

Timeline

  • 1 June 2026: Atlantic hurricane season officially began across the basin [19][6]
  • 1 September 2026: Edouard approached hurricane strength before making landfall near the Texas-Louisiana border [15]
  • 10 September 2026: Statistical peak passed with no Atlantic hurricane having formed [15]
  • 11 September 2026: National Hurricane Center confirmed that the season had surpassed the satellite-era record for the latest first hurricane [11][18]
  • 12 September 2026: Season reached 103 days without a hurricane [6][15]

Technical Details

  • Dry air and subsidence: El Niño also favors drier air and subsidence over the Atlantic, adding to conditions that hinder tropical-cyclone development [2][20].
  • Accumulated Cyclone Energy: ACE combines storm number, intensity and duration; it had reached 4.4 by Sept. 12, compared with an average near 62 for that date, according to Colorado State University data cited by WRAL [14].
  • Pacific temperature anomaly: Equatorial Pacific waters were more than 2 degrees Celsius above normal, and the pattern could persist deep into autumn [2].

Scientific Significance

  • Ocean heat versus atmospheric suppression: Atlantic waters remain warm enough to provide energy for storms, while atmospheric conditions have hindered tropical-cyclone development [2][20].

Historical Background

Satellite-era comparisons begin in 1966, when monitoring became consistent enough for meteorologists to compare the timing of Atlantic hurricane formation [11][2]. Earlier pre-1950 records include years without a hurricane this late, but meteorologists consider those observations incomplete because ocean monitoring was limited [3].
