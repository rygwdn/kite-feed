Samsung to add native Google Photos features on TVs

Category: Technology

Samsung says it plans to natively integrate Google Photos into its TV lineup starting in 2026, aiming to make personal photo libraries part of the day-to-day on-screen experience rather than something you only cast from a phone [1][5][10]. The rollout is expected to arrive in phases, beginning with Google Photos “Memories” and followed later by more AI-driven creation and discovery features that build slideshows and transform images [1][5][10]. Samsung is positioning the integration as a more social, living-room-friendly way to relive photos and videos on a larger display, while some coverage notes it is notable that this lands first on Samsung TVs rather than Android/Google TV hardware [3][11][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Rediscover and relive cherished moments

— Kevin Lee (Mashable)

Did you know: Samsung says its TVs will be the first to bring Google Photos’ Memories feature to televisions, with that experience initially exclusive to Samsung for six months [1][5].

Key Points

  • Six-month exclusivity: Samsung said the Google Photos “Memories” experience will be exclusive to Samsung TVs for six months after it launches [5][1].
  • Feature cadence: At launch, the native app is expected to start with “Memories,” with later 2026 updates adding photo editing (including Nano Banana) and a photo-to-video option [10][8].
  • TV UI integration: Samsung says Photos will tie into its “Vision AI Companion,” surfacing images during “contextual and convenient moments” while navigating the TV interface [1][8].
  • Platform surprise: Several outlets highlight that Google Photos still lacks a confirmed native Android TV app beyond casting and screensaver-style uses, even as Samsung gets a first wave of deeper integration [11][9].
  • Similar playbook: Early descriptions suggest the Samsung implementation may resemble how Google Photos already shows up on Google TV, implying familiar viewing patterns for users who have tried Google’s own TV ecosystem [2].

Perspectives

Samsung: The company frames the partnership as a living-room upgrade that blends personal media into the TV experience, including context-aware moments via Vision AI Companion and new photo experiences rolling out across 2026.

Sources: Engadget

Google/Android TV watchers: Coverage points out the irony that a deeper Google Photos experience is arriving first on Samsung TVs, while Android TV still has no announced native Google Photos app beyond casting and limited display functions.

Sources: Android Authority

Product analysts/press: Some reporting characterizes the move as a practical convenience upgrade—reducing the need for external drives, laptops, or casting—while noting that parts of the experience may feel similar to existing Google TV behavior.

Sources: Android Headlines

Technical Details

  • Vision AI Companion: Samsung says Google Photos will integrate with its Vision AI Companion so photos can appear during “contextual and convenient moments” while using the TV interface, not only inside a standalone gallery app [1].
  • Memories curation: The planned “Memories” experience will surface curated stories based on people, locations and meaningful moments, bringing Google Photos’ highlight-style organization to the big screen [1][3].
  • AI creation tools: Samsung says “Create with AI” will use Google DeepMind image generation/editing to apply themed templates and can turn still images into short videos [1].

Industry Impact

  • Smart TV competition: If Samsung delivers a smooth, native Photos experience, it could pressure other TV platforms to offer comparable personal-media integrations rather than relying on casting as the default [1][11].
  • AI in the living room: The planned photo remixing, templated edits, and photo-to-video features push generative AI into a shared household device, potentially normalizing “creation” workflows on TVs instead of phones and PCs [1][10].
  • Platform partnerships: The rollout highlights how major consumer features can land first through OEM partnerships even when a related Google-controlled TV platform exists, underscoring the leverage large hardware makers can have in distribution negotiations [11][9].

Historical Background

For years, watching Google Photos on TVs has largely meant casting from a phone or using ambient/screen-saver style photo displays rather than a full native TV app experience [11]. Google TV has already offered a more integrated, TV-first way to surface photos, making Samsung’s planned approach feel familiar to some observers even though it is arriving via Samsung’s own TV platform [2].

Q&A

Q: Which Samsung TV models will actually support the native Google Photos experience?

A: Samsung’s announcement describes the feature coming to its TV lineup in 2026, but the coverage does not list specific model ranges or year cutoffs yet [5][1].

Q: Will the Google Photos integration require a paid Google One/Photos storage subscription?

A: The articles describe features and timing but do not specify any subscription requirement for using Google Photos on Samsung TVs [10][1].

Q: Will viewers be able to stop “contextual” photo surfacing and keep Photos confined to an app?

A: Samsung says photos may appear at “contextual and convenient moments,” but the reporting does not detail user controls or opt-out settings for that behavior [1].

Action Items

  • Prepare a ‘TV-friendly’ Google Photos album: Create or tidy up albums in Google Photos (family highlights, trips, holidays) so they are ready to browse on a larger screen once the Samsung TV integration rolls out in 2026 [3].
  • Review shared-screen privacy: Decide which albums contain sensitive images and keep them separated, since Samsung’s concept involves photos appearing during TV usage rather than only when you deliberately cast them [1].
