Google expands Gemini in Chrome to India, Canada

Category: Technology

Google is expanding its built-in Gemini AI experiences in the Chrome browser beyond the US, rolling them out to users in India, Canada and New Zealand, and adding support for more than 50 languages [2][3][4][5][6]. The update focuses on a Gemini sidebar that lets people chat with the assistant alongside whatever they’re viewing—meant to cut down on constant tab switching during everyday browsing [3][4]. In India, Google highlighted support for several Indian languages, including Hindi, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Telugu and Tamil, with some outlets describing this as support for eight Indic languages [6][1]. Along with text help—like summarizing or answering questions about what’s on-screen—Gemini in Chrome also offers access to Google’s image generator and can connect with other Google apps such as Gmail, Maps, Calendar and YouTube [3][4]. Google says the feature is optional, can be hidden from the toolbar, and that more countries and languages will follow later in 2026 [3].

Key Points

  • Language examples: Alongside 50+ languages overall, Google and early coverage called out examples like French and Spanish, plus Hindi and Gujarati [3][8].
  • Model callout: Android Headlines describes the expansion as bringing Gemini 3.1 into the browser experience for the newly supported regions [4].
  • Study/workflow use cases: Android Headlines points to uses like summarizing long research papers or complex legal documents, pulling out key takeaways, and generating practice quizzes from what you’re reading [4].
  • India positioning: In India, Google pitched the built-in AI integration as a way to make browsing more intuitive, secure and helpful [1].

Perspectives

Google (official blog via Techmeme): Google framed the expansion as bringing Chrome’s AI features to more regions and languages to help people get more out of the browser [8].

Sources: Techmeme (via The Keyword)

Engadget: Engadget underscored that the Chrome integration includes built-in image generation (described as “Nano Banana”) and noted examples of newly supported languages such as French and Spanish as the rollout broadens [3].

Sources: Engadget

Android Headlines: Android Headlines highlighted practical reading/study workflows—like generating summaries and key takeaways from dense documents and creating practice quizzes—positioning the tool as a way to reduce “tab fatigue” [4].

Timeline

  • 2025: Gemini in Chrome first launched in the United States [2].
  • January 2026: Google introduced the Gemini sidebar interface update in Chrome [3].
  • 10 March 2026: Google announced expansion to India, Canada, and New Zealand and said Gemini in Chrome would support over 50 additional languages [2][6].

Historical Background

Chrome’s Gemini integration first rolled out with limited availability (largely US-only) and a smaller set of supported languages, even after Google’s January 2026 Chrome AI update broadened the overall feature set [5][2][3]. The March 2026 move is the first meaningful step toward wider regional and language support for those Chrome-integrated tools [2].

Action Items

  • Try Gemini in Chrome without changing tabs: In Chrome, click the sparkle icon at the top right to open the Gemini sidebar and chat while keeping your current page visible [3].
  • Remove the Gemini button if you don’t want it: Right-click the sparkle icon and select “unpin” to hide it from the toolbar [3].
  • Use Gemini with Google services you already use: Look for Connected Apps integrations to work across Gmail, Calendar, Maps or YouTube from the Gemini experience [3][4].
