Canada and South Korea sign new defence agreement

Category: Canada

Canada and the Republic of Korea signed a new defence agreement in Ottawa during Canada–Korea Foreign and Defence “2+2” ministerial talks [1][2][4][3]. Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand and National Defence Minister David J. McGuinty welcomed their Korean counterparts to Ottawa for the meeting [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • 2+2 meeting: The agreement was signed on the sidelines of the second Canada–Korea Foreign and Defence “2+2” Ministerial Meeting in Ottawa, hosted by Anita Anand and David J. McGuinty [1][2].
  • Negotiation timeline: Coverage said the two countries wrapped up negotiations for the defence deal in October, ahead of the signing [5].
  • Trade backdrop: Some outlets framed the deal as part of Canada’s push to diversify trade ties while deepening security relationships in the Indo-Pacific [4].
  • Middle-power framing: Several reports noted the agreement came about a month after Mark Carney urged middle powers to work together against economic coercion by major powers [4][3].
