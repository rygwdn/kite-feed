Four soldiers killed in Wainwright fire honoured as 25 remain hospitalized

Category: Canada

The remains of Pte. Aidan Goulding, 23, were escorted from Vancouver airport to a New Westminster funeral home on Wednesday as police, firefighters and members of the public saluted the soldier killed in the Oct. 17 fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta. Goulding was one of four soldiers killed when the grass fire overtook a live-fire training exercise. The funeral for Pte. Shaun Farrell, 23, took place in Prince Albert the same day [3][2][5]. The Department of National Defence said 25 injured soldiers remained hospitalized, 14 had been discharged and four had been transferred to facilities closer to their families in British Columbia and Ontario. The fire injured 39 soldiers in total. Three investigations are examining the incident: a military police investigation, a military board of inquiry and an investigation by Alberta’s chief medical examiner [7][8][9].

Canadians and families are rightly asking how that assessment was reached, what information it was based on, and whether different decisions should have been made.

— Maj.-Gen. Wade Rutland, Commander of the Army’s 1st Canadian Division (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Other victims: The other soldiers killed were Pte. Hayden Lykkemark, 19, of Langley, B.C., and Sgt. Morgan Wong, 26, of Edmonton; Farrell was from Prince Albert, Sask. [3][2]
  • Farrell remembered: At Farrell’s funeral, his commanding officer, Lt.-Col. James Cressall, recalled his determination and the tattoo on his side: “Comfort Kills Dreams” [5][6].
  • Fire assessment: Maj.-Gen. Wade Rutland, commander of the Army’s 1st Canadian Division, said troops had known for hours about the fire roughly one kilometre away, but it was not initially assessed as a threat [9].

Perspectives

Department of National Defence: The department said recovery will differ for each injured person and family, and that support remains available to injured personnel and Canadian Armed Forces members [7][8].

Brig.-Gen. Vanessa Hanrahan: Hanrahan said the fire began hours earlier on the base’s automatic grenade launcher range and was monitored for hours; she did not say whether live ammunition or explosive rounds started it [9].

  • Fire danger measures: The Fire Weather Index estimates potential fire intensity; it was rated “extreme” on Oct. 17, when winds were high and vegetation was very dry.
