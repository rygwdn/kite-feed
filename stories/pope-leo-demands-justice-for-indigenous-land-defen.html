Pope Leo demands justice for Indigenous land defenders killed in Amazon

Category: World

Pope Leo XIV traveled from Cusco to Pucallpa on Sunday, Nov. 15, and demanded justice for Indigenous leaders killed defending their territories, while denouncing deforestation, illegal mining and pollution in the Peruvian Amazon. He told Indigenous representatives and missionaries that protecting the rainforest cannot be separated from protecting the people who live there [3][8][29]. Peru lost 3.2 million hectares of forest between 2001 and 2024, according to government figures cited by Deutsche Welle. Illegal gold miners use mercury to extract gold, contaminating rivers and damaging forests; environmental groups estimate that 57 environmental defenders were murdered in Peru between 2010 and 2024 [8][2][3]. Leo underwent surgery to remove part of a lung on Oct. 13. He visited Cusco, more than 3,400 meters above sea level, before traveling to Pucallpa, where he celebrated Mass for about 100,000 people. He returned to Lima Sunday evening; a farewell Mass and departure for Rome were scheduled for Monday, Nov. 16, with his flight set for 1:45 p.m. [18][6][28][16]

The cry of the earth and the cry of the poor are inseparable.

— Pope Leo XIV (ABC News)

Did you know: The shihuahuaco tree planned for a symbolic planting can live for more than 1,000 years [2].

Key Points

  • Abuse cases: The Awajún and Wampis Women’s Council recorded 370 cases of sexual violence against Indigenous girls in Condorcanqui province from January through July; Leo condemned the complicity and impunity surrounding the abuse [3].
  • Mining raids: Police said three operations against illegal mining in Madre de Dios on Saturday destroyed rafts, motors and other equipment valued at $4.1 million [2].
  • Cusco traditions: At the Inca site of Saqsaywaman, Leo urged communities to pass on religious festivals and processions to younger generations as living traditions [2][7].
  • Church capacity: The Catholic vicariate serving Pucallpa’s vast Amazon region has only 25 priests [28].

Perspectives

Martín Quijano, bishop of Pucallpa: Quijano argued that political and commercial interests keep the Amazon region marginalized, making it easier for outsiders to enter and take its resources [21].
