OpenAI adds invisible text watermarks to ChatGPT and Codex in EU

Category: Technology

OpenAI will begin adding invisible, machine-readable watermarks to eligible ChatGPT and Codex text generated in the European Union over the coming weeks in response to transparency requirements in the EU AI Act [15][2][7]. The company’s textGrain system subtly influences model word choices to create a statistical pattern that a detector can identify; the text remains visually unchanged when read or copied [15][4]. API customers worldwide can opt in for selected models now, but the feature will remain off by default outside the EU [2][15]. OpenAI says the detector is imperfect: in 400-token passages, replacing 10% of words with synonyms reduced the detection rate from about 92% to 66%, and replacing 25% reduced it to 17%; shorter passages and mathematics are also harder to identify [2][4]. A detected watermark does not identify a user, establish how much a person contributed or verify accuracy, while failure to detect one does not prove human authorship [15][6]. OpenAI will initially limit detector access to approved researchers and expert organizations as it evaluates reliability [15][2].

Key Points

  • How it compares: OpenAI says textGrain matched or exceeded other approaches it tested, including SynthID for text [7].
  • Model performance: OpenAI reported no meaningful difference in overall performance with watermarking enabled on its Astra model, although benchmark results varied slightly across tests [5][6].
  • Planned release: OpenAI says it plans to release textGrain as open source and provide further technical details in the coming weeks [6][20].

Perspectives

OpenAI: The company says its EU-only rollout gives it room to learn from real-world use and feedback before broadening the approach [5][15].

Sources: PCMag

Anthropic: Anthropic chose to apply its Claude text watermark worldwide, saying it lacked a durable way to restrict the system by region [5][2].

Sources: PCMag

ETRI AI Safety Research Institute: Director Kim Myung-joo said watermarking alone is not reliable enough to justify penalizing someone for AI authorship, but could help screen suspicious content [38].

Timeline

  • 2024: OpenAI had developed text watermarking but delayed release over user-retention concerns [2][25].
  • August 2026: Anthropic announced worldwide watermarking for Claude-generated text [2][25].
  • 2 August 2026: EU AI Act transparency requirements for generative AI took effect [7][39].
  • 5 October 2026: OpenAI announced textGrain and worldwide API opt-in availability [15][2].
  • 2 December 2026: Established AI providers’ deadline to comply with EU rules, according to RBC-Ukraine [39].

Technical Details

  • Token-level signal: textGrain subtly adjusts the probabilities governing word choices during generation; the watermark is a statistical pattern in the text rather than a visible label or separate marker [11][36].
  • Passage detection rates: With a target false-positive rate of 1%, OpenAI detected watermarks in about 80% of 200-token psychology passages and about 95% of 400-token passages [4][24].
  • Translation limits: OpenAI said translated text can be harder to identify reliably [2].

Industry Impact

  • AI providers: The EU requirement applies beyond OpenAI; Anthropic, Google, Meta and Microsoft are among companies identified as committed to the bloc’s code of practice on AI-generated content [7][25].

Historical Background

Some Claude users objected that they had supplied the instructions and decisions, while the AI was only a tool [2][25].
