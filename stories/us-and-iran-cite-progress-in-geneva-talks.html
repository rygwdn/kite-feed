U.S. and Iran cite progress in Geneva talks

Category: World

Iranian officials said indirect nuclear talks with the U.S. in Geneva made “good progress,” and that both sides agreed on a shared set of “guiding principles” that could anchor a future deal [2][1][3][5][8]. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said major gaps remain, and that turning those principles into a detailed text will take time—so a deal is not close [1][5][8][11]. The diplomacy is unfolding against a tense backdrop. President Trump has called for a quick agreement and warned of possible U.S. military action if talks collapse, while U.S. officials say Tehran still has not accepted all of the President’s “red lines” [2][1][15][14]. Even so, officials on both sides said the talks would continue, keeping open a channel in a long-running dispute that each says it wants to resolve through negotiations [2][3][8].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Diplomacy with pressure attached: Vice President JD Vance said Iran has not acknowledged all of President Trump’s “red lines.” The same day, Iranian rhetoric included a warning that Iran could sink a U.S. warship recently deployed to the region—an example of how negotiations are moving forward amid rising threats [1][15][14][17][16].
  • Sanctions relief remains central: Iran has long sought relief from sweeping U.S. sanctions, one of Tehran’s main incentives in the talks [14].
  • Oman as go-between: Oman mediated the Geneva round, with the U.S. and Iran speaking indirectly [6][4].
  • A business-minded pitch: Iran described an eventual deal as a “trillion-dollar opportunity” for American firms, pointing to access to its energy and mineral resources. Analysts called it a pitch designed for President Trump’s deal-making style [6].

Perspectives

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi: Araghchi said the sides reached a preliminary understanding on “guiding principles” and are moving toward drafting a potential agreement. He also cautioned that closing the remaining gaps will take time, and that a deal is not imminent.

Sources: The New York Times

U.S. Vice President JD Vance: Vance said Iran has not agreed to all of President Trump’s “red lines,” signaling that Washington still sees major requirements unmet after the Geneva round.

Sources: NDTV

Analysts cited in South China Morning Post: Analysts said Tehran’s “trillion-dollar” framing is meant to match President Trump’s deal-first instincts, spotlighting potential business gains alongside security terms.

Sources: South China Morning Post

Al-Monitor analysis: Al-Monitor said the path ahead looks fragile, with sharp warnings, hardened red lines, and increased military signaling complicating diplomacy even as talks continue.
