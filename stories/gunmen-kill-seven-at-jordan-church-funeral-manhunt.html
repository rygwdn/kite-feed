Gunmen kill seven at Jordan church funeral; manhunt continues

Category: World

Gunmen opened fire during a funeral at the Greek Orthodox Church in Hamoud, in Jordan’s Karak governorate, on Friday, Oct. 23, killing seven people and injuring 34, according to the Public Security Directorate [4][2][17]. The attackers fled, and authorities were searching for them. Interior Minister Mazen Al-Faraya described them as extremists but said it was too early to establish links to known groups inside or outside Jordan. No group had claimed responsibility [23][7]. The shooting struck a Christian community in a country where attacks on churches are rare. State television footage, verified by the BBC, appeared to show three gunmen approaching the church and firing; witnesses said between 150 and 200 people were at or near the funeral [2][3]. Jordan’s King Abdullah II visited Karak and vowed a severe response, while the government declared three days of national mourning [7][13]. Authorities said the investigation and manhunt were continuing [14][17].

Key Points

  • Church security: Armed guards were placed at Christian houses of worship across Jordan within half an hour of the shooting, the Christian Science Monitor reported [19].
  • Attack footage: State television footage appeared to show two attackers with pistols and a third carrying a semiautomatic rifle; officials have not publicly identified them [4][11].
  • Mourner accounts: Witnesses said the shooting lasted about five minutes, while one attendee told the Associated Press that gunfire began roughly 15 minutes into the funeral [23][13].
  • Victim ages: A witness told Jordanian state media that most victims were between 50 and 70 years old [1][3].

Perspectives

Greek Orthodox Archdiocese in Jordan: The archdiocese said that if the attack was intended to drive Christians from the region, it would instead deepen their attachment to their homeland. It described the act as alien to Jordan’s moderate Islam.

Historical Background

In 2016, gunmen killed 10 people in Karak, including seven police officers, and wounded dozens in a series of assaults that included an ambush near a tourist site. Security forces killed the attackers after a standoff [4][1]. Karak has a significant Christian community, while Christians make up 2 to 3 percent of Jordan’s population [3].

Humanitarian Impact

The Public Security Directorate said 27 of the wounded were still receiving hospital treatment, and the health minister reported two people in critical condition [4][13].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇳 United Nations: Secretary-General António Guterres condemned the attack on civilians at a place of worship during a funeral and called for those responsible to be held accountable [13].
  • 🇱🇧 Lebanon: President Joseph Aoun condemned the attack, saying terrorism seeks to undermine coexistence and spread division [3].
  • 🇦🇪 United Arab Emirates: The UAE Foreign Ministry condemned what it called terrorist and criminal acts and rejected violence intended to undermine security and stability [17].
