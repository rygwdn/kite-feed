1Password lets AI agents sign in without seeing user passwords

Category: Technology

Password manager 1Password launched 1Password for Agents on Oct. 22, allowing users to approve an AI agent’s sign-in request while the service enters credentials without revealing them to the agent [3][10][2]. The system is designed to let agents complete online tasks without users entering passwords into prompts, which can expose credentials [3][4]. The feature is available at no extra cost to Individual and Family subscribers whose accounts are hosted on 1Password.com; it does not support accounts on 1Password.eu and 1Password.ca [4][11]. Muse and Grok Bot integrations are rolling out, while connections for agents including Claude Duet and OpenAI’s ChatGPT Work and Dots are planned [9][3]. The launch follows reports that U.S. banks have detected AI-agent activity in customer accounts but may not be able to tell whether users authorized it [3].

Primary Source: View original article

The user is involved in every decision.

— John Torrey, 1Password business chief (PCWorld)

Key Points

  • Narrow access: Each integration is opt-in, and connecting an agent does not grant access to the entire password vault [4][7].
  • Task risks: Even without seeing credentials, an agent can still make mistakes after logging in, such as placing an incorrect order or booking a trip that cannot be canceled [10].
  • More partners: 1Password lists future integrations with services including Perplexity, Browserbase, Ramp and KERNEL [5][8].

Perspectives

Chase: A spokesperson advised customers not to share their usernames and passwords [3].

Sources: TechCrunch

Historical Background

In July 2026, 1Password introduced a separate integration that let Claude Cowork sign in to websites without exposing credentials to the AI agent [1][6]. The new platform expands that approach to additional agent services [1][12].
