SpaceX's orbital catch leaves experts seeking proof of repeatable reuse

Category: Science

Aerospace specialists say SpaceX must show that its Starship spacecraft can survive repeated atmospheric returns and be prepared for another flight to demonstrate reliable reuse. In the Texas test, the company caught Starship’s upper stage after an orbital flight for the first time. SpaceX said the spacecraft’s systems, including its heat shield, appeared to work well, but experts cautioned that a single recovery does not show the vehicle is ready for sensitive payloads or missions [4][3]. Catching Starship was more demanding than catching the lower booster because the spacecraft returns from a higher altitude and at greater speed. Aerospace engineer Alistair John of the University of Sheffield said the heat shield and engines need to be checked for damage, while aerospace analyst Kathleen Curlee of Georgetown University said repeated successes are needed to demonstrate mission readiness [8][4]. NASA plans to use a Starship variant as a lunar lander. The agency is preparing an Artemis mission in which four astronauts are expected to practice docking with lunar landers in orbit; it is targeting Artemis IV for a lunar landing as soon as 2028 [3][21].

What SpaceX needs is multiple repeated successes to prove the capability is ready to handle sensitive payloads and missions.

— Analyst Kathleen Curlee, Georgetown University’s Center for Security and Emerging Technology (The Hindu)

Key Points

  • Flight profile: During the 22-hour mission, Starship completed 14 Earth orbits at an altitude of about 165 miles and deployed 26 Starlink satellites [3][4].
  • Booster setback: SpaceX said the booster’s engines stopped firing too soon during descent; engineers are investigating the cause [3].
  • Cadence target: Elon Musk has said SpaceX is aiming for 10,000 Starship launches annually by 2030 [7].
  • Launch expansion: SpaceX expects to begin Starship launches from Kennedy Space Center in Florida within the next few months [3].

Perspectives

Malcolm Macdonald, president of the Royal Aeronautical Society, said SpaceX has demonstrated each of the two recovery challenges separately but still needs to combine them and show the system works reliably, repeatedly and economically [21].

Timeline

  • April 2023: SpaceX began testing the full-scale Starship rocket system [16]
  • September 2026: A previous test reached orbit before ending in a controlled Pacific splashdown [16]
  • 13 November 2026: Flight 15 launched from Starbase for a planned two-stage recovery [14]
  • 14 November 2026: Starship returned from orbit and was caught at its launch tower [14]

Scientific Significance

  • Reuse evidence: SpaceX has reflown Super Heavy boosters on two occasions, with several months between flights, but has yet to reuse a Starship upper stage [7].
  • Remaining technical step: A lunar or Mars mission requires Starships to dock in orbit and transfer liquid methane and liquid oxygen, a capability the reporting still describes as unresolved [8][4].

Q&A

Q: What must Starship add before it can carry Artemis astronauts?

A: SpaceX needs to install a crew cabin and life-support system; the company is working on a full-scale cabin prototype [7].
