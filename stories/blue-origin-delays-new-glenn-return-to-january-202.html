Blue Origin delays New Glenn return to January 2027 after pad blast

Category: Science

Blue Origin, Jeff Bezos’ space company, has postponed its next New Glenn rocket launch from the end of this year to January 2027. CEO Dave Limp told employees that delays in rebuilding the launchpad at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, partly caused by summer weather, forced the change [1][3][4]. A May 28 explosion during a hot-fire test severely damaged the Florida launchpad shortly before a flight intended to deploy 48 satellites for Amazon’s Leo satellite-internet service. No injuries were reported. The delay has slowed Leo’s buildout, and Amazon has used other launch providers while Blue Origin repairs the site [1][3][5].

Returning New Glenn to flight remains our top priority — it's worth it.

Key Points

  • Pad repairs: Blue Origin has repaired and restacked the launchpad’s Vehicle Access Tower and restored the launch bridge [6].
  • Launch competition: SpaceX has phased out Falcon 9 launches to prioritize testing its Starship rocket [3][5].
  • Expansion plans: Blue Origin announced new investment in its Florida launch facilities in October, aiming for capacity to launch multiple flights per week by the end of the decade [3][5].

Perspectives

Dave Limp, Blue Origin chief executive: Limp told employees that rebuilding at that pace had not been easy and that the team had completed work in six months that many thought would take years [3][5].

Timeline

Technical Details

  • Engine valve anomaly: Limp said the anomaly that triggered the explosion originated at the main oxygen valve on one of New Glenn’s BE-4 engines [1].
  • First-stage propulsion: Seven BE-4 engines power New Glenn’s first stage, each producing 640,000 pounds of thrust. The reusable stage is designed for at least 25 flights [1].
