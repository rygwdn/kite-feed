US and partner forces launch ISIS strikes across Syria

Category: World

The US military says it carried out “large-scale” strikes across Syria targeting Islamic State positions on Saturday, describing them as retaliation for a December ambush near Palmyra that killed two US soldiers and a US civilian interpreter [15][6][4][13][21]. US Central Command said the operation aims to disrupt ISIS and help protect US and partner forces in the region [10][18][16][17]. Officials did not provide full details on specific locations or results, but several outlets reported the strikes hit multiple ISIS targets around the country [1][2][9][8].

Did you know: Palmyra’s ancient ruins are listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site [4].

Key Points

  • Operation name: US Central Command said the latest wave is part of “Operation Hawkeye Strike,” which it launched after the Palmyra ambush [13][21][12].
  • Defense secretary message: US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth responded publicly to the strikes by posting, “We will never forget, and never relent” [13].
  • Prior strike round: CENTCOM previously said a US-Jordan round of strikes last month hit “more than 70 targets,” and a war monitor later reported at least five ISIS members killed, including a cell leader [13].
  • European allies action: Britain and France announced joint strikes on January 3 targeting an underground facility they said ISIS had likely used to store weapons [13].
  • Syria north ceasefire: CBS reported Syria announced a ceasefire with Kurdish fighters after three days of clashes in the north, separate from the Palmyra incident [7].

Perspectives

US Central Command: CENTCOM says the strikes targeted ISIS across Syria to prevent future attacks and protect US and partner forces.

Syrian interior ministry: Syria’s interior ministry later said the Palmyra gunman was a member of the security forces who was set to be fired for extremism.

US Central Command / Pentagon framing: US officials describe the December Palmyra attack as carried out by an ISIS militant, and frame the response as part of the international campaign against ISIS.

RT reporting on Syrian politics: RT says Washington keeps about 1,000 troops in Syria and reports recent discussion about possible US personnel at an airbase in Damascus amid engagement with Syria’s new president.

Sources: RT

Timeline

  • December 13: Ambush near Palmyra kills two US soldiers and interpreter [15][4].
  • December 19: CENTCOM launches “Operation Hawkeye Strike” after the Palmyra attack [13][12].
  • January 3: Britain and France announce strikes on suspected ISIS weapons site [13].
  • Saturday: US says new “large-scale” strikes hit ISIS targets across Syria [2][21].

Technical Details

  • Operation Inherent Resolve: The US-led international campaign to combat ISIS; US personnel in Syria were described as supporting it when the Palmyra ambush occurred [13].
  • “Large-scale strikes”: CENTCOM used this term for a wave of attacks hitting multiple ISIS targets across Syria, without publicly listing each location in initial statements [21][9].
  • GBU-31 munition: A US Air Force image showed a GBU-31 being loaded onto an F-15E in support of Operation Hawkeye Strike [21].

Historical Background

The Islamic State seized large areas of Syria and Iraq in 2014 before losing most of that territory in subsequent years [common]. The US-led coalition mission against ISIS is known as Operation Inherent Resolve, and US personnel in Syria were reported as supporting that effort when the December ambush occurred [13]. Palmyra, where the December attack happened, is home to UNESCO-listed ruins and has previously been held by ISIS fighters during the war [4].

Q&A

Q: Which partner forces participated in the latest strikes beyond the United States?

A: CENTCOM said “partner forces” took part but did not name them in its public statement as reported by multiple outlets [9][12].

Q: Did officials report damage assessments or casualties from Saturday’s strikes?

A: Several reports noted officials did not provide detailed results or specific locations immediately after the strikes [8][1].

Q: How many US troops are currently reported to be in Syria?

A: RT reported Washington maintains about 1,000 troops in Syria [12].
