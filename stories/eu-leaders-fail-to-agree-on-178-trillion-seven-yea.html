EU leaders fail to agree on €1.78 trillion seven-year budget

Category: World

European Union leaders ended a Brussels summit early on Nov. 26 without agreement on the bloc’s 2028-2034 budget, a proposed €1.78 trillion package already cut by about €204 billion from the European Commission’s initial plan. European Council President António Costa said governments remained divided over the budget’s total size, spending allocations and new sources of EU revenue. The second day of talks was cancelled [7][5][12]. Germany and other net contributors want deeper cuts and more spending on defense and competitiveness, while France, Italy and other governments focused on cohesion funding oppose reductions to farm support and regional development. The dispute also became linked to a proposed €45 billion loan for Ukraine in 2027: 24 member states backed it, Bulgaria said it would fund only the non-military portion, and Hungary, the Czech Republic and Slovakia remained opposed [64][17][16]. Leaders are due to resume negotiations at a summit on Dec. 17-18, aiming to reach agreement by year-end. The budget requires unanimous approval, and French President Emmanuel Macron has warned that without a deal by Dec. 31, formal talks would have to wait until after France’s 2027 presidential election [64][63][29].

Key Points

  • Budget priorities: Agriculture and cohesion policy each account for about one-third of the EU budget, while the proposed package would devote roughly €740 billion to farmers and poorer regions [4][64].
  • Ukraine allocation: The proposed €45 billion loan divides into €20 billion for civilian needs and €25 billion for arms and ammunition; the 2028-2034 draft also includes up to €100 billion for Ukraine [2][26].
  • New revenue: A proposed digital-services levy could raise €35 billion, but Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin said it conflicted with efforts to improve European competitiveness [9][61].
  • ECB appointment: Leaders separately backed Spain’s Pablo Hernández de Cos, former Bank of Spain governor, to succeed Christine Lagarde as European Central Bank president in February [19][20].
  • Pandemic debt: The current budget proposal includes €140 billion to repay borrowing undertaken during the pandemic [64].

Industry Impact

  • Defense and security: The draft would more than triple defense funding in real terms and set aside €15.6 billion for routes to move troops and military equipment across the bloc [3].
  • Ukraine financing: The EU would borrow against its current budget, which expires at the end of 2027, to fund the proposed 2027 loan. The loan would be repaid only when Russia pays war reparations [63][64].

Historical Background

The EU’s budget framework is traditionally set for seven-year periods and has required unanimous approval by the European Council since the 2009 Lisbon Treaty [46]. The current 2021-2027 framework was agreed on Dec. 10-11, 2020, with the full set of financial acts published on Dec. 22, only nine days before it took effect [46].
