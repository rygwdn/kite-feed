King Charles expresses sorrow over slave trade but offers no apology

Category: World

King Charles III described the transatlantic slave trade as a “shameful chapter” that filled him with “horror” and “profound personal sorrow” in a speech opening the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda on Tuesday, but he did not apologize or discuss reparations [7][1][10]. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who is attending the summit, said he was open to a formal apology on behalf of the state but ruled out financial reparations. He proposed dialogue with affected communities and a debt-reset plan for poorer Commonwealth countries instead [2][1]. The 56-member Commonwealth includes Caribbean and other former British colonies [2][9]. Burnham discussed the issue with Barbados Prime Minister Mia Mottley on Monday. Downing Street said Britain was offering structured dialogue, and the prime minister also announced £40 million ($54 million) over three years for Caribbean infrastructure [2][10]. Caribbean leaders say any process must address the continuing effects of slavery and go beyond an apology. Antigua and Barbuda Prime Minister Gaston Browne called for inclusive dialogue on reparatory justice, while King Charles identified education, jobs, trade and climate resilience as areas for future cooperation [10][3].

Key Points

  • Caribbean demands: Caribbean Community chair Philip J Pierre called for dialogue on the bloc’s 10-point plan, which includes public health, education, cultural restitution, debt cancellation and monetary compensation [10].
  • UK opposition: Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch argued that Britain should not apologize, saying an apology could strengthen claims for financial compensation [2][5].
  • Protester response: Antiguan protester Shana Michelle said the effects of colonialism remain visible in how people describe their skin, hair and identity, and called an apology a starting point for repair [4][3].
  • Ghana’s position: Ghanaian President John Mahama backed a broader reparatory-justice agenda that includes restitution of cultural heritage, investments in education and science, action against systemic racism and partnerships addressing poverty and debt [9].
  • UK contribution: The Commonwealth Nature Initiative had secured more than $200 million in pledges to protect at least 30% of the world’s land and oceans by 2030, Antigua and Barbuda officials said [3][14].

Perspectives

Hilary Beckles, chair of the CARICOM Reparations Commission, called the visit a “very critical moment” for the reparations movement and said the UK leaders’ presence in the Caribbean was significant for the campaign [21].

Historical Background

Britain outfitted almost one-third of transatlantic slave voyages and facilitated the forced transportation of more than 3.2 million Africans, mainly to colonies in the Caribbean and North America [1]. Parliament outlawed British participation in the slave trade in 1807 and passed legislation abolishing slavery in most of the British Empire in 1833 [1][9].

International Reactions

  • 🇨🇦 Canada: Canada said it recognizes slavery and the transatlantic slave trade as grave historical injustices with continuing effects, while its government spokesperson did not state Canada’s position on reparations [9].
  • 🇳🇱 The Netherlands: The Dutch government was identified as the only country to have apologized for its role in the slave trade; no country had agreed to pay reparations [9].

Q&A

Q: When might Britain issue a formal apology?

A: There is no confirmed timetable; the BBC reported that Britain was likely to apologize during its G20 presidency in 2027 [2][11].

Q: What does the proposed debt reset involve?

A: The plan, led by former British prime minister Gordon Brown, could change interest rates on repayments for the poorest Commonwealth countries [2][11].
