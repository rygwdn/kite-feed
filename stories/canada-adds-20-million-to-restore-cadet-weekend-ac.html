Canada adds $20 million to restore cadet weekend activities

Category: Canada

Canada’s Department of National Defence will add $20 million to the cadet program budget to restore weekend training and other supplemental activities through March 31, the end of the fiscal year, Defence Minister David McGuinty said Monday [3][2]. The additional funding follows last week’s suspension of activities including hiking, camping, sailing and glider flights; weekly parade nights were to continue [3][4]. The department had allocated about $265 million to cadet programs this year, but summer costs rose faster than expected as enrolment grew 59% over three years. McGuinty said year-round intake made participation and spending difficult to forecast [2][3]. The government says it will review funding needs, while McGuinty said the department would do its best to provide adequate resources from April onward [1][4].

Key Points

  • Public pressure: A Change.org petition calling for activities to resume drew more than 52,000 signatures, while Conservative and Bloc Québécois MPs raised the issue in the House of Commons [2][8].
  • Northern access: Cadet league leaders said the pause cancelled activities planned for Whitehorse, Yellowknife and Iqaluit, limiting opportunities for northern youth to travel and meet peers [6].
  • Local costs: The Army Cadet Corps in Yellowknife faced a $9,000 annual rent bill after Local Support Allocation funding was cut, even as weekly meetings continued [6].
  • Department-wide restraint: The Department of National Defence was expected to find $459 million in savings while prioritizing equipment purchases [3].

Historical Background

A study funded by the Department of National Defence and completed in March found that each dollar spent on the cadet program generated an estimated $19.85 to $41.15 in lifetime social returns. It estimated lifetime net benefits to Canadian society of $295,600 to $630,500 per cadet and linked participation with educational, employment and civic outcomes [5].
