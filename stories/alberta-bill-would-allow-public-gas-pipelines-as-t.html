Alberta bill would allow public gas pipelines as TC Energy warns of risks

Category: Canada

Alberta introduced a bill Monday that would let the province plan natural-gas infrastructure and establish Crown entities to build infrastructure or transmit gas if private companies fail to meet demand. The Natural Gas Infrastructure Act does not create an entity, approve a pipeline or commit public money; those steps would require further government approvals. Energy Minister Brian Jean says capacity limits are holding back growth in data centres, oilsands and other industries, and describes public involvement as a last resort [4][5][9]. TC Energy, which operates the main transmission network, warned Premier Danielle Smith that changing the system could disrupt its expansion plans, unsettle investment and raise costs for customers and taxpayers. The company has proposed a 370-kilometre pipeline to the oilsands and additional capacity, while Alberta says the network is fully subscribed through 2029 and future additions remain uncertain [3][2][4]. The bill would also give cabinet a role in approving designated infrastructure plans; TC Energy is reviewing its implications [2][5].

If the private sector doesn't see the path to making money in a certain area, is the business proposition that much better for a publicly owned entity, for something that requires taxpayer dollars?

— Janetta McKenzie, oil and gas director at the Pembina Institute (CBC News)

Key Points

  • Utilities Commission authority: The Alberta Utilities Commission could require an owner to extend or expand transmission facilities where necessary to serve demand [2].
  • Capacity offer: TC Energy said its planned offering could add more than 1.3 billion cubic feet per day of delivery capacity from 2030 to 2032, subject to customer commitments [3].
  • Demand forecasts: Alberta modelling cited by The Hub projected a potential capacity shortfall of 1.3 billion to 5.1 billion cubic feet per day by 2034, depending on the scenario [3].
  • Public exposure: A leaked cabinet report estimated up to $2 million for preliminary work and as much as $60 million annually from 2027 to run two proposed Crown entities, excluding pipeline construction costs [6].

Perspectives

TC Energy: Chief Executive François Poirier argues that the integrated NGTL-ATCO network is the lowest-cost, most efficient route to meet demand, with coordinated investment aligned to demonstrated customer demand [3].

Sources: The Hub

Pembina Institute: Oil and gas director Janetta McKenzie notes that AI data centres can use energy sources other than natural gas, questioning assumptions that they will drive pipeline demand [4].

Alberta NDP: Utilities critic Kathleen Ganley argues that bringing pipeline infrastructure under government control could increase utility costs and says affordability should take priority [4].

  • September 2026: Leaked cabinet report detailed proposed Crown entities and stronger provincial powers [3][8]
  • 16 November 2026: TC Energy announced a proposed oilsands pipeline and additional capacity plans [3]
  • 21 November 2026: TC Energy CEO François Poirier urged Premier Danielle Smith to abandon the changes [2][3]
  • 23 November 2026: Alberta tabled the Natural Gas Infrastructure Act in the legislature [4][5]

Industry Impact

  • Pipeline investment: TC Energy has about $1.2 billion in sanctioned NGTL expansion projects underway, in addition to its newly announced plans [3].
  • Industrial gas users: The NGTL network extends more than 24,000 kilometres across Alberta and northeastern British Columbia, linking producing regions to industrial users, storage and export pipelines; changes to its integrated planning could affect the regional supply chain [3].
  • Consumers and taxpayers: The leaked cabinet report warned that higher household costs could disproportionately affect seniors and rural residents, but offered no concrete measures to limit those effects [6].

Historical Background

A provincial proposal in early 2025 considered government backing for a smaller Grande Prairie project; a broader company plan followed in June [3]. In September 2026, a leaked cabinet report outlined stronger provincial powers and possible Crown corporations. Premier Danielle Smith had by then publicly rejected the most interventionist option while criticizing TC Energy's dominant position [3][8].

Business Angle

  • TC Energy has invested more than $15 billion in NGTL over the past decade, and its rate base grew from approximately $6 billion to nearly $19 billion [3].
  • The leaked cabinet report projected that fully embracing publicly built pipelines could cost taxpayers billions over several years and leave Alberta exposed to stranded assets if gas prices or demand fall [6].
  • The leaked report proposed setting aside $8 million for lawsuits, including possible constitutional challenges from TC Energy and ATCO [6].
