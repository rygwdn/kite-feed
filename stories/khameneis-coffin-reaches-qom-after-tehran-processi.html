Khamenei's coffin reaches Qom after Tehran procession

Category: World

Ali Khamenei’s coffin arrived in Qom on Tuesday, where tens of thousands of mourners gathered at Jamkaran Mosque for funeral prayers before planned ceremonies in Najaf and Karbala in Iraq and a burial in Mashhad later this week [14][55][53]. The Qom ceremony followed Monday’s procession through Tehran, where the coffins of Khamenei and four family members moved through the capital after two days at the Grand Mosalla religious complex [1][13][34]. Iranian state media said millions attended the Tehran procession. The Associated Press reported that authorities had not immediately issued an official crowd count, and state television images showed crowds stretching for kilometers along Azadi Street [1][33][20][27]. Khamenei, 86, was killed on Feb. 28 in U.S.-Israeli airstrikes at the start of the war, along with members of his family whose coffins were also carried in the procession [19][27][34]. Mourners chanted anti-U.S. and anti-Israel slogans; some banners and placards called for the death of President Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu [19][3][15][27]. The ceremonies drew senior Iranian officials and regional allies, with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian seen walking among the crowd and greeting mourners in Tehran [19][35]. Monday’s procession was the largest public event so far in the multiday funeral, which began over the weekend and is scheduled to continue across Iran and Iraq before Khamenei is laid to rest in his birthplace, Mashhad [4][15][37].

Did you know: Authorities sprayed water from fire hoses above Tehran marchers to keep them cool during extreme heat [4][13][35].

Key Points

  • Successor unseen: Mojtaba Khamenei, Ali Khamenei’s son and successor, has not appeared publicly at the funeral ceremonies. A senior Iranian official told NBC News his injuries from the strike were “not very serious” [2][15][26].
  • Security controls: Iranian authorities closed Tehran’s airspace on Monday, imposed localized flight restrictions from July 7 until the burial and used concrete barriers near the coffin to manage crowd pressure [43][45][54].
  • Domestic discontent: BBC reporting from Tehran described residents who said they stayed away, citing unemployment, inflation around 80% and anger over January anti-government protests that ended in a deadly crackdown [21].
  • Diplomatic backdrop: Iran and the United States pursued negotiations over a permanent end to the war, and reports said talks were on hold until after the burial [20][46].

Historical Background

Ali Khamenei became Iran’s supreme leader after Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini’s death in 1989 [common]. Khomeini’s 1989 funeral drew an estimated 10 million people, and crowd surges killed more than 10 people and injured more than 10,000 [45][43]. The 2020 procession for Revolutionary Guard Gen. Qassem Soleimani drew more than 1 million people, and AP reported that Monday’s crowd appeared larger [20][27].
