Most of Canada springs forward as B.C. adopts year-round daylight time

Category: Canada

Most of Canada switches to daylight time early Sunday, nudging clocks ahead as spring approaches [2][3]. The change is meant to line up more waking hours with daylight, and it usually means later sunsets [2][3]. British Columbia stands out this year: coverage says the province is adopting year-round daylight time instead of switching back in the fall [2][3]. In Quebec, the shift is once again stirring debate over whether the province should keep changing clocks twice a year [4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Changeover moment: Daylight time begins at 2 a.m. Sunday, March 8, when clocks jump ahead one hour [2][3].
  • How long it lasts (where observed): Provinces that still switch stay on daylight time until Nov. 1, when clocks fall back to standard time [2][3].
  • B.C. exception: Coverage describes B.C. as moving to year-round daylight time, rather than returning to standard time in the fall [2][3].
  • Not uniform across Canada: La Presse says Quebec is sticking with the time-change routine, even as some provinces have dropped the switch [4].
  • Common impact: La Presse notes that “summer time” often costs people an hour of sleep while adding an extra hour of evening light [4].

Perspectives

Sleep experts (Quebec): La Presse reports two sleep specialists argue standard time has benefits, even though many Quebecers prefer staying on summer time.

Sources: La Presse

Quebec residents: La Presse describes growing resident frustration with the long-running time-change tradition in Quebec.

Sources: La Presse

Canadian news coverage: Coverage notes most of Canada still follows the seasonal clock change, with daylight time lasting until early November in participating provinces.
