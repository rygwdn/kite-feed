South Africa deploys police as anti-migrant marches spread

Category: World

Thousands of people marched in Johannesburg, Pretoria, Durban, Cape Town and other South African cities on Tuesday to demand that undocumented foreign nationals leave the country, after anti-migrant groups set June 30 as an unofficial deadline [2][4][13][15]. Police, private security and military assets in some areas were deployed to prevent violence and looting; shops closed in parts of Johannesburg and Durban and many foreign workers stayed home [4][6][17][31]. President Cyril Ramaphosa met some protest leaders on Monday night and said the right to protest does not permit intimidation, threats, vandalism or violence; the government said the deadline has no legal force and only authorities can enforce immigration law [13][15][12]. The Ministry of Police said demonstrations were largely peaceful nationwide, but police reported arrests for looting and attempted looting, protesters broke windows in Johannesburg's Yeoville area, and local media reported that demonstrators forced suspected foreign nationals from homes in Germiston [4][24][7]. South African police said 25,000 people had been repatriated so far, most from other African countries; AFP reported that at least two Mozambicans, an Ethiopian and a Malawian were killed in anti-immigrant violence in recent weeks [4][24][32][37].

Did you know: South Africa's 2022 census counted 2.4 million foreign-born residents, less than 4% of the country's 62 million people [55][65].

Key Points

  • Organising groups: The prominent groups behind the protests include March and March, Operation Dudula and Progressive Forces, with mobilisation also described as a loose coalition of minor parties and citizen-led vigilante groups [13][6][37].
  • Public sentiment: Reuters cited 2025 survey findings showing only one in six South African adults would welcome all foreigners, while 42% would welcome none, up from about one-third in 2021 [34].
  • False notices: Africa Check said fake notices using official-style imagery falsely claimed people could earn R250 for bringing an 'illegal foreign national' to authorities [11].
  • Repatriation plans: AFP reported Uganda announced an evacuation plan for nearly 750 citizens, while South African officials said about 15,000 Malawians had been processed for departure [32][36].

Industry Impact

  • Retail and informal trade: Foreign-owned shops and informal businesses in parts of Soweto, central Johannesburg and Durban closed, and Protea Gardens Mall opened only one gate as traders stayed away [9][4][6].
  • Employers and landlords: South African authorities warned that employing undocumented migrants could result in fines or other penalties, and vigilante groups also threatened landlords who rented to foreign nationals [32].
  • Security operations: South African police activated Operation 30 June with national and provincial command centres, Public Order Policing units, tactical teams and the Air Wing deployed across all nine provinces [41].

Historical Background

Officials and commentators repeatedly cited South Africa's 2008 anti-migrant riots, which left 62 people dead, as authorities prepared for the June 30 demonstrations [17][48]. In July 2021, unrest after former President Jacob Zuma's arrest killed more than 350 people, and security officials referenced the risk of large-scale disorder when preparing for the current protests [17][35].

Humanitarian Impact

Thousands of migrants from Malawi, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Ghana, Nigeria and other African countries have left South Africa or waited at shelters, consulates and temporary camps for transport home, with some reporting evictions, job losses and threats [32][58][56]. Many urgently need safe transport, documentation processing and protection from harassment during departure [32][58].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇳 United Nations: Secretary-General António Guterres' spokesperson said he was deeply concerned by reports of xenophobic attacks, harassment and intimidation targeting migrants and foreign nationals [55].
  • 🇲🇼 Malawi: Malawi organised or supported bus repatriations, with more than 8,000 Malawian citizens reported to have left by bus and thousands more gathering in Durban seeking to leave [55][32].
  • 🇳🇬🇬🇭 Nigeria and Ghana: Nigeria and Ghana used government-funded flights to repatriate nearly 2,000 people over safety concerns and said more evacuations would follow [55].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Police deployed across major cities, and motorists in Pretoria were advised to use alternative routes including Paul Kruger, Lilian Ngoyi and Francis Baard streets [47]. Avoid protest routes and crowded march areas.
  • Safety: Reports indicated demonstrators were handing suspected foreign nationals to police for document checks [4][13]. Keep immigration documents accessible.
  • Consular: Foreign nationals gathered at consulates and temporary sites in Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban seeking transport or repatriation assistance [32][56]. Contact your embassy or consulate if threatened.

Action Items

  • Report threats or forced removals: Contact South African police or your country's consulate if protesters, landlords or employers threaten you [13][32].
  • Check local road closures before travel: Use municipal traffic updates in cities with marches, including Pretoria routes identified by Tshwane Metro Police [47].
