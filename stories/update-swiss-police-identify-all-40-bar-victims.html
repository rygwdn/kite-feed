Update: Swiss police identify all 40 bar victims

Category: World

In the latest development, Swiss police say they have identified all 40 people who died in the New Year’s Eve fire at the Le Constellation bar in the Alpine resort of Crans-Montana, bringing an end to days of uncertainty for families [2][1][6][7][8][16]. Authorities say a criminal investigation is continuing to determine what caused the blaze, which also left many people seriously injured [14][8][11]. The resort has held public acts of remembrance—including a special Mass and a silent procession—reflecting a community-focused response as residents and visitors mourn together [3][4][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Crans-Montana is a Swiss Alpine resort that attracts international visitors for winter holidays and skiing [common].

Key Points

  • Victims’ ages: Police said more than half of those killed were under 18, and reports noted victims included teenagers as young as 14 [7][10][11][9].
  • International victims: Officials and reports said the dead included nine French citizens, underscoring how the holiday crowd drew people from abroad [6].
  • Forensic challenges: Investigators described identification work as slow because many victims suffered severe burns, requiring extensive forensic efforts before names could be confirmed [12].
  • Named individuals: Among those publicly identified by relatives and community reports were 16-year-old Swiss national Arthur Brodard and Israeli-UK-French teen Charlotte Niddam [2][5].
  • Memorial atmosphere: Witness accounts from commemorations described grief expressed through hugs, tears, and a quiet march through the resort, as locals created space for collective mourning [3][4].

Perspectives

Valais canton police: Police said they have now identified all victims while continuing a criminal inquiry into the fire’s cause, and they have not released further identity details publicly.

Families of victims: Relatives have confirmed and shared personal identifications, including the mother of 16-year-old Arthur Brodard, as families seek answers and mourn publicly.

Timeline

  • Friday: Switzerland plans a national day of mourning for victims [6][7].

Technical Details

  • Criminal investigation: Authorities described the probe as a criminal inquiry, a formal process that can include evidence collection, witness interviews, and forensic analysis to establish cause and potential liability [14][16].
  • Forensic identification: Because many victims sustained extreme burns, identification relied on time-consuming forensic work rather than rapid visual confirmation [12].
