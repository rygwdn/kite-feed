Norwegian crown princess’s son faces rape trial in Oslo

Category: World

In Oslo, the trial of Marius Borg Høiby, the 29‑year‑old son of Norway’s Crown Princess Mette‑Marit, has opened with prosecutors outlining 38 charges that include rape, abuse in close relationships, assault, drug possession and traffic offences allegedly committed between 2018 and late 2024 [1][5][8]. Høiby, who holds no royal title or official role, pleaded not guilty to four counts of rape and to a charge of domestic violence, but admitted several lesser offences such as certain assaults, narcotics offences, traffic violations and breaches of restraining orders [7][15][13]. Prosecutors say multiple women accuse him of attacks that followed consensual sex, often after heavy drinking when they say they were not able to defend themselves, while his defence argues he perceived all encounters as normal, consensual relations [2][guardian.com#1]. The high‑profile case, which could carry a prison sentence of up to 16 years if he is convicted of the most serious offences, has intensified scrutiny of the Norwegian royal family and comes as unsealed US documents highlighted Crown Princess Mette‑Marit’s past friendship with Jeffrey Epstein, adding further pressure on the monarchy’s public image [2][4][8][6].

Did you know: Despite his close family ties to the Norwegian throne, Marius Borg Høiby has never held a royal title or official public role and is not in the line of succession [8][4].

Key Points

  • New weekend arrest: Just days before the trial, police arrested Høiby again on suspicion of making threats with a knife, assault and violating a restraining order, and a court ordered him held in custody for four weeks [2][13].
  • Alleged incident setting: One of the alleged rapes is reported to have taken place during a 2023 holiday in Norway’s Lofoten Islands, where Høiby was travelling with Crown Prince Haakon, according to the indictment described in court [2][4].
  • Victim protections: At least one alleged victim testified on the opening day under strict media reporting limits intended to protect her privacy during the sensitive proceedings [2][14].
  • Courtroom atmosphere: Reporters describe a packed Oslo district courtroom where Høiby appeared in casual clothing, largely expressionless with his head bowed, occasionally fidgeting as prosecutors read the list of charges [2][guardian.com#1][15].
  • Next steps: Høiby is scheduled to testify early in the trial, which is expected to run for several weeks as the court hears from complainants, witnesses, and expert psychologists [2][8].

Perspectives

Prosecutors: Prosecutors argue that after evenings of heavy drinking, the women involved were no longer in a condition to resist or defend themselves when Høiby allegedly forced sex on them, and they describe a pattern of controlling and violent behaviour in several former relationships.

Sources: The Guardian

Defence team: Høiby’s defence lawyer maintains that his client believes the sexual encounters were consensual, contests the rape and abuse allegations, and notes that Høiby has chosen to plead guilty to certain lesser offences to show he is taking responsibility where he believes he did wrong.

Sources: dawn.com

Norwegian royal household: The royal household has stressed that Høiby has no title, carries out no official duties and is not in the line of succession, while acknowledging that the case is painful for the family and emphasizing trust in the independence of Norway’s judicial system.

Sources: nytimes.com

Victim advocates in Norway: Women’s rights and domestic violence groups say the case has sparked a necessary conversation in Norway about consent, intimate‑partner abuse and power imbalances, especially when accusations involve people connected to prominent families.

Sources: france24.com

Timeline

  • 2018–late 2024: Prosecutors say the alleged rapes, assaults and related offences occurred during this period [5][13].
  • Sunday evening: Police rearrested Høiby over alleged knife threats, assault and restraining‑order breach [2][13].
  • Tuesday: Trial opened at Oslo District Court, with Høiby entering his pleas [2][14][15].
  • Wednesday (scheduled): Høiby is due to take the stand to testify before the court [2][8].

Technical Details

  • Abuse in close relationships: Several charges relate to Norway’s legal category of abuse within close relationships, which covers violence, threats or coercive behaviour against partners or former partners and can lead to significant prison terms when a pattern of misconduct is proven [guardian.com#1][14].
  • Restraining order violations: Prosecutors say Høiby repeatedly violated court‑ordered bans on contacting or approaching former partners, and such breaches can lead to separate criminal penalties in Norway’s justice system in addition to any underlying offences [2][5].
  • Maximum sentencing exposure: If found guilty of the most serious counts, especially rape, Høiby could face a combined sentence of up to around 16 years in prison under Norwegian law, though judges have discretion to determine a final term within statutory limits [2][7].

Industry Impact

  • Monarchy’s public standing: The case has renewed debate over how European royal families manage the private conduct of extended members and how much such incidents should influence public support for taxpayer‑funded institutions, with commentators in Norway noting that increased transparency may ultimately strengthen trust if the legal process is seen as fair and independent [8][13].
  • Media and privacy norms: Strict reporting limits around victim testimony highlight how Norwegian courts balance open justice with privacy and may serve as a reference point for other European jurisdictions facing similar high‑profile abuse cases involving well‑known families [14][guardian.com#1].

Historical Background

Marius Borg Høiby is Crown Princess Mette‑Marit’s son from a relationship before she married Crown Prince Haakon in 2001, and he has grown up largely outside formal royal duties while still being a familiar figure to Norwegians [2][8]. In recent weeks, the monarchy has faced additional scrutiny after newly unsealed US court documents mentioned the crown princess numerous times in connection with her past friendship with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, prompting renewed debate about the royal family’s associations and accountability [4][8].

Q&A

Q: How has Norwegian public opinion toward the monarchy shifted since the charges became public?

A: Norwegian and international media report that the case has "shaken and shocked" many in the country and intensified debate about the royal family, but detailed polling data on changes in support for the monarchy have not yet been reported in these articles [13][8].

Q: What role, if any, do psychological assessments play in this trial?

A: According to coverage of the proceedings, the court plans to hear expert evidence, including from psychologists, to help assess both the alleged victims’ experiences and Høiby’s behaviour patterns, which is common in Norwegian cases involving claims of domestic abuse and sexual violence [8][13].
