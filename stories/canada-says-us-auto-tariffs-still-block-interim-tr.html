Canada says U.S. auto tariffs still block interim trade deal

Category: Canada

Canada’s chief trade negotiator, Janice Charette, told a federal advisory committee Thursday that several issues still block an interim trade deal with the United States, people briefed on the meeting told Global News. The biggest obstacle is the 25% U.S. tariff on Canadian automobiles. The effort follows the collapse of formal talks in August. Ottawa is seeking tariff relief before negotiations over the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement (CUSMA) and believes a deal could be reached by year-end, before Democrats take control of Congress in January. Some committee members question whether the political shift changes the negotiating dynamic [5][1]. After meeting President Donald Trump in Shenzhen on Nov. 18, Prime Minister Mark Carney said a trade deal was possible but not near completion. He said Canada would not accept terms that did not make sense for the country. He also pointed to recent agreements with Southeast Asian partners and the Philippines, as well as an expected deal with India in the coming months [6][9].

We would like to do the deal. But if the US doesn't want to do the deal, we're busy.

— Prime Minister Mark Carney (National Observer)

Key Points

  • Auto tariff offer: U.S. negotiators had offered to cut the auto tariff from 25% to 15% without exempting CUSMA-compliant vehicles [5].
  • Tariff exchange: Ottawa’s proposed framework would remove Canadian counter-tariffs in exchange for U.S. relief from 50% Section 338 duties on more than $20 billion of Canadian goods [5].
  • Congress limits: Democrats may be able to target Section 338 duties but could lack the votes to override a presidential veto [1].

Perspectives

Prime Minister Mark Carney: Carney described economic diversification as insurance against dependence on any single trading partner, saying deeper integration with one partner carries a large risk [6].
