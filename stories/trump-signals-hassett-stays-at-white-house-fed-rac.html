Trump signals Hassett stays at White House, Fed race shifts

Category: Business

President Trump said he would prefer to keep National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett in his White House role, casting doubt on Hassett as the next Federal Reserve chair [2][3][4]. The comments inject fresh uncertainty into the race to succeed Fed Chair Jerome Powell when Powell’s chair term ends May 15, and markets quickly repriced expectations—bond yields moved higher as investors weighed who could be next [1]. Several outlets said the shift improves the outlook for Kevin Warsh, a former Fed governor often viewed by investors as a more hawkish option [1][6]. Separately, attention on the Fed intensified after the Justice Department opened a criminal investigation involving Powell, raising questions about whether he stays at the Fed after his chair term ends [7][8][1].

Did you know: The Federal Reserve chair is nominated by the US president and confirmed by the Senate, and the job’s influence often comes as much from communication and agenda-setting as from the chair’s single vote on policy [common].

Key Points

  • White House rationale: President Trump said moving Hassett to the Fed would be a “serious concern for me,” pointing to Hassett’s current role advising the administration on economic policy [4].
  • Powell’s options: Even if Powell’s chair term ends in May, he could take the unusual step of remaining on the Fed’s Board of Governors afterward, keeping a foothold in monetary policy decisions [1][7].
  • Probe spotlight: Powell has framed the Justice Department subpoenas of the Fed as retaliation for the central bank not cutting rates as sharply as President Trump wanted, a characterization that has added political heat to a typically insulated institution [1].
  • Markets’ sensitivity: Investors treated the Fed-chair clues as consequential because the chair shapes the policy agenda and communications that influence borrowing costs across mortgages, corporate debt, and Treasury markets [1][6].

  • Friday: Trump said he prefers Hassett remain in the White House [2][4][3].

Technical Details

  • Fed chair vs. governor: Powell’s chair term ends May 15, but reports note he could remain on the Board as a governor, a separate position from chair that can extend influence on policy votes [1][7].
  • Bond yields: Treasury yields rose after Trump’s remarks, reflecting how expectations about the next Fed chair can affect projected paths for interest rates and long-term borrowing costs [1].
  • National Economic Council: Hassett leads the White House National Economic Council, an advisory role that helps coordinate economic policy across the administration—part of why Trump cited concern about moving him [4][1].

Industry Impact

  • Banking and credit: The bond-yield move highlighted how quickly Fed leadership signals can flow through to loan pricing and credit conditions, influencing everything from corporate bond issuance to consumer borrowing [1][6].
  • Housing and mortgages: Because mortgage rates commonly track longer-term Treasury yields, swings tied to Fed-chair expectations can affect affordability and housing demand even before the Fed changes policy [1][common].
  • Policy institutions: The combination of a chair search and a Justice Department investigation has put the Fed’s independence and internal continuity under a brighter spotlight than usual, which can matter for market confidence in the stability of the policy framework [7][8][1].

Historical Background

President Trump appointed Jerome Powell as Fed chair in 2018, but later criticized him after the Fed raised its key interest rate that year [1]. Under US law and Fed practice, the chairmanship is a distinct role from being a governor; chairs rotate, while governors can remain on the Board if their separate terms allow, which is why Powell’s post-chair status is now a live question in the reporting [1][7].
