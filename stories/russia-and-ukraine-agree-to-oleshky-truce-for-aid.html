Russia and Ukraine agree to Oleshky truce for aid and evacuations

Category: World

Russia and Ukraine have agreed to pause fighting around Russian-occupied Oleshky in southern Ukraine to allow aid deliveries and civilian evacuations under an arrangement brokered by U.S. first lady Melania Trump, officials said Friday [6][3][22]. Russia’s military chief and Ukraine’s General Staff said their forces would stop firing at 4 a.m. Moscow time on Oct. 27. The White House had announced Oct. 29 as the ceasefire’s start date, while Ukrainian negotiator Dmytro Lubinets, the country’s human rights ombudsman, said the humanitarian mission would begin on Oct. 29 and the pause would last several days [13][17][16]. Oleshky’s population has fallen from about 24,000 before the invasion to an estimated 1,700-2,000, including about 50 children. Residents face shortages of food, medicine and clean water, limited electricity, mined roads and continuing military attacks [3][6][5]. The first steps are expected to include assessing residents’ needs and clearing mines before aid convoys enter. Civilian teams are to coordinate transport for those choosing to leave for either Russia or Ukraine [17][7]. The ceasefire is expected to cover the Kherson region and neighboring areas essential to the operation. Lubinets said it would last several days; officials have not provided details on how compliance will be monitored [7][17]. Separately, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said technical-level talks involving Ukraine, Russia and the United States are planned for the first half of November in Abu Dhabi [13][11].

Key Points

  • Earlier failures: Previous humanitarian pauses were marred by accusations of violations, and a separate energy ceasefire announced by President Donald Trump collapsed as Russian attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure continued [2].
  • Aid by drone: The last reported food delivery to Oleshky was in May, and Ukrainian forces have tried to send supplies into the city by drone [2].
  • Media access: Melania Trump’s office did not name the U.S. media organization assigned to document the operation, but Newsmax said it would report on the teams’ work on the ground [4][33].
  • Peace talks: Russia continues to demand that Ukraine cede all of the eastern Donbas, including areas Moscow does not control, while Kyiv rejects giving up territory it holds [13].
