Webb findings reveal dusty ice and cold chemistry in interstellar comet

Category: Science

Scientists using the James Webb Space Telescope found that interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS contains tiny, dust-rich water-ice grains unlike those typically observed in solar-system comets, with similarities to some icy bodies beyond Neptune [1]. A separate Webb study measured an unusually high level of heavy hydrogen in the comet’s methane, pointing to formation in an extremely cold environment around another star or in the cloud that preceded it [3]. The measurements do not identify the comet’s specific birthplace [1][3]. The methane study found a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio about 13 times that measured in comet 67P, while the ice-grain analysis used three Webb observations between August 2025 and April 2026 [3][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Spectral puzzle: Webb’s Near-Infrared Spectrograph detected a strong water-ice absorption feature at three microns in 3I/ATLAS, while the expected features at 1.5 and two microns were weak or absent [1].
  • Methane comparison: The methane deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio was about three times the ratio previously measured in water from 3I/ATLAS [3].

Technical Details

  • Water-ice spectroscopy: Webb’s Near-Infrared Spectrograph measured the comet’s coma. Models of the unusual absorption pattern indicated sub-micron water-ice grains mixed with substantial dust, unlike the cleaner ice grains described for native solar-system comets [1].
  • Methane deuterium ratio: The study measured the methane’s deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio at about 3.2% [3].

Scientific Significance

  • Primordial material: Comets preserve material from the disks in which planets form [1].
  • Possible enrichment mechanism: The proposed setting had relatively few elements heavier than helium and intense cosmic-ray exposure, which can drive chemical reactions that enrich molecules in deuterium [3].

Historical Background

3I/ATLAS is the third known object identified as entering the solar system from interstellar space, after 1I/’Oumuamua was observed in 2017 and 2I/Borisov in 2019 [1].
