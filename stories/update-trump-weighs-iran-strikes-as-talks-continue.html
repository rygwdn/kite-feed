Update: Trump weighs Iran strikes as talks continue

Category: World

President Trump said he is “not happy” with the pace and substance of U.S.-Iran nuclear negotiations after the latest round of talks in Geneva. Still, he signaled he is not rushing a decision and will let further rounds play out [2][4][13][15]. He reiterated the U.S. position that Iran “cannot have nuclear weapons,” and said military force remains an option even as he stressed he would prefer a diplomatic outcome [2][17][10][7]. Oman, which is mediating the indirect talks, struck a more upbeat tone. Omani officials said the discussions made substantial progress and argued an agreement is “within our reach,” pointing to what they described as a breakthrough idea: Iran would agree to zero stockpiling of uranium and convert existing material into fuel [9][35][33][34]. The mixed messages come as the region takes precautions. The U.S. Embassy in Jerusalem moved to “authorized departure” for non-essential staff and family members, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio is set to visit Israel next week as tensions stay elevated [3][27][26]. As diplomacy continues, several governments have issued travel warnings and moved personnel amid fears of escalation—an indication of what’s at stake: avoiding another Middle East war while testing whether a new nuclear understanding is still possible [22][25][29][9].

We’re not exactly happy with the way they’re negotiating. They cannot have nuclear weapons.

— President Donald Trump (PBS NewsHour)

Key Points

  • Military posture: PBS reports the U.S. buildup alongside the negotiations has grown into the region’s largest Middle East military presence in more than two decades [1].
  • Israel staffing: U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee urged embassy employees that if they want to leave Israel, they “should do so TODAY,” as the mission shifted to authorized departure [27].
  • Enrichment red line: Trump said he wants Iran to have “no enrichment” of uranium at all—tougher than demands focused on stockpiles alone [9][16].
  • Inspection pressure: DW says the U.N. nuclear watchdog has urged Tehran to allow inspections at its nuclear sites as deadline pressure and diplomacy intensify [21].
  • War likelihood: CBS News cited experts who say war looks more likely than a deal even as talks resume, underscoring the gap between diplomatic hopes and escalation risks [12].

Perspectives

Oman (mediator): Oman’s foreign minister said the latest indirect talks made “substantial progress,” arguing a deal is “within our reach.” He pointed to an arrangement in which Iran would accept zero uranium stockpiling and convert existing material into fuel [9][35].

Sources: CBS News

U.S. administration: Trump said he wants a deal but insists Iran must not be able to obtain nuclear weapons. He has left military action on the table, while saying he has not made a final decision on strikes [13][17][7].

Sources: The New York Times

United Kingdom: Britain withdrew U.K. diplomatic staff from Iran and said the embassy will operate remotely. The FCDO continues to advise against all travel to Iran due to the security situation [25].

Sources: POLITICO Europe

IAEA / U.N. nuclear watchdog: DW reports the watchdog urged Tehran to allow inspections at its nuclear sites as Washington’s strike threat and the diplomatic timetable intensify [21].

Sources: DW
