Swallowable paper battery powers medical devices in pigs

Category: Science

Researchers developed a small battery made from magnesium and molybdenum trioxide that can be swallowed to power temporary medical devices inside the gastrointestinal tract. In pig tests, the battery produced a peak voltage of 1.84 volts and operated for up to three days, powering a wireless throat tag that transmitted to a receiver 1.5 metres away and a capsule that electrically stimulated the stomach for 20 minutes [2][1]. The battery uses materials intended to break down in the body after use, potentially removing the need to retrieve conventional batteries from temporary ingestible devices. The study remains at an early animal-testing stage: researchers tested a small number of pigs, and the circuit board did not fully degrade but passed in the animals' feces [2]. MIT said the researchers built two versions—a 7.5-millimetre disc and a 24-millimetre rectangular bar—for possible drug-delivery, sensing and other applications [3].
