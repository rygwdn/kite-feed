Samsung bans smart TV apps that share users' home internet

Category: Technology

Samsung said it will ban smart TV apps that share users' internet connections with third parties after Norwegian cybersecurity company Mnemonic reported that several apps on Samsung's TV platform contained residential-proxy code [5][1][3][10][13]. Mnemonic said the code could route strangers' web traffic through a user's home connection, making requests appear to come from the TV owner's internet address [5][10][7][13]. Mnemonic's research identified a Pac-Man game that Samsung had featured in its Editor's Choice section, and developers of some affected apps reported installation counts in the hundreds of millions [5][4][6][3]. Samsung told TechCrunch it had restricted new app registrations using those proxy functions, was adding platform-wide developer policies that explicitly ban residential-proxy SDKs, and was working to identify and remove existing apps containing the components [5][10][11][13].

Primary Source: View original article

We have already restricted new app registrations that incorporate such proxy functionalities on our Smart TV platform

— Samsung spokesperson (iPhone in Canada)

Key Points

  • Persistent routing: Mnemonic said opening an app with residential-proxy code could turn a TV into an exit node and that the TV could keep routing traffic even after the app is closed [5][4][6][7].
  • Review gap: Researchers said many affected smart TV apps were thin shells that loaded content from external servers, so store reviews may examine only a few lines of code rather than the code later executed from the server [5][4][11][12].
  • Proxy provider: Digital Trends reported that the Pac-Man app contained code from Bright Data, an Israel-based company that runs proxy networks and says it has access to millions of residential connections worldwide [6].
  • Other manufacturers: Engadget, citing KrebsOnSecurity, reported that LG said in July it would suspend apps capable of turning its TVs into residential proxies [10][8][14][15].

Timeline

  • July 2026: LG said it would suspend residential-proxy TV apps [10].
  • 3 August 2026: Mnemonic published research on proxy code in Samsung smart TV apps [5][15].
  • 3 August 2026: Samsung told TechCrunch it had restricted new proxy app registrations [5][11].

Technical Details

  • Server-side activation: Giga reported that proxy code can remain inactive by default and be enabled later through a server-side switch after the store submission process [15].

Action Items

  • Remove unfamiliar Samsung TV apps: Uninstall games or apps you do not use; Digital Trends reported that routing stays active until the app is removed from the TV [6].
  • Read consent prompts in TV apps: Review prompts before accepting them; Digital Trends reported that opening an affected app and accepting a consent screen could enable background residential-proxy routing [6][15].
