Blue Origin astronaut Amanda Nguyen describes depression after harassment

Category: Science

Astronaut and scientist Amanda Nguyen says she experienced depression after what she described as a “tsunami of harassment” following Blue Origin’s all-female spaceflight earlier this year [2][3]. The 11-minute suborbital flight in April drew intense public criticism, including debate over its purpose and environmental impact, and Nguyen said the backlash affected her well-being [2][3]. Her account has renewed attention on the human side of high-profile science and space milestones—how public scrutiny can shape who feels welcome in the space community [2][3].

Did you know: The flight was described as lasting 11 minutes, typical for suborbital tourism-style missions [3].

Key Points

  • Historic first: Nguyen became the first Vietnamese woman to go to space, a milestone highlighted alongside the flight’s broader visibility [3].
  • Celebrity crew: The crew also included Katy Perry, Gayle King and Lauren Sánchez, making the mission unusually pop-culture visible for a commercial spaceflight [3].
  • Aftermath described: Nguyen said she avoided leaving Texas for a week during the fallout after the launch, describing how the reaction disrupted her daily life [1].
  • Mental health focus: In her public statement, Nguyen tied the experience to depression, putting mental health—often under-discussed in astronaut culture—into the spotlight [2][3].
