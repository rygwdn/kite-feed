Yemen warns Houthis seek leverage over Red Sea shipping

Category: World

Yemen’s internationally recognized government said it was prepared for escalation with the Houthis after the group announced a maritime blockade targeting Saudi Arabia and claimed fresh drone attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure [15][6][12][18]. Foreign Minister-designate Afrah al-Zouba told journalists in Riyadh the Houthis were trying to copy Iran’s Strait of Hormuz strategy by disrupting shipping through Bab al-Mandeb, the narrow route linking the Red Sea, the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean [1][7][11][24]. Al-Zouba said an insufficient international response had emboldened the Houthis and that Houthi pressure over Bab al-Mandeb would threaten a second major waterway after disruption in the Strait of Hormuz [1][7][20]. The Houthis said on Monday they targeted Saudi crude supply and transport sites linking eastern Saudi Arabia to Yanbu, while Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree described the operation as a response to Saudi drone incursions into Yemeni airspace [27][23][15]. Saudi Arabia has responded with airstrikes on what it called Houthi military facilities in Yemen’s Hodeidah port and said it would protect commercial shipping [27][24]. Al-Zouba said Houthi forces and government troops were skirmishing daily in several parts of Yemen, while reports from Riyadh said neither side had launched a major ground offensive [15][5][13].

The Houthis want to copy the Iranian model and this will shut down two main straits, the gateways into the Gulf and Red Sea,

— Afrah Al-Zouba (Al-Monitor)

Key Points

  • Oil routing: Saudi Arabia has used its east-west pipeline to move crude to Yanbu on the Red Sea after fighting largely choked traffic through Hormuz [27][9][19].
  • Domestic planning: Prime Minister Shaya al-Zindani chaired a government meeting in Aden to review plans for keeping food, fuel and basic goods imports flowing if shipping disruption worsens [26].

Perspectives

Yemeni Armed Forces: Brig. Gen. Abdo Majali said the armed forces were coordinating with the Coalition to Restore Legitimacy in Yemen to deter Houthi threats, secure maritime routes and protect international shipping.

Sources: Asharq Al-Awsat

Yemeni prime minister: Prime Minister Shaya al-Zindani called for stronger international action to deter the Houthis, dry up their funding sources and prevent threats to regional and international interests.

Sources: Asharq Al-Awsat

Industry Impact

  • Container shipping: The Red Sea carries about 30% of global container traffic, so disruption near Bab al-Mandeb can affect carriers moving goods between Asia, Europe and Africa [1][12].

Historical Background

The Houthis seized Yemen’s capital, Sanaa, in 2014, and a Saudi-led coalition began a large-scale air campaign against them in 2015 [24][18]. The warring sides reached a truce in 2022 that expired but largely held, while recent direct Saudi-Houthi attacks have increased the risk of a return to wider war [18][24].

International Reactions

  • 🇮🇶 Iraq: Iraq said it would open an investigation after Saudi Arabia reported intercepting drones launched from Iraq toward oil facilities and the Riyadh area [19].
  • 🇬🇧 United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations: UKMTO received a report of a southern Red Sea incident in which a tanker witnessed a splash from an unidentified projectile nearby [22].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Houthi threats have targeted commercial vessels and Saudi-bound shipping. Avoid maritime transit near affected Red Sea routes unless operating under professional security guidance.
  • Logistics: Check carrier notices before booking cargo through the Suez-Red Sea route, as disruptions may cause delays and rerouting.

Action Items

  • Review Red Sea cargo exposure: If you ship goods through the Suez-Red Sea route, ask carriers about current transit status and potential rerouting delays.
  • Monitor maritime security alerts: Commercial operators should follow UKMTO incident notices for the southern Red Sea.
