US banks block AI agents from moving customer money

Category: AI

Charles Schwab and JPMorgan Chase are blocking outside AI agents, including Meta’s Muse, from moving customers’ money. Schwab CEO Rick Wurster cited the risks of customers sharing passwords with agents and uncertainty about who would be responsible if something went wrong [1][4]. Schwab says it detects blocked agents tens to 20 times a week and is preparing to allow limited access to account information and research, while requiring human approval for transfers and trading. JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon said the bank bars outside agents from retail customer accounts. Schwab’s own assistant, Charley, is initially limited to administrative and informational tasks [1][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Retail barriers: Amazon has removed Meta’s Muse and Delta blocks external agents from booking flights; retailers also face concerns that bots can bypass sponsored ads and product recommendations [3][2].
  • Bank economics: Apollo Global Management analysis cited in Android Headlines estimated that agents shifting idle checking balances into higher-yield savings could add tens of billions of dollars in interest costs for major banks [3].
  • Infrastructure hurdles: Cloudflare anti-bot shields and CAPTCHA checks can block legitimate AI agents, while a lack of standards makes it difficult to distinguish them from malicious scrapers [3].

Technical Details

  • Controlled agent access: Schwab plans to route external large-language-model connections through authenticated Model Context Protocol servers and its APIs to verify requests and limit what agents can do [1].
