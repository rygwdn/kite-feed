Apple sends legal letters to about 40 OpenAI workers

Category: AI

Apple has sent legal preservation letters to about 40 former Apple employees who now work at OpenAI, directing them to preserve potentially relevant documents and communications and asking them to meet with Apple’s lawyers, the Financial Times reported [5][4][6][14][20][21][18]. The letters follow Apple’s July 10 lawsuit in the Northern District of California, which accuses OpenAI of stealing Apple trade secrets and intellectual property for AI hardware development [8][14][20]. Apple’s complaint names OpenAI, io Products, former Apple vice president Tang Tan and former Apple senior system electrical engineer Chang Liu. It alleges they directed Apple employees interviewing with OpenAI to share information on unreleased devices, components, manufacturing processes and vendor relationships [8][20][23]. Apple says more than 400 former Apple employees now work at OpenAI and argues that evidence cited in its complaint may be only the “tip of the iceberg” [14][6][16][18]. OpenAI denied the allegations, saying it is “not aware of any evidence that this complaint has merit” [14][22].

Key Points

  • Recruiting claims: Apple alleges Tan told Apple employees interviewing with OpenAI to bring confidential physical parts from products under development for “show and tell” sessions [4][23].
  • IPO timing: TechCrunch and Korean reports said the lawsuit arrived as OpenAI is considering an IPO as early as later this year [1][2][18].
  • Evidence requests: Computerworld and Korean reports said the letters also ask some recipients to meet with Apple's lawyers [6][9][35].
  • Named executives: Tan worked at Apple for 24 years before becoming OpenAI's chief hardware officer, while Liu joined OpenAI's hardware team after serving as a senior system electrical engineer at Apple [8][24][12].
  • Device concept: Reports described OpenAI’s planned hardware as a ChatGPT-powered home computer or smart speaker, with one report saying it would be palm-sized, screenless and equipped with microphones and cameras [23][31].
  • Hardware plans: Apple is seeking damages and an injunction barring OpenAI from using Apple information during development of its AI hardware device [6][14][16].
  • IPO timing: TechCrunch said the legal fight adds pressure while OpenAI is reportedly eyeing an IPO as early as later this year [1][2].

Timeline

  • 2024: Tan co-founded io Products with Jony Ive [23].
  • 2025: OpenAI acquired io Products for $6.5 billion [23].
  • 10 July 2026: Apple filed its California trade secrets lawsuit [8].
  • 14 July 2026: OpenAI rejected Apple’s evidence claims in Bloomberg statement [22].
  • 17 July 2026: FT reported Apple’s preservation letters to workers [5].

Industry Impact

  • AI hardware: Apple’s lawsuit targets OpenAI’s move into consumer devices after its $6.5 billion acquisition of io Products, putting a named hardware strategy under legal scrutiny [23][14].
  • Tech hiring: Apple’s letters involve about 40 former employees at a company where more than 400 ex-Apple workers are employed, making talent movement a central evidentiary issue [5][14][31].
  • Public markets: TechCrunch linked the dispute to OpenAI’s reported IPO planning and said the timing could complicate scrutiny of the company’s legal and hardware strategy [1][2].

Historical Background

OpenAI and Apple previously worked together to integrate OpenAI technology into Siri, according to Korean reports summarizing the dispute [18][28]. Apple later worked with Google on a ChatGPT-style voice and text assistant feature unveiled in June using a Google AI model [18][28].

Business Angle

  • An injunction barring OpenAI from using Apple information could interrupt product development, a longer-running effect than a one-time damages award [6][14][common].
  • OpenAI’s large number of former Apple employees creates discovery risk if Apple convinces the court that alleged misconduct extended beyond the named defendants [14][6].
  • An injunction that restricts OpenAI's use of disputed Apple information could delay or narrow its first consumer hardware products [8][6].
  • The case focuses on risks for AI hardware startups that hire from incumbent device makers while building products in adjacent markets [30][common].
  • Apple's complaint cites manufacturing and product-design know-how; if the case proceeds, discovery could put sensitive details into the litigation record [37][common].
  • The lawsuit names senior hardware figures and overlaps with reported IPO planning at OpenAI [1][2].
