Canadian, U.S. jets mark Fourth of July in Ottawa

Category: Canada

Canadian and American air forces staged a ceremonial flypast over Ottawa on Saturday, July 4, as the United States marked the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence [1][4][6]. The Royal Canadian Air Force and the U.S. Air Force flew two Canadian CF-18 Hornets alongside two U.S. F-35 Lightning II jets, with the flypast scheduled for about 6:30 p.m. ET [1][4][6]. The jets flew at a low altitude over Lornado, the official residence of U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra, where guests had gathered for the annual Fourth of July party in Ottawa [4][5]. Prime Minister Mark Carney also issued messages marking the U.S. anniversary [1].

Key Points

  • NORAD link: The Defence Department said the joint appearance demonstrated routine Canada-U.S. air cooperation through NORAD, the binational continental defence command [4].
  • Diplomatic gestures: The Ottawa event was one of Canada’s gestures marking the U.S. anniversary, along with Carney’s messages and the maple-tree gift [1].
  • Fighter aircraft: The participating U.S. aircraft were F-35 Lightning II jets, the same fighter type Canada plans to add to its fleet [1][5].

Perspectives

U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra: Hoekstra used the Independence Day event in Ottawa to promote Canada’s planned F-35 fleet.

Historical Background

The Fourth of July marks the United States’ adoption of the Declaration of Independence in 1776 [common]. Canada and the United States jointly defend North American airspace through NORAD, a binational command created in 1958 [common].
