Carney and Ford commit $8.7 billion to tariff-hit Ontario industries

Category: Canada

Prime Minister Mark Carney and Ontario Premier Doug Ford announced an $8.7-billion federal-provincial package on Oct. 21 for Ontario’s steel and auto industries and roads to the mineral-rich Ring of Fire region. The plan allocates about $5.2 billion to steel over five years, $2.5 billion to autos over two years and up to $1 billion in federal funding for roads over three years, matching Ontario’s contribution [10][5][4]. The governments say the investments will help mills modernize, support auto production and reduce reliance on U.S. markets as tariffs disrupt trade. The package also includes stronger enforcement against steel dumping, a joint steel task force and a coordinated process for mine permits. A working group will engage First Nations on economic development and conservation, including possible Indigenous protected areas [7][14][15].

Their approach has changed, and so must ours.

— Prime Minister Mark Carney (CBC News)

Key Points

  • Tariff exposure: Canada’s auto exports to the U.S. have fallen more than 10 per cent, while the Canadian Vehicle Manufacturers’ Association puts Ontario’s annual U.S. tariff bill at $7 billion [5].
  • Job targets: Carney said the steel support is intended to protect 100,000 jobs and the auto funding 120,000, while Stelco’s planned cuts put hundreds of steel jobs at risk [9][4].
  • Policy changes: Ottawa will keep new-vehicle emissions standards at 2026 levels through the 2029 model year, with annual increases due to resume in 2030 [10].
  • Funding questions: The Globe and Mail reported it was unclear whether all the announced funding is new money or partly drawn from previously announced programs [5].

Perspectives

Pacific Manufacturing Association of Canada: Its head, Brendan Sweeney, said the auto funding falls far short of the sector’s stated annual tariff costs and that negotiating a free-trade deal remains the industry’s priority [5].

Sources: The Globe and Mail

Canadian Vehicle Manufacturers’ Association: President Brian Kingston said the support could help protect jobs, production and investment, and argued that Canada cannot diverge greatly from U.S. vehicle rules while maintaining an industry of its current scale [10][5].

Sources: CBC News, The Globe and Mail

Clean Energy Canada: Associate director Evan Pivnick said delaying tighter emissions rules sets back the government’s electric-vehicle sales targets by postponing policies intended to expand Canadians’ access to EVs [10].

Sources: CBC News

First Nations communities in the Ring of Fire region: Some First Nations support the road, while others oppose development over concerns that accelerated construction could affect environmental protections and treaty rights [6][4].

Sources: Castanet, Canada’s National Observer

Timeline

  • August 2026: Canada left trade talks; both countries later imposed further tariffs [5]
  • September 2026: Stelco announced plans to cut as many as 500 jobs [14]
  • 21 October 2026: Carney and Ford announced the provincial-federal industry support package [10]
  • 5 November 2026: Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne set the federal budget date [9]

Industry Impact

  • Steelmaking: The five-year steel fund combines $3.55 billion from Ottawa and $1.67 billion from Ontario, with planned support for mill upgrades, market diversification and stronger anti-dumping enforcement [10][7].
  • Automotive: The $2.5-billion auto package comprises $1.5 billion from Ottawa and $1 billion from Ontario for factory machinery and production-line upgrades [10].
  • Critical minerals: The federal government’s commitment of up to $1 billion for roads matches Ontario’s planned contribution. The package also includes coordinated mine permitting and First Nations engagement [15][10].

Historical Background

Stelco’s U.S.-based owner, Cleveland-Cliffs, cited trade pressures and weak demand as reasons for its planned layoffs. Ottawa says the cuts may violate commitments made under the Investment Canada Act; Industry Minister Mélanie Joly has warned of legal action and possible forced divestiture [14][9].

Business Angle

  • Ontario estimates Ring of Fire development could generate C$22 billion in economic activity and more than 70,000 jobs over 30 years [15].
  • Ontario has established a C$500-million Critical Minerals Processing Fund to support processing and refining projects and retain more value from mineral production in the province [15].
  • Algoma says its new electric arc furnaces are in place and its diversification plans could position it to supply lower-carbon steel for infrastructure, defence and shipbuilding projects [7].

Q&A

Q: What happens to Canada’s electric-vehicle sales target?

A: The government kept its target for electric vehicles to make up 75 per cent of new-vehicle sales by 2035 [5][10].

Q: How large is the planned road network to the Ring of Fire?

A: Ontario has proposed more than 500 kilometres of all-season roads to connect the remote mineral region with the provincial highway network [15].
