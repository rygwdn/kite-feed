Bella Coola to compare bear DNA as residents face curfew

Category: Canada

British Columbia conservation officers say they will collect DNA from the site of a suspected fatal bear attack in Bella Coola and compare it with samples from the 2025 grizzly attack on schoolchildren to investigate whether the same bear was involved. Officials are investigating the deaths of Jennifer Balcarras and her daughter Selah Reed, found near the Snooka West trail, and have not determined whether one or more bears were involved [1][8][4]. The recommended curfew is not enforced. The Nuxalk Nation is also running a daily shuttle, offering to remove fruit from trees and asking residents to manage bear attractants. Conservation officers and RCMP are conducting night patrols [8][1]. Inspector Jeff Tyre of the B.C. Conservation Officer Service said investigators had received reports of bear sightings, but officers had not trapped any bears as of Wednesday. The bear involved in the 2025 school-group attack was never found. Officers say they will assess any bear captured in the current investigation and kill any found responsible, under provincial public safety guidelines [8][1][4].

Primary Source: View original article

We do have the DNA from the last attack site and we will be working to collect DNA; that would be the only linkage we could put toward that.

— Inspector Jeff Tyre, B.C. Conservation Officer Service (The Globe and Mail)

Key Points

  • Attack statistics: B.C. recorded 64 grizzly attacks and two fatal attacks from 2012 through 2025; conservation officers or other groups killed 479 grizzlies in human-wildlife conflict over the same period, according to provincial figures cited by CBC [8].
  • Community precautions: The Nuxalk Nation is urging residents to dispose of organic hunting and fishing waste properly and remove fruit that could attract bears [8].
  • Local school: Balcarras joined the Bella Coola school community as a counsellor in August, and the Central Coast Teachers’ Association said she supported students’ well-being [4][1].
  • Prior search: After the 2025 school-group attack, authorities captured and tagged eight bears during their search [1].

Perspectives

Nuxalk Nation: Chief administrative officer Terry Webber said residents were re-triggered by the latest deaths and that the Nation’s priority was keeping them safe and supporting the community [4].

Kim Titchener, founder of Bear Safety & More Inc.: Titchener cited habitat loss and food stress related to drought and climate change as pressures that can increase bear-human conflict, while saying she did not know whether drought affected Bella Coola [14][18].

Historical Background

British Columbia banned grizzly hunting at the end of 2017; First Nations retained the ability to harvest bears under treaty or Aboriginal rights for food, social or ceremonial purposes [1].
