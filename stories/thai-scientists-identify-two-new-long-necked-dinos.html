Thai scientists identify two new long-necked dinosaur species

Category: Science

Thai paleontologists have identified Uragasaurus kalasinensis, a newly named long-necked, plant-eating dinosaur from fossils found at the Phu Noi fossil site in Kalasin Province in northeastern Thailand [2][3][4][5][6][7]. The animal lived about 150 million years ago in the Late Jurassic, measured up to 20 metres and belonged to Mamenchisauridae, a sauropod family known for extremely long necks [2][4][5][6]. Researchers identified the species from a dorsal vertebra recovered from the Phu Kradung Formation. CT scans showed diagnostic features, including a Y-shaped arrangement of supporting laminae and an internal air-cavity system [2][5][6]. Most known mamenchisaurid fossils have been found in China. The Thailand specimen is reported as the first formally named mamenchisaurid from Thailand and, according to VietnamPlus, the first formally named member of the family in Southeast Asia [2][3][4][5]. A separate discovery in Thailand is a sauropod from Chaiyaphum Province dated to roughly 100 million to 120 million years ago [1]. The fossils were first noticed near a drying pond in 2016, and scientists formally confirmed the species on May 14, 2026; ScienceBlog reported that it was 27 metres long, weighed 27 tonnes and was the largest dinosaur yet found in Southeast Asia [1].

Key Points

  • Fossil density: Dinosaur fragments made up more than 90% of fossils excavated at Phu Noi, where survey teams also found dinosaur teeth and bones [2][6].

Perspectives

Dr Apirat Nilphanaphan: The Mahasarakham University researcher said the Kalasin specimen came from a large fossil collection at a site first identified in 2008. He said its air-cavity structure was unlike that of any other known dinosaur.

Sources: BBC

Phu Wiang Dinosaur Museum: The museum, under Thailand's Department of Mineral Resources, said the Kalasin fossil came from Phu Noi in the Phu Kradung Formation and identified Uragasaurus kalasinensis as Thailand’s 15th officially recognised dinosaur species.

Timeline

  • 2008: Local fragments led researchers to identify the Phu Noi fossil site [2]
  • 2016: Thanom Luangnan reported exposed Chaiyaphum bones near a pond [1]
  • 2016-2019: Scientists conducted multiple field seasons at the Chaiyaphum site [1]
  • 14 May 2026: Researchers formally confirmed the Chaiyaphum sauropod species [1]
  • 10 July 2026: A Scientific Reports study identified Uragasaurus from a single bone [3]

Scientific Significance

  • Biogeographic range: Uragasaurus kalasinensis adds Thailand to the confirmed geographic distribution of formally named mamenchisaurids, whose cited fossil record is centered on China [2][3][5].
  • Specimen limits: Researchers defined Uragasaurus from a well-preserved dorsal vertebra. Nearby sauropod bones were reported, but only that vertebra carried the diagnostic traits needed to name the species [5][2].
