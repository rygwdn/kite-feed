Indonesia releases eight orangutans rescued from fires into protected Borneo forest

Category: Science

Indonesian authorities and the YIARI wildlife rehabilitation foundation released eight orangutans into Batu Nyambu Protected Forest in West Kalimantan on Friday after wildfires threatened the animals’ habitat on Borneo [3][4][5]. The group includes three mothers with their offspring and two adolescent males. The 23,000-hectare reserve was chosen for its food and water sources and connections to other forest areas [3][5]. Authorities and YIARI rescued 46 orangutans affected by fires between August and October 9. El Niño conditions and land-clearing practices have been cited as factors in Indonesia’s intensified fires this year [3][4]. The Indonesian government has warned that the blazes, which have hit Sumatra and Borneo, could continue until November [7][9].

Protection of habitats will always be a top priority to ensure orangutans continue to have safe forests to live in, but as long as their habitat remains threatened, rescue is one of the best options we can take to help them survive

— Karmele Llano Sanchez, YIARI chief executive (AFP)

Key Points

  • Long transfer: The journey took six hours by car, one hour by boat and two hours on foot. The animals were carried in cages suspended from bamboo poles, and conservationists checked their health before the move [9][10].
  • Individual rescues: Fires burned the forest around Mama Nopi and her offspring, limiting their access to food and bringing them closer to people. Habitat degradation and food shortages prompted Mama Lifa’s rescue [4].
  • Fire extent: Fires burned about 202,000 hectares in Indonesia from January through July, and NGOs estimated that at least another 600,000 hectares had burned since then [9][10].
  • School closures: Deteriorating air quality prompted school closures in parts of Malaysia [9].

Perspectives

Argitoe Ranting, YIARI: He said the relocation should give the rescued orangutans a chance to survive in their natural habitat, rather than simply move them to another location [3].

Timeline

  • June 2026: Rescuers began monitoring Mama Man and her baby near a plantation [4]
  • September 2026: Mama Nopi and offspring were rescued after fires burned surrounding forest [4]
  • September 2026: Rescuers found Mama Lifa and her baby surrounded by plantations [4]
  • 9 October 2026: Eight orangutans were transferred to Batu Nyambu Protected Forest [3][9]

Scientific Significance

  • Habitat exposure: The International Union for Conservation of Nature warned that fires were burning in habitat used by 52% of Borneo’s orangutan population. The BBC also reported that continued climate change could make a large portion of orangutan habitat inhospitable [4][6].
  • Fire drivers: Reports linked this year’s intensified fires to El Niño and land conversion. Clearing land for plantations can replace naturally fire-resistant forest with more open vegetation, allowing fires to spread more readily [3][4].

Historical Background

Around 104,000 orangutans remain in Borneo and Sumatra, down from more than 230,000 a century ago, according to WWF [3][4]. Indonesia is home to all three recognized orangutan species [9][10].

Humanitarian Impact

Haze from the fires has been linked to 175,000 respiratory-illness cases in Indonesia; air pollution also affected neighboring Singapore and Malaysia [9][10].

Q&A

Q: What types of forest are found in Batu Nyambu Protected Forest?

A: The reserve contains primary and secondary forest [3][5].

Q: How did fires affect Mama Man’s access to other forest areas?

A: Fires near the plantation where Mama Man and her baby lived reduced the area they could roam and left them without a safe route to other forest areas [4].
