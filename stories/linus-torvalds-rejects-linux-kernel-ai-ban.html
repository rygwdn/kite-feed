Linus Torvalds rejects Linux kernel AI ban

Category: AI

Linus Torvalds, creator and top-level maintainer of the Linux kernel, told developers in a kernel mailing-list discussion that Linux is not an anti-AI project and that opponents of AI use can “fork it” or “walk away” [4][6][7]. He called AI a tool and “clearly a useful one,” while saying separate questions remain about AI’s eventual economics [4][7]. The exchange followed debate over Sashiko, an agentic Linux kernel code-review system used to analyze submitted kernel patches [6][17][11]. Participants raised concerns about maintainer workload, unreliable output and whether AI-generated findings should go directly to patch authors or first be triaged by maintainers [6][17]. Torvalds did not announce any requirement that kernel developers use AI coding assistants, automated review systems or LLM-generated patches; CybersecurityNews and TechRadar cited his position that developers remain free not to use such tools [10][13].

Primary Source: View original article

AI is a tool, just like other tools we use. And it's clearly a useful one. It may not have been that "clearly" even just a year ago, but it's no longer in question today.

— Linus Torvalds (Simon Willison’s Weblog)

Key Points

  • Review guidance: ComputerBase reported that Software Freedom Conservancy recommendations call for supporting developers who do not want to use generative AI, rejecting mandatory AI use, requiring human review of AI-assisted contributions and disclosing such tool use [17].
  • Other projects: Tom’s Hardware identified Gentoo Linux, Curl and Ghostty as projects that have limited or banned LLM-created contributions, while ZDNET cited the Zig language project’s strict policy against AI-generated code [8][16].
  • Changed stance: TechSpot said Torvalds described AI technology in 2024 as “90% marketing and 10% factual reality,” and Tweakers reported that his position has become more nuanced in recent months [7][20].
  • Maintainer burden: Torvalds called AI a “somewhat painful tool” for maintainer workloads and for finding “embarrassing bugs,” but argued that kernel developers should not refuse to engage with it [7][14].

Perspectives

Linus Torvalds: Torvalds said developers are not being forced to use AI, but said he would “very loudly ignore” arguments for keeping AI tools out of Linux development.

Sources: TechSpot

Linus Torvalds: Torvalds said Linux is not a “social warrior project,” pushing back against ideological objections to AI in kernel development, Windows Central reported.

Sources: Windows Central

Kernel developer opposing SFC framing: ComputerBase described another developer as saying the SFC-linked requirements were broadly hostile to LLMs because they could undercut Sashiko’s goal of reducing maintainer review work.

Sources: ComputerBase

Technical Details

  • Sashiko bug-yield test: Sashiko’s creators claim the review system independently found 53.6% of bugs that human coders later fixed in subsequent commits [6].
  • False-positive load: Sashiko’s maintainers estimate false-positive bug reports are “well within” a 20% range; participants debated how maintainers should triage that output [6].

Historical Background

Linux began in 1991 as Linus Torvalds’s kernel project. The kernel is distributed under the GPLv2, which allows recipients to study, modify and redistribute the source code, including forks [common]. That licensing model makes “fork it” a real governance option in open-source disputes, not just a rhetorical challenge [common]. Linux kernel development has long relied on public mailing-list review, so changes in review tooling affect maintainers before patches reach the mainline tree [common].
