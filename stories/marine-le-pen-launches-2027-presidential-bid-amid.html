Marine Le Pen launches 2027 presidential bid amid French unrest

Category: World

Marine Le Pen, leader of France’s far-right National Rally (RN) party and a frontrunner in the 2027 presidential race, formally launched her campaign at a rally in Orléans on Oct. 25, promising to restore public order and national confidence [10][5]. Her launch followed a day of nationwide demonstrations against the cost of living, when more than 400,000 people marched and police made more than 1,300 arrests after clashes broke out [2][11]. Le Pen accused President Emmanuel Macron’s centrist government of lacking authority and made security and immigration central to her campaign, pledging tougher border controls, the expulsion of foreign offenders and “zero tolerance” policing [10][6]. Unions and Yellow Vest protesters have said they will continue demonstrations in the weeks ahead [2]. The presidential vote is expected in April 2027, with a runoff in May if no candidate wins a majority [5][10]. Le Pen is appealing a conviction for embezzling European Parliament funds. The National Rally congress also re-elected Jordan Bardella as party president and presented him as her choice for prime minister if she wins [10][6].

Key Points

  • Polling lead: An Ipsos survey put Le Pen at 32% to 34% in the first round, compared with 17% for Jean-Luc Mélenchon, leader of the left-wing France Unbowed party [11][19].
  • Economic pledges: Le Pen proposed lower business taxes, spending restraint, reduced bureaucracy and support for industry and artificial intelligence, while opposing another increase in the retirement age [10].
  • Budget limits: France’s public debt and deficit exceed European Union limits, leaving the next president little room for new spending as Macron’s government struggles to pass a cost-cutting budget [5][19].
  • Party controversy: Jewish student activists interrupted Bardella’s speech over allegations that he made antisemitic comments as a teenager; he denies the accusations [6][36].
  • Foreign policy: Le Pen said France should reduce the influence of European Union institutions and avoid wars abroad; she is also critical of NATO and French support for Ukraine [10][11].

Perspectives

French Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez attributed much of the violence during the demonstrations to “ultra-left” extremists [2][11].

Historical Background

Le Pen took over leadership of the National Rally from her father, Jean-Marie Le Pen, in 2011 and has since sought to broaden its appeal beyond its traditional anti-immigration base [10]. She has run for president three times, making the 2027 contest her fourth bid [11][36].
