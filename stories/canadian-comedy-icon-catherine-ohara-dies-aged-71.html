Canadian comedy icon Catherine O’Hara dies aged 71

Category: Canada

Canadian actor and comedian Catherine O’Hara, born in Toronto, has died at age 71, according to multiple reports published Friday [1][2][5][6][9]. Her publicist confirmed the death to Variety, according to La Presse [2]. O’Hara built a career spanning roughly five decades, moving from early work in Canada’s sketch-comedy scene to internationally recognized film and television roles [6]. Many tributes highlighted the warmth she brought to comedy, and the coverage emphasized how widely her work—ranging from “Home Alone” to “Schitt’s Creek”—connected with audiences in Canada and beyond [1][2][7]. While the reports focus on the loss, they also point to a hopeful, shared note: fans are revisiting her work and celebrating the joy she delivered across generations [3][4].

Catherine O’Hara was one of a kind. A rare light in this world and her passing hits with a weight I can’t fully put into words.

— Michael Bublé (The Lethbridge Herald)

Did you know: O’Hara joined Second City Toronto’s cast in 1974, stepping into a spot previously held by Gilda Radner [8].

Key Points

  • Second City roots: O’Hara started at Second City Toronto as a waitress, joined the cast in 1974 replacing Gilda Radner, and later became part of “SCTV,” where her national profile grew [8][6].
  • Cause not stated: Some coverage notes the cause of death has not been disclosed in early reporting about her passing [5].

  • Tribute messages: Singer Michael Bublé wrote on Instagram that O’Hara was “one of a kind” and “a rare light in this world,” as tributes spread from friends and collaborators [7].

Historical Background

O’Hara emerged from Toronto’s comedy pipeline, including Second City and “SCTV,” which helped launch a wave of Canadian performers into international film and TV [8][6]. Over time, she became widely recognized for mainstream movies and later for television work that introduced her to new audiences, including “Schitt’s Creek” [1][2].
