Ontario court denies MAID request for mental illness

Category: Canada

An Ontario judge denied a motion by Claire Brosseau, a 49-year-old Toronto resident, that would have allowed her to apply for medical assistance in dying on the basis of mental illness alone [1][2][4][3]. Brosseau filed the motion in May seeking a constitutional exemption from the federal law that currently bars MAID applications when mental illness is the sole underlying condition [1][4]. Brosseau’s lawyers argued in court on July 20 that she needed emergency relief from persistent suffering that began in childhood and intensified over 35 years. She said she has been treated for bipolar disorder, an eating disorder, substance use disorder and post-traumatic stress disorder [1][4]. Justice Carissima Mathen found Brosseau’s evidence of decades of suffering credible, but said the consequences of granting an exemption could not be overlooked. The court also required the Attorney General of Canada to respond to Brosseau’s constitutional challenge, launched two years ago [4].
