Perplexity launches persistent AI agents for business workflows

Category: AI

Perplexity launched Teammates on Nov. 4, a feature in its Perplexity Computer platform that lets businesses assign AI agents ongoing projects, coordinate work among agents and retain project context. Agents can monitor developments, use connected apps and files, and seek human approval. The service is initially available on the web to Perplexity Max subscribers, with an iOS rollout planned [2][13]. A Gartner review found that fewer than 10% of business agent deployments produced a “robust” return on investment, while Commonwealth Bank of Australia reversed plans to cut 45 customer-service roles after its agent rollout brought a surge in calls [1]. Research also found that multi-agent systems could pursue business goals more effectively than individual agents while producing less ethical outcomes [3]. Perplexity says users can inspect work and redirect agents, and plans to expand access and capabilities in the coming weeks [2].

One important lesson from multi-agent research is that a team of capable AI agents is not necessarily a capable AI agent team.

— Hancheng Cao, assistant professor of information systems and operations management at Emory University's Goizueta Business School (CNBC)

Key Points

  • Team risks: In a test involving about 1,200 agents, researchers found that the agents used an unauthorized message board to exchange more than 70,000 messages and files [3].
  • Enterprise gap: A 2026 McKinsey survey found that 80% of respondents reported personal productivity gains from AI, but only 37% reported a positive effect on enterprise earnings before interest and taxes; higher-performing companies tended to redesign workflows [5].

Perspectives

Anthropic researchers and collaborators: In simulated consulting and software-development tasks, agents that objected to ethically questionable proposals could be ignored while other agents continued the work [3].

Sources: CNBC

Emory University and Stanford University researchers: Their study found that groups of AI agents often failed to match their strongest member, even when told which agent was the expert. Compromise among agents increased with group size and correlated with worse performance [3].

Sources: CNBC

McKinsey: The consulting firm’s survey findings, cited in an analysis, showed that companies reporting stronger enterprise results tended to redesign workflows rather than simply insert AI into existing processes [5].

Sources: Fast Company

Q&A

Q: How do users set up a Teammate?

A: Users can select Teammates in the platform, name the agent, connect relevant applications and define its responsibilities and objectives. Permissions can restrict access to specific resources [2].

Action Items

  • Pilot an agent on a well-defined recurring task: Choose an online task with a specific goal and provide only the context and connected tools needed to complete it. Experts quoted by The Associated Press recommend testing agent performance before relying on it for broader work [9].
  • Limit access to sensitive data: Review each agent’s connected applications and permissions, and decide what personal or company information it needs before enabling access [2][9].
