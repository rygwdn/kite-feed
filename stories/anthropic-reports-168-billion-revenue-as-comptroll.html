Anthropic reports $16.8 billion revenue as comptroller urges IPO pause

Category: AI

Anthropic told investors its third-quarter revenue rose 45% to $16.8 billion, while New York City Comptroller Mark Levine urged the company to delay its planned U.S. stock-market debut until it provides greater transparency and addresses AI safety concerns [5][18][11][28]. The company said it posted a second consecutive quarter of adjusted profit and reported an operating margin of 11%, though executives warned that planned spending on computing capacity and biomedical research could affect profitability [27][28]. SemiAnalysis estimated Anthropic’s annualized revenue at about $74 billion, up from roughly $65 billion at the end of July [27]. Anthropic is preparing for a November IPO, with reports putting its potential valuation as high as $2 trillion, but the listing plan could change [27]. Levine’s call followed an investor presentation by CEO Dario Amodei and finance chief Krishna Rao in San Francisco on Wednesday [5][28].

Key Points

  • Year-end pace: Analysts who met with Anthropic leaders said its annualized revenue could reach $110 billion by year-end [5][28].
  • Cash generation: Anthropic reported positive free cash flow, meaning it took in more cash than it spent [27].
  • IPO banking: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley were reported to be leading the planned offering [27].
  • Slowing growth: Revenue had more than doubled between the first and second quarters, but the latest results fell short of some investors’ expectations [27][28].

Anthropic leadership: At the investor meeting, Amodei emphasized the company’s focus on AI safety and avoided discussing threats of human extinction [28].

  • Compute revenue ratio: The company reported earning about $1.70 in revenue per dollar spent on computing power, a metric tied to the cost of delivering AI services [27].
  • Custom chip development: Anthropic told investors it is developing a specially tailored chip for its own operations [28].

Historical Background

Anthropic’s revenue rose twelvefold in 2025 to nearly $4.6 billion, but it recorded an operating loss of more than $8 billion that year [28]. A draft prospectus described in September reporting put the 2025 loss at $42 billion, including $34 billion in accounting costs related to earlier fundraising rounds [27]. The company confidentially filed IPO paperwork with U.S. regulators in June 2026, but had not published a full prospectus by October 15 [27].
