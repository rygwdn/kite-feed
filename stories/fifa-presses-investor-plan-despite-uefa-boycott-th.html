FIFA presses investor plan despite UEFA boycott threat

Category: World

FIFA said Friday it would continue an “open and democratic” consultation on President Gianni Infantino’s proposal to bring private investors into a new commercial structure for its competitions. It said “nobody is selling football” after UEFA threatened to boycott FIFA events if the plan remains active [3][6][17][52]. UEFA’s 55 member associations voted Thursday to refuse participation in FIFA competitions unless FIFA fully abandons the proposal and gives binding assurances against private ownership of its governance or competitions [7][12][18][44]. The proposal centers on FIFA Forward Enterprise, a commercial subsidiary valued at about $20 billion that would handle major event business operations, with private investors able to buy a minority stake. Reports said the plan could raise up to $4.2 billion [6][22][25][33]. FIFA has tied the plan to increased funding for its 211 member associations, with sources describing possible payments of $20 million per association in early 2027 and higher funding allocations for the 2027-2030 cycle [4][22][25][33]. CONCACAF’s 41 members also rejected the proposal Thursday, citing concerns over due process, the short deadline and lack of review by FIFA governance bodies [14][17][21][46]. The Asian Football Confederation warned the plan would not succeed without support from all six regional blocs, while Semafor reported that FIFA’s Asia chief called it “unacceptable” [26][30][5].

Key Points

  • Vote arithmetic: Infantino needs 106 of FIFA’s 211 member associations to approve the proposal, while UEFA and CONCACAF together account for 96 votes [17][3].
  • Next test: The first senior competition affected by UEFA’s stance could be the Women’s World Cup play-offs in October, with England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland among teams involved [18][19].
  • Investor link: The proposed investor group is expected to be led by Thrive Eternal, linked to Thrive Capital founder Joshua Kushner, whose brother Jared Kushner is President Trump’s son-in-law [6][14][15].
  • Commercial scope: FIFA Forward Enterprise would consolidate commercial and event operations, including broadcast, sponsorship, ticketing and licensing contracts for FIFA competitions [6][27][38].

Perspectives

FIFA: FIFA said incorrect reports had disrupted its consultation, that every member association should vote based on facts and that FFE would not be established without member support.

Technical Details

  • Non-controlling stake: FIFA described the outside investment as minority and non-controlling, while UEFA argued any ownership interest would still create shareholder pressure over calendars, formats and other competition decisions [18][4][35].
  • Governance path: A FIFA spokesperson said the proposal would be presented to the 211 member associations and the 37-member FIFA Council, which would make the final decision [56].

Industry Impact

  • Women’s competitions: UEFA’s stance could affect the 2027 Women’s World Cup in Brazil, the next senior women's tournament after the 2026 World Cup [20][26].
  • Youth tournaments: The Women’s Under-20 World Cup hosted by Poland from September 5 is the next scheduled FIFA tournament in Europe [52][19].
  • Host planning: Spain and Portugal are co-hosts of the 2030 men’s World Cup, while the four British federations are FIFA’s only bidder for the 2035 Women’s World Cup [26][52].
