Microsoft names Asha Sharma gaming CEO, vows limits

Category: Technology

Microsoft has tapped Asha Sharma—previously president of the company’s CoreAI product group—to lead its gaming organization after Phil Spencer’s retirement. The leadership shift also coincided with the departure of Xbox president Sarah Bond [6][5][8][1][7]. Sharma has framed the change as a push to prioritize “great games,” stressing human-led creativity while noting that AI has long been part of game development [6][3]. In early messaging and interviews, Sharma drew a firm line on AI use. She said she has “no tolerance for bad AI,” and promised Microsoft won’t chase “short-term efficiency” by flooding its ecosystem with low-quality, automated content [6][3]. The reaction online has been split: some fans question her gaming background, while others argue strong leaders can succeed by backing great creative teams—especially as the industry goes through another turbulent stretch [7][6][8]. The timing also matters for Xbox’s business strategy. Microsoft still faces questions about console momentum and how its services are performing; recent reporting pointed to declines in Xbox hardware and overall gaming revenue in the latest holiday quarter [8].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Industry headwinds: Fast Company cited a recent holiday-quarter drop of 32% in Xbox hardware revenue year over year, along with a 9% decline in overall gaming revenue and a 5% decline in Xbox content and services [8].
  • None
  • Trust message: In remarks reported by Variety and relayed by Slashdot, Sharma said she’s entering gaming as a “platform builder” and wants to earn trust through consistency, grounding decisions in community feedback [3].
  • Bond’s response: Mashable reported former Xbox president Sarah Bond publicly addressed her surprise departure after the shake-up [2].

Perspectives

Asha Sharma (Microsoft Gaming): She noted that AI has been used in games for years, but argued that the “great stories” players remember are created by humans. She also said she has “no tolerance for bad AI” and warned Microsoft won’t chase “short-term efficiency” by flooding the ecosystem with “soulless AI slop,” even as the line between helpful tools and “bad AI” remains contentious [6].

Xbox fan community: The BBC described a divided online reaction, with some posts predicting dire outcomes for Xbox and others pushing back on gatekeeping over who is “qualified” to lead [7].
