AI infrastructure sell-off puts data-center returns under scrutiny

Category: AI

South Korean data-center operator Elice fell as much as 33% on its Seoul trading debut. The decline followed the failed listing of Australian data-center developer Firmus, whose proposed valuation had at one point exceeded Telstra’s before it had delivered its promised earnings [48][6]. AI-linked shares rebounded Monday after recent losses [18][25]. Anthropic reported third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion, up 45% from the previous quarter, compared with nearly 60% growth from the first to second quarter. Analysts cited competition and falling prices as potential pressures on revenue growth [25]. Hourly rental prices for Nvidia’s A100 chips fell from $3.10 in 2021 to $1.50 in 2025, while data-center operators still must repay infrastructure financing [2]. Meta and Tesla are scheduled to report earnings Wednesday, Oct. 21, with investors looking for details on AI spending and returns [39][32].

Key Points

  • Debt structure: The IMF reviewed 14 AI-related transactions since 2023 involving about $300 billion in equity and $650 billion in debt, tied to $1.4 trillion in future revenue commitments [9].
  • Provider exposure: Ten publicly listed neocloud companies had borrowed a combined $100 billion—about three times their annual revenue—and only two were profitable, Kathimerini reported [37].
  • Revenue reporting: OpenAI reportedly cut its annualized recurring-revenue figure by $20 billion from a previously reported level near $70 billion; the measure is a projection and can vary depending on how sales are counted [25].
  • Hardware risk: Dario Perkins, managing director of global macro at Global Data.TS Lombard, warned that chip makers' orders for extra semiconductors to guard against shortages could create a glut if demand slows, given the hardware sector's cyclical nature [27].

Perspectives

Dario Perkins, managing director of global macro at Global Data.TS Lombard: Perkins argued that much of the AI industry's revenue growth may come from spending within the AI ecosystem rather than from outside businesses and consumers paying for AI services, raising the risk of companies recycling capital spending as revenue [27].
