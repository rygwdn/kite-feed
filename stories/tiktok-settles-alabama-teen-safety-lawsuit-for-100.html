TikTok settles Alabama teen safety lawsuit for $100 million

Category: Technology

TikTok and its parent company, ByteDance, agreed Sept. 25 to pay Alabama at least $100 million and change features for teen users, settling the state’s lawsuit before a jury trial scheduled to begin Sept. 28 [4][2][21][9]. Alabama alleged that the platform was designed to keep young users engaged and that TikTok misled parents about safeguards against harmful content. The settlement resolves Alabama’s claims [20][29]. The agreement sets a two-hour daily use limit for teens in Alabama and requires additional safety measures. The state described it as the first settlement between TikTok and a U.S. state in litigation over social media’s effects on teen wellbeing [13][21][1].

Primary Source: View original article

Tonight, they can rest easier knowing real protections are in place to shield their children from the dangers of social media addiction.

— Steve Marshall, Alabama attorney general (The Guardian)

Key Points

  • Additional safeguards: The agreement calls for pauses after 15, 60 and 90 minutes of use, blocks access from midnight to 6 a.m., and limits use during school hours [5][17][13].
  • Teen account changes: TikTok must strengthen age checks, restrict adult contact with teen accounts, ban cosmetic filters for teens and offer a non-personalized feed [5][12][17].
  • Payment schedule: Alabama’s first payment is due within 45 days; the state could receive up to $300 million if 40 other states reach qualifying agreements within the set period [5][21][19].
  • Wider litigation: At least 27 other states and Washington, D.C., have filed similar youth-safety lawsuits against TikTok [21][29].

Perspectives

A spokesperson for TikTok’s U.S. operations said the agreement builds on the company’s commitment to strengthen safety tools for teens [2][21].

Sources: TechCrunch

Timeline

  • 2025: Alabama filed a lawsuit accusing TikTok of harming young users [3][21]
  • August 2026: TikTok agreed to a $400 million federal settlement over child privacy claims [2]
  • 25 September 2026: Alabama announced the settlement days before the trial was due to begin [13][20]

Industry Impact

  • Social media litigation: The deal follows Meta’s agreement with states over similar claims. Meta’s settlement was reported at up to $18 billion and included platform safety provisions [3][1][24].
