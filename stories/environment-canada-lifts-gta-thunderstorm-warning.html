Environment Canada lifts GTA thunderstorm warning after severe-weather alerts

Category: Canada

Environment Canada lifted a thunderstorm warning for the Greater Toronto Area on Saturday and downgraded an air quality warning after earlier alerts for severe weather and smoke-related risks [3]. The agency had warned that thunderstorms could bring wind gusts up to 110 km/h, quarter- to ping-pong-ball-size hail and rainfall totals up to 40 millimetres as a cold front over Michigan pushed a line of storms southeast across the region [3]. Toronto’s Air Quality Health Index was listed at 4, 5 or 6, or “moderate risk,” at 5 p.m. Saturday. Ottawa’s Saturday AQHI reading was 7, or “High Risk,” as a yellow alert for possible severe thunderstorms continued into the evening in the capital region [3][2].

Key Points

  • Forecast uncertainty: Rain-cooled air and wildfire smoke made the intensity and extent of Ontario’s Saturday storm threat harder to forecast than usual [5].
  • Storm mechanics: The Weather Network forecast described high shear, helicity and CAPE from London to Cornwall, with embedded rotation creating a brief tornado risk near lake-breeze boundaries [5].
  • Affected corridor: Communities watching for severe weather included Prince Edward County from Cobourg to Kingston and areas from St. Marys to Lake Erie [5].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Environment Canada urged residents to stay indoors if they heard thunder during the GTA storm threat [3]. Move indoors when thunder is audible.
  • Driving: Environment Canada warned of difficult driving conditions as storms capable of strong winds, hail and heavy rain moved through the region [3]. Delay or slow travel during heavy storms.

Action Items

  • Enable emergency alerts: Keep phone emergency alerts active because some Halton and Peel residents received tornado-warning alerts before the warning ended [3].
  • Check hourly AQHI: Use Environment Canada’s local AQHI readings before outdoor exercise or events when wildfire smoke affects southern Ontario [3][2].
