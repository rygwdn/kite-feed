Fossil studies detail ancient animals from seven sites

Category: Science

Recent paleontology studies and reports described fossil discoveries and reanalyses, including a 125-million-year-old crocodylomorph from Catalonia, a 324-million-year-old insect relative from West Texas, 130-million-year-old mammal footprints in Brazil and 452-million-year-old crinoid tube feet from Quebec [1][2][7][8][6]. Researchers used ultraviolet light on the Montsecosuchus depereti specimen to detect traces of skin, possible color patterning, sensory structures and chest cartilage; cross-polarized light helped identify Chosha praecursor as an insectlike animal with six thoracic legs and 18 abdominal legs, rather than a juvenile crustacean [1][2][7]. Other reports described fossil specimens or traces with new identifications: researchers named an incomplete skull in the Natural History Museum of Denmark as a new glyptodont species, Glyptotherium deuterophractum; attributed Brazilian trackways to a mammal about 1.55 meters long; and said retreating ice at Chile’s Tyndall Glacier exposed more than 90 Early Cretaceous ichthyosaur skeletons, including one preserving a fetus [5][8][9]. A separate French report said a 110-million-year-old pliosaur vertebra was found during the draining of Lac d’Orient [10].

Did you know: Before the Quebec discovery, researchers had described tube-foot traces in only one specimen in the 485-million-year crinoid fossil record [6].

Key Points

  • Museum reanalysis: The glyptodont holotype skull was collected by Peter Wilhelm Lund at Brazil’s Lagoa Santa caves in the 19th century [5].
  • Quebec preservation: The two Dendrocrinus simcoensis fossils came from Saint-Joachim Quarry northeast of Quebec City [6].
  • Patagonia sample: The ichthyosaur material includes newborn, juvenile and adult individuals, giving researchers specimens across different life stages [9].

Perspectives

Erik Tihelka: The University of Cambridge paleoentomologist reexamined the Chosha fossil after seeing an old line drawing in a secondhand Russian textbook and questioning its crustacean identification.

Glyptotherium study authors: Édison Vicente Oliveira and co-authors compared cranial features, teeth, ankle bones and osteoderms from Brazil and Venezuela with known North and Central American glyptodonts before naming the new species.

Sources: Sci.News

Ichthyosaur research team: Specialists from Argentina, Chile, Germany and the United Kingdom examined the Tyndall Glacier assemblage for evidence on anatomy, feeding, reproduction and death.

Sources: MDZ Online

Timeline

  • 1875: Reinhardt classified the Danish glyptodont skull under another name [5].
  • c. 1900: The Montsecosuchus depereti specimen was discovered at Pedrera de Meià, Catalonia [1].
  • 1985: The Chosha fossil was collected near Rough Creek Ranch, Texas, and initially interpreted as a juvenile of Tesnusocaris goldichi [2].
  • 1990s: The Montsecosuchus specimen received limited study before ultraviolet imaging [1].
  • 28 August 2026: Glyptotherium deuterophractum was reported as a newly named species [5].
  • 29 August 2026: Quebec crinoids were reported with the oldest known tube feet [6].

Scientific Significance

  • Body-plan evolution: The Chosha fossil adds evidence for early insect relatives before the modern six-legged insect body plan became fixed, with traits that suggest a stage in the transition from aquatic, crustacean-like ancestors to terrestrial insects [2][7].
  • Functional anatomy: The Montsecosuchus study provides the first detailed soft-tissue anatomy for an early crocodylomorph, showing that sensory structures and chest cartilage like those of modern crocodilians had already evolved by 125 million years ago; the Quebec crinoids' tube feet have a short, widely spaced arrangement unknown in living species, pointing to an extinct feeding posture [1][6].
  • Evidence limits: The Brazilian mammal interpretation is based entirely on footprints rather than skeletal remains, although the researchers cited proportions, not just size, as the reason for identifying the trackmaker as a mammal [8].
