CrowdStrike-Zscaler deal targets AI agents with unified network security

Category: Technology

The proposed CrowdStrike-Zscaler combination would link endpoint sensors with controls over connections among users, applications and AI agents, showing security teams where agents operate and which systems they can reach [3][14]. CrowdStrike agreed on Nov. 23 to acquire cloud-security provider Zscaler in a $43.9 billion all-stock deal [6][10]. CrowdStrike plans to bring Zscaler’s network telemetry into its Falcon security platform, combining device and network data in a single dataset and using it to improve the company’s SafeMind security models [14][23]. The companies say the goal is to help organizations control AI agents’ access to corporate systems; Zscaler CEO Jay Chaudhry has described agents as a potential security weak link [16]. The companies already have 16 product integrations but plan to operate separately until the deal closes [6][21]. The transaction still needs Zscaler shareholder approval and regulatory clearances, with closing expected in the second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal 2028 [16][17].

Security used to be about keeping the adversary out. Today, safety is required to let AI in.

Key Points

  • Product overlap: Both companies offer protections for AI applications, including against prompt-injection threats, and compete in secure-browser products [23].
  • Zero-trust checks: Zscaler’s approach verifies users and devices when they seek access to internal resources, limiting the damage from stolen credentials [3][19].
  • Quarterly results: CrowdStrike’s fiscal third-quarter revenue rose 28% year over year to $1.58 billion, while Zscaler reported first-quarter revenue of $949.3 million, up 20% [30][4].
  • Deal economics: CrowdStrike forecasts about $1 billion in annualized revenue synergies within 30 months of closing and $500 million in annualized cost savings within 18 to 24 months [16][13].
  • Customer overlap: The companies already share about 2,000 customer accounts [28][10].
  • Stock reaction: CrowdStrike shares fell nearly 9% on the announcement day while Zscaler closed 17.7% higher at $229.24; Bernstein and Jefferies subsequently cut their Zscaler ratings [26][31][32].
  • Partner channels: The companies plan to sell the combined offering through global system integrators, resellers, distributors and cloud marketplaces. They also identify managed-security providers as a growth channel [21].
  • Existing customer base: The companies have about 2,000 shared customers, providing a base for cross-selling [21][6].
  • Market scale: The combined companies had a market value above $265 billion at the prior Friday’s close, compared with $282 billion for cybersecurity rival Palo Alto Networks [28].

Technical Details

  • Network footprint: Zscaler’s Zero Trust Exchange is distributed across more than 200 public data centers and thousands of private sites, supporting connection inspection across geographically dispersed deployments [8][15].
  • AI-agent identity: Monitoring an agent’s device activity and inspecting its network destination do not establish its identity or exact permissions, a gap that Frank Dickson, founder of Dickson Research, said remains important [38].
  • Endpoint and network telemetry: The proposed integration would link Falcon’s visibility into device activity with Zscaler’s inspection of network traffic destinations. The companies say they intend to combine those signals in CrowdStrike’s Next-Gen SIEM [38].
  • AI-agent protection: CrowdStrike’s Falcon Guardian, designed to secure AI agents across an enterprise, passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue in its first year after the quarter ended [14].
  • SafeMind capabilities: CrowdStrike says SafeMind tools are intended not only to find software vulnerabilities but also to develop detections and automated workflows for spotting hacking attempts [23].
  • Existing integrations: The companies already have 16 product integrations, and some customers send Zscaler data into CrowdStrike’s Next-Gen SIEM [38].

Historical Background

About two and a half years before the deal announcement, a flawed CrowdStrike software update caused an IT outage affecting millions of Windows PCs, servers and other equipment, according to the Financial Times. CrowdStrike shares fell by more than a third in the two weeks after the incident. By January 2025, the company had recovered the roughly $30 billion in market value it lost [9].

Business Angle

  • Zscaler CEO Jay Chaudhry and affiliates, who hold about 34% of its outstanding shares, have entered voting agreements supporting the transaction [20].

Q&A

Q: What will Zscaler shareholders receive under the offer?

A: Each Zscaler share would be exchanged for 1.135 CrowdStrike shares, valuing it at about $260.41 per share based on CrowdStrike’s Nov. 20 closing price, a roughly 34% premium to Zscaler’s closing price that day [6][10].

Q: What happens if Zscaler seeks another buyer or withdraws before closing?

A: Zscaler cannot solicit other bids under the agreement and may owe a $1.4 billion break fee if it exits in specified circumstances; termination rights apply after Nov. 23, 2027, subject to regulatory-delay provisions [32].
