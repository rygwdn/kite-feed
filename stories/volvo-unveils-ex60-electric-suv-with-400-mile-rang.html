Volvo unveils EX60 electric SUV with 400-mile range

Category: Technology

Volvo unveiled the EX60, a new mid-size electric SUV positioned between the EX30 and EX90, as the brand’s dedicated EV entry in the popular XC60-sized segment [1][11][9]. Across three powertrain trims (P6, P10, P12), Volvo says the lineup targets long range, very fast DC charging, and a more software-centric cabin experience driven by high-end computing hardware and onboard AI [1][11][6]. The EX60 also aims to make EV ownership easier by adding a factory NACS charge port for the US market while keeping familiar smartphone ecosystems through CarPlay, plus deeper native audio and assistant integrations [1][4][9]. Reviewers note the approach looks promising on paper, while also pointing out Volvo’s recent history of EV software issues as a key “wait and see” factor [11].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Ars notes the EX60 is Volvo’s first US model sold with a built-in NACS port from the factory [1].

Key Points

  • Trim breakdown: Volvo detailed three variants: rear-drive P6 (369 hp) plus dual-motor AWD P10 (503 hp) and P12 (670 hp), with Engadget listing curb weights that rise with battery size (about 4,663 lbs for P6 and 5,137 lbs for P12) [1][11].
  • Charging hardware: The EX60 becomes Volvo’s first US-market model with a built-in NACS port, aiming to simplify access to compatible fast-charging infrastructure without adapters [1][6].
  • Cross Country reveal: Volvo also introduced an EX60 Cross Country variant with more outdoorsy styling and a taller suspension, expanding the EV lineup beyond city-focused trims [8].
  • Cabin ecosystems: Volvo says the EX60 will ship with a pre-installed Apple Music app that supports Dolby Atmos (Spatial Audio) through the car’s native infotainment, while still offering wireless Apple CarPlay and iPhone/Apple Watch-based Digital Key Plus [4][9].
  • Production timing: Ars reports EX60 production is scheduled to start in April 2026, indicating a runway of several months before ramp-up [1].

Perspectives

Volvo (product positioning): Volvo frames the EX60 as a high-tech, mid-size EV built for efficiency and everyday practicality in the brand’s core SUV segment, sitting between the EX30 and EX90 in its lineup.

Sources: CNET Roadshow

Tech-focused reviewers: Review outlets highlight the EX60’s emphasis on onboard computing and AI (Google Gemini plus Nvidia/Qualcomm hardware), treating the vehicle as a software-defined platform as much as a new SUV.

Sources: Engadget

Cautious shoppers: Some consumer coverage argues buyers may want to wait for early real-world results, citing potential first-year “teething pains” as Volvo rolls out a new EV platform and technology stack.

Sources: SlashGear

Technical Details

  • SPA3 platform: The EX60 debuts Volvo’s SPA3 EV architecture, a dedicated platform intended to underpin this next generation of Volvo electric vehicles [5][1].
  • Megacasting: Volvo says the EX60 uses “megacasting” manufacturing techniques, a method of producing large structural castings to reduce parts count and simplify assembly [2].
  • Onboard AI assistant: Volvo integrates Google’s Gemini for natural-language vehicle control, running on in-car computing hardware sourced from Nvidia and Qualcomm [4][1][6].

Industry Impact

  • Fast-charging race: By advertising very high peak DC charging capability (up to 400 kW) and sub-20-minute 10–80% claims on compatible hardware, Volvo adds pressure on rivals to improve charging curves and thermal management, not just battery size [1][5].
  • In-car software: The EX60’s mix of Google Gemini, high-performance chips, and first-party media apps (like Apple Music with Atmos) reflects a broader shift toward “software-defined vehicles,” where infotainment and AI assistants become key differentiators [4][11][7].
  • Connector shift: Volvo’s move to a built-in NACS port for the US market aligns with the industry trend toward a common charging connector, which can reduce adapter reliance and simplify charging choices over time [1][6][common].

Historical Background

Volvo has been expanding its EX-branded EV lineup, with the smaller EX30 and larger EX90 arriving ahead of this mid-size model [11]. Reviewers note the EX90’s rollout faced software issues, which has increased attention on whether Volvo can execute a smoother, more reliable software launch for the EX60 [11].

Q&A

Q: What does Volvo’s 112 mph speed limit mean in practice?

A: Ars reports Volvo continues its policy of electronically limiting top speed to 112 mph (180 km/h) on the EX60, which caps maximum speed regardless of powertrain output [1].

Q: How does wheel size affect the EX60’s efficiency?

A: Ars reports the P6’s EPA-estimated range changes with wheel size, dropping when moving from 20-inch to 21- or 22-inch wheels [1].

Q: Why are some outlets quoting different range numbers?

A: Ars notes US outlets typically cite EPA test-cycle range while some non-US publications use Europe’s WLTP cycle, which often produces different estimates [1].

Action Items

  • Match trims to your charging reality: If you road-trip often, compare Volvo’s stated 10–80% charging times and peak rates by variant, then check whether chargers on your routes can actually deliver 320–400 kW before choosing a configuration [1][5].
  • Decide on music integration: If you use Apple Music, consider whether you prefer the built-in Apple Music app (with Dolby Atmos) or wireless CarPlay, since Volvo says both are supported on EX60 [4].
