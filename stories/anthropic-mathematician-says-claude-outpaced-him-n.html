Anthropic mathematician says Claude outpaced him near proof's finish

Category: AI

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says the company’s Claude AI took a larger role as they worked on a claimed proof of the Riemann hypothesis, a famous problem about prime numbers. Alpöge said he guided the effort by combining two mathematical ideas, but Claude moved faster than he did near the end. Separate Lean and Mathlib groups have since verified the formalized proof [1][17]. The collaboration lasted about six weeks, from early October to Nov. 16, according to Alpöge’s account [6]. He has not yet published a readable mathematical explanation, leaving researchers to work out the argument behind the computer-checked result [29][8]. The announcement came a day after Anthropic’s stock-market debut, prompting questions about its timing. Alpöge called the timing “horrible” and said he would have finished earlier if he could. He denied that the release was coordinated with the listing [17][29].

Did you know: Before the Riemann claim, Alpöge had used Claude while posting a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, another longstanding mathematics problem [3].

Key Points

  • A specific advance: While extending Stepanov’s method, Claude proposed setting the theta-lift chain length to √X, which Alpöge said helped move the work forward [1].
  • Scope: Alpöge said the proof also covers the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions [8].
  • Company process: Alpöge said he consulted Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei before publishing [10].

Perspectives

Nestor Guillen, New York University mathematics professor: Guillen said Alpöge’s work was a more human-directed collaboration than OpenAI’s Navier–Stokes announcement, with AI working “more like a tool” rather than appearing as an autonomous authority [10].
