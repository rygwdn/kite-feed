US Senate passes Russia sanctions bill 86-11

Category: World

The U.S. Senate voted 86-11 on Aug. 7 to pass the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, sending a sanctions package aimed at Russia’s energy revenues to the House of Representatives [18][5][2][19]. The bill would target Russian officials, oligarchs, financial institutions and energy revenue, and would authorize President Trump to impose tariffs of up to 100% on goods from major buyers of Russian oil and gas, including China and India [5][9][10][20]. Lawmakers named the measure for the late Republican Sen. Lindsey Graham, who had pushed for tougher Russia measures before his death in July. The package also includes expanded Iran sanctions sought by President Trump [2][21][23][22]. Because Congress is in summer recess, the House will not vote until at least early September. The final Senate bill followed negotiations with the White House that left tariff decisions to the president [6][9][10][26].

Key Points

  • House hurdles: Some lawmakers warned that the tariff authority could raise costs for U.S. importers and consumers, while Republicans hold slim majorities in both chambers [21][23][37].
  • Tariff design: The measure can target the five largest Russian crude oil or natural gas buyers and the five countries most active in helping Russia evade energy sanctions [26][57][53].
  • Iran provision: The bill would extend the Iran Sanctions Act of 1996 until 2031, preserving penalties tied to Iran’s energy sector [22][33][42].
  • Vote split: Sen. Rand Paul was the only Republican senator to vote no, joining independent Bernie Sanders and nine progressive Democrats in opposition [7][56][17].

Technical Details

  • Presidential waiver: The negotiated bill includes waiver authority that allows the White House to waive sanctions in certain circumstances, leaving implementation partly dependent on presidential decisions [10][48].
  • Top-five trigger: The tariff mechanism focuses on the top five Russian energy importers and can be recalculated over time, rather than applying automatically to all Russian energy buyers [59][57].
  • Gas exemption: The bill allows an exemption for countries that import less than 15% of Russia’s total natural gas exports and are taking steps to reduce those purchases [36][45][59].

Industry Impact

  • Indian energy trade: India accounts for 36% to 38% of Russia’s oil exports, making it a central potential target under tariff provisions aimed at major Russian energy buyers [4].
  • Russian oil shipping: The package targets the so-called shadow fleet, including older or reflagged tankers used to evade restrictions on Moscow’s oil exports [10][27][45].
  • U.S. import costs: Lawmakers warned that new tariff powers could raise costs for U.S. importers and consumers and create political blowback for Republicans [21][23][37].
  • Iran energy investment: Extending the Iran Sanctions Act until 2031 would continue penalties on companies investing in Iran’s energy sector [33][42].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇦 Ukraine: President Volodymyr Zelenskyy thanked senators for supporting the legislation and wrote that Ukraine was grateful to the Senate and everyone who supports Ukraine [35][58].
  • 🇷🇺 Russia: Russia’s embassy in the United States condemned the bill, saying it did a disservice to the current U.S. administration [45][58].
