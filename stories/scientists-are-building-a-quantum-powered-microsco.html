Scientists are building a quantum-powered microscope for sensitive samples

Category: Science

Researchers in Austria are building an electron microscope integrated with a quantum computer, aiming to extract more information from each electron than conventional instruments. The project involves TU Wien, the University of Vienna, JKU Linz and the University of Innsbruck, and could reduce electron exposure for samples that can be damaged by microscopy [1]. A separate instrument called Curie, associated with the Rosalind Franklin Institute, is being used to study inflammatory bowel disease, including Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis. Researchers say its resolution is about 10 times that of conventional microscopy and could help them examine disease mechanisms and assess whether treatments act on the intended targets [2].

Primary Source: View original article

However, this requires a large number of electrons. And not every sample can be exposed to so many electrons without being damaged.

— Philipp Haslinger (ScienceDaily)

Technical Details

  • Built-in ion traps: The proposed instrument uses ion traps to perform quantum-computing operations within the electron-microscope system [1].
  • Curie resolution: The Curie microscope is reported to have roughly 10 times the resolution of conventional microscopy, allowing researchers to examine biological structures at a finer scale [2].

Scientific Significance

  • Electron-microscopy baseline: Electron microscopes can reveal structures far smaller than those visible with ordinary light [1].
  • Limitations: The quantum-electron microscope remains under construction, and the articles provide neither performance measurements from a completed instrument nor evidence of clinical treatment benefits [1].
