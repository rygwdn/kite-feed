Chinese humanoid robot runs 100 meters in 9.39 seconds

Category: World

A Chinese humanoid robot ran 100 meters in 9.39 seconds in a preliminary heat at the World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing on Saturday, faster than Usain Bolt’s 9.58-second human world record set in 2009 [2][3][4][6][7][8][10][11][12][14][15][22][32][43][50]. Organizers identified the heat winner as Tiangong Ultra, developed by the Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center. It overtook Honor’s Lightning near the finish after falling behind early; Lightning finished in 9.47 seconds [11][12][14][22][30][59]. The second edition of the five-day games opened at Beijing’s National Speed Skating Oval and is scheduled to run from August 22 to August 26 [17][43][34]. Organizers listed 2,056 humanoid robots from 666 teams across 16 countries, with 51 events and 1,301 competitions spanning sports and practical tasks [32][48][33][vg.no]. The games opened in the same week as Beijing’s 2026 World Robot Conference, where companies showcased around 3,000 products, including humanoid robots [8][15][19][1].

Key Points

  • Stopping problems: After the sprint, robots slammed into thick mats beyond the finish line. Tiangong stumbled toward the sidelines, and Lightning was carried off after collapsing [14][22][31][50].
  • Robot mishaps: Some robots tripped, fell, broke apart or caught fire during competitions [2].
  • Record status: Bolt’s 9.58-second mark remains the official human 100m world record recognized by World Athletics because robot performances took place under different conditions and rules [17][11].
  • Test pace: Before the official heats, Chinese state media said Honor’s Lightning ran 100 metres in 9.32 seconds in a preparatory test [18][17][27][36].
  • Event design: The program includes 51 events and 1,301 competitions, with newer contests such as weightlifting, tug-of-war and table tennis testing physical structures and core components [32][48].
  • Jump mark: A separate humanoid robot reached 2.88 meters in a standing high jump, exceeding Javier Sotomayor’s 2.45-meter human world record from 1993 and last year’s 0.95-meter humanoid benchmark [2][4][6][37].
  • Jump mark: A humanoid robot reached 2.88 metres in a standing high jump, above the 2.45-metre human high-jump record set by Cuba’s Javier Sotomayor in 1993 [6][2][37].
  • Test run: Chinese state media said Honor’s Lightning ran 100 meters in 9.32 seconds during a preparatory test, reaching a peak speed of 14.5 meters per second [17][18][27][24][36].
