India’s Modi addresses Israeli Knesset, vows deeper ties

Category: World

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi used a two-day visit to Israel to underscore India’s support after Hamas’s October 7, 2023 attack. In Jerusalem, he addressed the Knesset and met Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu [1][2][9][6][11][22]. Modi offered condolences for those killed on October 7 and framed India’s stance as part of a broader “zero tolerance” approach to terrorism. He also said that “no cause justifies killing civilians,” and voiced support for efforts aimed at durable peace and regional stability [1][22][6]. Indian coverage also described him as backing a Gaza peace plan [8][19]. Alongside the security messaging, the trip put practical cooperation front and center. Modi and Netanyahu discussed expanding trade and defence ties, and Modi pointed to opportunities in high-tech areas such as quantum technologies, semiconductors, and artificial intelligence [1][12][7]. The visit also featured public displays of warmth—Netanyahu greeted Modi at the airport and praised their relationship in the Knesset—while some outlets noted the trip has drawn criticism and protests tied to the war in Gaza and allegations of genocide [1][2][3].

We feel your pain, we share your grief. India stands with Israel firmly with full conviction in this moment and beyond.

— Narendra Modi (Dawn)

Key Points

  • Parliamentary honour: Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana awarded Modi the newly established Medal of the Knesset, crediting his “significant contributions to the State of Israel and the Jewish people” [24][18].
  • Tech diplomacy: After the Knesset address, Modi and Netanyahu visited a technology exhibition together; Modi also posted a photo of the two riding in the same car [10][17][16].
  • Commemoration visit: Indian outlets said Modi planned to visit Yad Vashem, Israel’s official Holocaust memorial in Jerusalem, as part of his day-two schedule [4][15].
  • Terror framing: Indian coverage highlighted Modi’s “zero tolerance” message on terrorism, including his call for “no double standard” and references to the broader global impact of terror attacks [6][19].
  • Day-two agenda: The Hindu reported Modi was set to meet President Isaac Herzog and engage with members of the Indian-Jewish community [4].

Perspectives

Benjamin Netanyahu (Israel): Netanyahu praised Modi in the Knesset as “more than a friend, a brother.” Other coverage focused on the visit’s personal diplomacy, including Netanyahu’s remarks about the “Modi hug” [2][14].

Sources: DW, NDTV

Narendra Modi (India): Modi said India follows an uncompromising “zero tolerance” policy on terrorism, with “no double standard.” Indian coverage said he referenced past attacks such as 26/11 to argue for coordinated action [6][19].

Sources: The Hindu, Firstpost

Critics and protesters: RT said the state visit unfolded amid protests over Gaza and allegations of genocide, portraying the trip as drawing public backlash [3].

Sources: RT

Timeline

  • 7 October 2023: Hamas attacks Israel; Modi later cites condolences [1][9].
  • 25 February 2026: Modi arrives in Israel and meets Netanyahu [1][7].
  • 25 February 2026: Modi addresses the Knesset; condemns October 7 attack [1][2][6].
  • 26 February 2026: Indian media say Modi visits Yad Vashem [4][15].

Historical Background

India established full diplomatic relations with Israel in 1992, after decades of limited official engagement [common]. Modi became the first Indian prime minister to visit Israel in 2017, a trip that helped normalize high-level, public diplomacy between the two countries [common].
