Supreme Court hears challenge to Canada’s ban on 2,500 firearm types

Category: Canada

The Supreme Court of Canada began hearing a challenge Monday to the federal ban on more than 2,500 types of firearms the government classifies as assault-style weapons. The appeal follows two lower-court defeats for gun owners, advocacy groups and businesses challenging cabinet’s authority to prohibit the guns through regulations. The ban includes some firearms used for hunting and sport shooting [1][4][2]. The government prohibited 1,500 models and variants, including the AR-15 and Ruger Mini-14, in May 2020 and later expanded the list. Federal lawyers argue cabinet acted reasonably to address public-safety risks, while appellants say the regulations exceeded the authority Parliament granted and failed to account for lawful sporting and hunting uses [3][6]. The hearings continue Tuesday, and a decision is expected by the end of 2027 at the latest. The possession amnesty runs until 90 days after the court rules [1][4].

Key Points

  • Buyback payments: By mid-2026, Ottawa had paid businesses $96 million for about 74,000 guns, averaging roughly $1,300 per weapon [4].
  • Ownership estimate: Federal submissions estimate that 3% to 5% of Canadian firearms owners owned firearms prohibited in 2020 and say thousands of other firearms remain available for hunting and sport shooting [6][3].
  • Self-represented litigants: The case has four challengers, including several people arguing without lawyers, and five other gun groups also oppose the ban [4].

Perspectives

Canadian Coalition for Firearms Rights and other appellants: The Coalition says the regulations were too vague to fit cabinet’s powers, warning that an ambiguous standard would leave people subject to “standardless whim” [4].

Alberta, Saskatchewan and Ontario: The provinces argue the federal government overstepped its powers; Alberta says hunting and shooting sports fall within provincial jurisdiction, while Ontario explicitly calls the regulations unconstitutional [4].

Canadian Coalition for Gun Control: The group supports Ottawa’s position that the Criminal Code permits cabinet to prohibit firearms by regulation, citing public safety and the ban’s consistency with the rule of law [4].

  • October 2023: Federal Court judge rejected a challenge to the ban [3].

  • March 2026: Supreme Court agreed to hear the firearms-ban appeals [4].
  • 9 November 2026: Supreme Court began hearing arguments on the ban’s validity [1].

Historical Background

Ottawa used its regulatory powers rather than legislation to impose the ban. A Liberal government attempt to codify the prohibited firearms in law failed in 2023 [4].
