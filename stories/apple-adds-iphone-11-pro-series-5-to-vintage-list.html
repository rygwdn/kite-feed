Apple adds iPhone 11 Pro, Series 5 to vintage list

Category: Technology

Apple updated its service-status list on December 31, adding the iPhone 11 Pro, Apple Watch Series 5, and the final Intel-based 13-inch MacBook Air to its “vintage” category, alongside several other older products [1][2][5][3]. In Apple’s terminology, “vintage” generally means the company stopped distributing the product for sale more than five years ago, and repairs may still be available through Apple or authorized providers if parts can be obtained [2]. For many owners, the upbeat takeaway is simple: “vintage” is mostly about repair logistics—not whether the device still works day-to-day—and it can be a helpful nudge to plan ahead for battery or hardware service while parts still exist [5][2].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: MacRumors says the final Intel MacBook Air remained on sale for only about eight months before Apple replaced it with the M1 version [2].

Key Points

  • Which models: MacRumors lists additional newly vintage items including iPhone 8 Plus (128GB), iPad Air 3 (Wi‑Fi + Cellular), and a wide range of Apple Watch Series 5 materials/editions (aluminum, stainless steel, titanium, ceramic, Hermès, and Nike in 40mm/44mm variants) [2].
  • Intel era detail: The newly listed MacBook Air is described as the March 2020 13-inch Retina model that shipped with configurable 1.1GHz dual-core i3, 1.1GHz quad-core i5, or 1.2GHz quad-core i7 Intel processors [2].
  • Transition context: MacRumors notes that this Intel MacBook Air stayed on the market for about eight months before Apple discontinued it when the M1 MacBook Air arrived as part of Apple’s initial Apple silicon launch in November 2020 [2].
  • What changes: AppleInsider emphasizes that the “vintage” shift affects repair eligibility rather than software support or basic usability, framing it mainly as a service-window milestone [5].
  • Beats footnote: MacRumors adds that Apple also moved the 2018 Mickey Mouse 90th anniversary special-edition Beats Solo3 Wireless headphones into a different status, shifting them from vintage classification [2].

Perspectives

Apple (policy definition): Apple considers a device “vintage” once more than five years have passed since it stopped distributing it for sale, and repairs may be offered only while parts remain available.

Timeline

  • March 2020: Apple released the final Intel-based 13-inch MacBook Air [2].
  • November 2020: Apple debuted the M1 MacBook Air, discontinuing the Intel model [2].
  • September: iPhone 11 Pro Max was added to Apple’s vintage list [2].
  • December 31: Apple added iPhone 11 Pro and Watch Series 5 as vintage [1][2][5].

Technical Details

  • Apple “vintage” status: Apple uses “vintage” for products it stopped distributing for sale more than five years ago; repair support can continue, but only when parts remain available [2].
  • Authorized repair channel: For vintage devices, Apple and Apple Authorized Service Providers may still perform repairs, but the limiting factor becomes whether replacement components can be sourced [2].

Industry Impact

  • Repair market: As more popular models enter vintage status, repair outcomes can depend increasingly on parts availability, potentially shifting some demand toward third-party repair, refurbished parts channels, and device trade-in markets [2][common].
  • Enterprise IT planning: Organizations still deploying Intel-era MacBook Airs may treat the vintage designation as a procurement signal, since long-term maintainability can hinge on access to authorized repairs and parts [5][2].
  • Secondhand devices: Clear “vintage” labeling can make the used market more transparent by helping buyers weigh a device’s remaining official repair runway against price, which can reduce uncertainty for budget-conscious consumers [2][common].

Historical Background

Apple publicly tracks older hardware as “vintage” or “obsolete,” a lifecycle practice that helps set expectations for repairs and parts sourcing [1][2]. The Intel-based MacBook Air models sat at the tail end of Apple’s long shift away from Intel processors toward Apple-designed chips, a transition that began in earnest with the first Apple silicon Macs in 2020 [2][common]. Over time, these status changes create a clearer (and often more predictable) runway for customers: first full support, then limited repair options, and eventually no official service once parts disappear [2][common].

Q&A

Q: Which iPhone 8 Plus storage sizes were already vintage before the latest update?

A: MacRumors reports that only the iPhone 8 Plus 128GB was newly added, noting that other capacities were already vintage [2].

Q: Are Wi‑Fi-only iPad Air 3 models included in the vintage update?

A: MacRumors specifies that iPad Air 3 Wi‑Fi + Cellular models were listed as vintage, while Wi‑Fi-only versions were not yet vintage [2].

Q: Does Apple’s “vintage” label automatically end software updates for these devices?

A: AppleInsider describes the change as affecting repair eligibility rather than software support or day-to-day functionality [5].

Action Items

  • Plan any proactive repairs for a newly vintage device: If you rely on an iPhone 11 Pro, Series 5 watch, or the Intel MacBook Air, consider booking battery or hardware service sooner rather than later, since Apple notes vintage repairs depend on parts availability [2].
  • Confirm service eligibility before buying used: When shopping secondhand, check whether a model is listed as vintage, because that status can affect whether Apple or authorized providers can still repair it if parts are scarce [2].
