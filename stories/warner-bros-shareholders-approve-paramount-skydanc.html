Warner Bros. shareholders approve Paramount Skydance $110 billion deal

Category: Business

Warner Bros. Discovery shareholders voted on April 23 to approve the Paramount Skydance deal involving the company, clearing a key hurdle for a transaction that outlets valued at about $110 billion to $111 billion, with some reporting an $81 billion figure tied to the takeover structure [1][2][3][5][6][8][9][10]. The transaction would bring together a wide range of entertainment and news assets — including Warner Bros., HBO Max, CNN, Paramount Pictures, Paramount+, and CBS — under one company, combining some of Hollywood’s biggest film, television, and streaming brands [1][2][7][9]. The vote moves the merger closer to the finish line, but it does not complete the process. The deal still faces regulatory reviews in the United States and abroad, including scrutiny from the U.S. Department of Justice, and several reports said the companies expect it to close in the third quarter of 2026 if approvals come through [2][5][7][8]. For investors, the result carried a mixed message: shareholders backed the strategic combination while separately rejecting, in a nonbinding vote, a proposed executive pay package for Warner Bros. Discovery leaders including CEO David Zaslav [7][10].

Key Points

  • Premium offered: Warner Bros. Discovery’s board unanimously recommended the all-cash transaction, which Quartz said offered shareholders a 147% premium to the company’s unaffected stock price [4].
  • Pay backlash: Investors voted against an executive compensation plan that Business Insider said could have paid David Zaslav up to nearly $887 million, while the Financial Times put the package at about $700 million; the vote was symbolic rather than binding [7][10].

Perspectives

Warner Bros. Discovery board: The board unanimously recommended the all-cash sale, signaling that it sees the offer and premium as attractive for investors.

Timeline

  • 23 April 2026: Shareholders approved the merger at a special meeting [1][5][9]
  • 2026-Q3: The companies expect the deal to close, pending regulatory approvals [5][7]

Industry Impact

  • Streaming: The merger would put HBO Max and Paramount+ inside the same company, potentially reshaping how the combined group bundles content and competes for subscribers [2][7].
  • News media: CNN and CBS could end up under one corporate parent, extending the deal’s reach beyond entertainment and into the U.S. television news business [2][7].

Historical Background

Warner Bros. Discovery itself was formed in 2022 through the merger of WarnerMedia and Discovery [common]. Paramount has spent years dealing with the same industry pressures facing its peers, especially the shift from cable television to streaming platforms [common]. This proposed tie-up fits a broader media-industry pattern of consolidation as companies chase larger content libraries, stronger streaming scale, and cost savings in a tougher advertising and subscriber environment [common].

  • Watch governance votes: Read proxy and annual-meeting materials for companies you own to see whether pay packages align with performance, as this vote showed that merger support and pay skepticism can diverge [7][10].
