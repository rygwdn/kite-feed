NASA releases Psyche spacecraft time-lapse of Mars flyby

Category: Science

NASA released a time-lapse made from images taken by its Psyche spacecraft during a monthlong Mars flyby in May, when the asteroid-bound probe used the planet’s gravity to adjust its course toward the main asteroid belt [2][4]. The spacecraft made its closest approach on May 15 and came no closer than 4,609 kilometers, or 2,864 miles, from the Martian surface [2][4]. The sequence shows Mars changing from a distant crescent into a nearly full globe, with rust-colored plains, dark volcanic terrain, large craters, windblown dust streaks, the southern ice cap and the Valles Marineris canyon system [2][4]. Psyche continues toward the metal-rich asteroid 16 Psyche, which lies between Mars and Jupiter, and Popular Science reported that the spacecraft is expected to reach its target in 2029 [2][4].

Key Points

  • Camera checks: Engineers used the flyby to test Psyche’s camera calibrations and scattered-light sensitivity before the spacecraft uses the instruments at its asteroid target [4].
  • Moon search: Engineers practiced identifying Phobos and Deimos, a procedure they can use to search for possible moonlets near asteroid 16 Psyche [4].
