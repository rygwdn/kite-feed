Google launches Nano Banana 2 image model

Category: AI

Google has rolled out Nano Banana 2, its newest AI image generator, and set it as the default image model inside the Gemini app. The pitch: cleaner, more reliable results—especially when prompts include lots of text [1]. Latent Space describes the release as a “Flash-tier” Gemini 3.1 image model preview [3]. At the same time, Google is tightening up its creative toolkit. Flow (in Google Labs) is getting a redesigned interface that brings in assets and workflows from ImageFX and Whisk, so people can stay in one place as they move from image generation toward Veo video projects [2]. Wired’s hands-on calls Nano Banana 2 a capable, photo-editor-like tool for convincing edits, though it can still be hit-or-miss [4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Two borrowed features: PCMag says Nano Banana 2 picks up two notable features from the Pro model, and Google is framing them as upgrades to instruction-following and image quality [1].
  • Pricing signal: Latent Space points to third-party evaluator rankings (Arena and ArtificialAnalysis) that place the model at #1, while listing pricing at $67 per 1,000 images—about half the cited generation price of Nano Banana Pro and in line with similar rivals [3].
  • Flow adoption context: TestingCatalog says the revamped Flow targets artists, filmmakers, marketers, and other early adopters—and notes users have already made more than 1.5 billion creations since Flow’s initial launch [2].
  • Transfer window: TestingCatalog says existing Whisk and ImageFX users can start transferring projects and assets into Flow in March [2].
  • Documentation gap: Latent Space notes Google didn’t publish a detailed research paper or technical report with the release, which limits what can be independently verified beyond observed performance and public listings [3].

Technical Details

  • Character consistency: Latent Space says the model includes “6 character consistency,” along with search grounding and improved text rendering [3].
  • SynthID watermarking: Latent Space says every output is stamped with SynthID, a provenance tag meant to help identify AI-generated content [3].

Action Items

  • Try Nano Banana 2 in Gemini for text-heavy designs: In the Gemini app, use the default image generator to make posters, menus, or labels that need readable text—an area Google says it has improved [1].
  • Consolidate projects into Flow (if you used ImageFX/Whisk): Starting in March, transfer your Whisk and ImageFX projects into Flow to keep assets and works-in-progress in one place [2].
  • Check for SynthID when sharing AI images: When you share AI-generated images, look for provenance indicators such as SynthID tagging, which Latent Space says is applied to every generation [3].
