Pentagon sets Friday deadline for Anthropic AI access

Category: Technology

U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has pressed AI company Anthropic to loosen limits on how the Department of Defense can use its models, warning the firm it could be cut from the Pentagon’s supply chain if it does not agree by Friday [1][2][3]. Several outlets say the dispute centers on Anthropic’s reluctance to allow uses it has barred, including domestic surveillance and certain lethal military applications with no direct human control, while the Pentagon is seeking permission for “any lawful” military use [1][5][7]. Ars Technica and others report Hegseth also raised the possibility of using the Defense Production Act, a law that can empower the President to direct domestic industry for national defense [1][5][7]. At the same time, Anthropic has updated its AI-safety framework. Multiple reports say the company dropped its earlier pledge to automatically pause training if it could not guarantee safety in advance, replacing it with a more flexible approach that weighs competitor behavior and leans on transparency measures such as regular risk reporting [6][7][8][9][10]. Together, the Pentagon standoff and the policy rewrite highlight a familiar tension in tech: racing to meet competitive and national-security demand, while trying to keep meaningful guardrails—and public trust—intact [6][8].

Key Points

  • Pentagon stakes: Reason describes Anthropic as an official U.S. military AI supplier and says its model is the only one that can be used with the military’s classified systems, raising the stakes of any supply-chain cutoff [5].
  • Internal sign-off: Slashdot says CEO Dario Amodei and Anthropic’s board approved the revised Responsible Scaling Policy unanimously [9].
  • Conditional slowdown: PCWorld says the updated policy’s “delay” concept depends on Anthropic believing it is comfortably ahead of competitors, rather than committing to an absolute stop-when-unsafe pledge [7].
  • Civil liberties focus: Techdirt argues the Pentagon is pressuring a private firm to enable surveillance and urges companies not to accept that role [4].

Historical Background

The Defense Production Act dates to 1950 and gives the U.S. government powers to prioritize and direct industrial production in the name of national defense [common]. In recent years, U.S. agencies have increasingly explored AI for intelligence analysis and operational planning, sharpening debates over how to constrain surveillance and autonomous-weapons pathways while keeping pace with rivals [common]. Anthropic built its early reputation around a “Responsible Scaling Policy” introduced in 2023 that emphasized safety pre-commitments, which it has now revised amid competitive pressure [9][10].
