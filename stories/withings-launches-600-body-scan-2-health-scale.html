Withings launches $600 Body Scan 2 health scale

Category: Technology

Withings unveiled its second-generation Body Scan 2 smart scale at CES 2026, positioning it as a more comprehensive at-home health check-in than a typical body-composition scale [1][2][4][8]. The company says the device can track more than 60 biomarkers and aims to surface cardiovascular and metabolic signals—like hypertension risk—during a guided weigh-in [2][6][4][7]. Body Scan 2 is priced at $599.95 and is expected to arrive in Q2 2026, with some metrics pending FDA clearance [1]. The upbeat promise: more people could spot early risk signals at home and have more informed conversations with clinicians, as long as users treat readings as guidance rather than a diagnosis [2][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Withings’ first-generation Body Scan tracked 40 biomarkers, while Body Scan 2 is described as tracking 60 [2].

Key Points

  • Hardware design: Withings keeps a multi-electrode approach—eight electrodes on the platform plus additional electrodes in a retractable handle—to incorporate upper-body data rather than relying only on foot measurements [1][4].
  • New heart tests: Engadget highlights added Impedance Cardiography (ICG) for assessing the heart’s ability to pump blood, alongside other new cardiovascular-focused readings [2].
  • Hydration and cells: The scale also adds Bioimpedance Spectroscopy (BIS), which uses a low-level electrical current to estimate total body water and derive metrics Withings describes as cellular age, active cell mass, and metabolic efficiency [2].
  • On-device display: A notable tweak moves the display onto the retractable handle used for measurements, which AppleInsider says pairs with a 90-second scan flow and a claimed 15-month rechargeable battery life [4].
  • Health app tie-in: AppleInsider reports Apple Health integration, aiming to make long-term tracking easier for people who already log health data in an ecosystem of apps and devices [4].

Perspectives

Withings: The company frames Body Scan 2 as a "longevity"-oriented, clinic-like at-home tool that uses an AI model it describes as clinically validated to turn frequent measurements into guidance on health trajectory and actionable tips [2][1].

Sources: Engadget

Tech reviewers (Engadget): Engadget notes the readability benefit of putting the screen on the handle, but also flags a practical durability concern: concentrating key components in one place could create a single point of failure [2].

Sources: Engadget

Tech reviewers (The Verge): The Verge emphasizes the measurement approach—adding the handle to capture upper-body data—as a reason the lineup may estimate body composition more accurately than many foot-electrode-only smart scales [1].

Sources: The Verge

Value-focused question (ZDNET): ZDNET frames the release around consumer tradeoffs—whether readers would pay about $600 for deeper at-home health readings, including hypertension risk and cardiac efficiency—highlighting the cost/benefit decision buyers will face [7].

Sources: ZDNET

Technical Details

  • Impedance cardiography (ICG): ICG is presented as a measurement used by the scale to monitor the heart’s capacity to pump blood to organs, expanding the device’s cardiovascular focus beyond weight and basic composition [2].
  • Bioimpedance spectroscopy (BIS): BIS uses a low-level electrical current to estimate total body water, which Withings then uses to derive metrics it describes as cellular age, active cell mass, and metabolic efficiency [2].
  • Multi-electrode body scan: By combining electrodes on the platform with a retractable handle, the device captures upper-body signals rather than extrapolating from feet alone, an approach meant to improve overall body assessment [1][4].

Industry Impact

  • Connected health market: By pushing a scale toward cardiovascular and metabolic screening signals, Withings raises consumer expectations for what "smart scales" can measure, potentially pressuring competitors to add more clinically oriented sensors and coaching features [1][8].
  • Primary care workflows: At-home risk indicators like hypertension risk could drive more data-rich patient conversations, but they may also increase the need for clinicians to interpret consumer-device readings and decide when confirmatory testing is needed [6][7].
  • Platform ecosystems: Apple Health integration positions Body Scan 2 to compete not just on hardware but on how well it fits into existing health-data hubs, where easier data sharing can improve continuity of tracking across devices [4].

Scientific Significance

  • Scientific meaning: Body Scan 2 reflects a broader move in consumer tech toward bringing screening-style measurements into the home, aiming to translate signals from impedance-based methods and ECG-style sensing into interpretable cardiovascular and metabolic guidance [2][1].
  • Uncertainty and confidence: Several outlets note that some metrics require FDA clearance and that Withings describes its AI model as clinically validated, underscoring that measurement quality and clinical relevance depend on regulatory review and validation rather than raw sensor availability [1][2].
  • Limitations and future work: Because the product is framed as risk and trajectory guidance (not a diagnosis) and depends on clearance for some metrics, users will likely still need follow-up with clinicians to confirm conditions like hypertension, and independent validation will matter for trust as these features reach homes [1][7][6].

Historical Background

Withings introduced its first-generation Body Scan in 2023 as a premium scale that included features like a six-lead ECG and segmented body composition, helping set expectations that a scale could act like a light clinical checkpoint [2]. These CES 2026 announcements build on that path: Withings is expanding the number and type of measurements and trying to make "daily weigh-ins" feel more like routine preventive monitoring at home [5][1].

Q&A

Q: How long is a full measurement session, and how often do you need to recharge it?

A: AppleInsider says the scale can complete a full scan in 90 seconds and claims about 15 months of battery life on a rechargeable battery [4].

Q: What’s the practical difference between Withings’ Body Scan line and its Body Comp scale?

A: Ubergizmo notes Body Scan 2 is positioned as a more comprehensive at-home assessment tool, while Body Comp is framed more around advanced body-composition features [8].

Q: What part of the device changed from the first generation in day-to-day use?

A: Engadget reports the retractable handle now contains the scale’s display, a shift from the prior design [2].

Action Items

  • Plan around regulatory availability: If you’re interested in specific metrics, wait for the company’s Q2 2026 launch details and confirm which features ship after FDA clearance, as noted in CES coverage [1].
  • Set up health data syncing: If you use Apple Health, check that your Withings account and Apple Health permissions are enabled so measurements can be stored alongside your other health records [4].
