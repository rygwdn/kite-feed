US approved 879,868 AI chips for Chinese firms, records show

Category: AI

The U.S. Commerce Department authorized the export of 879,868 Nvidia and AMD AI chips to Chinese companies under 21 licenses between December 2025 and May 11, 2026, according to records released Thursday by the House Foreign Affairs Committee. Companies had requested permission to export about 3.4 million chips, including Nvidia H200 processors and AMD MI325X accelerators. The approvals covered 150,000 chips for Tencent, 75,000 for Alibaba, 43,000 for Baidu and 4,000 for BYD [3][2][7]. All four recipients are now on the Pentagon’s list of Chinese companies alleged to have military ties. Tencent was listed before the approvals, while Alibaba, Baidu and BYD were added after some licenses had been granted [21]. The released figures record authorizations, not confirmed deliveries. Nvidia says it has stopped shipping H200 chips to China, and AMD says it follows U.S. export-control rules [3][21]. The Commerce Department says it reviews applications case by case under a January 2026 rule with additional national-security requirements. House lawmakers are seeking further answers about chip export approvals, and former Nvidia chief Jensen Huang is scheduled for a closed-door congressional interview on Dec. 1 [3][7].

The fact that over 30% of the AI chips sent to China were shipped to companies that the Department of War has designated as Chinese military companies is astonishing, and calls into question whether Commerce conducted any serious national security review before approving these licenses.

— Chris McGuire, former U.S. official and expert on U.S.-China technology (Reuters)

Key Points

  • Uneven approvals: Alibaba received approval for 75,000 of the roughly 950,000 chips it sought, while Tencent, Baidu and BYD received approval for their full requests, according to the licensing data [21].
  • Suspected intermediaries: House Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman Brian Mast identified Maginfra and New Matrix, which appeared in Nvidia license applications, as suspected smuggling fronts. Neither firm responded to requests for comment [7][23].
  • Remote access: A new U.S. rule issued this week restricts Chinese companies’ access to advanced chips through data centers in third countries, including Thailand and Malaysia [18][7].
  • China’s chip plans: Tencent said the new access limits would reduce available computing capacity and slow model training, while Alibaba CEO Eddie Wu said the company may lower its long-term 20-gigawatt data-center target [22].
  • Market response: Nvidia shares fell 3.3% and AMD shares dropped 7.7% on Thursday as the Nasdaq and S&P 500 also declined [1].

Perspectives

Gregory Meeks, ranking Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee: Meeks said the Bureau of Industry and Security had failed to enforce export-control rules and called for rigorous congressional oversight of the administration’s policies [2][7].

Timeline

  • 2022: Biden administration began restricting China’s access to advanced AI chips [3][7]
  • December 2025: Trump gave the green light for Nvidia to ship H200 chips to China [3]
  • January 2026: Commerce rule set additional security conditions for case-by-case H200 licenses [3]
  • July 2026: Commerce official Jeffrey Kessler told lawmakers shipments had begun in very small numbers [3][9]
  • 19 November 2026: House committee invoked the Export Control Reform Act to release the licensing records [3][2]

Industry Impact

  • Domestic chip production: Tencent and Alibaba expect Chinese chipmakers to produce advanced AI hardware at scale in 2027 or 2028 [22].
