B.C. lets thousands return home as wildfire orders ease

Category: Canada

British Columbia emergency officials allowed some Okanagan wildfire evacuees to return home Aug. 14 as orders eased around Summerland, Peachland and parts of the Central Okanagan. Thousands of other properties remained under evacuation orders tied to the Bald Range wildfire [7][10][16][17]. Global News reported that more than 18,000 people had been forced from their homes by the Bald Range fire, and The Canadian Press reported that about 400 Trout Creek properties east of Highway 97 were cleared to return while Peachland orders were scheduled to be downgraded at 6 p.m. [1][16][17]. Residents of more than 2,600 properties elsewhere in the Central Okanagan were also allowed back after the separate Quilpituk Creek fire was declared held [16][17]. Officials staged the return process. Summerland said re-entry would proceed area by area after wildfire officials and the emergency operations centre confirmed conditions were safe and crews restored services and cleared hazards [7]. Evacuees who arrived in Penticton and nearby communities needed places to sleep, food and basic supplies, and volunteers and local organizations responded; The Globe and Mail reported that the Penticton Sikh Temple had fed thousands of evacuees [1][11].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • New order: Increased activity on other flanks of the Bald Range fire prompted a new evacuation order for some properties on Princeton-Summerland Road west of Summerland [17].
  • Return instructions: Summerland told returnees to fill their gas tanks, bring at least three days of food and essential supplies, and coordinate the safe return of pets and livestock [16][17].
  • Highway limits: Highway 97 through Summerland reopened daily from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., with no stopping, closed exits and side roads, and speed restrictions because crews were working near the roadway [8][13][15].
  • Migrant workers: British Columbia orchards, farms and ranches depend on migrant workers, and advocates said wildfire evacuations left some workers facing job and housing uncertainty [14][2].
  • Quilpituk changes: After the Quilpituk Creek fire north of West Kelowna was declared held, orders from Cinnabar Creek to Westshore Estates were downgraded to alerts and alerts south of Cinnabar Creek in Wilson’s Landing were lifted [6].

Perspectives

District of Summerland: The district said re-entry would happen “in phases, area by area,” and warned that timelines were not fixed because conditions could change quickly.

Sources: Vancouver Sun

Ron Kubek, owner of Lightning Rock Winery: Kubek described returning to his winery as “almost a melancholy feeling” because his property was safe but five people he knew had lost homes.

Sources: Vancouver Sun

Migrant-worker advocates: Advocates said provincial and federal governments need to do more for migrant workers affected by evacuations in agricultural communities.

Sources: Canada's National Observer

B.C. Ministry of Transportation and Transit: The ministry told drivers not to arrive before highway opening times, to follow traffic-control instructions and to prepare for congestion, delays and changing wildfire conditions.

Sources: Government of British Columbia

Timeline

  • 8 August 2026: Summerland’s mayor signed a townwide evacuation order [7].
  • 13 August 2026: Highway 97 reopened with daily time limits and stopping restrictions [8][12].
  • 14 August 2026: Trout Creek east of Highway 97 returned to alert status [7][17].
  • 14 August 2026: Peachland evacuation orders were scheduled to be downgraded at 6 p.m. [10][16].

Travel Advisory

  • Route planning: The province advised travellers to allow extra time, carry water and snacks, leave with a full tank, and use Highway 33 for commercial vehicles or drivers unfamiliar with forest-service-road detours [8]. Prepare for delays and use posted detours outside opening hours.

Action Items

  • Check official wildfire and road updates before travelling: Use EmergencyInfoBC for evacuation information and DriveBC for road advisories, the resources listed in the Vancouver Sun’s B.C. wildfire update and the provincial Highway 97 advisory [6][8].
