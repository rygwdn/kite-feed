Apple foldable iPhone may face limited 2026 launch

Category: Technology

Apple’s rumored first foldable iPhone, widely referred to as the iPhone Ultra or iPhone Fold, may be announced with the iPhone 18 Pro lineup in September but reach buyers later because early production is expected to be constrained, according to supply-chain analyst Ming-Chi Kuo [3][4][5][7][8][10][11][12][15][28][30]. Kuo estimates Apple’s assembly partners will ship only 500,000 to 1 million foldable units in the third quarter of 2026, out of roughly 7 million to 8 million units in the second half of the year [2][3][4][10][14][19][20][23][25][27]. Kuo compared the possible rollout with Apple’s 2017 iPhone X strategy, when Apple announced the redesigned model in September but opened sales later after production challenges tied to new hardware features [3][4][5][15][19][28]. The foldable model’s reported manufacturing hurdles stem from its folding display and more complex design. Apple has not confirmed the device’s existence, name or specifications [2][1][14][13][16]. Several reports citing Kuo put the expected U.S. price between about $2,300 and $2,500. Kuo said discussions with carriers, sales channels and resellers point to strong demand despite the price [3][5][12][20][26][27]. Kuo said preorders could sell out quickly and delivery times could stretch through December, depending on when Apple opens orders [2][20][26][27].

Key Points

  • Production comparison: Kuo expects Apple to have 20 million to 22 million iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max units ready in the third quarter, compared with the foldable model’s reported early volume [3][10][12][19][23][25].
  • Resale market: Kuo said scarce initial supply, a recognizable design and a new user experience could support a short-term resale premium, with some reports describing possible markups of 50% to 100% [2][14][22][25][31].
  • Possible design: Leaks cited by several sites describe a 5.5-inch exterior display, a 7.8-inch interior display and a body less than 5mm thick when unfolded [12][13][19][22].

Perspectives

Ming-Chi Kuo: Kuo said the demand picture would become clearer in late 2026 and early 2027, after launch hype fades and production improves.

9to5Mac: Ben Lovejoy wrote that Apple may accept limited early availability as part of an Ultra positioning strategy rather than treat it solely as a supply problem.

Computerworld: Jonny Evans wrote that gray-market prices after the initial rush may be a better demand signal than launch-day sellouts for a high-priced foldable iPhone.

Timeline

  • 12 September 2017: Apple announced iPhone X with iPhone 8 models [4].
  • November 2017: iPhone X reached buyers weeks after announcement [4][28].
  • 5 July 2026: Kuo posted foldable iPhone supply estimates on X [4][10].
  • September 2026: Reports expect Apple to announce new iPhones in September [5][12].
  • 2026-Q4: Kuo says foldable sales may start in fourth quarter [20].
  • 2027-Q1: Kuo expects clearer demand once supply limits ease [25][31].

Technical Details

  • Foldable display stack: The reported production constraint centers on Apple’s first folding iPhone display and hinge architecture, which would need to tolerate repeated bending while meeting smartphone thickness and image-quality targets [2][19].
  • Sub-5mm unfolded body: Several reports cite an unfolded thickness below 5mm, a constraint that leaves little room for batteries, hinge components and display layers in a two-screen device [12][19].

Industry Impact

  • Foldable market: Computerworld cited a recent report that Apple raised initial foldable production to 10 million units and could take 29% of the foldable market, though Kuo’s latest estimate remains 7 million to 8 million units for the second half of 2026 [21].
  • Competitors: Apple would enter a foldable smartphone category already served by companies including Samsung and Google [12].

Action Items

  • Avoid resale markups: If Apple announces the device, compare official Apple and carrier delivery dates before paying any resale premium, because reports cite possible 50% to 100% markups [22][25][31].
