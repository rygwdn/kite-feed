Iran holds mass funeral for slain leader Khamenei

Category: World

Hundreds of thousands of mourners gathered at Tehran’s Grand Mosalla as Iran held dayslong funeral ceremonies for its late supreme leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, who was killed at age 86 in a Feb. 28 U.S.-Israeli airstrike at the start of the Iran war [9][30][32][41]. Authorities displayed Khamenei’s flag-draped coffin in a glass case beside the coffins of family members killed in the same strike, while mourners dressed in black beat their chests, wept and chanted for revenge against Israel and the United States [9][37][43][49]. The ceremonies entered their second day Sunday, July 5, with funeral prayers in Tehran and a public holiday declared in Iran [2][15][45][53]. A performer identified as the poet Mohammad Rasouli called for President Trump’s death before a crowd in Tehran, drawing chants of “Death to America!” and “Death to Israel!”; his remarks followed posters and graffiti at the funeral calling for the killing of Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu [11][15][22][18]. Khamenei ruled Iran from 1989 until his death. The ceremonies are expected to continue through Tehran, Qom and Iraqi Shia holy cities before burial in Mashhad on July 9 [44][45][46][52]. President Trump said “neither side will shoot the other during the funeral proceedings” and said U.S.-Iran talks would continue after the ceremonies [16][29].

Did you know: The black turban on Khamenei’s coffin identified him as a direct descendant of the Prophet Muhammad [49].

Key Points

  • Succession questions: Mojtaba Khamenei, Ali Khamenei’s son and reported successor, has not appeared publicly since his father’s killing, prompting questions in Iranian political circles about who is directing the state [5][15][42].
  • Hormuz warning: Iran’s top negotiator warned France and the United Kingdom after they discussed possible joint patrols near the Strait of Hormuz, a waterway through which a fifth of global oil and natural gas once passed in peacetime [9][30][32][21].
  • Attendance estimates: Iranian authorities said they expected 15 million to 20 million participants in Tehran over the first three days of ceremonies, while other reports cited millions expected across Iran and Iraq [42][54][62][10].
  • Foreign delegations: Representatives of Hamas, Hezbollah and Yemen’s Houthis attended the ceremonies, and reports said foreign dignitaries from many countries were present [19][4][53].
  • Crowd measures: Volunteers and organizers used water misters, sprayed cooling water and distributed cold drinks as mourners stood in Tehran’s summer heat [17][36][49].

Historical Background

Ali Khamenei became Iran’s supreme leader in 1989 after the death of Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, the founder of the Islamic Republic [common]. Khomeini’s 1989 funeral drew about 10 million mourners, and Iranian reporting said Khamenei’s ceremonies were expected to rival or exceed that scale [44].
