Armed groups launch coordinated attacks across Mali

Category: World

Armed groups launched coordinated attacks across Mali on Saturday, including in Bamako, Kati, and cities in the center and north, in one of the country’s largest such assaults in recent years [1][4][10][11][13]. Multiple reports said the al-Qaeda-linked Jama’at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin, or JNIM, claimed responsibility, while Reuters reported that SITE Intelligence Group said JNIM and the Tuareg-led Azawad Liberation Front, or FLA, jointly claimed full responsibility [3][11][14]. Witnesses described explosions and gunfire near Bamako’s airport and around Kati, home to a major military base, as security forces responded and blocked roads [2][4][6][8]. Mali’s army said it was fighting armed “terrorist groups” and later said the situation was under control, though operations were still continuing [6][8][9]. The scale and geographic spread of the attacks pointed to a sharp escalation in Mali’s long-running conflict, which has involved jihadist groups, separatist movements, and a military-led government backed in recent years by Russian security support [3][7]. The immediate picture remained fluid, with competing claims about battlefield gains and no independently verified nationwide casualty toll available across the cited reports [3][7][14].

Primary Source: View original article

Did you know: Kidal, cited in the latest claims, was retaken by Malian forces in November 2023 after years outside Bamako’s control [8].

Key Points

  • Cities targeted: Reports said strikes or fighting hit Bamako, Kati, Sévaré, Gao and Kidal, showing an unusually broad operational reach across southern, central and northern Mali [1][4][8][11].
  • Airport disruption: The U.S. Embassy in Bamako issued a security alert after explosions and gunfire near Kati and the international airport, telling U.S. citizens to shelter in place and avoid travel in the area [2].
  • Government claim: Army spokesperson Col. Souleymane Dembele said at least 80 militants were eliminated, while another report said the military claimed several hundred assailants had been killed; those figures had not been independently verified in the source set [8][15][12].

Perspectives

Malian army: The military said it was engaging armed groups, later declared the situation under control, and said follow-up operations were still underway.

Industry Impact

  • Air travel: Attacks reported near Bamako’s international airport raised immediate security concerns around one of Mali’s main gateways, and embassy alerts signaled likely disruption for flights and airport access [2][11].
  • Security sector: The offensive increased pressure on Mali’s military-led government and its Russian-backed security model, a central issue highlighted in Financial Times coverage of the regime’s response capacity [7].

Historical Background

Mali has faced insurgencies by jihadist and separatist groups for more than a decade, after a 2012 Tuareg rebellion and the subsequent militant expansion in the north [common]. The military seized power in coups in 2020 and 2021, and the junta later deepened security cooperation with Russian forces after relations with France deteriorated [common]. RT reported that Malian forces recaptured Kidal in November 2023, making the city’s status especially symbolic in the current fighting [8].

Action Items

  • Check embassy advisories before travel: Review your country’s embassy or foreign ministry alerts for Bamako and central or northern Mali before booking or starting any trip [2].
  • Delay airport-area travel: Postpone plans involving Bamako’s international airport or nearby roads until authorities confirm conditions are stable [2][4].
