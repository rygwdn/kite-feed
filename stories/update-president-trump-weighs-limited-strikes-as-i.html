Update: President Trump weighs limited strikes as Iran drafts proposal

Category: World

President Donald Trump said he is considering “limited” military strikes on Iran as Washington tries to push Tehran toward an agreement that would curb Iran’s nuclear programme — even as the US continues a major military buildup in and around the Middle East [1][2][3][8][11][12][13][14][21][25]. His comments come as indirect negotiations continue, with Iran’s foreign minister saying Tehran is preparing a draft counterproposal, or draft deal text, that could be ready within days to hand to US envoy Steve Witkoff [4][15][19][20][24]. Reporting across several outlets says the US posture has widened to include major naval and air assets, including a carrier moving into the Mediterranean and an overall deployment described as among the region’s largest in decades [2][26][27][29][30]. Analysts and diplomats quoted in Reuters-based reports warn the buildup is starting to crowd out diplomacy and raises the risk of miscalculation, even as both sides still point to a negotiated outcome as the goal [6][10][5][9]. One encouraging sign in an otherwise tense standoff: several reports say the talks have moved far enough that both sides expect written proposals soon, keeping a diplomatic off-ramp in view despite the rhetoric and deployments [4][15][12][24][28].

I guess I can say I am considering that.

— Donald Trump (PBS NewsHour)

Key Points

  • Draft timeline: Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran’s draft text could be ready in the next “two, three days” for internal review and, once approved by his superiors, passed to US envoy Steve Witkoff. He also suggested more meetings may be needed after the drafts are exchanged [19][4][20].
  • Deadline pressure: President Trump publicly suggested Iran had about 10 days to agree to a deal before facing possible action, according to reporting on his remarks [21].
  • Red-line dispute: Araghchi said US negotiators had not asked Iran for “zero enrichment,” which he said clashes with some US statements insisting Iran must not enrich uranium at any level [19].
  • Additional kit: Reporting citing US officials and public tracking said the Pentagon is sending more weaponry to the region beyond headline naval moves, including extra air defenses and submarines, as it prepares contingencies for possible strikes [28].
  • Basing constraints: Flight-tracking-based reporting said Saudi Arabia and the UAE will not allow US aircraft to use their airspace to strike Iran, pushing many fighters to operate from Jordan and increasing reliance on aerial refueling [30].

Perspectives

Iranian foreign ministry: Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said a counterproposal, or draft text, could be ready within days. He framed the talks around keeping Iran’s enrichment peaceful rather than ending it outright.

Sources: Financial Times

White House: Press secretary Karoline Leavitt said talks had made “some progress” but that “a lot of details” still need to be worked through, as the administration continues moving military assets into the region.

Sources: AllSides

US officials (planning): Two US officials told Reuters that planning has reached an advanced stage, with options that could include targeting individuals and even pursuing leadership change if ordered.

Sources: The Guardian

Trump advisers (politics): Some aides have urged President Trump to keep the focus on domestic economic concerns, with Reuters analysis highlighting the political risk of escalation ahead of US midterm elections.

Sources: Al-Monitor

Regional diplomats and analysts: Diplomats quoted in Reuters-based reporting said Gulf neighbours and Israel increasingly see conflict as more likely than a negotiated settlement as the US buildup accelerates, making diplomacy harder to sustain.

Sources: Al-Monitor

Timeline

  • Tuesday: Indirect US-Iran talks held in Geneva, officials cite progress [28].
  • Thursday: President Trump referenced a roughly 10-day deal window [21].
  • Friday: Trump says he is considering limited strikes on Iran [21][13][25].
  • Friday: USS Gerald R. Ford enters Mediterranean amid US buildup [2][26].
  • Next two, three days: Araghchi says draft proposal could be ready soon [19][15].

Historical Background

The current standoff follows years of dispute over Iran’s nuclear activities and sanctions, including the 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) and the US decision to withdraw in 2018 under President Trump [common]. Since then, attempts to revive or replace the arrangement have alternated with new sanctions and regional flare-ups, with recurring disagreements over enrichment and verification [common].
