Supreme Court lets Trump resume third-country deportations

Category: World

The U.S. Supreme Court on Sept. 29 temporarily allowed President Donald Trump’s administration to resume deporting migrants to countries other than their own without first giving them a chance to challenge the destination based on possible harm. The 6-3 order granted the Justice Department’s emergency request to pause a lower-court ruling that had blocked the policy; it does not settle whether the policy is legal [2][3][11]. The court scheduled oral arguments for December on the policy’s legality and related questions, with a final ruling to follow. Human rights groups say more than 25,000 migrants have been sent to 29 countries since the policy began in 2025, with the vast majority sent to Mexico [6][13].

Key Points

  • Canceled flight: The administration said the lower-court requirements forced it to cancel a flight carrying about 70 people bound for three countries [9][11][17].
  • Government rationale: The Trump administration has defended third-country removals as necessary when migrants’ home countries will not accept them. The solicitor general described the policy as a tool to remove certain people, including some with serious criminal records [1][5].
  • Destination deals: The administration has relied on agreements with countries including Liberia and Guyana; some agreements have been described as secret [11][13].

Perspectives

National Immigration Litigation Alliance: Executive director Trina Realmuto said the stay permits removals to places where people may face persecution, torture, imprisonment or other grave danger [17].
