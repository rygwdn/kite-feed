AI computing crunch pushes tech rivals into partnerships and infrastructure race

Category: AI

An intensifying shortage of AI computing capacity is prompting former rivals and venture firms to cooperate to secure processing power. Chip rental prices are rising, and cloud providers are seeking multi-year commitments with upfront payments of up to 30%, CNYES reported, citing The Wall Street Journal [3][6]. Data-center expansion faces constraints in electricity, transmission, cooling and space. Indian electricity officials are dealing with pressure on power networks and higher upgrade costs, while AI-focused facilities require dense GPU systems and substantial power and cooling capacity [11][10]. South Korea’s SK Telecom plans to invest 750 billion won in SK Hyper through 2030 to develop gigawatt-scale data centers. It also aims to launch a beta AI service in October, followed by a full release by year-end [5].

Primary Source: View original article

Having hardware is great, but if you don't have the ecosystem and the software infrastructure for it, you really can't capitalize on some of the capability,

— Michael Nordquist, AMD corporate vice president for client product marketing (The Manila Times)

Key Points

  • Compute brokerage: Executives from Anthropic, Google and Y Combinator are personally arranging capacity, while venture firms reserve clusters for portfolio startups, CNYES reported [3].
  • Trade contribution: AI-related semiconductors and servers accounted for 47% of global merchandise-trade growth in the first half of 2026; trade in those goods rose 67% year over year, according to figures cited by Vietnam’s Nhan Dan [1].
  • Trade forecast: The WTO forecast global merchandise-trade growth of 3.9% in 2026, more than double its earlier projection [1].
  • Grid suppliers: South Korean cable maker LS Cable reported an 8.5 trillion-won backlog of cable orders, while Gaon Cable signed a roughly 5 trillion-won contract to supply bus ducts to a technology company [12].

Perspectives

AMD: The company said it has shipped tens of millions of AI PCs across more than 250 designs, while shipments of agentic PCs have topped 500,000 units across more than 50 designs [2].

Sources: The Manila Times

International Monetary Fund: Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said AI’s growth gains have so far accrued mainly to a limited number of countries, particularly high-income economies with stronger technology infrastructure and skilled workforces [1].

Sources: Nhan Dan

SK Telecom: The company is developing its A.X K2 model and plans to use it alongside other domestic AI models in an assistant that could progress from search and summaries to agents handling tasks such as public-service applications [5].

Sources: The Korea Economic Daily

Timeline

  • June 2026: SK Telecom, Nvidia and SK Group leaders agreed to pursue full-stack AI cloud cooperation [5]
  • July 2026: SK Telecom announced its vision of making South Korea an Asian AI infrastructure hub [5]
  • August 2026: The company announced a spin-off for data-center and submarine-cable operations [5]

Industry Impact

  • Data-center demand: AI accounts for about 15% of data-center workloads worldwide, a share projected to reach 40% in five years; there are about 10,000 data-center facilities globally [10].

Business Angle

  • LS Group reported record first-half consolidated revenue of 20.528 trillion won and operating profit of 1.0717 trillion won [12].
  • Nhan Dan reported that a sharp correction is possible if actual returns fail to meet expectations; CNYES reported that cloud providers are seeking multi-year contracts and upfront payments of up to 30% [1][3].
