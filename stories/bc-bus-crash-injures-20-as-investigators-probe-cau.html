B.C. bus crash injures 20 as investigators probe cause and communications

Category: Canada

A shuttle bus carrying workers subcontracted by IREN, an Australian data-centre company, left Highway 39 near Mackenzie, British Columbia, on Nov. 4, injuring 20 people. Police have not determined why it crashed [5][4][1]. Five people remained hospitalized on Thursday, including one airlifted to another facility, while 15 had been treated and released, Northern Health said [2][5]. The crash triggered mass-casualty alerts at hospitals in Mackenzie and Prince George, and crews used six ground and six air ambulances [5][6]. A lack of cellphone reception and poor satellite coverage at the crash site complicated the response, North District RCMP Cpl. Jennifer Cooper said. First responders also had difficulty with radio communications [1][3]. B.C. Highway Patrol Sgt. Michael McLaughlin said he had no information indicating that radio problems affected the crash investigation and that medical treatment was timely [1]. WorkSafeBC has opened an investigation alongside police, and investigators are seeking witnesses and dashcam footage [4][5][6].

Key Points

  • Coverage gap: The province has said about 38% of B.C. highways lack cellphone service, and limited access to reliable power is a key barrier to extending coverage [1][3].
  • Investigation status: Police have made no arrests and say the cause remains uncertain, with nothing ruled out; WorkSafeBC says it is coordinating with law-enforcement partners [5].

Perspectives

Kiel Giddens, member of the B.C. legislature for Prince George-Mackenzie: He said unreliable cellphone service is a regular problem for northern residents and praised local hospital staff and the coordination of transfers as “phenomenal” [1].
