B.C. leaders target Surrey as election race tightens before Saturday vote

Category: Canada

British Columbia’s party leaders focused on Surrey in the final days before Saturday’s provincial election, with New Democratic Party Leader David Eby accusing interim Conservative leader Lorne Doerkson of prioritizing fundraising over media questions. Doerkson had held five fundraising events in the previous week and had two more scheduled; the party said he was scheduled to return to public campaigning Thursday and would not take questions on election night [1][3]. Surrey has 10 seats. Eby said a Conservative government would replace the planned Fraser River tunnel with a bridge and cut health care; the Conservatives said they would build the bridge and pledged a $50-million fund for rural emergency health services. Elections BC reported about 711,000 advance voters in the first five days, 9% fewer than in the same period in 2024. Voting day is Oct. 24 [1][7].

The Conservatives are conducting themselves as if they've won the election, but they do not deserve anybody's vote

Key Points

  • Poll numbers: An Innovative Research Group survey of 1,153 people put the Conservatives at 43% of decided voters, the NDP at 38% and the Greens at 12%; 65% said it was time for a change in government [4]
  • Close contests: In the 2024 election, the NDP won Surrey-Guildford by 22 votes after a judicial recount, while Surrey-Panorama and Surrey-City Centre were decided by margins of 1.5% and 1.6% [5]
  • Other platforms: The Greens proposed protections for people in housing under long-term leases and a tuition freeze, while CentreBC leader Elenore Sturko criticized the NDP’s deficit and presented her party as socially progressive [1]
  • Campaign promises: Both the NDP and Conservatives pledged to fund another Surrey hospital and a SkyTrain extension to Newton [5]
