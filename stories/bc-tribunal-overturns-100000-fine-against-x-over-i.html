B.C. tribunal overturns $100,000 fine against X over intimate image

Category: Canada

British Columbia’s Civil Resolution Tribunal has overturned a $100,000 penalty against social media platform X for failing to delete an intimate image posted without a woman’s consent, ruling that blocking access to it in Canada was a reasonable step under provincial law. The tribunal said the law did not make X liable after it blocked Canadian access to the image, which remained available outside Canada. The decision reversed a September 2025 penalty for failing to comply with an order to delete it [4][3][5]. The tribunal accepted X’s argument that requiring worldwide removal could interfere with other countries’ legal systems and cited a 2024 Australian case as persuasive but not binding. Outgoing B.C. Attorney General Niki Sharma criticized X’s refusal to remove the image and said governments may need to strengthen laws or pursue legal action. The tribunal also rejected X’s request to require the complainant to pay more than US$100,000 in legal fees [2][4].

What we found with platforms like X is that they will fight out everything legally before they take actual steps for safety.

— Niki Sharma, outgoing British Columbia attorney general (The Canadian Press)

Key Points

  • U.S. legal conflict: X argued that U.S. law would not require removal and that the B.C. order could not be enforced in the United States; the tribunal agreed the image likely would not be unlawful there [5].
  • The complainant represented herself. The tribunal rejected X’s request that she pay its legal fees, saying that exposing applicants to substantial financial risk could deter efforts to enforce protection orders [4][3].

Perspectives

The complainant: The woman, whose identity is under a publication ban, asked the tribunal to impose a further penalty because the image remained on X [5].

Timeline

  • 2023: B.C. government tabled intimate-images protection legislation [4]
  • March 2025: Tribunal issued a protection order directing X to delete the image [4][5]
  • September 2025: Tribunal imposed a $100,000 penalty for X’s failure to delete it [3][4]
  • 2 November 2026: Tribunal reversed the penalty in its latest ruling [2][4]

Historical Background

The legislation was partly prompted by the 2012 death of teenager Amanda Todd, who experienced cyberbullying and online sexual exploitation [4][2].

Q&A

Q: Did the tribunal find that the image was not intimate under B.C. law?

A: No. The tribunal had already found the image qualified as intimate under the act and did not change that conclusion in its latest ruling [5].
