Toronto man charged after allegedly faking pilot status

Category: Canada

U.S. federal prosecutors say Dallas Pokornik, a 33-year-old from Toronto and former flight attendant for a Toronto-based airline, used false identification and airline-employee credentials to obtain hundreds of free flights on U.S. carriers [1][2][3][4]. Authorities say he posed as both a commercial pilot and a current flight attendant to access tickets typically reserved for airline crew [1][4]. He was indicted on wire fraud charges in federal court in Hawaii, arrested in Panama, extradited to the United States, and pleaded not guilty in court [1][4]. The case underscores how airlines and border agencies coordinate internationally to investigate identity-related travel fraud, which can help protect legitimate staff travel programs for working crews [1][4].

  • Employment history: Court documents cited in the reporting say Pokornik worked as a flight attendant for a Toronto-based airline from 2017 to 2019 [3][4].
  • Airlines involved: Prosecutors allege the free travel involved three unnamed U.S. airlines whose employee-travel systems were accessed using false credentials [2][4].
  • How it worked: The Globe and Mail reports investigators allege he used fake employee identification from his former Canadian carrier to book tickets reserved for pilots and flight attendants on other airlines [4].
  • Legal posture: The New York Times reports Pokornik denied the allegations as prosecutors described repeated use of false identification to obtain flights [2].
