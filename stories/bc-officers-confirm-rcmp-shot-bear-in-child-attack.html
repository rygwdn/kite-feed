B.C. officers confirm RCMP shot bear in child attack

Category: Canada

The B.C. Conservation Officer Service now says RCMP killed the black bear that attacked a three-year-old girl in Maple Ridge on Aug. 9, reversing an earlier assessment that the wrong animal had been shot [1][3][5]. The service said Friday that it confirmed the Mounties killed the bear responsible after reassessing evidence, gathering additional evidence and conducting DNA analysis [1][5]. The girl was attacked while playing on a deck or patio at her family’s home. A parent chased the bear away, and Maple Ridge RCMP later shot it nearby after police said it returned to the area several times [1][5]. The Conservation Officer Service said no other bears had been killed in connection with the incident and that it was closing its investigation [1].

We recognize the impact of our earlier assessment and apologize for the confusion it caused. We’re grateful to the RCMP officers who responded quickly to protect public safety. With these findings, the (service) is concluding its investigation.

— Sgt. Jeremy Pauls (Vancouver Sun)

Humanitarian Impact

A three-year-old girl, identified by the Vancouver Sun as Anaiyah Lekei, suffered serious neck and head wounds and was flown to B.C. Children’s Hospital; she continues to recover [1].

Action Items

  • Report bear sightings near homes: Contact local authorities or the B.C. Conservation Officer Service if a bear repeatedly returns to residential areas, as Maple Ridge RCMP said happened before the animal was shot [5].
  • Leash dogs on bear-area trails: Use a leash in parks and wooded areas where bears may be present; an off-leash dog encounter preceded a reported bear injury at Stoney Hill Regional Park [1].
