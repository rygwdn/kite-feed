Aortic dissection cited in Lindsey Graham’s death

Category: Science

A preliminary medical examiner report cited an aortic dissection due to arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease in the death of U.S. Sen. Lindsey Graham, who died Saturday at 71 after what his office called a “brief and sudden illness” [49][37][43]. The New York Times described aortic dissection as a sudden, deadly and difficult-to-treat condition, quoting one expert who compared it to “a knife to the heart” [2]. South Carolina Gov. Henry McMaster appointed Darline Graham Nordone, Graham’s sister, on Monday to serve the remaining months of his Senate term, which expires in January [1][5][41][10]. Nordone leads the South Carolina Commission for the Blind and had not previously held elected office, but she appeared with Graham at campaign events and in advertisements during his political career [1][33]. Senate Majority Leader John Thune said Nordone would be sworn in Tuesday at 2:30 p.m. ET [10].

Key Points

  • Aortic mechanism: The Mayo Clinic defines an aortic dissection as a tear in the inner layer of the aorta that lets blood rush through and split the artery’s inner and middle layers [49].
  • Pending tests: Graham’s death certificate will remain pending until toxicological and microscopic testing is final, according to a statement citing the District of Columbia medical examiner [49].
  • Chest pains: CNN reported that Graham asked a staffer to call 911 after reporting chest pains before his death [3].
  • Special primary: South Carolina Republicans scheduled an Aug. 11 special primary to choose a nominee for the November election for Graham’s Senate seat [14][40][24].
  • Senate history: Nordone is expected to become the first woman to represent South Carolina in the U.S. Senate [9][41][42][46].
