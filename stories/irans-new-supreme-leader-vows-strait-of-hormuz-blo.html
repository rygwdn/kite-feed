Iran’s new supreme leader vows Strait of Hormuz blockade

Category: World

Iran’s new supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, delivered his first public message since taking power, vowing to keep the Strait of Hormuz closed and press on with retaliatory actions around Gulf oil routes as the regional war widens [8][4][11][10]. The message, read on Iranian state television rather than delivered in person, cast the closure as strategic leverage and promised revenge for those killed in recent strikes [1][6][12]. Oil prices climbed back above US$100 a barrel after ships came under attack in the Persian Gulf, reigniting fears of a prolonged disruption at one of the world’s most important energy chokepoints [3][2][9].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Market threshold: Analysts called oil’s return to “three digits” a psychological threshold that could raise pressure on the United States to seek an end to the war, according to Bloomberg as cited by Semafor [2].
  • Leader profile: In a statement carried by The Hindu, the 56-year-old cleric said Iran would continue an “effective and regret-inducing defence” [6].
  • Regional basing appeal: Khamenei urged neighboring countries to shut down US military bases on their soil, adding political pressure on Gulf states that host American forces [12].
  • Expert framing: Iranian academic and former nuclear-negotiations adviser Mohammad Marandi said Iran could use Hormuz as leverage while tailoring its military approach to limit shockwaves in global energy markets [5].
  • Succession context: Several outlets said Khamenei succeeded his slain father, a sharp leadership shift unfolding amid active hostilities [10][4].

Perspectives

Iran (Supreme leader Mojtaba Khamenei): In a written statement read on state TV, he said Iran would keep Hormuz blocked, continue targeting US bases in the region, and “avenge the blood of our martyrs.”
