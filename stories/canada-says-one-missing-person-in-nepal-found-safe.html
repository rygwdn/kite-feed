Canada says one missing person in Nepal found safe

Category: Canada

Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand said one of 30 Canadian citizens and permanent residents reported missing after catastrophic flooding in Nepal’s border region with China had been found safe, leaving 29 still unaccounted for [3][4][5][7][8]. Anand said the person had been unreachable because communications were down [4][5][7][8]. More than 1,100 people were killed and more than 3,900 remain missing. Nepal’s Foreign Ministry said about 590 foreign nationals from 39 countries are still unaccounted for [4][8][7]. Canadian families are still waiting for word on relatives, including a Toronto family of four who were travelling near the affected region [7][1].

Key Points

  • Rescue efforts: Landslides and destroyed roads have hampered rescue efforts, and private and military helicopters have been deployed to reach areas cut off by flooding [4][1].
  • Body identification: Kathmandu police have buried dozens of unidentified bodies in trenches because authorities had too many to keep or identify individually [1].

Timeline

  • 26 August 2026: A glacier collapse triggered flash flooding near the Nepal-China border [8][1].
  • 31 August 2026: Helicopters operated in areas of Nuwakot district cut off by flooding [4][1].
  • 1 September 2026: The missing Canadian reached officials after communication outages [4][8].
  • 2 September 2026: Anand said 29 Canadians or residents were still missing [4][8].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Flooding, landslides and damaged roads have cut off areas in Nepal’s affected border region [4][1]. Travellers with plans near the affected region should verify route conditions and maintain direct contact with family, tour operators and consular officials.
  • Consular: Canadian officials were tracking missing Canadian citizens and permanent residents after the disaster [3][4][8]. Canadians in Nepal should contact Global Affairs Canada or register their location if communication access allows.

Action Items

  • Report missing Canadian relatives: Contact Global Affairs Canada with the traveller’s full name, itinerary, last known location and any local contacts if a relative in Nepal remains unreachable.
  • Prepare Nepal travel check-ins: Share routes, accommodation details and satellite or backup contact methods with family before travelling in remote Himalayan areas.
