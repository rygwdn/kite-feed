Death toll from Japan quake reaches 30 in Kumamoto

Category: World

Japan's government said the death toll from Tuesday's earthquake in Kumamoto prefecture on the southern island of Kyushu had risen to 30, as rescuers searched damaged buildings two days after the shock [4][8][29]. Japan's Meteorological Agency put the quake at magnitude 7.1; the U.S. Geological Survey measured it at 6.8. It struck at 16:27 local time on Tuesday and injured at least 62 people [5][4][18][13]. Search teams focused on the partially collapsed Aeon shopping centre in Kashima, a Nippon Paper Industries factory in Yatsushiro and collapsed homes across the region [4][12][10]. A 5.8-magnitude aftershock and temperatures of up to 35C slowed rescue operations on Thursday, when nearly 5,000 Self-Defence Forces personnel were deployed [4]. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said the work remained urgent because people were still waiting to be rescued [21][17].

Key Points

  • Mall evacuation: Aeon said about 3,000 customers were moved to a car park before a suspected gas explosion, and those caught in the blast were shop workers who had returned inside [4][18].
  • Factory collapse: A chimney collapse at a Nippon Paper Industries factory in nearby Yatsushiro killed five people, while seven others were rescued from the debris [10].
  • Missing count unclear: Officials had not given a confirmed missing-person count by Thursday, while local media said dozens remained unaccounted for as rescuers approached the 72-hour mark after the quake [13].
  • Aftershock warning: Authorities warned residents in the hardest-hit areas to stay alert for further strong quakes for about a week after the main shock [3].

Industry Impact

  • Manufacturing: Many major manufacturers in Kyushu suspended operations on Tuesday. Some found no major damage and planned to resume work on Wednesday; Reuters-cited reporting also named Sony and Honda among companies that suspended production [22][61].
  • Transport: The quake disrupted Shinkansen bullet train services and some airport operations, complicating movement into and within the affected prefecture [31][51].

Historical Background

An earthquake in Kumamoto 10 years earlier killed 50 people in the region [7]. The Aeon complex had reopened only last month after detailed renovation work following damage from the 2016 Kumamoto earthquakes [23].

Humanitarian Impact

More than 10,000 people were staying in roughly 400 shelters, and almost 23,000 homes remained without electricity in the affected area [32][34]. Relief operations focused on drinking water, restored power and cooling, with many of Yatsushiro's roughly 58,000 households still without running water or electricity on Thursday [36][30].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Avoid damaged buildings, monitor local aftershock notices, and follow instructions from authorities [3].
  • Transport: Bullet train services and some airport operations were suspended after the quake, and Aso Kumamoto Airport's runway was reported closed at one stage [31][22]. Confirm rail and airport status before travelling in Kyushu.
