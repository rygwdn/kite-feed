NASA astronauts complete ISS solar array spacewalk

Category: Science

NASA astronauts Jessica Meir and Anil Menon completed a spacewalk lasting about 6 1/2 hours outside the International Space Station on Aug. 6, installing mounting hardware for a future solar array upgrade [2][3][8][12][14]. The spacewalk began at 8:33 a.m. EDT and ended at 3:00 p.m. EDT, lasting six hours and 27 minutes [8][12][14]. The work centered on the station’s 3B power channel on the Starboard 6 truss segment. The astronauts assembled hardware there for future ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays, or IROSA [3][8][12][14]. It was Meir’s sixth spacewalk and Menon’s first, and NASA identified it as the 281st spacewalk in support of the space station [4][8][12].

Primary Source: View original article

This work will enable the future installation of roll-out solar arrays, called IROSA, to provide additional power for the orbiting laboratory, supporting critical systems and the safe, controlled deorbit of the space station

— NASA (Free Press Journal)

Key Points

  • Upgrade sequence: The 3B array will be the seventh of eight planned IROSA upgrades in a modernization program that began in 2021 [14][13].
  • Power channels: NASA said six of the station’s eight power channels had been upgraded before this work, with remaining equipment to be delivered later [13].
  • Upcoming delivery: The new panels are expected to arrive by the end of 2026, according to reports citing NASA’s upgrade schedule [10][14].
  • Next spacewalks: NASA has scheduled additional U.S. spacewalks for Aug. 13 and Aug. 25 to continue work on the station’s power system and communications infrastructure [13][16].

Timeline

  • 2021: NASA began the IROSA power-modernization program [14].
  • 6 August 2026: Meir and Menon started the spacewalk at 8:33 a.m. EDT [8].
  • 6 August 2026: The astronauts returned inside at 3:00 p.m. EDT [12].
  • 13 August 2026: NASA plans another U.S. spacewalk for ISS upgrades [13].
  • 25 August 2026: A further U.S. spacewalk is scheduled later this month [13].

Technical Details

  • IROSA deployment: The roll-out arrays do not need motors to unfurl; stored energy in composite structures opens them to about 19 meters in roughly six minutes [14].
  • Array output: DSL.sk reported that an IROSA array measures 18.2 meters by 6 meters, can generate up to 28 kW when new and has an expected normal output above 20 kW [20].
  • Mission designation: The spacewalk was designated US Spacewalk 96, continuing the sequence of American extravehicular activities at the station [16].

Historical Background

The International Space Station has hosted continuous human presence in low Earth orbit since November 2000 [common]. To supplement the station’s aging original solar arrays, whose power output has declined over time, NASA launched the IROSA modernization program [14][20].
