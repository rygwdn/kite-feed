Russia blocks WhatsApp, throttles Telegram, promotes state Max app

Category: Technology

Russian authorities say they have fully blocked WhatsApp in Russia, arguing that Meta has not complied with local legal requirements. In recent days, they have also moved to restrict or slow access to Telegram [1][2][4][5][8][10][11][12]. Officials and state-linked guidance are urging people to switch to “Max,” a domestic, state-backed messaging app that multiple outlets describe as lacking end-to-end encryption [1][4][6][8][10][11][12]. The impact is immediate and broad. Multiple reports put WhatsApp’s Russian user base at more than 100 million, which means many people and organizations will have to rethink how they communicate—and how they verify information online [3][8][10][11][12]. Authorities cast the measures as law enforcement and a way to protect citizens from crime, fraud, and content they describe as terrorist. Critics and some residents, meanwhile, worry that nudging people toward an unencrypted domestic alternative could erode privacy and make timely information harder to access [7][8][10][11].

Primary Source: View original article

Due to Meta's unwillingness to comply with Russian law, such a decision was indeed taken and implemented

— Dmitry Peskov (Slashdot)

Key Points

  • Distribution controls: Engadget reported that authorities removed WhatsApp from an online directory, effectively wiping it from Russia’s internet—an approach that can disrupt updates and access even beyond straightforward blocking [8].
  • Meta’s status: Engadget and 9to5Mac noted that Russia has labeled Meta an “extremist organization,” and previously blocked or removed Facebook and Instagram, adding context for why WhatsApp is under heavier pressure [8][6].
  • Telegram slowdown scale: TechSpot reported Roskomnadzor said it would slow Telegram nationwide for about 60 million users. That drew criticism from military bloggers, who say the app is important for Russian troops’ communications in Ukraine [9][10].

Perspectives

Kremlin (Dmitry Peskov): The Kremlin said WhatsApp was blocked because Meta would not comply with Russian law and urged people to switch to the state-backed Max messenger; it also said compliance could lead to unblocking [5][10].

Sources: Slashdot

Meta/WhatsApp: A WhatsApp spokesperson said Russian authorities’ action aimed to “drive users to a state-owned surveillance app,” adding that isolating “over 100 million people” from private communication would reduce safety [10].

Sources: Fast Company

Roskomnadzor (communications watchdog): Roskomnadzor said it would introduce new restrictions on Telegram over alleged long-running violations and refusal to comply with the law, according to reporting [10][9].

Sources: TechSpot

Activists/critics of Max: Critics and activists described Max as a potential surveillance tool and pointed to its lack of end-to-end encryption as a key concern [1][11].

Sources: Insider Paper

Regional officials near Ukraine border: Engadget reported a regional governor warned that slowing Telegram could hinder information flow if the situation worsens, reflecting local reliance on the app for alerts [8].

Sources: Engadget

Timeline

  • 2022: Russia ramps up internet controls after Ukraine invasion [10][8].
  • Last August (year not specified in provided sources): Authorities block calls on WhatsApp and Telegram, citing crime concerns [11].
  • July 2025: Lawmaker says WhatsApp likely to face restricted-software listing [8].
  • Earlier this week: Russia says it is restricting Telegram access (some reports describe slowing/throttling) [7][10][9].
  • Thursday: Kremlin confirms WhatsApp fully blocked and urges switching to Max [1][2][4][5][11][12].

Historical Background

Russia has steadily tightened controls on major online platforms since its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and it previously blocked Meta’s Facebook and Instagram [10][8]. Dawn reported that authorities started by blocking calls on WhatsApp and Telegram last August, then later signaled an outright WhatsApp ban unless the service complied with Russian legislation [11].

Action Items

  • Line up a backup secure chat channel: Before traveling to Russia or working with contacts there, agree on an alternative end-to-end encrypted messenger that both sides can still reach, since WhatsApp is reported as fully blocked and Telegram has faced restrictions [8][10].
  • Harden account recovery: Turn on two-factor authentication and update recovery methods on messaging accounts so a sudden app or store removal doesn’t lock you out of contacts or groups [common].
