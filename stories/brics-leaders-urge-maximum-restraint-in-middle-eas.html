BRICS leaders urge maximum restraint in Middle East war

Category: World

BRICS leaders adopted a joint declaration in New Delhi on September 12 calling for “maximum restraint” in the Middle East, protection of civilians and civilian infrastructure, and avoidance of actions that could further escalate the conflict [3][4][23]. The statement also condemned deliberate attacks on peaceful nuclear facilities under International Atomic Energy Agency safeguards, saying nuclear safety and security must be maintained during armed conflicts [3][4]. It did not name the countries responsible for the strikes [52][50]. All 11 BRICS members adopted the declaration, including Iran and the United Arab Emirates, despite being on opposing sides of the conflict’s regional fallout [2][38]. The group’s foreign ministers failed to agree on a Middle East statement in May, but host India secured consensus at the leaders’ summit after diplomatic negotiations [19][53]. Chinese President Xi Jinping urged BRICS to help pursue peace, while Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said the region does not need a “regional policeman” [2][1].

Key Points

  • Sanctions criticism: The declaration condemned unilateral sanctions not authorized by the U.N. Security Council as measures that undermine international law and the U.N. Charter [4][59].
  • Trade protection: BRICS members called for uninterrupted trade, supply chains, energy flows and maritime routes as the conflict disrupts regional commerce [3][22].
  • Additional ceasefires: The statement called for ceasefires in Gaza, Lebanon, Sudan and Syria [3].

Perspectives

BRICS leaders: The declaration called for resolving international disputes through dialogue, consultation and diplomacy [23][24].
