Conservative MP Larry Brock sets September resignation date

Category: Canada

Conservative MP Larry Brock said he will resign his Brantford-Brant South-Six Nations seat on Sept. 18, triggering another federal byelection and adding to a series of departures from Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre's caucus [2][3][4][5]. Brock said he plans to return to the Crown attorney's office in Brantford, Ont., where he worked as a prosecutor for almost 19 years before entering federal politics [2][3]. Brock was first elected in 2021 and was re-elected in 2025 with more than 52% of the vote, carrying the riding by 11 percentage points in the last federal election [2][1]. He served as the Conservatives' shadow justice minister before Ontario MP Arpan Khanna replaced him in June [2]. Polling analyst Dan Arnold, chief strategy officer at Pollara Strategic Insights and a former Liberal Party research director, said the riding has historically been considered a safe Conservative seat but could become competitive in a byelection if the Conservative vote is demoralized and the Liberals gain momentum [1]. Brock told The Brantford Expositor that opposition work limited what he could do on justice policy [1][2].

Key Points

  • Byelection count: The National Post said Brock's resignation would trigger an eighth federal byelection, while Le Devoir reported he is the seventh Conservative MP to leave the party since the last election [2][5].
  • Stated reason: Brock said in his resignation statement that his commitment to community safety had not changed and that working in court would let him pursue justice issues locally [2].
  • Riding history: The riding covers Brantford-Brant South-Six Nations in southwestern Ontario. Brock replaced longtime Conservative MP Phil McColeman when he entered Parliament in 2021 [2][3].
