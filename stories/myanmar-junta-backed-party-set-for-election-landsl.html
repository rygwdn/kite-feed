Myanmar junta-backed party set for election landslide

Category: World

Myanmar has completed the third and final phase of a month-long general election, with the military-aligned Union Solidarity and Development Party widely expected to win by a large margin [4][3][5]. Voting did not take place in many areas affected by fighting, and multiple reports say numerous parties were barred while Aung San Suu Kyi’s party has been dissolved, leaving critics to argue the process favors the military’s allies [5][4]. Junta chief Min Aung Hlaing, who has suggested he could become president after the vote, cast the election as a route to stability and a return to civilian rule, even as conflict continued during the polling period [1][3][2].

Primary Source: View original article

This is the path chosen by the people

— Min Aung Hlaing (Dawn (via AFP))

Did you know: Myanmar had a decade of civilian-led reforms after long periods of military rule before the 2021 coup, according to Dawn [4].

Key Points

  • Turnout footprint: Several outlets report that roughly half the country did not vote, with polling absent in areas outside junta control amid the civil war [5][4].
  • Mandalay scenes: AFP reporting carried by Dawn described early-morning voting at a Mandalay high school and quoted a teacher saying he voted hoping for “a better country,” even while expecting little change [4].
  • Military branding: Dawn reported Min Aung Hlaing toured Mandalay polling stations wearing civilian dress, a detail that underscores the junta’s effort to present the ballot as a civilian transition [4].
  • Critics’ claims: Rights monitors cited in Dawn said the run-up in junta-held areas featured coercion and a crackdown on dissent, while The Guardian described international denunciations of the vote [4][3].
  • Earlier precedent: Dawn noted the USDP previously won more than 85% in an earlier election, and described the party as filled with retired officers and viewed by analysts as a military proxy [4].

Perspectives

Myanmar junta (Min Aung Hlaing): The junta leader rejected criticism and framed the vote as reflecting public support and a return to democracy and stability, saying people can back whoever they want [3][4].

Timeline

  • Month-long poll: Voting ran for about a month and ended Sunday [4][3].
  • Sunday: Third and final phase held as fighting continued [4][2].
  • A week shy of coup’s five-year anniversary: Final phase ended shortly before the coup anniversary [4].

Technical Details

  • Bicameral legislature: AP described the vote as electing Myanmar’s bicameral legislature, meaning two chambers make up the national parliament [6][common].
  • Phased voting: Multiple reports characterize the election as conducted in stages over roughly a month, culminating in a third and final phase [4][2].
  • Constituency exclusions: Reports say voting was not held in rebel-held or conflict-affected constituencies, shaping overall representation in the results [4][5].

Historical Background

Myanmar’s military seized power in a February 2021 coup, detaining Aung San Suu Kyi, after which civil war erupted and the country slid deeper into humanitarian crisis conditions, according to Dawn’s account [4]. Dawn also notes Myanmar experienced about a decade of civilian-led reforms after a long history of military rule, before the 2021 takeover reversed that trajectory [4].

Q&A

Q: What does it mean that Myanmar’s vote was held in phases?

A: The reports describe a month-long election conducted in multiple stages, with the third stage completing the process, rather than a single nationwide election day [4][2].

Q: Why was Aung San Suu Kyi’s party not on the ballot?

A: Dawn and the BBC report that Suu Kyi was detained after the 2021 coup and that her party was dissolved, with many parties also barred from competing [4][5].

Q: What is the USDP and why is it central to this election?

A: Dawn describes the Union Solidarity and Development Party as packed with retired officers and viewed by analysts as a military proxy expected to dominate the junta-run vote [4].
