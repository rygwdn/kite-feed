Canada commits $100 million to Nova Scotia offshore wind preparations

Category: Canada

Canada announced $100 million on Oct. 15 to prepare for Wind West, a proposed offshore wind project off Nova Scotia that could become the country’s first. The money will fund surveys and planning—not turbine construction—to give developers more information before bidding. The project’s proposed first phase would cost $60 billion and generate five gigawatts as early as 2033, more than twice Nova Scotia’s current electricity needs of 2.4 gigawatts [5][1]. The funding includes $5 million for floating instruments to measure wind and ocean conditions, nearly $3 million for transmission modelling and marine surveys, and the rest for subsea drilling over the next year [5][4]. The work is intended to inform a call for bids planned for winter 2027 [6]. Quebec and Massachusetts have expressed interest in buying power from the proposed project [5].

Nearly 30 countries are ahead of Canada in developing offshore wind and it’s time to change that.

— Tim Hodgson, federal Energy and Natural Resources Minister (Global News)

Key Points

  • Regional grid: Energy Minister Tim Hodgson said Ottawa is working toward a single electricity system operator for the Maritimes, which could coordinate power links among Atlantic provinces and Quebec [5][1].
  • Affordability concerns: A Canadian Renewable Energy Association poll found 33% of Canadians ranked electricity affordability as a top priority for new supply [4].
  • Local partnerships: The association’s Maritime policy director Eddie Oldfield said a regional transmission framework should include clear investor timelines and partnerships with Mi’kmaq, Indigenous, fishing and coastal communities [4].
  • Full build-out: Nova Scotia officials say all phases of Wind West could reach 60 gigawatts, while Premier Tim Houston has said surplus output could meet 27% of Canada’s electricity demand [5][4].

Perspectives

Tim Houston, Nova Scotia premier: The federal funding should shorten development timelines by at least a year and give prospective developers more confidence, Houston said. He described the public spending as a way to help unlock tens of billions of dollars in private investment [5][4].
