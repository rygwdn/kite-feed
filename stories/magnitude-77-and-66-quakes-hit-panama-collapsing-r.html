Magnitude 7.7 and 6.6 quakes hit Panama, collapsing roads

Category: World

A magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck south-central Panama on Friday, Oct. 9, followed about two and a half hours later by a magnitude 6.6 quake and a prolonged series of aftershocks. The U.S. Geological Survey located the first quake near Pitaloza Arriba in Herrera province at a depth of about 10 to 12.6 kilometers. People felt the shaking in Panama City and neighboring Colombia [15][12][9]. The quakes damaged homes, roads and public buildings, mainly in Herrera, Coclé, Veraguas and Los Santos. A cathedral, a hotel and government offices in Penonomé sustained damage or partly collapsed. Officials reported minor injuries, but no deaths or missing people in the latest account [41][12][21]. President José Raúl Mulino mobilized government resources and called the situation a major disaster [41][20][51]. Authorities later lifted tsunami alerts around the eastern Pacific. Power failures disrupted services, and Tocumen airport temporarily closed before reopening. The Panama Canal continued operating normally [3][41][20]. Emergency teams are waiting for tremors to subside before inspecting damaged structures [41].

Key Points

  • Shaking estimate: The USGS estimated that about 4.6 million people experienced moderate to severe shaking, including roughly 43,000 who experienced at least severe shaking [9][5].
  • Economic risk: The USGS estimated potential economic losses at 1% to 10% of Panama’s gross domestic product, pending damage assessments [3][26].
  • Aftershock sequence: Panama’s University recorded more than 45 aftershocks, while emergency operations director René García later reported 147. Some reached magnitude 6.6 [12][41].
  • Precautionary closures: Authorities suspended school classes nationwide, and Panama City’s Metro halted service on two lines and evacuated passengers from some stations [41][28].
  • Regional alerts: Tsunami advisories covered Pacific coastlines including Colombia, Ecuador, Peru and Mexico before warning systems declared the threat over [20][11].

Technical Details

  • Shallow focus: The USGS placed the first quake at a depth of roughly 10 to 12.6 kilometers. Shallow earthquakes can produce stronger surface effects than deeper ones [12][20].
  • Second quake: The magnitude 6.6 tremor was centered about 8 miles west of Río Grande on Panama’s Pacific coast, at a depth of about 6 miles [12].

Humanitarian Impact

Several patients were evacuated from an affected hospital in Penonomé and received care outdoors [41]. Panama City’s public transit system suspended service on two metro lines and evacuated passengers from some stations [41].

International Reactions

  • 🇺🇸 United States: The U.S. Embassy in Panama said it was monitoring the aftermath and checking on the welfare of U.S. citizens in the country [7].

Travel Advisory

  • Coastal safety: During active tsunami alerts, the embassy advised people near the coast to move to higher ground and follow current local guidance [53].

Q&A

Q: Why was the earthquake felt in Panama City, far from the epicenter?

A: Panama’s geosciences institute director said seismic signals traveled across the country from the active Azuero-Soná fault system, making the shaking widely perceptible [3].

Action Items

  • Avoid entering earthquake-damaged buildings: Follow the U.S. Embassy’s advice to stay away from damaged structures and monitor official local emergency broadcasts for updates [53].
  • Check airport and flight status before traveling through Panama: Tocumen resumed operations after a temporary closure, but travelers should confirm current arrangements with their airline or airport [41].
