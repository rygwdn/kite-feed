Minibus collision at French Channel Tunnel terminal injures 19

Category: World

A minibus carrying truck drivers struck a departing vehicle-shuttle train at the Channel Tunnel terminal in Coquelles, northern France, on Thursday, Oct. 15, injuring 19 people, French authorities said [4][8][7]. The injured included the minibus driver and 18 passengers. The driver and one passenger were seriously hurt and airlifted to a hospital in Lille. Officials said none of the injuries were life-threatening [1][4][7]. The crash occurred on a loading platform as the drivers were preparing to board LeShuttle, which carries vehicles and passengers through the tunnel. Authorities opened an inquiry and said the cause was unknown. The Boulogne-sur-Mer prosecutor’s office said it was investigating involuntary injuries [12][14]. LeShuttle service resumed after a suspension of about 90 minutes while emergency crews treated and evacuated the injured. Eurostar passenger trains and freight services were not disrupted, authorities and Eurostar said [5][4][12].

Key Points

  • Emergency response: About 200 emergency responders and other personnel went to the scene; consulates representing the injured passengers’ nationalities were notified [4][7][12].
  • Passenger details: All 19 injured people were men of various nationalities, and the minibus driver was an employee of tunnel operator Eurotunnel [4][14].
  • Tunnel traffic: The tunnel carried 3.4 million vehicles in 2025, while Eurostar reported 11.8 million passengers that year [4][1].

Historical Background

The Channel Tunnel has previously experienced fires involving freight shuttles. After a November 1996 fire, freight traffic was suspended for several months for repairs and reviews of safety procedures. A September 2008 fire also reduced traffic for several months [14][16].
