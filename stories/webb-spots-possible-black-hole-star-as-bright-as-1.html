Webb spots possible ‘black hole star’ as bright as 100 billion suns

Category: Science

Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope have identified an unusually bright red object in the early universe that may represent a new class of object, described as a “black hole star” [2][5][6][7][8][9]. The object appears about the size of the solar system, dates to roughly 660 million years after the Big Bang, and radiates about 100 billion Suns’ worth of energy, far more than any known star could physically produce [1][6][7][8][9]. The Nature study described in several reports proposes that the source is powered not by conventional nuclear fusion but by a central accreting black hole embedded in a dense, reddening gas envelope [1][5][6][7]. Lead author Rohan Naidu and colleagues estimate the hidden black hole could be about 100,000 times the mass of the Sun, while the surrounding gas makes the object resemble an enormous star [6]. The research targets the unresolved identity of JWST’s “little red dots,” compact red objects common in early-universe images since Webb began revealing them in 2022 [3][5]. A separate NASA-linked analysis of the spiral galaxy WISEA J123635.56+621424.2, nicknamed the “Saguaro,” found that if the galaxy were observed at much greater distance, its spiral structure would vanish and leave only a compact red center resembling those early objects [3].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Population: About 300 little red dots have been found, spanning roughly 600 million to 1.6 billion years after the Big Bang [5].
  • Rejected explanation: Researchers first considered whether ordinary dust reddened the source, but its spectrum did not match that interpretation [6].
  • Distribution: LRDs appear common at high redshift but decline sharply at lower redshifts, leaving astronomers to ask what they become as the universe ages [3].

Perspectives

Rohan Naidu and co-authors: The research team argues that gas-enshrouded black holes could account for little red dots and help explain the unexpectedly massive supermassive black holes JWST has detected in the very early universe.

Sources: Universe Today, The Hindu

NASA-linked Saguaro research team: The team suggests little red dots may be a temporary phase of highly active supermassive black holes, based on how the Saguaro galaxy’s compact red center would appear if viewed at early-universe distances.

Sources: ScienceDaily

Scientific Significance

  • Uncertainty: The evidence depends on spectral interpretation and modeling rather than direct imaging of the black hole, and the researchers describe their interpretation of the object as evolving rapidly [6].
  • Limitations and future work: The Saguaro analysis proposes one possible evolutionary pathway for little red dots, not a single explanation for every compact red source JWST has found [3].

Historical Background

JWST began science operations in 2022 and soon revealed compact red sources in the early universe that became known as little red dots [3][common]. Their abundance at high redshift challenged earlier hypotheses involving nearby active galactic nuclei, low-metallicity Population III stars or ordinary new-star interpretations [5].
