President Trump predicts Iran war will end soon

Category: World

President Trump told reporters in the Oval Office on Aug. 6 that he believed the war with Iran would end “pretty soon,” saying he did not think Tehran could “go much longer” [6][10][42][59]. He said he was involved in talks with Iran and that an agreement “could be soon,” while declining to say that a Strait of Hormuz deal had been reached [23][49][60][29]. Trump described the Strait of Hormuz as “sort of open right now,” said a U.S. Navy-led blockade gave Washington control over the waterway, and warned that mines or attacks could still deter commercial shipping [31][43][54][38]. He also said the U.S. had near-unlimited supplies of some powerful munitions but tighter inventories of others, after Reuters reported that the U.S. Army had used much of its global stockpile of highly accurate long-range missiles during the five-month war [6][39][51][45]. Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf responded on X by accusing Washington of switching between threats and negotiation offers as a failed pressure tactic [7][62][38].

Key Points

  • Exit options: Reuters described Trump as facing either a provisional Iran-Oman arrangement over Hormuz or sharper military escalation, with analysts saying neither path offers an easy exit [25][53][36][35].
  • Production push: Trump said U.S. defense companies are building additional facilities for Patriot and Tomahawk missiles as Washington tries to replenish constrained inventories [37][41][39][45].
  • Internal dispute: The White House and Pentagon denied a Washington Post account that Trump pressed Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth at Camp David over ammunition shortages that could limit new Iran strikes [63][2][54].
  • Nuclear rationale: Trump said his Iran policy aimed to prevent Tehran from obtaining a nuclear weapon and said he preferred a deal because he did not want people killed [33][57][31].

Perspectives

Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf: Ghalibaf wrote that Washington should “acknowledge the facts” and fulfill commitments, rejecting Trump’s sequence of attack threats and negotiation claims.

Technical Details

  • Inventory stress: Reports said five months of strikes consumed about 65% of U.S. Patriot interceptors, 38% of THAAD missiles, and nearly half of Tomahawk cruise missiles [27][6].

Industry Impact

  • Oil shipping: Iran’s draft proposal reported by Fars would bar U.S., Israeli and other “hostile” vessels from Hormuz and impose fines of up to 20% of cargo value for violations [19].
  • Energy markets: Trump linked an end to the war with lower gasoline prices and said crude oil had fallen to around $75 per barrel [33][23].

International Reactions

  • 🇴🇲 Oman: Oman has negotiated with Iran over the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said geographic areas for a route had been agreed while a joint statement was being finalized [19].

Travel Advisory

  • Safety: Maritime travel near the Strait of Hormuz remains risky due to potential mines or attacks; avoid nonessential voyages unless official navigation authorities clear the route [31][38].
  • Air travel: Qatar Airways resumed flights to countries affected by Iranian strikes [18]. Check airline and airport notices before booking routes through the Gulf region.
