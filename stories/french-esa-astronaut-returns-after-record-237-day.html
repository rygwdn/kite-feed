French ESA astronaut returns after record 237-day space station mission

Category: Science

ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot returned to Europe on Oct. 10, landing in Cologne after spending 237 days, five hours and 18 minutes aboard the International Space Station with Crew-12 [3][4]. Her mission set a European record for a single spaceflight, surpassing Luca Parmitano’s previous mark by more than 35 days [3][4]. Adenot launched on Feb. 13; Crew-12 undocked on Oct. 7 and splashed down on Oct. 8 before travelling to Houston for initial post-flight activities [3]. She will spend two to three weeks adjusting to Earth’s gravity and participating in data collection at the German Aerospace Center’s:envihab facility near the European Astronaut Centre, then continue rehabilitation and follow-up [3][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Spacewalk record: Adenot completed three spacewalks in 14 days, spending 19 hours and 42 minutes outside the station; she was the first French woman and second European woman to conduct an extravehicular activity [3][4].
  • Research workload: She spent about 218 hours working on 35 European payloads and more than 25 hours maintaining or upgrading systems in ESA’s Columbus research module [3][4].
  • Technology tests: The mission included testing a new European exercise device, metal 3D printing in orbit and commercial research payloads [3][4].

Timeline

  • 13 February 2026: Adenot launched from Cape Canaveral, Florida, for the εpsilon mission [3]
  • 7 October 2026: Crew-12 undocked at 12:05 GMT (14:05 CEST) [3]
  • 8 October 2026: Crew-12 splashed down after a 27-hour, 29-minute return journey [3]
  • 10 October 2026: Adenot arrived in Cologne aboard a French Air and Space Force Airbus A330 MRTT Phénix, landing at 04:21 GMT (06:21 CEST) [3]
