Google links Gemini to personal apps in Android Auto

Category: Technology

Google began rolling out Personal Intelligence for its Gemini AI assistant in Android Auto on Oct. 20, letting it draw—with user permission—on Gmail, Calendar, Photos, Maps, YouTube and Search to respond to drivers’ voice requests [35][3][10]. The feature can combine information across apps: a driver could ask for a flight’s gate and delay status, navigate to an appointment from a calendar entry, or find a business shown in a photo [35][18]. The rollout covers eligible personal Google accounts held by users 18 and older. It excludes Workspace business, enterprise and education accounts, as well as users in the European Economic Area, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and Nigeria [9][8]. Users who previously enabled Personal Intelligence in Gemini may find that permission carries over to the car. Google says users can choose which apps to connect or turn the feature off, and core navigation, calls and music remain available without personalization [2][35]. Vehicles with Google built-in, which run Google software in their infotainment systems rather than projecting a phone interface, are due to receive the feature in the coming months [11][10]. In those cars, Google says personalized responses appear only when the user’s phone is connected [35].

Personal Intelligence has two core strengths: retrieving specific details from, say, an email, photo or a place you saved in Google Maps to answer your question and reasoning across complex sources.

— Karan Daryanani, senior product manager for Gemini (Google blog)

Key Points

  • Google built-in controls: Ars Technica says drivers can switch off Personal Intelligence through the car’s settings even if it remains enabled on the phone [11].
  • Data handling: Google says personal context is not used to train foundational Gemini models, while Ars Technica notes that the company uses Gemini inputs and outputs and cannot promise that every personal detail will be filtered out [13][11].
  • Existing concerns: An XDA Developers reviewer said Gemini in Android Auto had produced long answers, handled commands inconsistently and asked unnecessary follow-up questions before this update [33].

Perspectives

Google: Google frames Gemini as a hands-free assistant for everyday tasks and brainstorming, with Personal Intelligence adding context to those voice interactions [35].

Ars Technica: The publication cautions that other people may interact with a car’s screen, so personal details in Gemini replies could be exposed to passengers [11].
