Iran strikes Bahrain as Hormuz tanker attacks deepen Gulf standoff

Category: World

Bahrain said an Iranian attack caused a fire at a civilian industrial facility on Saturday, Nov. 21, causing material damage but no injuries. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had instead targeted U.S. positions at the Al-Riffa and Awali bases with missiles and drones, claiming it hit two Patriot air-defense radars. Bahrain reported intercepting several incoming attacks, and the U.S. military had not confirmed damage to its bases [1][4][2][5]. Projectiles also struck two tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz, according to the U.K. Maritime Trade Operations center. Iran’s army said another vessel caught fire after hitting a mine in a restricted area, while the U.S. military said it had cleared mines from the main routes. The strait carried about one-fifth of global oil before the war. Iran has sought to restrict shipping through it, while the United States has imposed a counter-blockade of Iranian ports [3][12][2]. Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said a Qatari delegation held two hours of mediation talks in Tehran on Friday. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran would not discuss reopening the strait until its demands—including an end to the war and U.S. blockade—were met. Iran’s electricity company said rolling power cuts would begin Sunday, Nov. 22, citing fuel supply conditions [1][4][13].

Our position is clear: as long as Iran's demands are not met, the discussion on the Strait of Hormuz and its opening is pointless.

Key Points

  • Tanker damage: The maritime agency said one tanker was hit in the stern and another in its engine room; no casualties were reported in either incident [2][12].
  • Oil movement: U.S. Central Command said last month it had helped Gulf states export 1.5 billion barrels through Hormuz since the summer [3][1].
  • Nuclear red line: Araghchi said Iran would not allow its enriched-uranium stockpiles to leave the country, a central dispute in U.S.-Iran relations [3][13].
  • Regional warning: Bahrain’s air-defense interceptions caused shrapnel damage, and authorities issued public shelter alerts before and after an all-clear [2][5].
  • Power cuts: Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said strikes on energy infrastructure had worsened domestic energy problems ahead of winter [1][3].

Perspectives

Bahrain’s Foreign Ministry: It called on the U.N. Security Council to take firm measures to make Iran implement Resolution 2817, which condemns attacks on Gulf states and demands a halt to hostilities and maritime interference.

  • 🇶🇦🇩🇪 Qatar and Germany: Their joint statement condemned attacks and threats against commercial vessels and called for compliance with U.N. Security Council Resolution 2817 [22].

Q: What is Iran demanding before it will discuss reopening Hormuz?

A: Tehran has said it wants the war ended on all fronts, Iranian assets unfrozen, oil sanctions lifted and the U.S. counter-blockade of Iranian ports ended [4][2].
