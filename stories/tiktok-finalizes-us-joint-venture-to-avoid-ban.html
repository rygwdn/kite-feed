TikTok finalizes US joint venture to avoid ban

Category: Technology

TikTok says it has finalized a deal creating a new U.S. operating entity, TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC, designed to meet U.S. regulatory requirements and prevent the app from being banned in the United States [1][8][4][22]. Under the arrangement, ByteDance will keep a minority stake below the 20% threshold described by multiple outlets, while a group of non‑Chinese investors takes majority ownership [8][6][18][3]. TikTok says the new structure aims to protect U.S. user data and address national-security and privacy concerns that have driven years of political pressure and legal wrangling [6][3][10][4]. For users and creators, the immediate practical outcome is continuity: TikTok remains available in the U.S., with the company promising continued access to global content while it works on U.S.-focused safeguards around data, algorithms, and moderation [8][12][5].

Historical Background

U.S. pressure to separate TikTok’s U.S. operations from its Chinese parent has built over several years, driven by recurring claims about privacy and national-security risk tied to ByteDance’s China links [4][3][6]. Dawn reported that a law passed under President Joe Biden required ByteDance to sell TikTok’s U.S. operations or face a ban, setting the legal backdrop that the new joint venture aims to satisfy [6]. Several outlets describe the agreement as concluding a long-running saga over whether TikTok could keep operating in the U.S. [3][22][8].
