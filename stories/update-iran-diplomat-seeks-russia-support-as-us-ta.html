Update: Iran diplomat seeks Russia support as US talks stall

Category: World

In the latest turn, Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi traveled from Pakistan to Russia on April 27 after shuttle diplomacy that also took him through Oman, while planned in-person talks with U.S. envoys in Pakistan were put on hold [1][3][9][20][24]. President Trump said Tehran could contact Washington directly by phone if it wanted to negotiate an end to the war, after canceling a trip by U.S. envoys to Islamabad and citing confusion inside Iran’s leadership [1][7][10][4][15]. Pakistan has kept up its mediation effort and remains a key channel for messages between Tehran and Washington, with Araghchi meeting senior Pakistani civilian and military leaders during his brief return to Islamabad [1][12][19][22]. Uncertainty remains high, but the talks now seem to be shifting toward indirect exchanges, including messages relayed through mediators and Araghchi’s talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin [20][2][13].

Key Points

  • Pakistan channel: President Trump said Islamabad would remain part of the process even if talks continue by telephone instead of immediate face-to-face meetings [21].
  • Iran messages: Iran reportedly sent written messages to the United States through Pakistan outlining red lines, including on nuclear issues and the Strait of Hormuz, though Fars said these were not formal negotiations [20][23].
  • Hormuz proposal: Axios, as cited by multiple outlets, reported that Iran sent a proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end the war while postponing nuclear negotiations until a later stage [16][20].
  • Moscow meeting: Araghchi said his meeting with President Vladimir Putin would be a good opportunity to discuss developments in the war, underscoring Russia’s role as a diplomatic backstop for Tehran [2][16][13].

Perspectives

United States: President Trump said the United States canceled its envoys’ Islamabad trip and wants any renewed diplomacy to begin with direct outreach from Tehran, while insisting Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon.

Technical Details

  • Strait of Hormuz chokepoint: The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow maritime passage linking the Persian Gulf to open ocean routes, so blockades or shipping restrictions there can quickly disrupt global oil and gas flows [common].
  • Indirect diplomacy: The current process relies on written messages carried by mediators and shuttle visits through capitals such as Islamabad, Muscat, and Russia rather than a formal negotiating table, which can keep contact alive when direct meetings collapse [20][1][24].

Historical Background

The current diplomacy follows a war that Dawn said began with U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran on February 28, 2026 [22]. Earlier in April, the United States and Iran held face-to-face talks that raised hopes for a second round in Pakistan before that meeting was canceled [11][19]. The Strait of Hormuz has long been a strategic chokepoint for global energy supplies [common], which helps explain why maritime access and blockade issues are now central to the latest negotiations [20][18].
