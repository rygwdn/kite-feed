UK space cleanup mission poll puts Binface among leading names

Category: Science

A reader poll by The Register put “Binface” and “Binny McBinface” among the leading proposed names for the UK’s Active Debris Removal Mission, a planned effort to remove two defunct British satellites from orbit [2]. The suggestions are not described as the UK Space Agency’s official choice. ClearSpace UK is developing the mission under a contract worth about $81 million. The target launch date for its spacecraft is 2029, from Scotland’s SaxaVord Spaceport, with the two satellites due to be removed by 2032 [1][2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Other suggestions: Readers also proposed “Dusty Bin,” “Project TIDY” and “SHART,” an acronym for Satellite Harvesting And Re-entry Tug [2].
  • Mission approach: The spacecraft is designed to capture each satellite, move it to a lower orbit and release it, after which the satellite is expected to re-enter and burn up [1].
  • Reuse plans: ClearSpace is designing the cleanup spacecraft for possible refueling and reuse, which could allow it to conduct more than one disposal operation [1].

Scientific Significance

  • Orbital debris risk: The European Space Agency counted about 47,140 objects regularly tracked in Earth orbit as of Oct. 14, 2026, while estimating that millions of smaller pieces also circle the planet [1].
  • Mission demonstration: The project is intended to test whether one spacecraft can capture and dispose of multiple inactive satellites, but engineers have yet to demonstrate that it can do so safely [1].
