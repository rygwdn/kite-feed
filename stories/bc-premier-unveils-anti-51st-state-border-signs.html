B.C. premier unveils anti-51st-state border signs

Category: Canada

British Columbia Premier David Eby unveiled new signs for U.S. border crossings that read, “Welcome to British Columbia, Canada. Strong, proud, and will NEVER be the 51st state. Sorry!” as Canada’s counter-tariffs against the United States took effect Tuesday [2][4]. Speaking in Victoria beside the B.C. legislature, Eby said the province supports Ottawa’s response to President Trump’s tariffs and will keep U.S. liquor and wine off B.C. Liquor Store shelves [2][3][4]. Eby tied the message to the 25th anniversary of the Sept. 11, 2001, attacks, saying Canadians helped 8,500 stranded passengers at Vancouver International Airport and would do so again if needed [2]. He said “nobody wanted this trade war,” but argued that standing up for Canadian workers, businesses and the economy was a responsibility when they were targeted [3][4]. The premier also said the province is working with Ottawa on additional counter-measures, including a renewed Buy B.C. campaign and efforts to diversify trade beyond the United States [2][4]. Asked about potential losses, Eby said his government was working with Ottawa to identify the sectors likely to be hardest hit [3].
