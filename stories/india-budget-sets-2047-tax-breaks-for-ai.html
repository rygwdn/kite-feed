India budget sets 2047 tax breaks for AI

Category: Technology

India’s 2026–27 Union Budget put digital infrastructure and AI at the center of its tech-growth plan, including a proposal to offer foreign cloud providers a zero-tax regime on certain offshore revenues through 2047 if they run those workloads from Indian data centers [1][3][5][4]. The government and supporting coverage frame the move as a way to pull more global AI and cloud computing demand into India, while boosting domestic data-center capacity as hyperscalers expand locally [3][4]. Alongside cloud incentives, the budget introduced manufacturing-focused tax and policy tweaks that could make it easier for foreign firms to supply equipment to contract manufacturers without triggering taxation purely due to machine ownership—an adjustment Reuters and Firstpost describe as a win for Apple’s India supply-chain model [2][6]. The broader package also spotlights semiconductors, rare earths, and applied AI programs (including tools for agriculture), positioning these as building blocks for jobs, skills, and resilient supply chains [firstpost.com#7][firstpost.com#8][firstpost.com#9][firstpost.com#10][12].

Did you know: Rare earth elements are used in high-strength permanent magnets found in products like smartphones, wind turbines, and electric vehicle motors [common].

Key Points

  • Semiconductor funding: The budget’s “India semiconductor mission 2.0” includes an allocation of Rs 40,000 crore, which sources say drew praise from experts for strengthening India’s chip ambitions [firstpost.com#10].
  • Rare earth corridor: Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced a “rare earth corridor” spanning Odisha, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu to support domestic manufacturing and reduce import dependence [firstpost.com#9].
  • Farm AI tool: The government unveiled “Bharat Vistaar,” described as a multilingual AI agriculture tool aimed at improving productivity and reducing risk for farmers [firstpost.com#8].
  • Industry response: US-India industry bodies welcomed the budget as investment-friendly while urging faster execution of announced measures [11].
  • Supply-chain focus: DigiTimes highlighted new budget emphasis on electronics components plus AI-linked infrastructure to strengthen supply chains and boost manufacturing competitiveness [12].

Perspectives

Government of India (finance ministry): Budget messaging emphasizes “Make AI work for India,” pairing cloud incentives with funding for semiconductors, R&D, and applied AI programs [firstpost.com#7][firstpost.com#8][firstpost.com#10].

Global cloud providers and investors: Coverage frames the long tax incentive as designed to attract global AI workloads as Amazon, Google, and Microsoft expand data-center investment in India [3].

Apple and contract manufacturing ecosystem: Reuters and Firstpost describe the equipment-ownership tax change as benefiting foreign firms funding machines for Indian contract manufacturers, a policy shift Apple lobbied for [2][6].

Timeline

  • Sunday: Finance minister presented Union Budget 2026–27 with AI focus [firstpost.com#7].
  • February 1, 2026: Finance ministry unveiled budget emphasizing electronics and supply chains [12].

Technical Details

  • AI workloads: AI workloads are compute-intensive training and inference tasks that cloud providers run on large-scale GPU/accelerator clusters, making data-center capacity and power availability key constraints [common].
  • Data center localization: The budget-linked incentive targets foreign cloud services sold outside India if the provider runs those workloads from Indian data centers, effectively encouraging infrastructure siting in-country [3][5].
  • Contract manufacturing equipment: The tax rule change described in coverage aims to ensure that foreign ownership of machinery supplied to Indian contract manufacturers does not by itself create taxable income for the foreign entity [2][6].

Industry Impact

  • Cloud infrastructure: A long tax incentive for offshore cloud revenues could intensify competition among countries to host AI compute, pushing hyperscalers and colocation firms to evaluate India more seriously for global workloads [3][4].
  • Electronics manufacturing: The equipment-ownership tax change may make India contract manufacturing structures simpler for foreign brands and could encourage additional tooling and capex commitments [2][6].
  • Critical minerals: The proposed rare earth corridor could affect magnet, motor, and electronics component supply chains by aiming to expand domestic inputs used across EVs, consumer electronics, and industrial tech [firstpost.com#9][12].
  • Agri-tech adoption: A government-backed multilingual AI tool for farmers may spur broader experimentation with advisory and risk-management services in rural markets, creating opportunities for local language AI and data platforms [firstpost.com#8].

Historical Background

Over the past decade, India has used policy incentives to expand electronics manufacturing and attract global supply chains, with contract manufacturing becoming central to smartphone production [common]. The current budget builds on that trajectory by pairing cloud/data-center incentives with new spending on semiconductors and materials that underpin modern hardware stacks [12][firstpost.com#10].

Q&A

Q: Which cloud revenues qualify for the proposed zero-tax treatment?

A: Coverage describes the benefit as applying to services sold outside India when workloads run from Indian data centers, but it does not detail every qualifying revenue category in these articles [3][5][4].

Q: Does the budget specify which agencies will deliver “Bharat Vistaar”?

A: The articles describe Bharat Vistaar as a multilingual AI agriculture tool launched under the budget but do not provide implementing-agency details in the provided coverage [firstpost.com#8].

Q: What kind of execution steps do industry bodies want accelerated?

A: NDTV reports the groups called for faster execution broadly; the article does not enumerate specific step-by-step reforms in the excerpted description [11].

Action Items

  • If you run a startup, reassess India cloud placement options: Compare latency, compliance needs, and potential tax treatment for serving non-Indian customers from Indian regions as rules become clearer [3][5].
  • If you work in hardware, track semiconductor and rare-earth programs by state: Follow announcements tied to the semiconductor mission 2.0 and the four-state rare earth corridor to identify likely clusters for suppliers and hiring [firstpost.com#10][firstpost.com#9].
