U.S. strikes Iran’s Qeshm Island as Tehran offers to reopen Hormuz

Category: World

U.S. forces carried out limited strikes on Iran’s Qeshm Island on Monday, according to U.S. officials, as Tehran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days if Washington accepts its conditions. President Donald Trump said a deal with Iran “could happen,” while Vice President JD Vance said negotiations had made progress [13][6][3][5]. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said indirect talks were continuing through Qatar and Pakistan after talks in Doha ended without a breakthrough. Tehran’s proposal links reopening the shipping route to a broader settlement that would end the war, lift sanctions and the U.S. blockade on Iranian ports, and release frozen Iranian assets [2][17][8]. The talks continued ahead of the Nov. 3 U.S. midterm elections as the eight-month conflict drove volatility in energy markets. An official familiar with the talks said a draft agreement was circulating for feedback and another meeting might follow. Iran warned that any further attack would draw an immediate response [1][13][37].

Key Points

  • Two-day sequence: Washington would take specified steps on the first day, with shipping through Hormuz resuming on the second [17][8].
  • Nuclear condition: Vance said Washington wants a firm, verifiable commitment that Iran will not rebuild its nuclear program, while also saying the program had already been destroyed [5].
  • Negotiation tracks: Araghchi said reopening Hormuz was an immediate issue separate from nuclear and other disputes, and Tehran wanted to resolve it before broader talks [17].
  • Election pressure: The Nov. 3 midterms were taking place as higher fuel prices and dissatisfaction over the war weighed on Trump’s Republicans [2][13].
  • Risk assessment: Iranian sources said they believed a new U.S. attack was still more likely than a deal, despite Monday’s fall in oil prices [1].

Perspectives

U.S. administration: Vance said the administration had not set a deadline for renewed strikes [5][3].

Historical Background

The June memorandum provided for an immediate halt to hostilities on all fronts, including Lebanon. Washington later accused Tehran of violating the agreement’s Hormuz terms before resuming large-scale strikes. Iran’s September plan set a seven-day sequence: the strait would reopen on day six, followed by renewed negotiations on day seven; Trump rejected it [12][14][20][17].
