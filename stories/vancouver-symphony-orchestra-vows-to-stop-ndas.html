Vancouver Symphony Orchestra vows to stop NDAs

Category: Canada

The Vancouver Symphony Orchestra says it will no longer ask people who report sexual misconduct to sign confidentiality agreements, following public allegations by former VSO violinist Esther Hwang about pressure to accept an NDA during a 2019 complaint process [1]. In an open letter, the orchestra also said it does not intend to pursue legal action against Hwang after she spoke publicly despite an NDA [1][3][4]. The announcement is being framed by the orchestra as a step toward more transparent, survivor-centered handling of misconduct complaints in the arts community [1].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Open letter: The VSO circulated an open letter addressed to the classical-music community outlining its commitment to avoid confidentiality agreements in sexual-misconduct cases [1].
  • Retaliation claim: Hwang has alleged she experienced retaliation from the orchestra after reporting that she had been raped by a senior musician, according to coverage of her account [3][4].
  • Settlement pressure: Hwang told The Globe and Mail that she felt the organization pressured her to sign an NDA as part of resolving her complaint, an allegation that prompted the VSO’s public response [1].
  • Legal restraint: Multiple reports say the orchestra stated it will not sue Hwang for breaking the NDA to speak publicly [1][3][4].

Technical Details

  • Non-disclosure agreement (NDA): A legal contract that restricts someone from sharing specified information; in this case, it was used in connection with resolving a sexual-misconduct complaint, which the VSO now says it will no longer do [1].
  • Confidentiality agreement: A broader term for agreements that limit disclosure; the VSO says it will not ask sexual-misconduct complainants to sign these going forward [1].

Industry Impact

  • Performing arts workplaces: The VSO’s stance may add momentum to sector-wide discussions about whether confidentiality agreements discourage reporting and limit public accountability in arts organizations [1].
  • Employment practices: By stating it won’t pursue legal action against a former employee who spoke out, the orchestra signals a less adversarial approach that could influence how other cultural institutions manage dispute resolution and reputational risk [1][3].

Historical Background

The VSO’s announcement follows allegations tied to a complaint Hwang says she made in 2019 against a man described as a senior member of the orchestra at the time [1]. The current policy shift comes after Hwang went public in late 2025 about her experience with the complaint process and the use of a confidentiality agreement [1].
