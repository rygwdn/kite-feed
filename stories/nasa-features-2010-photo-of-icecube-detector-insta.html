NASA features 2010 photo of IceCube detector installation in Antarctica

Category: Science

NASA’s Oct. 11 astronomy feature presented a 2010 photograph of one of the last IceCube detector strings being lowered into a borehole near the South Pole [2]. The feature described the installation as part of an observatory built to detect high-energy neutrinos from powerful cosmic processes [2]. The photograph shows how astronomers installed IceCube: They lowered a string of light detectors into a hole melted in the ice, which later refroze around the detectors [2]. NASA said the detectors register blue light from secondary particles created when neutrinos collide with molecules in the surrounding ice [2].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Detector scale: IceCube uses almost 100 vertical holes near the South Pole and spans about one cubic kilometer of Antarctic ice [2][4].
  • New observation: Neutrinos provide an additional way to study the universe, complementing other forms of astronomical observation [4].
  • Facility record: NASA described IceCube as the largest neutrino detector ever created [2].

Technical Details

  • Sensor size: Each vertical string carries basketball-sized light detectors [2].
  • Detection record: IceCube has detected many neutrinos [2].

Scientific Significance

  • Nobel recognition: The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics recognized Francis Halzen’s contributions to IceCube; NASA noted that he was a founding leader of the collaboration [4][2].
