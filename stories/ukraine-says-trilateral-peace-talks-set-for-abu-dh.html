Ukraine says trilateral peace talks set for Abu Dhabi

Category: World

Ukraine says U.S.-brokered trilateral talks with Russia will resume in Abu Dhabi on February 4-5, with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy saying Kyiv is ready for a “substantive discussion” aimed at a “real and dignified end to the war” [1][4][6][7][12][3]. The renewed diplomacy comes as fighting continues, including Russian drone attacks reported by Ukrainian officials that caused deaths and injuries and struck civilian infrastructure [10][5][2][3][11]. Reports also describe severe winter strain on services, with widespread heating and power disruptions in Kyiv after a grid malfunction, highlighting the pressure on civilians as negotiations restart [3][7].

Primary Source: View original article

Ukraine is ready for a substantive discussion, and we are interested in ensuring that the outcome brings us closer to a real and dignified end to the war.

— Volodymyr Zelenskyy (Al-Monitor (Reuters))

Did you know: Ukraine’s energy minister said the Moldova-linked line malfunction triggered automatic protection systems in Ukraine’s grid [7].

Key Points

  • Scheduling shift: Zelenskyy said meetings previously expected Sunday were pushed back, and he had been awaiting clarity from U.S. mediators on timing and venue [7][12].
  • US-Russia contact: U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff said he held “productive and constructive” discussions in Florida with Kremlin representative Kirill Dmitriev ahead of the next round [7].
  • Sticking point: Multiple reports say territory—particularly the Donbas—remains central to the impasse, with Ukraine rejecting Moscow’s demands to cede more land [7][3][12].
  • Energy disruption: Ukraine’s energy minister said a technical malfunction affecting lines linking Ukraine and Moldova triggered cascading outages and automatic protection shutdowns, leaving large parts of Kyiv without heat after a broader grid failure [7][3].
  • US attendance unclear: RT cited U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio as saying U.S. negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner would not attend the next Abu Dhabi round, though “there might be a US presence” [12].

  • Saturday: Grid malfunction triggered outages; heating cut to many buildings [3][7].
  • Sunday (February 1, 2026): Zelenskyy announced Abu Dhabi talks set for Feb 4-5 [4][6].
  • Wednesday and Thursday: Delegations expected to meet in Abu Dhabi for next round [1][6].

Technical Details

  • Cascading outage: Ukraine’s energy minister described a cascading outage in the power grid after a malfunction on interconnector lines, which can trigger automatic protection systems that shut parts of a grid to prevent equipment damage [7][common].
  • Drone strikes: Several outlets attribute recent casualties and damage to Russian drone attacks, underscoring how relatively low-cost unmanned systems can still inflict serious harm and disrupt civilian life [10][2][5].

Industry Impact

  • Energy utilities: The reported grid malfunction and cascading outages, including impacts extending to neighboring Moldova, highlight how cross-border interconnectors can transmit disruptions and raise resilience demands for operators [7][3].
  • Negotiation logistics: The Abu Dhabi venue and the reported uncertainty over who will attend from the U.S. side show how diplomatic scheduling and delegation makeup can shift quickly amid other global crises [7][12].

Historical Background

Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in February 2022, escalating the conflict into a prolonged war [common]. Several articles note the current negotiations mark a return to a U.S.-mediated “trilateral” format, with an initial late-January round described as constructive but not resolving territorial questions [12][3].
