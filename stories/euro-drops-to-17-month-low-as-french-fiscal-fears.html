Euro drops to 17-month low as French fiscal fears spread

Category: Business

The euro fell as much as 0.8% to $1.1161 in Asian trading on Monday, its weakest level since May 2025, as investors reacted to French fiscal and political uncertainty and the prospect of an early Spanish election [1][2][13][10]. The currency had already declined for four consecutive weeks, while elevated U.S. yields and demand for safer assets supported the dollar [15][18]. The yield gap between French and German government bonds reached its widest since 2011 on Friday, as concerns mounted that political gridlock could obstruct efforts to rein in France’s public finances before its 2027 election [6][13]. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez announced an early election for Nov. 29, adding to political uncertainty [10]. Some analysts warn that the euro remains vulnerable to further losses and that France’s market stress could spread; others say it is too soon to call this a euro-area crisis [13][11]. France’s government plans €54 billion in savings for 2027, while Spain’s vote is scheduled for Nov. 29 [9][10].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Fiscal plans: Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu’s government plans €54 billion in savings for 2027 while France prepares to issue about €340 billion in new government bonds [9].
  • Currency pairs: The euro also lost about 0.5% against the Swiss franc and 0.26% against sterling, extending its decline against the franc to a third session [6][13].
  • Dollar support: The Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index reached its highest level since late June [6].

Perspectives

Chris Weston, Pepperstone: Weston cautioned investors against betting on a quick euro rebound and said the move could mark the start of contagion from France’s fiscal problems [11].

Sources: HotNews

Ninghui Liu, State Street Investment Management: Liu described the situation as a France-specific problem rather than a euro-area crisis at that stage, but said other euro-area countries would not stand aside if pressure intensified [11].

Sources: HotNews

JPMorgan strategists: The strategists warned that the euro had not fully priced in French bond-market stress and remained vulnerable to further losses, particularly against the Swiss franc and yen [13].

Sources: 24 Chasa

Timeline

  • September 2026: French finance ministry projected public debt at 119.3% of GDP this year [7]
  • 2 October 2026: French-German bond yield gap reached its widest level since 2011 [6][13]
  • 5 October 2026: Euro touched $1.1161 during Asian trading, its weakest since May 2025 [13][6]
  • 29 November 2026: Spain is scheduled to hold the early election announced by Sánchez [10]

Technical Details

  • Option-triggered selling: Traders said Asian hedge funds sold euros against dollars in spot markets; the move triggered option levels and prompted additional selling [10][13].
  • Sovereign-risk pricing: Credit-default swaps provide protection against a borrower’s failure to repay debt. A sharp rise in French sovereign CDS costs last week drew attention to the public finances of France and other highly indebted euro-area countries [13][6].

Business Angle

  • France’s plan to issue about €340 billion in new government bonds exposes it to higher refinancing costs if bond yields remain elevated [9].
  • A stronger dollar can affect companies’ reported overseas revenues and the cost of dollar-denominated inputs. The current move reflects both U.S. yields and euro-area risk [common][15][18].
