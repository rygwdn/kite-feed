OpenAI to test ads in ChatGPT for U.S. adults

Category: Technology

OpenAI says it will begin testing advertising inside ChatGPT in the coming weeks, starting with logged-in adult users in the United States on the Free tier and the new ChatGPT Go plan [5][3][6]. The company says subscribers to higher-priced premium tiers will remain ad-free [7][6]. OpenAI frames the move as a way to support the high costs of developing and running AI systems while continuing to offer a free option, and it says it will not sell user conversations or data to advertisers [7][5][1].

Primary Source: View original article

You need to know that your data and conversations are protected and never sold to advertisers

— OpenAI (The Hacker News)

Did you know: The first ad tests are expected to focus on adult users in the U.S. before expanding elsewhere [3].

Key Points

  • New $8 tier: ChatGPT Go is positioned as a budget subscription option that sits between free access and higher premium plans, offering expanded usage limits, access to GPT-5.2, and enhanced memory features [4].
  • Minor safeguards: OpenAI says ChatGPT will avoid showing ads to users who indicate they are under 18, and it may also suppress ads when it detects a user is a minor [6].
  • Rollout scope: OpenAI says the initial ad tests will be limited to the U.S. and to users who are logged in, focusing on adults before any broader expansion [5].
  • Search-like shift: The change highlights how chatbot conversations are increasingly being treated as an “intent” surface similar to search, where commercial messages can appear alongside answers [3].
  • Competitive context: Android Police notes that ads have started appearing in Google’s AI Mode in Search, while Google has publicly pushed back on the idea of ads in Gemini “for the time being,” underscoring different monetization approaches across major AI products [2].

Perspectives

OpenAI: The company says advertising helps fund AI development while keeping a free option, and it emphasizes that conversations and user data are protected and not sold to advertisers.

Technical Details

  • Logged-in targeting: OpenAI says early ad tests will apply to logged-in adult users in the U.S., indicating ads will be gated by account state and age eligibility rather than shown broadly to everyone at once [5][3].
  • Age gating: The rollout includes protections intended to reduce ad exposure for minors, including honoring an under-18 setting and using detection signals to identify likely minor users [6].
  • Memory feature: ChatGPT Go includes “enhanced memory,” a feature that stores helpful details to personalize future responses; product coverage ties this to Go’s positioning between free and premium tiers [4].

Industry Impact

  • Publishers and SEO: If users increasingly ask ChatGPT instead of using traditional search, publishers may see traffic patterns shift further, while ad budgets could follow attention into chat-based interfaces [3].
  • Ad-tech competition: The move puts OpenAI into more direct competition with established ad platforms, as coverage compares it with how ads are already appearing in Google’s AI Mode in Search and discusses differing stances on chatbot ads [2].
  • Consumer subscriptions: A cheaper paid tier that still includes ads may pressure other consumer AI tools to offer more granular plan choices (free, low-cost, premium) instead of a simple free-vs-paid split [4][5].

Historical Background

Online services commonly use a freemium model—free access supported by ads, with subscriptions removing ads and adding features [common]. Search engines built large ad businesses by matching ads to user intent expressed through queries; conversational AI products now increasingly handle similar “help me decide” moments that advertisers value [common]. OpenAI has historically monetized ChatGPT largely through subscriptions, so adding ads marks a shift toward a second major revenue stream [1][2].

Q&A

Q: Will ads change how answers are ranked or worded inside ChatGPT?

A: OpenAI’s announcement emphasized that conversations are protected and not sold to advertisers, but the provided reports do not specify how (or whether) ads will influence response generation beyond being displayed to eligible users [5].

Q: What ad formats will appear first—banners, sponsored links, or inline recommendations?

A: The coverage says OpenAI plans to begin testing ads in the coming weeks, but it does not detail specific formats in the cited articles [3][1].

Q: How will OpenAI determine whether someone is a minor if they don’t self-report?

A: TechSpot reports OpenAI may suppress ads if it detects a user is a minor, but it does not describe the detection method in the article [6].

Action Items

  • Review your ChatGPT plan settings: If you want to avoid ads, compare whether upgrading to a premium tier makes sense for your usage, since premium users are expected to remain ad-free [7][6].
  • Check age settings for teen accounts: For households with teens, ensure the user’s age is set correctly because OpenAI says it will avoid showing ads to users who indicate they are under 18 [6].
