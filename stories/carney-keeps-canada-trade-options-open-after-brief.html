Carney keeps Canada trade options open after brief Trump meeting

Category: Canada

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and U.S. President Donald Trump met for just under 20 minutes in Shenzhen on Nov. 18. Their offices said they would continue working toward a trade agreement. The meeting was not on Carney’s public itinerary. Carney said Canada could not simply wait for Washington and was pursuing other markets [10][2]. At a parliamentary hearing, Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc defended Carney’s account of why talks broke down in August, which U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer had disputed. Canada’s chief trade negotiator, Janice Charette, told MPs the government must prepare for an arrangement that may not be tariff-free [11]. Carney has pointed to agreements with Southeast Asian partners and the Philippines and said Canada expects to sign a trade deal with India in the coming months. He has also defended trade diversification as protection against overdependence on any single partner [2][17].

We’re not going to sign something that doesn’t make sense for Canada

— Prime Minister Mark Carney (SooToday)

Key Points

  • Tariff objective: Chief trade negotiator Janice Charette said Ottawa aims for the lowest tariff rate on the narrowest range of goods, with the best access for Canadian businesses and workers [11].
  • Congressional limits: Analysts cited by the National Observer said Democrats may be able to challenge tariffs imposed under Section 338, but are less likely to target separate sectoral duties or the 10% levy tied to forced-labour claims [11].
  • China trade: Carney said Canada had reduced tariffs and resolved more than 90% of trade irritants in negotiations with China [2].

Perspectives

Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand said regular contact between Carney and Trump had helped Ottawa keep raising its concerns during negotiations. She described the leaders’ relationship as productive [2].

Sources: SooToday

Conservative international trade critic Stephanie Kusie said closer engagement with China raised concerns because of its human-rights record and past tariffs on Canada. She accused Carney of using diversification to distract from the lack of a U.S. deal [15].

White House trade adviser Peter Navarro called Carney’s remarks about possible Chinese automakers in Canada “offensive in the extreme” and warned that China could overwhelm Canada economically [13].

Sources: Agence France-Presse via Insider Paper

Timeline

  • 2025: Carney began restoring ties with Xi at APEC in South Korea [2]
  • January 2026: Carney and Xi met in Beijing, continuing efforts to restore ties [2]
  • August 2026: Carney’s government walked away from formal trade talks with Washington [11][2]

Historical Background

Canada’s relationship with China had nearly collapsed after Beijing detained two Canadians in retaliation for Canada’s arrest of a Chinese telecom executive at the request of the United States. Accusations of Chinese interference in Canadian federal elections added to the tensions [2].
