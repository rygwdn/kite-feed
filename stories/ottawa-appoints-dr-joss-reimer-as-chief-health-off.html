Ottawa appoints Dr. Joss Reimer as chief health officer

Category: Canada

Canada has appointed Dr. Joss Reimer as the country’s next chief public health officer for a three-year term. The appointment takes effect April 1, 2026, according to the federal government and multiple news outlets [3][1][2][6][7][8]. Reimer will succeed Dr. Theresa Tam, according to the reporting [2][7]. Reimer steps into the role as health officials and commentators flag renewed concerns about vaccine-preventable diseases—and the broader work of rebuilding public confidence in public health guidance [1][6][8]. In interviews and early remarks covered by Canadian media, Reimer has pointed to countering health misinformation and disinformation and restoring trust as central to making public health advice land more effectively across the country [6][8][4].

Primary Source: View original article

Key Points

  • Announcement: Health Minister Marjorie Michel announced Reimer’s appointment as Canada’s next chief public health officer [3].
  • Top focus (how): Reimer told media she wants Ottawa to take a more proactive approach to countering false health information, with the goal of rebuilding public trust [6][8][4].
  • Leadership background: The Toronto Star noted Reimer has held multiple leadership roles, including chief medical officer for the Winnipeg Regional Health Authority [7].
  • Succession: The Globe and Mail and the Toronto Star reported Reimer is replacing Dr. Theresa Tam, who retired in June [2][7].
