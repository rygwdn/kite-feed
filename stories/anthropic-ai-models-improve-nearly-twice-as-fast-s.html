Anthropic AI models improve nearly twice as fast, study finds

Category: AI

Anthropic’s AI models are improving on well-defined tasks nearly twice as fast as they did from March 2024 to October 2025, according to an independent assessment by AI safety nonprofit Model Evaluation and Threat Research (METR). After an 11-week review of company data, staff interviews and results from unreleased models, METR estimated that benchmark progress since October 2025 was 1.9 times the earlier rate, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5 to 2.8 times [15][2]. METR judged that AI tools likely account for most of the acceleration, mainly by assisting researchers rather than autonomously handling the most important parts of model training. It found no convincing evidence that faster growth in computing power used for training caused the change. It also found no clear bottlenecks in computing power, data or algorithmic progress likely to slow development over the next year, though it cautioned that this assessment was uncertain [15][6]. The group warned that further automation could compound AI-driven progress and make risks harder to assess. Anthropic co-founder Sam McCandlish said the company withheld much of the supporting evidence because it was commercially sensitive and could help competitors accelerate development. He said Anthropic plans to publish its own analysis and safety measures in the coming weeks. The company is preparing for a potential public listing later in November [5][16].

Slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models.

Key Points

  • Estimate uncertainty: METR’s alternative modeling choices produced acceleration estimates ranging from 1.1 to 2.9 times the earlier rate. The group said its benchmark measure may not reflect progress on less clearly defined real-world tasks [6][15].
  • Unreleased models: METR examined three Anthropic models trained since July 2026, including one whose training finished in September or October [7][22].
  • Staff productivity: In an internal poll, staff reported average productivity gains of about 12 times from using Anthropic’s most capable internal model. METR called the poll weak evidence because respondents may not represent staff broadly [22][6].
  • Review limits: METR conducted the review with the equivalent of about 2.5 full-time staff and roughly 15 employee interviews. Anthropic could redact nonpublic material and removed much of the evidence METR considered most informative [6][15].
  • Benchmark saturation: METR noted that one internal model scored above 50% on all 15 benchmarks used to assess it, a saturation issue that could make future capabilities harder to measure [6].

Perspectives

Dario Amodei, Anthropic chief executive: He advocated embedding external evaluators within leading AI labs and named METR as an appropriate third-party evaluator.
