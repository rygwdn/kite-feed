Microsoft outage exposed server-health failures across cloud workplace systems

Category: Technology

Microsoft’s investigation into its global Microsoft 365 outage points to backend servers that failed automated health checks, leaving the remaining systems struggling with heavier traffic. Engineers’ attempts to restart servers also failed when traffic returned, while a later network change exposed a problem introduced during an earlier capacity upgrade [13][9][12]. The failure disrupted Exchange Online, Microsoft’s cloud email and scheduling service, and affected Outlook, Teams and Copilot across government, business and public services. Microsoft said neither a cyberattack nor artificial-intelligence agents caused the incident. Engineers gradually restored services while working through an email backlog [36][3][5]. Digital Trends reported that engineers deployed a patch targeting an authentication and routing component. Charles Lamanna, Microsoft executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform, said the company would publish a review with a step-by-step timeline and repair measures. Microsoft’s notice to customers said it would issue a preliminary report within two business days and a final report within five [9][12].

I'm working closely with the engineering team to complete the full post-mortem.

— Charles Lamanna, Microsoft executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform (CBS News)

Key Points

  • Public-service workarounds: British police said they could not receive online crime reports and urged people with urgent reports to phone; NHS staff turned to paper records [3][5].
  • Self-hosted systems: Organizations running Microsoft services on their own servers were less affected than customers relying on cloud-hosted services [17][30].
  • Outage reports: Downdetector recorded more than 7.4 million reports worldwide, peaking at nearly 20,000 a minute; these were outage reports, not a count of distinct affected users [17][36][9].
  • Business deadlines: Workhorse Group postponed its earnings release to Nov. 16, while Swiss luxury group Richemont briefly delayed its results [17][1].

Timeline

  • 12 November 2026: Exchange Online mailbox problems were logged before the wider Microsoft 365 disruption [40].
  • 13 November 2026: Microsoft reported partial recovery in some areas, followed by broader improvements in North America and Europe and encouraging recovery in Asia-Pacific [10].
  • 14 November 2026: Microsoft said it restored service to remaining affected customers and regions [40].

Technical Details

  • Email delivery timeout: Microsoft extended the email delivery timeout window to 48 hours while restoring systems to help prevent messages from being permanently lost during recovery [1].
  • Alternative health checks: Engineers validated an alternative method for checking server health to restore partial capacity and prepared automated tools to detect and restart unresponsive servers [1].

Industry Impact

  • Market workarounds: One Taiwanese financial company used fax to confirm trades, while European exchange operator Euronext reported email disruptions [17][36].

Historical Background

Microsoft had several other significant service disruptions during 2026: a January outage lasted nearly 10 hours, a July bug in its automated network-maintenance system affected Azure and Microsoft 365, and an Exchange Online outage on Aug. 31 also affected Teams, OneDrive and SharePoint [12].

Q&A

Q: Were all Microsoft apps affected?

A: No. The outage hit features tied to Exchange Online, while applications not connected to it remained operational [36].
