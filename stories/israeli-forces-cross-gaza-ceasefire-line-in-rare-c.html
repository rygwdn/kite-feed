Israeli forces cross Gaza ceasefire line in rare central incursion

Category: World

Israeli forces launched a ground operation in central Gaza on Wednesday, November 18, advancing tanks west of the Yellow Line near the Nuseirat and Al-Bureij refugee camps. The Israeli military said troops and Israel’s Shin Bet security service were trying to dismantle a tunnel that posed an immediate threat to its forces. Witnesses reported artillery fire and tanks moving along Salah al-Din road, which was closed in the affected area [8][5][7][11]. Israeli authorities warned residents to leave and told aid organizations to avoid the operation zone, prompting displaced families to flee again. Gaza’s Civil Defence agency said three Palestinians were killed in separate drone strikes on Wednesday. Israel said it targeted militants in two of the strikes and had no information about the third. It was unclear whether the strikes were linked to the ground operation [3][5][12]. Hamas called the incursion a ceasefire violation, while Israel said it was responding to violations by Hamas [16][7]. The operation was the first reported Israeli ground advance across the line into Hamas-controlled central Gaza since the October 2025 ceasefire began, residents told news organizations. Gaza health authorities report more than 1,600 Palestinian deaths since the truce took effect. A US-backed Board of Peace report issued the day before the incursion said Israel and Hamas had both failed to meet commitments to end hostilities [14][8][10].

Key Points

  • Area orders: COGAT designated residential blocks 660, 661 and 666 near the camps as the operation area, barred movement there and closed Salah al-Din road without giving a reopening time [24][29].
  • Truce toll: Gaza health authorities say at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began; Israel’s military has reported seven soldiers and one civilian contractor killed over the same period [16][3].
  • Peace report: The Board of Peace was expected to present its findings to the Security Council by the end of November [29].
  • Winter needs: The WFP warned that funding shortfalls and access restrictions put lives at risk as families faced the first nights of winter [1].

Perspectives

Israeli military: Said its troops remained deployed under the ceasefire arrangements and would act against immediate threats and attempts by Hamas to harm Israeli civilians or soldiers [8].

  • 18 November 2026: Israeli forces began the ground operation near Nuseirat and Al-Bureij [5][8]

Historical Background

The ceasefire terms initially left Israeli forces controlling about 53% of Gaza, while Israel later said its forces controlled more than 60% [20][7].

Humanitarian Impact

More than 1.9 million people—about 90% of Gaza’s population—remain displaced, and 1.4 million are living in tents or makeshift shelters, according to the UN. The IOM and WFP also warned that 1.4 million people face acute hunger and 1.98 million need shelter assistance [8][1].

Q&A

Q: What remains unresolved in the ceasefire plan’s next phase?

A: The second phase is intended to take further steps toward ending the conflict and establishing Gaza’s future governance; Hamas disarmament remains a key sticking point [6][12].
